# Multiplying and dividing fractions

::: formel "Multiplying fractions"
When multiplying

- **two fractions**, the numerators are multiplied together and the denominators are multiplied together
- **an integer and a fraction**, the integer is multiplied by the **numerator**.
:::

::: exempel "Example 1: Multiplication"
**Calculate<br>a)&nbsp;$\dfrac{8}{3} \cdot \dfrac{7}{2}$&emsp;&emsp;b)&nbsp;$3 \cdot \dfrac{4}{5}$**

::: handskrift
typ: brakmult
:::

::: textlosning
**a)** Numerator times numerator and denominator times denominator. Simplify
the answer if you can:

$$
\frac{8}{3} \cdot \frac{7}{2} = \frac{8 \cdot 7}{3 \cdot 2} = \frac{56}{6}
= \frac{56/2}{6/2} = \frac{28}{3}
$$

**Answer:** $\dfrac{28}{3}$

**b)** The integer is multiplied by the numerator:

$$
3 \cdot \frac{4}{5} = \frac{3 \cdot 4}{5} = \frac{12}{5}
$$

**Answer:** $\dfrac{12}{5}$
:::
:::

::: formel "Multiplying fractions with large numbers"
In some calculations the numbers in the numerator and/or denominator become large, and then
it is harder to simplify the fraction correctly. In that case, try **factorising**
the numerators and denominators and simplifying the fractions *before* you carry out
the multiplication.
:::

::: exempel "Example 2: Factorise and simplify first"
**Calculate $\dfrac{7}{36} \cdot \dfrac{6}{21}$ by factorising
the numerators and denominators and then simplifying.**

::: handskrift
typ: faktorisera
:::

::: textlosning
$$
\frac{7}{36} \cdot \frac{6}{21} = \frac{7 \cdot 6}{36 \cdot 21}
= \frac{7 \cdot 6}{6 \cdot 6 \cdot 7 \cdot 3}
$$

We can now simplify by 7 and 6, since those factors are in both the numerator
and the denominator. **NOTE!** When 7 and 6 are cancelled in the numerator, 1 is left,
since $\dfrac{7 \cdot 6}{7 \cdot 6} = 1$:

$$
\frac{7 \cdot 6}{6 \cdot 6 \cdot 7 \cdot 3} = \frac{1}{6 \cdot 3} = \frac{1}{18}
$$

**Answer:** $\dfrac{1}{18}$
:::
:::

## Dividing by fractions

::: formel "Dividing by fractions"
When dividing by fractions, you replace the large division sign with
multiplication and at the same time **invert** (swap the numerator and
denominator of) the fraction in the denominator. Then you calculate as usual.

The division sign can be drawn straight or slanted:

$$
\dfrac{\;\dfrac{a}{b}\;}{\;\dfrac{c}{d}\;} = \frac{a}{b} \Big/ \frac{c}{d}
$$

::: härledning "Proof: Dividing by fractions"
We prove the rule for dividing by fractions. Suppose we divide two
arbitrary fractions $\dfrac{a}{b}$ and $\dfrac{c}{d}$. To get 1 in the denominator
we expand the fraction by the inverse of the fraction in the denominator, that is, by
$\dfrac{d}{c}$:

$$
\dfrac{\;\dfrac{a}{b}\;}{\;\dfrac{c}{d}\;}
= \dfrac{\;\dfrac{a}{b} \cdot \dfrac{d}{c}\;}{\;\dfrac{c}{d} \cdot \dfrac{d}{c}\;}
= \dfrac{\;\dfrac{a}{b} \cdot \dfrac{d}{c}\;}{\;\dfrac{c \cdot d}{d \cdot c}\;}
= \dfrac{\;\dfrac{a}{b} \cdot \dfrac{d}{c}\;}{1}
= \frac{a}{b} \cdot \frac{d}{c}
$$

So
$\dfrac{\;\dfrac{a}{b}\;}{\;\dfrac{c}{d}\;} = \dfrac{a}{b} \cdot \dfrac{d}{c}$,
which was to be shown.
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 3: Division"
**Calculate<br>a)&nbsp;$\dfrac{\;\dfrac{3}{4}\;}{\;\dfrac{2}{5}\;}$&emsp;&emsp;b)&nbsp;$\dfrac{4}{5} \Big/ \dfrac{2}{7}$&emsp;&emsp;c)&nbsp;$5 \Big/ \dfrac{3}{4}$&emsp;&emsp;d)&nbsp;$\dfrac{2}{3} \Big/ 12$**

::: handskrift
typ: brakdiv
:::

::: textlosning
**a)** Replace division with multiplication and invert the fraction in the denominator:

$$
\dfrac{\;\dfrac{3}{4}\;}{\;\dfrac{2}{5}\;} = \frac{3}{4} \cdot \frac{5}{2} = \frac{15}{8}
$$

**Answer:** $\dfrac{15}{8}$

**b)** The same rule with a slanted division sign:

$$
\frac{4}{5} \Big/ \frac{2}{7} = \frac{4}{5} \cdot \frac{7}{2} = \frac{28}{10}
= \frac{28/2}{10/2} = \frac{14}{5}
$$

**Answer:** $\dfrac{14}{5}$

**c)** Here we only have a fraction in the denominator, but the same rule still
applies. Some people find it easier to rewrite 5 as a
fraction, that is, as $\dfrac{5}{1}$:

$$
5 \Big/ \frac{3}{4} = \frac{5}{1} \Big/ \frac{3}{4} = \frac{5}{1} \cdot \frac{4}{3} = \frac{20}{3}
$$

**Answer:** $\dfrac{20}{3}$

**d)** In a similar way as in part c, we rewrite 12 as
$\dfrac{12}{1}$ and then calculate as usual:

$$
\frac{2}{3} \Big/ 12 = \frac{2}{3} \Big/ \frac{12}{1} = \frac{2}{3} \cdot \frac{1}{12}
= \frac{2}{36} = \frac{2/2}{36/2} = \frac{1}{18}
$$

**Answer:** $\dfrac{1}{18}$
:::
:::

## A fraction of something

::: formel "A fraction of something"
When we want to calculate a fraction *of* something, we **multiply** by
the fraction.
:::

::: exempel "Example 4: A fraction of an amount"
**How much is $\dfrac{5}{7}$ of 63 kr?**

::: handskrift
typ: brakdel
:::

::: textlosning
$$
\frac{5}{7} \cdot 63 = \frac{5 \cdot 63}{7}
$$

We simplify by 7:

$$
\frac{5 \cdot 63/7}{7/7} = \frac{5 \cdot 9}{1} = 5 \cdot 9 = 45
$$

**Answer:** 45 kr
:::
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Multiplication"
- **Two fractions**: multiply numerator by numerator and denominator by denominator.
- **Integer times fraction**: multiply the integer by the **numerator**. The denominator
  stays the same.
:::

::: sampunkt "Simplify first"
- If the numbers get large: **factorise** the numerator and denominator and simplify
  **before** you multiply.
- That gives smaller numbers to handle and less risk of mistakes.
:::

::: sampunkt "Division"
- Replace the division sign with **multiplication** and **invert** the fraction in
  the denominator.
- $\dfrac{\frac{a}{b}}{\frac{c}{d}} = \dfrac{a}{b} \cdot \dfrac{d}{c}$
- The proof: expand by $\dfrac{d}{c}$, and the denominator becomes 1.
:::

::: sampunkt "Integers involved"
- An integer can be written as a fraction with denominator 1:
  $5 = \dfrac{5}{1}$.
- Then the usual rules apply, so
  $5 \Big/ \dfrac{3}{4} = \dfrac{5}{1} \cdot \dfrac{4}{3} = \dfrac{20}{3}$.
:::

::: sampunkt "A fraction of something"
- "Of" means **multiply**.
- One third of 60 is $\dfrac{1}{3} \cdot 60 = 20$.
:::
:::
