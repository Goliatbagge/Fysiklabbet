# Summary: Geometry

This chapter was about geometry: angles and how they are formed by a
transversal, the angle sum in triangles and polygons, how to carry out
a logical proof with implication and equivalence, Pythagoras' theorem,
coordinate geometry, and similarity, congruence and the theorems that
apply to triangles and circles (the top triangle theorem, the transversal
theorem, the angle bisector theorem, the inscribed angle theorem and the intersecting chords theorem). After
the chapter you should be able to justify every step of a geometric calculation
briefly and carry out simple proofs.

## Concepts to know

- **transversal**: a line that crosses at least two other lines.
- **pairs of angles at parallel lines**: corresponding angles,
  vertically opposite angles and alternate angles are equal in pairs; angles on a straight line
  add up to 180°.
- **bisector**: a ray that divides an angle in half.
- **polygon**: a closed figure that consists only of line segments and
  vertices; three vertices give a triangle, four a quadrilateral and so on.
- **right-angled, isosceles and equilateral triangle**: classification by
  angles/sides: a right-angled triangle has a right angle, an isosceles triangle has at least two
  equal sides (and equal base angles), an equilateral triangle has all sides and
  angles (60°) equal.
- **implication (⟹)**: a statement that leads to another, but not
  necessarily the other way round.
- **equivalence (⟺)**: an implication that holds in both directions.
- **counterexample**: a single example that is enough to show that an
  implication is false.
- **axiom, definition and theorem**: an axiom is a basic statement without
  proof, a definition is an agreement about a concept and a theorem
  is a proved statement.
- **hypotenuse and legs**: in a right-angled triangle the hypotenuse is
  the longest side (opposite the right angle); the other two sides
  are called legs.
- **coordinate geometry**: studying geometric figures with the help of
  a coordinate system, for example with the distance formula and the midpoint formula.
- **similar figures (~)**: figures with the same shape but possibly
  different size, rotation or reflection.
- **scale factor**: the number that states how much larger/smaller a
  similar figure is compared with another.
- **congruent triangles (≅)**: triangles that are similar AND the same
  size; decided with the conditions SAS, SSS or ASA.
- **inscribed angle, central angle and chord**: the inscribed angle has its vertex
  on the circumference of the circle, the central angle at the centre; the chord is a
  line segment between two points on the circumference.
- **cyclic quadrilateral**: a quadrilateral whose four vertices all lie on
  the circumference of a circle.

## Formulas

::: formel "The formulas of the chapter"
**Angle sums**

$$
\text{Triangle: } 180° \qquad \text{Quadrilateral: } 360°
$$

**Implication and equivalence**

$$
A \implies B \qquad A \iff B
$$

$A \implies B$ means that $A$ leads to $B$ (but not necessarily
the other way round). $A \iff B$ means that the implication holds in both directions.

**Numbers in proofs**

$$
\text{Even number: } 2k \qquad \text{Odd number: } 2k + 1
$$

$$
n, \quad n + 1, \quad n + 2, \quad \ldots
$$

where $k$ and $n$ are integers. Used to prove statements about
divisibility and sequences of numbers.

**Pythagoras' theorem**

$$
a^2 + b^2 = c^2
$$

where $a$ and $b$ are the legs and $c$ is the hypotenuse of a right-angled
triangle. The relationship is an equivalence: $a^2 + b^2 = c^2 \iff$ the triangle
is right-angled.

**The distance formula**

$$
d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}
$$

where $d$ is the distance between the points $(x_1, y_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2)$.

**The midpoint formula**

$$
(x_m, y_m) = \left( \frac{x_1 + x_2}{2},\ \frac{y_1 + y_2}{2} \right)
$$

where $(x_m, y_m)$ is the midpoint between $(x_1, y_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2)$.

**The top triangle theorem**

$$
\frac{AD}{AB} = \frac{AE}{AC} = \frac{DE}{BC}
$$

where a parallel transversal through $D$ and $E$ forms a top triangle
$ADE$ that is similar to the triangle $ABC$.

**The transversal theorem**

$$
\frac{AD}{DB} = \frac{AE}{EC}
$$

where the parallel transversal divides the sides $AB$ and $AC$ in the same
ratio.

**The angle bisector theorem**

$$
\frac{AD}{BD} = \frac{AC}{BC}
$$

where the bisector from the vertex $C$ divides the opposite side $AB$ at the point
$D$.

**The inscribed angle theorem**

$$
y = 2x
$$

where $x$ is the inscribed angle and $y$ is the central angle on the same
arc.

**The intersecting chords theorem**

$$
a \cdot b = c \cdot d
$$

where $a, b$ and $c, d$ are the parts of the two chords at the
point of intersection.

**Opposite angles in a cyclic quadrilateral**

$$
u + v = 180°
$$

where $u$ and $v$ are opposite angles in a quadrilateral inscribed in a
circle.
:::

## Important relationships and methods

- At a transversal through two parallel lines, corresponding
  angles, vertically opposite angles and alternate angles are equal, while
  angles on a straight line together make 180°. Always justify which pair of angles
  you are using.
- An implication is shown to be false with a single counterexample; to decide
  whether it holds in both directions, you investigate each direction
  separately.
- In a proof, every step must be justified on the basis of axioms, definitions
  or previously known theorems; finish with QED.
- Even numbers are written $2k$ and odd numbers $2k + 1$; consecutive
  numbers are written $n, n + 1, n + 2, \ldots$, which is useful in
  proofs in number theory.
- To decide that TWO polygons (more than three vertices) are
  similar, BOTH conditions are required: corresponding angles equal AND
  the side ratio the same. For TRIANGLES, ONE of the
  conditions is enough, since the third angle follows from the angle sum.
- An unknown side in similar figures can be determined either by
  setting up an equation with side ratios, or by first
  calculating the scale factor and then multiplying.
- Two triangles are congruent if they match by SAS, SSS or
  ASA. In figures, equal sides are marked with the same number of tick marks and
  equal angles with the same angle arc.
- Use the top triangle theorem when the base of the triangle is among the line segments you
  are calculating with; otherwise the transversal theorem is simpler and quicker.
- Corollaries of the inscribed angle theorem: inscribed angles on the same arc
  are equal, and an inscribed angle on a semicircular arc is always 90°.
- In circles the following also applies: the angle between a tangent and the radius to
  the point of contact is 90°, and two radii always form an isosceles
  triangle.
- Justify every step of a calculation briefly in brackets, for example
  "(alternate angles)" or "(angle sum of a triangle)". It is a
  central part of solving geometry tasks correctly.

## Before the test

- Can you name angles based on size (acute, right, obtuse, straight)
  and based on a transversal through parallel lines (corresponding,
  vertically opposite, alternate angles and angles on a straight line)?
- Can you determine the angle sum in a triangle (180°) and in
  polygons with more vertices by dividing them into triangles?
- Do you know the difference between implication (⟹) and equivalence (⟺), and can
  you show that an implication is false with a counterexample?
- Can you distinguish between an axiom, a definition and a theorem, and carry out a
  complete proof where every step is justified?
- Do you know Pythagoras' theorem, and can you decide whether a triangle is right-angled
  from the lengths of its sides?
- Do you know the distance formula and the midpoint formula, and can you calculate the distance
  and the midpoint between two points in a coordinate system?
- Do you know the conditions for two polygons to be similar (both:
  angles AND side ratio) and for triangles (one condition is
  enough)?
- Can you determine an unknown side in similar figures, both with
  an equation and with a scale factor?
- Do you know the three congruence conditions (SAS, SSS, ASA), and can you decide whether two
  triangles are congruent?
- Do you know the top triangle theorem, the transversal theorem and the angle bisector theorem,
  and do you know when to use which?
- Do you know the inscribed angle theorem and its corollaries (inscribed angles on the same
  arc, inscribed angle on a semicircular arc)?
- Do you know the intersecting chords theorem and the theorem about opposite angles in a cyclic
  quadrilateral?
