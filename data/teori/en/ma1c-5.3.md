# Correlation and causation

If there is a relationship between two variables we have a **correlation**.
Whether there is a relationship can be investigated with a **scatter diagram**.
If the points in a scatter diagram lie clearly on a line, the
correlation is **strong**. If the points lie around a line, but spread out,
the correlation is **weak**. If the points lie around a line with a positive
slope, the correlation is **positive**. If the points lie around a line
with a negative slope, the correlation is **negative**.

a scatter diagram. The daughters' heights in cm are marked on the *y*-axis and

## Causation

A cause-and-effect relationship between two variables is called **causation**.

We return to the example of daughters' and mothers' heights. Is the *cause*
of the daughters becoming tall that the mothers are tall? The answer is *yes*,
since the genes from the mother (partly) decide how tall the daughter becomes. So
there is causation.

::: exempel "Example 1: Life expectancy and the internet"
**A scatter diagram of average life expectancy (years, *y*-axis) and access
to the internet (%, *x*-axis) for a few different countries shows points spread
around a rising line. Decide whether there is<br>a) correlation, and if so
what type&emsp;&emsp;b) causation.**

::: handskrift
typ: korrelation
:::

::: textlosning
**a)** The measured values are spread around a line with a positive slope.

**Answer:** Weak positive correlation.

**b)** We think "Is the cause of the increased average life expectancy that
people have access to the internet?". The answer is *no*.
Internet access does not give a longer life. This is a so-called
**spurious correlation**.

The likely explanation is that rich countries (where there is good access
to the internet) to a greater extent have better health care, which in
turn gives a longer life.

**Answer:** No, there is no causation.
:::
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Correlation"
- A **relationship** between two variables.
- Investigated with a **scatter diagram**, where each point is a
  pair of measurements.
:::

::: sampunkt "Strong or weak"
- **Strong**: the points lie clearly along a line.
- **Weak**: the points lie around a line but spread out.
- **No correlation**: the points lie without a pattern.
:::

::: sampunkt "Positive or negative"
- **Positive**: the line slopes upwards. If one increases, the other increases.
- **Negative**: the line slopes downwards. If one increases, the other decreases.
:::

::: sampunkt "Causation"
- A **cause-and-effect relationship**: one variable actually causes the other.
- Mothers' and daughters' heights have causation, since the genes decide.
:::

::: sampunkt "Correlation is not causation"
- Two variables can go together without one causing the other.
- Average life expectancy and internet access correlate, but the internet does not make anyone
  older. Both depend on the country's **prosperity**.
- Always ask: is there a third, underlying cause?
:::
:::
