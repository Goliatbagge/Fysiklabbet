# Amplitude and period

The distance from the equilibrium position to the top (or bottom) of the curve is called the
**amplitude**. You can also see the amplitude as half the difference between
the function's largest and smallest value.

The smallest angle after which a graph repeats itself is called the **period**. You can also
see the period as the distance in the $x$-direction between two corresponding points on the curve,
for example the distance between two peaks.

Both of the basic functions $y = \sin x$ and $y = \cos x$ have the amplitude 1 and
the period $360^\circ$ (in degrees) or $2\pi$ (in radians).

## The coefficient in front of the function changes the amplitude

We investigate how a coefficient in front of the trigonometric function
affects the graph. Since every value of $\sin x$ is multiplied by 2 in
the function $y = 2 \sin x$, the position in the $y$-direction becomes twice as large at every point.
The amplitude of $y = 2 \sin x$ is therefore 2, and in the same way $y = 3 \sin x$ has
the amplitude 3.

If we draw $y = -2 \sin x$ we see that the graph is a **reflection** of
$y = 2 \sin x$ in the $x$-axis: every positive $y$-coordinate becomes negative and
vice versa. The amplitude is still 2, since an amplitude can never be negative.
In general, $y = A \sin x$ has the amplitude $|A|$.

## The coefficient inside the argument changes the period

The coefficient inside the argument instead affects the period. Since every
$x$-value in the angle is multiplied by 2 in the function $y = \sin 2x$, the
curve oscillates twice as fast. The period becomes $\dfrac{360^\circ}{2} = 180^\circ$.
In the same way $y = \sin 3x$ has the period $\dfrac{360^\circ}{3} = 120^\circ$,
since it oscillates three times as fast. In general, $y = \sin Bx$ has
the period $\dfrac{360^\circ}{B}$ (in degrees) or $\dfrac{2\pi}{B}$ (in radians).

::: formel "Amplitude and period"
The function $y = A \sin Bx$ has the **amplitude** $|A|$ and the **period**

$$
\frac{360^\circ}{B} \quad \text{(in degrees)} \qquad \text{or} \qquad \frac{2\pi}{B} \quad \text{(in radians)}
$$
:::

Try for yourself how the coefficients shape the curve. Drag the slider for the amplitude
$A$ and see how the height of the curve changes, and drag the slider for $B$ and see how
the period gets shorter as the curve oscillates faster. With $A = 2$ and $B = 1$ you get
$y = 2 \sin x$.

::: exempel "Example 1: Find the amplitude and period"
**Find the amplitude and the period of the function $y = 5 \sin 8x$.**

::: handskrift
typ: amplitudperiod
:::

::: textlosning
The coefficient in front of the sine is the amplitude, and the coefficient in front of $x$ controls
the period via $\dfrac{360^\circ}{B}$.

$$
\text{amplitude} = 5
$$

$$
\text{period} = \frac{360^\circ}{8} = 45^\circ
$$

**Answer:** The amplitude is 5 and the period 45°.
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 2: Negative coefficient"
**Find the amplitude and the period of the function $y = -25 \sin 5x$.**

::: handskrift
typ: negamplitud
:::

::: textlosning
A negative coefficient in front of the sine reflects the graph in the $x$-axis, but the amplitude
is a distance and can never be negative. We therefore take the absolute value of
the coefficient.

$$
\text{amplitude} = |-25| = 25
$$

$$
\text{period} = \frac{360^\circ}{5} = 72^\circ
$$

**Answer:** The amplitude is 25 and the period 72°.
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 3: Division in the argument"
**Find the amplitude and the period of the function $y = \dfrac{3}{5}\cos\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right)$.**

::: handskrift
typ: divargument
:::

::: textlosning
Note that the argument contains a division. We rewrite it as a
multiplication to find the coefficient $B$:

$$
\frac{x}{2} = \frac{1}{2}x = 0.5x
$$

$$
\text{amplitude} = \frac{3}{5} = 0.6
$$

$$
\text{period} = \frac{360^\circ}{0.5} = 720^\circ
$$

**Answer:** The amplitude is 0.6 and the period 720°.
:::
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Amplitude and period"
- The **amplitude** is the distance from the equilibrium position to the peak, that is half the difference between the largest and smallest value.
- The **period** is the smallest angle after which the graph repeats itself, for example the distance between two peaks.
- $y = \sin x$ and $y = \cos x$ have the amplitude 1 and the period $360^\circ$, that is $2\pi$.
:::

::: sampunkt "The formula"
- $y = A \sin Bx$ has the **amplitude** $|A|$.
- The period is $\dfrac{360^\circ}{B}$ in degrees, or $\dfrac{2\pi}{B}$ in radians.
- The coefficient **in front of** the function controls the height, the coefficient **inside** the argument controls the pace.
:::

::: sampunkt "Negative coefficient"
- A negative $A$ **reflects** the curve in the $x$-axis.
- The amplitude is still positive, since it is a distance. Hence the absolute value.
- $y = -25\sin 5x$ has the amplitude 25 and the period $72^\circ$.
:::

::: sampunkt "Division in the argument"
- Rewrite the division as a multiplication to find $B$: $\dfrac{x}{2} = 0.5x$.
- A $B$ less than 1 gives a **longer** period: $\dfrac{360^\circ}{0.5} = 720^\circ$.
- A large $B$ means that the curve oscillates faster and the period is short.
:::
