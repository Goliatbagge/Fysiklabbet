# Gravitational force and normal force

## Gravitational force

[[The gravitational force|The force that acts between all masses. At the Earth's surface it is called the gravitational force (weight).]] from the Earth is called **the Earth's pull** or
**[[the gravitational force|The force with which the Earth pulls all objects towards its centre. The gravitational force gives an object its weight and is directed straight down towards the centre of the Earth.]]**.

The amount of matter (or a measure of the [[inertia|An object's resistance to a change in its motion. Larger mass → larger inertia.]]) of an object is called its **mass**,
is written *m* and has the [[SI unit|The International System of Units, with seven base units (m, kg, s, A, K, mol, cd).]] kg (kilogram). The mass of an object is
**the same** whether it is on the Earth or on the Moon, since the amount of
matter in the object is unchanged.

The gravitational force on an object is called its **weight**, is written $F_G$ and has, just
like all [[forces|An influence that can change the motion or shape of an object. SI unit newton (N). A vector quantity.]], the SI unit N (newton). The weight of an object is **different**
depending on whether it is on the Earth or the Moon. *The larger the mass of a celestial body,
the larger the gravitational force it gives rise to.* So the weight of an object is
larger on the Earth than on the Moon, since the Earth has a larger mass than
the Moon. How large the gravitational force is on each kilogram is called the **gravitational field strength**,
is written *g*, has the SI unit N/kg and in Sweden is 9.82 N/kg.

::: formel "Gravitational force"
$$
F_G = m \cdot g
$$

where

- $F_G$ = gravitational force or weight (N)
- *m* = mass (kg)
- *g* = gravitational field strength (9.82 N/kg in Sweden)

::: härledning "Gravitational field strength and acceleration due to gravity"
Does the numerical value 9.82 look familiar? We already know that [[the acceleration due to gravity|The acceleration that a freely falling object gets from the gravitational force, about 9.82 m/s² at the Earth's surface. Written *g*.]] in
Sweden is 9.82 m/s². Gravitational field strength and acceleration due to gravity basically stand for
**exactly the same thing**, and this can be derived with [[Newton's second law|Resultant force = mass × acceleration: *F* = *m* · *a*.]].

For an object that falls freely, the only force acting is
the gravitational force $F_G = m g$. This is then the [[resultant force|The sum of all the forces acting on an object (a vector sum). Gives the object its acceleration.]], which according to
Newton's second law can be written $F_R = m a$. This gives

$$
F_R = F_G \quad\Rightarrow\quad m a = m g \quad\Rightarrow\quad a = g
$$

This means that if you have the acceleration due to gravity, the gravitational field strength is the same,
and vice versa. Even their units N/kg and m/s² stand for the same thing:

$$
1\ \mathrm{N} = 1\ \mathrm{kg \cdot m/s^2}
$$

$$
1\ \frac{\mathrm{N}}{\mathrm{kg}}
= 1\ \frac{\mathrm{kg \cdot m}}{\mathrm{s^2 \cdot kg}}
= 1\ \frac{\mathrm{m}}{\mathrm{s^2}}
\qquad \text{that is}\quad 1\ \mathrm{N/kg} = 1\ \mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
:::
:::

## Normal force

When an object is in contact with a surface, the surface exerts a force on the object that
prevents it from passing through the surface. This force is called the **[[normal force|The force a surface exerts perpendicular to an object resting on it.]]**,
is written $F_N$ and always acts **perpendicular to the surface**. (Normal is a
mathematical term for a line that is perpendicular to a plane.)

If we put a weight on a table, a gravitational force $F_G$ acts on it downwards.
Since the weight is standing still (is at rest), the resultant
force must be 0, according to [[Newton's first law|A body remains at rest or in uniform motion if the resultant force is zero.]]. So there must be an
upward force that is exactly as large. That is the normal force $F_N$, and it
stands for the force from the table on the weight.

::: textlosning
The forces acting on the boom are its own weight $F_{G\text{boom}}$ (downwards), the weight
of the counterweight $F_{G\text{weight}}$ (downwards) and the normal force from the stand $F_N$ (upwards).
Since the boom is at rest, the resultant force must be 0, that is the
downward forces must be as large as the upward forces.

Equilibrium of forces gives

$$
F_N = F_{G\text{boom}} + F_{G\text{weight}}
$$

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
F_{G\text{boom}} = m_\text{boom} \cdot g = 12\ \mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.82\ \mathrm{N/kg} = 117.84\ \mathrm{N} \\
F_{G\text{weight}} = m_\text{weight} \cdot g = 24\ \mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.82\ \mathrm{N/kg} = 235.68\ \mathrm{N}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
F_N = 117.84 + 235.68 = 353.52\ \mathrm{N} \approx 350\ \mathrm{N} = 0.35\ \mathrm{kN}
$$

**Answer:** 0.35 kN
:::
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Mass and weight"
- **Mass** ($m$, kg) is the amount of matter. It is **the same** on the Earth
  and on the Moon.
- **Weight** ($F_G$, N) is the gravitational force on the object. It is **different** on
  the Earth and the Moon, since the celestial bodies have different masses.
:::

::: sampunkt "Gravitational force"
- $F_G = m \cdot g$
- **The gravitational field strength** $g$ is the gravitational force per kilogram. In Sweden
  $g = 9.82\ \mathrm{N/kg}$.
- The larger the mass of a celestial body, the larger the gravitational force it gives.
:::

::: sampunkt "Gravitational field strength and acceleration due to gravity"
- They stand for **exactly the same thing**, and have the same numerical value 9.82.
- In free fall the gravitational force is the only force, so
  $ma = mg$ gives $a = g$.
- The units are also the same:
  $1\ \mathrm{N/kg} = 1\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$.
:::

::: sampunkt "Normal force"
- **The normal force** $F_N$ is the force from a surface on the object, which
  prevents it from going through the surface.
- It **always acts perpendicular to the contact surface**. On an inclined plane
  it therefore points at an angle, not straight up.
- The point of application lies in the **contact surface**.
:::

::: sampunkt "Typical task"
- An object at rest on a horizontal surface: $F_R = 0$ gives
  $F_N = F_G$.
- If several weights rest on the same support, the support carries them all:
  $F_N = F_{G1} + F_{G2}$.
- Calculate each weight with $F_G = m \cdot g$ and round only at the end.
:::
:::
