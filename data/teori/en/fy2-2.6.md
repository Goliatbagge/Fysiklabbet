# Resonance

If a system is left alone after it has been set oscillating, it performs
a **free oscillation**. The [[frequency|The number of oscillations per second. SI unit hertz (Hz) = 1/s.]] of the free oscillation is called
the **natural frequency**.

The opposite of free oscillation is **forced oscillation**, where a system
oscillates with a certain frequency given by an external periodic [[force|An influence that can change an object's motion or shape. SI unit newton (N). A vector quantity.]]. This is
how, for example, an ordinary electrodynamic loudspeaker works: a paper membrane
is attached to a coil that is forced to vibrate with a certain frequency. The coil can
move in a fixed [[magnetic field|The region around a magnet or a current-carrying conductor where other magnetic objects are affected by a force.]], and changes in the [[current|Amount of charge per unit time: *I* = *Q* / *t*. SI unit ampere (A).]] in the coil make its position
change.

::: formel "Natural frequency"
$$
f = \frac{1}{T}
$$

where

- *f* = natural frequency (Hz)
- *T* = [[period|The time for one complete oscillation or one complete wave: *T* = 1 / *f*.]] (s)
:::

Examples of natural frequencies are the sound frequencies produced when you knock
on a wall or ping a glass. The natural frequency of a wall is low, and
that is why bass notes are heard well through walls (to the annoyance of the neighbours).

## Resonance

If energy is supplied to an oscillation at the right moment, with the same
frequency as the natural frequency, the [[amplitude|The maximum displacement of a wave from the equilibrium position.]] of the oscillation increases more and more. This
amplification of the oscillation is called **resonance**.

::: kuriosa "Examples of resonance"
Some examples of resonance phenomena:

- the guitar's **sound box**, which amplifies the sound
- a **swing** that is pushed just when it has turned and is on its way down
- **bass sound** passing through a wall
- a **wine glass that shatters** when exposed to loud sound
- **loose parts in a car** that start to rattle at a certain [[velocity|Displacement per unit time, with direction (a vector). SI unit m/s.]]
- **"freezing" lamp posts** that vibrate in the wind
- the collapse of the **Tacoma Bridge** (1940): the wind set the bridge into resonance
  and it was torn apart
- **ground resonance**, which can tear helicopters apart (can be avoided
  by lifting off the ground)
:::

The collapse of the Tacoma bridge was also caught on film. The bridge, which had been nicknamed
"Galloping Gertie" for its rolling oscillations, twisted more and more violently
in the wind on 7 November 1940 until the roadway broke and plunged into the strait.
The film is free to show and one of the most famous clips in the history of physics:

::: exempel "Example 1: The natural frequency of a guitar string"
**A guitar string oscillating freely performs 100 oscillations in 1.22
seconds. What is the natural frequency of the string?**

::: handskrift
typ: egenfrekvens
:::

::: textlosning
We set up the formula for natural frequency.

$$
f = \frac{1}{T} \qquad (1)
$$

The string performs 100 oscillations in 1.22 seconds, so the period is

$$
T = \frac{1.22\ \mathrm{s}}{100} = 0.0122\ \mathrm{s}
$$

Substitution into (1) gives

$$
f = \frac{1}{0.0122} = 81.967\ldots\ \mathrm{Hz} \approx 82\ \mathrm{Hz}
$$

**Answer:** 82 Hz
:::
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Free and forced oscillation"
- **Free oscillation**: the system oscillates by itself after being set
  in motion.
- Its frequency is called the **natural frequency**, $f = \dfrac{1}{T}$.
- **Forced oscillation**: an external periodic force determines the frequency,
  as in a loudspeaker.
:::

::: sampunkt "Resonance"
- If energy is supplied at **the right moment**, with **the same frequency as the
  natural frequency**, the amplitude grows more and more.
- That amplification is called **resonance**.
:::

::: sampunkt "Examples of resonance"
- The guitar's **sound box** amplifies the sound.
- A **swing** pushed exactly at the turning point.
- **Bass notes** passing through walls, since the natural frequency of the wall is low.
- A **wine glass that shatters** from loud sound.
- **Loose parts in a car** that rattle at a certain speed.
:::

::: sampunkt "When resonance becomes dangerous"
- **Tacoma Bridge**, 1940: the wind set the bridge into resonance and it was torn
  apart.
- **Ground resonance** can tear a helicopter apart. It is avoided by
  lifting off the ground.
:::
:::
