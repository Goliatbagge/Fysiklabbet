# Angles in triangles and polygons

A closed figure that consists only of line segments and vertices is called a
**polygon**. A polygon with three vertices is called a **triangle**. A
polygon with four vertices is called a **quadrilateral** (tetragon), a
polygon with five vertices is called a **pentagon** and so
on.

Triangles are often described by their angles and sides.

The angle sum in a

- triangle is 180°
- quadrilateral is 360°

::: exempel "Example 1: The angle sum in a pentagon"
**What is the angle sum in a pentagon?**

::: handskrift
typ: femhorning
:::

::: textlosning
We draw an arbitrary (any) pentagon. Then we start from one
vertex and draw line segments to all the other vertices. Finally we mark
all the angles.

::: figur
<svg viewBox="14 18 136 124" width="245" height="223" xmlns="http://www.w3.org/2000/svg" role="img" font-family="Poppins, system-ui, sans-serif" aria-label="A pentagon where diagonals from one vertex divide it into three triangles."><polygon points="80,25 22.9,66.5 44.7,133.5 115.3,133.5 137.1,66.5" fill="none" stroke="#1f2530" stroke-width="1.8"/><line x1="80" y1="25" x2="44.7" y2="133.5" stroke="#1f2530" stroke-width="1.2" stroke-dasharray="6 5"/><line x1="80" y1="25" x2="115.3" y2="133.5" stroke="#1f2530" stroke-width="1.2" stroke-dasharray="6 5"/></svg>
:::

All the angles in the pentagon have been marked. We see that they are made up
of three triangles. Since the angle sum in a triangle is 180°,
the angle sum in a pentagon must be

$$
3 \cdot 180° = 540°
$$

**Answer:** 540°
:::
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Polygon"
- A **closed** figure made only of line segments and vertices.
- Three vertices: triangle. Four: quadrilateral. Five: pentagon.
:::

::: sampunkt "Types of triangle"
- **Right-angled**: has a right angle, and Pythagoras' theorem applies.
- **Isosceles**: at least two sides of equal length, and **the base angles are
  equal**.
- **Equilateral**: all sides of equal length and **all angles $60^\circ$**.
:::

::: sampunkt "Angle sums"
- Triangle: $180^\circ$.
- Quadrilateral: $360^\circ$.
:::

::: sampunkt "The angle sum in a polygon"
- Draw line segments from **one** vertex to all the others. The figure is then divided into
  triangles.
- A polygon with $n$ vertices is divided into $n - 2$ triangles.
- The angle sum is $(n - 2) \cdot 180^\circ$.
- Pentagon: $3 \cdot 180^\circ = 540^\circ$.
:::
:::
