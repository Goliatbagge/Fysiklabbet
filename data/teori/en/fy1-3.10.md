# Example: Pulleys

A wheel with a rim that a thread, line or rope runs around is called a **pulley**. Unless
otherwise stated, a pulley is assumed to turn without frictional forces.

::: tips "Remember"
The [[tension|The force acting along a taut rope, line or spring.]] in a string is **the same throughout the string** and at its
attachment points.
:::

**What a) [[acceleration|How quickly the velocity changes. SI unit m/s². A vector quantity.]] do the masses get, b) tension is there in the string?**

::: handskrift
typ: trissor
:::

::: textlosning
**a)** The acceleration is determined by [[Newton's second law|Resultant force = mass × acceleration: *F* = *m* · *a*.]].

$$
F_R = m \cdot a \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad a = \frac{F_R}{m}
$$

We first look at **the whole system**. The weight of the 3-kilogram mass $F_{G3}$ pulls
to the right and the weight of the 1-kilogram mass $F_{G1}$ pulls to the left, so

$$
F_R = F_{G3} - F_{G1} = m_3 \cdot g - m_1 \cdot g
= 3.0 \cdot 9.82 - 1.0 \cdot 9.82 = 19.64\ \mathrm{N}
$$

The mass of the whole system $m_\text{tot}$ is given by

$$
m_\text{tot} = m_3 + m_1 = 3.0 + 1.0 = 4.0\ \mathrm{kg}
$$

Substituting gives

$$
a = \frac{19.64}{4.0} = 4.91\ \mathrm{m/s^2} \approx 4.9\ \mathrm{m/s^2}
$$

**Answer:** 4.9 m/s²

**b)** To determine the [[force|An influence that can change an object's motion or shape. SI unit newton (N). A vector quantity.]] in the string we look at **one of the masses**,
for example the 1-kilogram mass. We draw the forces on the mass. Since it will
accelerate upwards, the tension in the string upwards must be larger than the
[[gravitational force|The force with which the Earth pulls all objects towards its centre. The gravitational force gives an object its weight and points straight down towards the centre of the Earth.]] downwards.

We write an expression for the [[resultant force|The sum of all forces acting on an object (vector sum). Gives the object its acceleration.]] and solve for
the tension.

$$
F_R = F_S - F_{G1} \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad F_S = F_R + F_{G1}
$$

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
F_R = m_1 \cdot a = 1.0 \cdot 4.91 = 4.91\ \mathrm{N} \quad (\text{NB! Only the mass of the 1-kilogram mass}) \\
F_{G1} = m_1 \cdot g = 1.0 \cdot 9.82 = 9.82\ \mathrm{N}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
F_S = 4.91 + 9.82 = 14.73\ \mathrm{N} \approx 15\ \mathrm{N}
$$

**Answer:** 15 N
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Pulley"
- A **pulley** is a wheel that a line runs over. It is assumed to turn without
  friction unless otherwise stated.
- **The tension is the same throughout the string**, including at the attachment points.
:::

::: sampunkt "Step 1: the acceleration from the whole system"
- Treat the connected masses as **one single body**.
- Resultant force: the driving weight minus the braking one,
  $F_R = m_3 \cdot g - m_1 \cdot g$.
- The mass is **all of them together**:
  $m_\text{tot} = m_1 + m_3$.
- $a = \dfrac{F_R}{m_\text{tot}}$
:::

::: sampunkt "Step 2: the tension from ONE mass"
- Now look at **one single** mass and draw the forces on just that one.
- The mass that accelerates upwards: the tension must be **larger** than
  the gravitational force.
- $F_R = F_S - F_{G1}$ gives $F_S = F_R + F_{G1}$.
- Here **only the mass of that one body** is used in $F_R = m_1 \cdot a$. That is the
  most common mistake.
:::

::: sampunkt "Check"
- Calculate the tension from the other mass too. The answer should be the same.
- The tension always ends up **between** the two weights.
:::
:::
