# Diffraction and interference

## Diffraction

called **[[diffraction|When waves bend around corners or spread out after passing through a narrow opening.]]**.

## Interference

When waves affect each other as they collide, this is called **[[interference|When two waves meet and reinforce or cancel each other depending on their phase.]]**. It
occurs, for example, when a wave meets an obstacle with **several openings**.

Lines in an interference pattern where the [[amplitude|The maximum displacement of a wave from the equilibrium position.]] of the waves is 0, that is,
where the particles of the wave are completely still, are called **nodal lines**. On nodal lines
the waves meet **in antiphase** (a crest from one opening always meets a
trough from the other). When two waves cancel each other according to
the principle of superposition, this is called **destructive interference**.

Lines midway between the nodal lines, where the waves reinforce each other
as much as possible, are called **antinodal lines**. When two waves reinforce each other,
this is called **constructive interference**.

::: tips "Path difference on antinodal and nodal lines"
The path difference from a point on a

- **nodal line** to each of the openings is **a half-integer number** of wavelengths:
  $\dfrac{\lambda}{2},\ \dfrac{3\lambda}{2},\ \dfrac{5\lambda}{2},\ \ldots$
  (the waves meet in antiphase).
- **antinodal line** to each of the openings is **a whole number** of
  wavelengths: $0,\ \lambda,\ 2\lambda,\ 3\lambda,\ \ldots$ (the waves
  meet in phase).
:::

::: formel "Constructive interference (on antinodal lines)"
$$
\Delta s = n \cdot \lambda
$$

where

- Δ*s* = path difference (m)
- *n* = order number of the antinodal line (0, 1, 2, …)
- *λ* = [[wavelength|The distance between two crests (or other identical points) in a wave. Denoted *λ*. SI unit metre (m).]] (m)
:::

::: formel "Destructive interference (on nodal lines)"
$$
\Delta s = \left(n + \tfrac{1}{2}\right) \cdot \lambda
$$

where

- Δ*s* = path difference (m)
- *n* = order number of the nodal line (0, 1, 2, …)
- *λ* = wavelength (m)
:::

**When we walk from point C (on the centre line between A and B) to point E,
the loudness decreases and reaches a minimum at D (opposite loudspeaker B).
After that the loudness increases as we continue walking from D towards E. What
[[frequency|Number of oscillations per second. SI unit hertz (Hz) = 1/s.]] did the tone emitted by the loudspeakers have?**

typ: hogtalare

::: textlosning
We solve for the frequency from the formula for wave speed $v = f \cdot \lambda$:

$$
f = \frac{v}{\lambda} \qquad (1)
$$

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
v = 340\ \mathrm{m/s}\ (\text{the speed of sound in air}) \\
\lambda = \ ?
\end{array} \right]
$$

We must determine the wavelength λ. At C (midway between A and B) we are on
**the 0th antinodal line**. When we reach D we reach our first minimum,
so we are on **the 0th nodal line** and have destructive interference. We set
up the formula for destructive interference and solve for λ:

$$
\Delta s = \left(n + \tfrac{1}{2}\right) \cdot \lambda \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad \lambda = \frac{\Delta s}{n + \tfrac{1}{2}} \qquad (2)
$$

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
\Delta s = \ ? \\
n = 0\ (\text{we are on the 0th nodal line})
\end{array} \right]
$$

We must determine Δ*s*. For the path difference we have

$$
\Delta s = AD - BD \qquad (3)
$$

By Pythagoras' theorem: $AD^{2} = AB^{2} + BD^{2}$. Since C is midway between
A and B and CD = 3.2 m, we have $AB = 2 \cdot 3.2 = 6.4$ m.

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
AD = \sqrt{AB^{2} + BD^{2}} = \sqrt{6.4^{2} + 12^{2}} = 13.6\ \mathrm{m} \\
BD = 12\ \mathrm{m}
\end{array} \right]
$$

Substituting into (3), $\Delta s = AD - BD$, gives

$$
\Delta s = (13.6 - 12)\ \mathrm{m} = 1.6\ \mathrm{m}
$$

Substituting $\Delta s = 1.6$ m and $n = 0$ into (2) gives

$$
\lambda = \frac{1.6}{0 + \tfrac{1}{2}} = 3.2\ \mathrm{m}
$$

Substituting $\lambda = 3.2$ m and $v = 340$ m/s into (1) gives

$$
f = \frac{340}{3.2} = 106.25\ \mathrm{Hz} \approx 110\ \mathrm{Hz}
$$

**Answer:** 110 Hz

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Diffraction"
- Waves that pass through an **opening** bend at the edges.
- That is why sound can be heard around a corner.
:::

::: sampunkt "Interference"
- Waves affect each other when they meet. Typically occurs with **several
  openings**.
- **Constructive interference**: the waves meet **in phase** and reinforce
  each other.
- **Destructive interference**: the waves meet **in antiphase** and cancel
  each other.
:::

::: sampunkt "Nodal and antinodal lines"
- **Nodal lines**: the amplitude is 0, the particles are still. Here a
  crest is always met by a trough.
- **Antinodal lines**: lie midway between the nodal lines, where the reinforcement is
  greatest.
:::

::: sampunkt "The path difference decides"
- **Antinodal line**: the path difference is a **whole** number of wavelengths,
  $\Delta s = n \lambda$.
- **Nodal line**: the path difference is a **half-integer** number of wavelengths,
  $\Delta s = \left(n + \dfrac{1}{2}\right) \lambda$.
- $n$ is the order number of the line, counted from the middle: 0, 1, 2 and so
  on.
:::

::: sampunkt "How to solve"
1. Calculate the distance from the point to **each** of the sources, often
   with Pythagoras' theorem.
2. Take the difference, that is, $\Delta s$.
3. Substitute into the right condition and solve for $\lambda$.
4. The frequency is given by $f = \dfrac{v}{\lambda}$, with
   $v \approx 340\ \mathrm{m/s}$ for sound.
:::
:::
