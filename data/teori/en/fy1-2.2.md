# Vector addition

There is a difference between [[distance|The total length of path an object has moved. A scalar quantity, measured in metres.]] and [[displacement|The vector from the start position to the end position. Differs from distance, which is the total length of path.]]. How far something travels is called the
**distance** and is written *s*. How far something has moved compared with
its starting point is called the **displacement** and is written Δ*s*. Both have the [[SI unit|The International System of Units, with seven base units (m, kg, s, A, K, mol, cd).]]
m (metre).

The distance we walk is 4 + 3 = 7 metres. The displacement we make is 5 metres (the blue
arrow). So when we talk about displacement, the direction matters, which
is not the case for distance.

::: formel "Vectors and scalars"
A [[quantity|A property that can be measured with a number and a unit, for example distance, time, force.]] that has

- both *magnitude and direction* is called a **[[vector|A quantity with both magnitude and direction, for example velocity, force or acceleration. Differs from a scalar, which has only magnitude.]]**.
- *magnitude*, but *no direction* is called a **[[scalar|A quantity with magnitude only, without direction. For example time, mass, energy, temperature.]]**.
:::

| Examples of vectors and scalars | |
| --- | --- |
| **Scalar** | **Vector** |
| distance | displacement |
| speed | velocity |
| also, for example, mass and time | also, for example, force and acceleration |

So when we add vectors we need to take the direction into account. For
vectors along the same line we often define the positive direction as upwards or to the
right and the negative direction as downwards or to the left. Directions to the right and
upwards then get positive values and directions to the left and downwards get negative
values.

::: formel "Resultant vectors and moving vectors parallel"
If two or more vectors act in different directions, we can construct the
resultant vector by moving the vectors to be added, without changing
their magnitude or direction. This is called **moving a vector parallel to itself**. We start
with any vector as our starting point and then move the next vector so that
it starts where the first one ends. The resultant vector (the **resultant**)
is the vector that goes from the starting point to the tip of the last vector added.
If the vectors are perpendicular, the magnitude of the resultant vector can be
calculated with **Pythagoras' theorem**.
:::

::: exempel "Example 1: Kayla in the train carriage"
**Kayla walks at a [[speed|The magnitude of velocity, without direction (a scalar). Measured in m/s or km/h.]] of 2 m/s in a train carriage relative to the carriage. The carriage moves at
10 m/s. What is Kayla's [[velocity|Change of position per unit time, with direction (a vector). SI unit m/s.]] relative to the ground if she walks a) in the train's
direction of motion, b) against the train's direction of motion?**

::: handskrift
typ: tagvagn
:::

::: textlosning
**a)** $v_\text{tot} = v_1 + v_2$

Both velocity vectors are in the same direction and so have the same sign.

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
v_1 = 10\ \mathrm{m/s} \\
v_2 = 2\ \mathrm{m/s}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
v_\text{tot} = 10 + 2 = 12\ \mathrm{m/s}
$$

**Answer:** 12 m/s (in the same direction as the train)

**b)** $v_\text{tot} = v_1 + v_2$

Kayla's velocity is now directed against the train's direction (negative).

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
v_1 = 10\ \mathrm{m/s} \\
v_2 = -2\ \mathrm{m/s} \quad (\text{Note the minus sign in front!})
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
v_\text{tot} = 10 + (-2) = 8\ \mathrm{m/s}
$$

**Answer:** 8 m/s (in the same direction as the train)
:::
:::

Only the velocity perpendicular to the river, *v*₁, and the width of the river,
*s*₁, decide the time it takes to cross the river. We write down the formula for
[[constant|A value that does not change. For example the acceleration due to gravity *g* or the speed of light *c*.]] velocity and solve for the time.

$$
v_1 = \frac{s_1}{t} \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad t = \frac{s_1}{v_1}
$$

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
s_1 = 45\ \mathrm{m} \\
v_1 = 1.5\ \mathrm{m/s}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
t = \frac{45}{1.5} = 30\ \mathrm{s}
$$

**Answer:** 30 s.

**b)** We add the two velocity vectors *v*₁ and *v*₂ to get the
resultant velocity vector *v*. We move the vector *v*₁ parallel to itself to
the tip of the vector *v*₂ and draw the resultant vector *v*.

A right-angled triangle has formed and we calculate *v* with Pythagoras' theorem.

$$
v^2 = v_1^2 + v_2^2 \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad v = \sqrt{v_1^2 + v_2^2}
$$

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
v_1 = 1.5\ \mathrm{m/s} \\
v_2 = 4.0\ \mathrm{m/s}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
v = \sqrt{1.5^2 + 4.0^2} = \sqrt{18.25} = 4.272\ldots \approx 4.3\ \mathrm{m/s}
$$

**Answer:** 4.3 m/s

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Distance and displacement"
- **Distance** ($s$): the whole length of path travelled.
- **Displacement** ($\Delta s$): how far the object has ended up from
  its starting point, with direction.
- If you walk $4\ \mathrm{m}$ to the right and $3\ \mathrm{m}$ down, the distance is $7\ \mathrm{m}$ but the displacement is
  $5\ \mathrm{m}$.
:::

::: sampunkt "Vector and scalar"
- **Vector**: has both magnitude and direction.
- **Scalar**: has magnitude only.
- Scalars: distance, speed, mass, time.
- Vectors: displacement, velocity, force, acceleration.
:::

::: sampunkt "Vectors along the same line"
- Choose a positive direction, usually upwards or to the right.
- Vectors in that direction are positive, the opposite direction negative.
- Then add with signs: $10 + (-2) = 8$ m/s.
:::

::: sampunkt "Moving vectors parallel and the resultant"
- Move the next vector so that it **starts where the previous one ends**, without
  changing its magnitude or direction.
- The **resultant** goes from the starting point to the tip of the last vector.
- If the vectors are perpendicular, Pythagoras' theorem gives the magnitude of the resultant:
  $v = \sqrt{v_1^2 + v_2^2}$.
:::
:::
