# Total resistance and equivalent resistance

The combined [[resistance|Opposition to electric current. According to Ohm's law: *U* = *R* · *I*. SI unit ohm (Ω).]] in a circuit is called the **total resistance** and determines
how large a [[current|Amount of charge per unit time: *I* = *Q* / *t*. SI unit ampere (A).]] the battery sends out. If we have several resistors in a
circuit, we get the total resistance by replacing the resistances of the
resistors $R_1$, $R_2$, $R_3$, … with a single resistance $R_\text{tot}$
or *R*. When we replace two or more resistances with a single
resistance, it is called the **equivalent resistance**.

How large the equivalent resistance is depends on whether the resistors are connected in
series or in parallel.

::: formel "Equivalent resistance"
**Resistors in series:**

$$
R_\text{tot} = R_1 + R_2 + \ldots
$$

**Resistors in parallel:**

$$
\frac{1}{R_\text{tot}} = \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2} + \ldots
$$

where

- $R_\text{tot}$ = equivalent resistance (Ω)
- $R_1$, $R_2$, … = resistance 1, resistance 2, … (Ω)

::: härledning "Equivalent resistance in series"
We draw a circuit diagram with two resistors connected in series. They are to
be replaced by a single resistor *R*.

We already know that the partial voltages across each resistor, $U_1$ and
$U_2$, together are as large as the [[voltage|Energy difference per charge between two points. SI unit volt (V).]] from the battery *U*, that is

$$
U = U_1 + U_2
$$

From [[Ohm's law|The relationship between voltage, current and resistance: *U* = *R* · *I*.]] we have $U = R \cdot I$, which inserted into the equation above
gives

$$
R_\text{tot} \cdot I = R_1 \cdot I + R_2 \cdot I
$$

Since the current is equally large through all components in a series connection,
we can divide both sides by *I* and get

$$
R_\text{tot} = R_1 + R_2
$$

So we see that for a series connection the equivalent resistance is the sum of
the resistances of the resistors. **QED**
:::

::: härledning "Equivalent resistance in parallel"
We draw a circuit diagram with two resistors connected in parallel. They are to
be replaced by a single resistor *R*.

We already know that the partial currents in each branch, $I_1$ and $I_2$,
together are as large as the current leaving the battery (in
the main line), that is

$$
I = I_1 + I_2
$$

From Ohm's law we have $I = U/R$, which inserted into the equation above gives

$$
\frac{U}{R_\text{tot}} = \frac{U}{R_1} + \frac{U}{R_2}
$$

Since the voltage is equally large across all components connected across a
battery, we can divide both sides by *U* and get

$$
\frac{1}{R_\text{tot}} = \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2}
$$

So we see that for a parallel connection the inverse of the
equivalent resistance is the sum of the inverses of the resistances of the resistors.
**QED**
:::
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Equivalent resistance"
- The combined resistance in the circuit. It determines how large a current
  the battery sends out.
- Several resistors are replaced by a single one, $R_\text{tot}$.
:::

::: sampunkt "Series connection"
- $R_\text{tot} = R_1 + R_2 + \ldots$
- The resistance is always **larger** than the largest individual one.
- Comes from the voltages adding up while the current is shared.
:::

::: sampunkt "Parallel connection"
- $\dfrac{1}{R_\text{tot}}
  = \dfrac{1}{R_1} + \dfrac{1}{R_2} + \ldots$
- The resistance is always **smaller** than the smallest individual one.
- Comes from the currents adding up while the voltage is shared.
:::

::: sampunkt "Common pitfall"
- Do not forget to **invert back** at the end. The formula gives
  $\dfrac{1}{R_\text{tot}}$, not $R_\text{tot}$.
:::

::: sampunkt "How to solve"
1. Calculate $R_\text{tot}$.
2. Current from the battery: $I = \dfrac{U}{R_\text{tot}}$.
3. Two resistors of $20\ \mathrm{Ω}$ and $30\ \mathrm{Ω}$ at $9.0\ \mathrm{V}$ give $0.18\ \mathrm{A}$ in series but
   $0.75\ \mathrm{A}$ in parallel.
:::
:::
