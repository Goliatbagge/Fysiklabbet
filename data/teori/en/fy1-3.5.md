# The law of gravitation

Two objects with mass act on each other with a mutual attractive
[[gravitational force|The force that acts between all masses. At the Earth's surface it is called the gravitational force (weight).]]. The larger the masses and the shorter the distance, the larger the [[force|An influence that can change the motion or shape of an object. SI unit newton (N). A vector quantity.]].
This relationship is called **Newton's law of gravitation**.

::: formel "Newton's law of gravitation"
$$
F_G = G \cdot \frac{m_1 \cdot m_2}{r^{2}}
$$

where

- $F_G$ = gravitational force (N)
- *G* = the gravitational constant (6.67 · 10⁻¹¹ Nm²/kg²)
- *m*₁ = mass of object 1 (kg)
- *m*₂ = mass of object 2 (kg)
- *r* = the distance between the centres of gravity of the objects (m)
:::

In Physics level 2 we will derive Newton's law of gravitation using the formula
for [[centripetal force|The force needed to keep an object in circular motion, directed towards the centre of the circle.]].

::: exempel "Example 1: Gravitational force between friends"
**You and a friend are sitting 1.0 metre apart and both have a mass of 60 kg.**

**a) How large a gravitational force do you exert on each other?**
**b) Can the force be felt?**

::: handskrift
typ: gravkompisar
:::

::: textlosning
**a)** We write down Newton's law of gravitation.

$$
F_G = G \cdot \frac{m_1 \cdot m_2}{r^{2}}
$$

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
G = 6.67 \cdot 10^{-11}\ \mathrm{Nm^2/kg^2} \\
m_1 = 60\ \mathrm{kg} \\
m_2 = 60\ \mathrm{kg} \\
r = 1.00\ \mathrm{m}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
F_G = 6.67 \cdot 10^{-11} \cdot \frac{60 \cdot 60}{1.00^{2}}
= 2.4 \cdot 10^{-7}\ \mathrm{N}
$$

**Answer:** 2.4 · 10⁻⁷ N

**b)** To find out whether the force can be felt, we work out which mass
corresponds to this weight. We write down the formula for the [[gravitational force|The force with which the Earth pulls all objects towards its centre. The gravitational force gives an object its weight and is directed straight down towards the centre of the Earth.]] and solve for
the mass *m*.

$$
F_G = m \cdot g \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad m = \frac{F_G}{g}
$$

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
F_G = 2.4 \cdot 10^{-7}\ \mathrm{N} \\
g \approx 10\ \mathrm{N/kg}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
m \approx \frac{2.4 \cdot 10^{-7}}{10} = 2.4 \cdot 10^{-8}\ \mathrm{kg}
= 2.4 \cdot 10^{-5}\ \mathrm{g}
= 2.4 \cdot 10^{-2}\ \mathrm{mg}
= 0.024\ \mathrm{mg}
$$

So the force corresponds to the weight of a mass of 0.024 mg and therefore cannot
be felt.

**Answer:** No
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 2: The gravitational field strength at the Earth's surface"
**Determine the gravitational field strength *g* at the Earth's surface using Newton's law of gravitation
and astronomical data.**

::: handskrift
typ: tyngdfaktor
:::

::: textlosning
We have an arbitrary mass *m* of an object on the Earth's surface and the Earth's mass
*m*ⱼ. The gravitational force on the mass can then be written in two ways:

$$
F_G = m \cdot g \qquad\text{and}\qquad F_G = G \cdot \frac{m \cdot m_j}{r^{2}}
$$

We set these equal and divide both sides by *m* to solve for
the gravitational field strength *g*.

$$
m \cdot g = G \cdot \frac{m \cdot m_j}{r^{2}} \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad
g = G \cdot \frac{m_j}{r^{2}}
$$

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
G = 6.67 \cdot 10^{-11}\ \mathrm{Nm^2/kg^2} \\
m_j = 5.972 \cdot 10^{24}\ \mathrm{kg} \quad (\text{the Earth's mass, from a table}) \\
r = 6\,378\ \mathrm{km} = 6\,378 \cdot 10^{3}\ \mathrm{m} \quad (\text{the Earth's radius, from a table})
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
g = 6.67 \cdot 10^{-11} \cdot \frac{5.972 \cdot 10^{24}}{(6\,378 \cdot 10^{3})^{2}}
= 9.792\ldots\ \mathrm{N/kg} \approx 9.8\ \mathrm{N/kg}
$$

**Answer:** 9.8 N/kg
:::
:::

So from Example 2 we see that Newton's law of gravitation explains **why**
the gravitational field strength on the Earth is exactly 9.82 N/kg!

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Newton's law of gravitation"
- All objects with mass pull on each other with a **mutual attractive**
  gravitational force.
- $F_G = G \cdot \dfrac{m_1 \cdot m_2}{r^{2}}$
- Larger masses give a larger force. A longer distance gives a much smaller force,
  since $r$ is **squared**.
:::

::: sampunkt "The symbols"
- $G$ is **the gravitational constant**,
  $6.67 \cdot 10^{-11}\ \mathrm{Nm^2/kg^2}$.
- $m_1$ and $m_2$ are the two masses (kg).
- $r$ is the distance between the **centres of gravity** of the objects (m), that is from
  centre to centre.
:::

::: sampunkt "The force is weak between everyday objects"
- Two people of $60\ \mathrm{kg}$ one metre apart pull on each other with
  only $2.4 \cdot 10^{-7}\ \mathrm{N}$.
- That corresponds to the weight of $0.024\ \mathrm{mg}$, so it is completely unnoticeable.
- Gravitation is only noticeable when at least one of the masses is enormous, like a
  planet.
:::

::: sampunkt "Why g is 9.82 N/kg"
- The gravitational force can be written in two ways:
  $m \cdot g = G \cdot \dfrac{m \cdot m_j}{r^{2}}$.
- The mass $m$ cancels out, leaving
  $g = G \cdot \dfrac{m_j}{r^{2}}$.
- With the Earth's mass and radius this gives $9.8\ \mathrm{N/kg}$.
  So the law of gravitation explains **why** the gravitational field strength has exactly that
  value.
:::
:::
