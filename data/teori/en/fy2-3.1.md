# Magnetism and magnetic fields

The ability of certain metals to attract other metal objects is called
**magnetism**.

::: kuriosa "Etymology: Magnetism"
The word *magnetism* comes from the Turkish city of **Magnesia** (present-day
Manisa), where magnetic materials were found for the first time.
:::

::: kuriosa "History: Magnetism"
The knowledge that iron-bearing minerals can attract other objects is
old: it has existed for **4 500 years**. It was not until the **12th century**
that the phenomenon had a major impact: it was found that small iron needles
always pointed in the same direction, and **the compass** had been invented! Arab
ships were the first to use them to navigate at sea.

The theory that **the Earth works like a giant magnet** was put forward by
the Englishman William Gilbert in the 17th century. He also believed that this
[[force|An influence that can change an object's motion or shape. SI unit newton (N). A vector quantity.]] kept the planets in their orbits around the Sun. This is wrong, but it
was the first time anyone had tried to explain an astronomical phenomenon
scientifically and not from a myth.
:::

Just like gravity and electric forces, magnetic forces act
**at a distance**.

Metals that have magnetic properties for a long time, for example
fridge magnets or bar magnets, are called **permanent magnets** and are
made of so-called **ferromagnetic materials** such as iron, steel and
nickel.

## Poles

The ends of a magnet are called **poles**. The end that turns towards
north is called the **north end** (N) and the one that turns towards south is called
the **south end** (S). The north end is usually marked red and the south end white.

::: tips "North end, not north pole"
In many books the ends of a magnet are called the *north pole* and *south pole*. Here we keep
the words north pole and south pole for the Earth's geographic poles and call
the ends of the magnet the north end and the south end. Then they can never be mixed up,
which is important: the Earth's magnetic pole in the north is in fact a south end
(see the section on the Earth's magnetic field).
:::

**Like poles repel** each other and **unlike poles attract** each other
(compare with [[electric charge|A fundamental property of matter, can be positive or negative. SI unit coulomb (C).]]).

::: minisim
typ: magnetpoler
titel: Like poles repel, unlike poles attract
:::

::: demo "Demonstration: Poles come in pairs"
We take a bar magnet and break it in the middle. We investigate how the white
end of the magnet behaves and see that **the broken end has now
become a north end**.

::: minisim
typ: magnetdelning
:::

When a permanent magnet is divided in the middle, two new smaller magnets are formed.
:::

**Magnetic poles always come in pairs.** If a magnet is divided, you get two new,
shorter magnets. You can **never** create a magnet with only one pole.

## Magnetic fields

::: demo "Demonstration: Magnetic field"
We place a bar magnet on a plate with compass needles. We see how
the compass needles line up, which visualises the magnetic field
and its direction in two dimensions. We also place a bar magnet inside
a glass cylinder with iron filings in a viscous liquid, which
visualises the magnetic field in three dimensions.
:::

The field of force around a magnet is called a **[[magnetic field|A region around a magnet or current-carrying conductor where other magnetic objects are affected by a force.]]**. If a small
compass is placed in the magnetic field, the direction of the field is defined **as the direction
the north end of the compass points in**.

Magnetic fields are drawn with **field lines** that, outside the magnet, go from
**the north end to the south end**. Inside the magnet they continue from the south end
back to the north end, so every field line is a **closed curve** with no
beginning or end. This can be seen clearly in a coil, where the field lines run straight
through the open interior of the coil. The closer together the field lines, the stronger the
magnetic field.

::: kuriosa "What actually is magnetism?"
It is difficult to explain simply where magnetic forces come from.
Magnetism comes from a property of electrons called **"spin"**
and their spin quantum number, which is part of quantum physics. Spin gives a
kind of "magnetic moment", and when these line up in parallel and act together,
the result is magnetic properties.
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Magnetism"
- The ability of certain metals to attract other metal objects.
- Acts **at a distance**, just like gravity and electric forces.
- **Permanent magnets** keep their properties for a long time and are made of
  **ferromagnetic** materials: iron, steel and nickel.
:::

::: sampunkt "Poles"
- The **north end** turns towards north, the **south end** towards south.
- The north end is marked red and the south end white.
- We keep the words north pole and south pole for the Earth's geographic poles.
- **Like poles repel, unlike poles attract**, just like electric
  charges.
:::

::: sampunkt "Poles always come in pairs"
- If a magnet is divided in two, you get **two new complete magnets**, each
  with both a north end and a south end.
- You can **never** create a magnet with only one pole.
- This is an important difference from electric charges, which can perfectly well
  be on their own.
:::

::: sampunkt "Magnetic fields"
- The field of force around a magnet.
- The direction is defined as the direction in which the **north end** of a small compass needle
  points.
- Outside the magnet the field lines go **out of the north end and into
  the south end**; inside they close from south to north. Every field line is
  a **closed curve**.
- **Closer** field lines mean a **stronger** field.
:::

::: sampunkt "Where magnetism comes from"
- From the **spin** of electrons, a quantum physical property.
- Spin gives a magnetic moment. If many line up in parallel, the
  material becomes magnetic.
:::
:::
