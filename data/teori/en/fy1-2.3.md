# Average velocity, instantaneous velocity and position-time graphs

The mean [[velocity|Change of position per unit time, with direction (a vector). SI unit m/s.]] of an object is called its **[[average velocity|The mean velocity over a time interval: the displacement Δ*s* divided by the time Δ*t*.]]**. The
exact velocity of an object at one instant is called its **instantaneous velocity**. Remember that
velocity is a [[vector|A quantity with both magnitude and direction, for example velocity, force or acceleration. Differs from a scalar, which has only magnitude.]].

::: formel "Average velocity"
$$
v_m = \frac{\Delta s}{\Delta t} \left( = \frac{s_2 - s_1}{t_2 - t_1} \right)
$$

where

- Δ*s* = [[displacement|The vector from the start position to the end position. Differs from distance, which is the total length of path.]] (m)
- Δ*t* = time (s)
- *v*ₘ = average velocity (m/s)
:::

A graph with [[position|The location of an object relative to a chosen starting point (the origin). Can be negative and is measured in metres.]] on the *y*-axis and time on the *x*-axis is called a
**position-time graph** or an ***s-t* graph**.

A velocity that does not change is called a **[[constant|A value that does not change. For example the acceleration due to gravity *g* or the speed of light *c*.]] velocity**. At constant
velocity the graph in an *s-t* graph is a straight line. The line's
[[slope|How steeply a line rises in a graph: Δ*y* divided by Δ*x*. In an *s-t* graph the slope is the velocity, in a *v-t* graph the acceleration.]] corresponds to the velocity, and the steeper the slope, the higher the velocity.

::: formel "In an s-t graph …"
… **the average velocity over a certain interval** corresponds to the average slope over the same
interval, or the **slope of the secant**.

… **the instantaneous velocity** corresponds to the slope at a point, or the **slope of the tangent**.
:::

Whether we are calculating an average velocity, a constant velocity or an
instantaneous velocity from an *s-t* graph, we do it by calculating
the slope of some kind of line. The slope of the line is found by choosing any two
points (*t*₁, *s*₁) and (*t*₂, *s*₂) on the line and substituting into

$$
v = \frac{\Delta s}{\Delta t} = \frac{s_2 - s_1}{t_2 - t_1}
\qquad \text{(compare with } k = \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x} = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1} \text{ from mathematics)}
$$

::: textlosning
The constant velocity corresponds to the slope of the line.

$$
v = \frac{\Delta s}{\Delta t}
$$

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
\Delta s = (0.72 - 0.22)\ \mathrm{m} = 0.50\ \mathrm{m} \\
\Delta t = (0.50 - 0.15)\ \mathrm{s} = 0.35\ \mathrm{s}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
v = \frac{0.50}{0.35} = 1.428\ldots\ \mathrm{m/s} \approx 1.4\ \mathrm{m/s}
$$

**Answer:** 1.4 m/s
:::

::: textlosning
We write down the formula for average velocity.

$$
v_m = \frac{\Delta s}{\Delta t} = \frac{s_2 - s_1}{t_2 - t_1}
$$

We read off the coordinates on the orange graph at $t_1 = 0\ \mathrm{min}$ and
$t_2 = 25\ \mathrm{min}$ and get (0, 6) and (25, 0), which gives

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
t_1 = 0 \\
t_2 = 25\ \mathrm{min} = 25 \cdot 60\ \mathrm{s} = 1\,500\ \mathrm{s} \\
s_1 = 6\ \mathrm{km} = 6\,000\ \mathrm{m} \\
s_2 = 0
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
v_m = \frac{s_2 - s_1}{t_2 - t_1} = \frac{0 - 6\,000}{1\,500 - 0} = \frac{-6\,000}{1\,500} = -4.0\ \mathrm{m/s}
$$

**Answer:** −4.0 m/s (Note: the minus sign shows that the direction is "back".)
:::

We choose any two points on the tangent (the orange line) and substitute them
into the formula

$$
v = \frac{s_2 - s_1}{t_2 - t_1}
$$

We choose, for example, the points (2, 20) and (8, 50), which give the values

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
t_1 = 2\ \mathrm{s} \\
t_2 = 8\ \mathrm{s} \\
s_1 = 20\ \mathrm{m} \\
s_2 = 50\ \mathrm{m}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
v = \frac{50 - 20}{8 - 2} = \frac{30}{6} = 5\ \mathrm{m/s}
$$

**Answer:** 5 m/s

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Average velocity"
- $v_m = \dfrac{\Delta s}{\Delta t} = \dfrac{s_2 - s_1}{t_2 - t_1}$.
- Based on the **displacement**, so it is a vector and can be negative.
- A negative answer means that the direction is back towards the starting point.
:::

::: sampunkt "Instantaneous velocity"
- The velocity at a single instant, not an average over an
  interval.
:::

::: sampunkt "Position-time graphs"
- Position $s$ on the $y$-axis, time $t$ on the $x$-axis. Also called an
  *s-t* graph.
- **The slope is the velocity.** A steeper line means a higher velocity.
- Constant velocity gives a straight line.
:::

::: sampunkt "Secant and tangent"
- **The average velocity** over an interval is the slope of the **secant** between
  the end points of the interval.
- **The instantaneous velocity** is the slope of the **tangent** at the point. The tangent
  should just touch the curve and slope as much as it does.
:::

::: sampunkt "Calculating the slope"
- Choose two points on the line and substitute into
  $v = \dfrac{s_2 - s_1}{t_2 - t_1}$.
- The same thing as $k = \dfrac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$ from mathematics.
:::
:::
