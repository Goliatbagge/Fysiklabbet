# Buoyancy in gases

::: demo "Weighing air"
Pump as much air as possible out of a conical flask. Weigh the flask afterwards
to calculate the mass of the air we pumped out. Then dip the flask's tube
under water and open the valve. Water flows in. The volume of the water is
the same as that of the air pumped out. Now we can determine the [[density|Mass per unit volume: *ρ* = *m* / *V*. SI unit kg/m³. Determines whether an object floats in a medium.]] of air!
:::

[[Archimedes' principle|An object immersed in a liquid is acted on by a buoyant force equal to the weight of the displaced liquid.]] (the formula for the [[buoyant force|The upward force from a liquid or gas on an object that is immersed in it.]]) also applies to gases. We live
in a "sea of air" whose density is *ρ* = 1.293 kg/m³. The buoyant force is given by the
weight of the displaced gas.

::: kuriosa "The buoyant force on you"
If we estimate the volume of your body at 0.1 m³, you are acted on by a buoyant force
corresponding to the weight of the mass of the displaced air

$$
m = \rho \cdot V = 1.293 \cdot 0.1 = 0.1293\ \mathrm{kg} \approx 0.13\ \mathrm{kg}
$$

which is small, but still a mass we would feel if we held it (a little
more than a hundred grams). It also means that the bathroom scales would show 0.1 kg
more if we pumped the air out of the bathroom.
:::

A hot-air balloon rises because the weight of the air that is displaced
(the cold air) is greater than the weight of "balloon + basket + hot air". This is
because hot air has a lower density than cold air.

**a) large a [[force|An influence that can change the motion or shape of an object. SI unit newton (N). A vector quantity.]] is needed to hold the balloon so it does not fly away?**
**b) many balloons are needed to lift Algot?**

::: handskrift
typ: heliumballong
:::

::: textlosning
**a)** When Algot holds the balloon still there is equilibrium of forces, which means
that the downward forces are as large as the upward one:

$$
F_S + F_G = F_L \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad F_S = F_L - F_G
$$

We start by determining the buoyant force $F_L$.

$$
F_L = \rho_\text{air} \cdot V \cdot g
$$

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
\rho_\text{air} = 1.293\ \mathrm{kg/m^3} \\
V = 11\ \mathrm{litres} = 0.011\ \mathrm{m^3} \\
g \approx 9.82\ \mathrm{N/kg}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
F_L = 1.293 \cdot 0.011 \cdot 9.82 = 0.1396\ldots\ \mathrm{N}
$$

Then we determine $F_G$ (the weight of balloon + helium).

$$
F_G = (m_\text{balloon} + m_\text{helium}) \cdot g
$$

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
m_\text{balloon} = 4.0\ \mathrm{g} = 0.0040\ \mathrm{kg} \\
m_\text{helium} = \rho_\text{helium} \cdot V_\text{helium}
   = 0.178 \cdot 0.011 = 0.001958\ \mathrm{kg} \\
g \approx 9.82\ \mathrm{N/kg}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
F_G = (0.004 + 0.001958) \cdot 9.82 = 0.0585\ldots\ \mathrm{N}
$$

Substitution gives

$$
F_S = 0.1396\ldots - 0.0585\ldots = 0.0811\ldots\ \mathrm{N}
\approx 0.081\ \mathrm{N} = 81\ \mathrm{mN}
$$

**Answer:** 81 mN

**b)** From part a we have that one balloon gives the tension
$F_S$ = 0.0811… N. If we have *n* balloons, the
total lifting force is therefore

$$
F = n \cdot F_S
$$

For the balloons to lift Algot, the total lifting
force *F* must be at least as large as Algot's weight $F_G = m \cdot g$. This gives

$$
m \cdot g = n \cdot F_S \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad n = \frac{m \cdot g}{F_S}
$$

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
m = 20\ \mathrm{kg} \\
g \approx 9.82\ \mathrm{N/kg} \\
F_S = 0.0811\ldots\ \mathrm{N}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
n = \frac{20 \cdot 9.82}{0.0811\ldots} = 2\,419.613\ldots \approx 2\,400\ \text{balloons}
$$

**Answer:** 2 400 balloons

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Archimedes' principle also applies to gases"
- $F_L = \rho \cdot g \cdot V$, just as for liquids.
- Here $\rho$ is the density of the **gas**. Air:
  $\rho = 1.293\ \mathrm{kg/m^3}$.
- We live in a sea of air, so everything around us is acted on by a buoyant force.
:::

::: sampunkt "The buoyant force on you"
- Your body of about $0.1\ \mathrm{m^3}$ displaces air with a mass of
  $0.13\ \mathrm{kg}$.
- The bathroom scales would show a hundred grams more if the air were pumped out of the room.
:::

::: sampunkt "Why a hot-air balloon rises"
- Hot air has a **lower density** than cold air.
- The weight of the displaced cold air is greater than the weight of the balloon,
  basket and hot air together.
:::

::: sampunkt "Typical task: a balloon on a string"
- Equilibrium of forces: $F_S + F_G = F_L$, so
  $F_S = F_L - F_G$.
- $F_L$ is calculated with the density of **air** and the volume of the balloon.
- $F_G$ is the weight of the **balloon skin plus gas**. Do not forget the mass of the gas,
  $m = \rho_\text{gas} \cdot V$.
:::

::: sampunkt "How many balloons?"
- Each balloon lifts with the force $F_S$.
- $n = \dfrac{m \cdot g}{F_S}$
- A 20-kilogram person requires about 2 400 helium balloons.
:::
:::
