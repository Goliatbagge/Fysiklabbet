# The definition of the derivative and rules of differentiation

We review the definition of the derivative and the rules of differentiation from Mathematics continuation level 1c.
But first we introduce a new notation for the derivative and the second derivative.

::: formel "Leibniz notation"
$$
y' = f'(x) = \frac{dy}{dx} \qquad\qquad y'' = f''(x) = \frac{d^2y}{dx^2}
$$
:::

The notation is called **Leibniz notation**. The expression $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ is read
"the derivative of $y$ with respect to $x$". If $y = x^3$ then the derivative is
$y' = 3x^2$, which in Leibniz notation is written $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 3x^2$.

Even though $y'$ is shorter, $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ has several advantages:

- **Clarity.** The notation shows which variable we differentiate with respect
  to. If a function depends on both $x$ and $t$, $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ tells us that
  we mean the change in $y$ when $x$ changes, while $\dfrac{dy}{dt}$
  would mean the change when $t$ changes.
- **Link to the definition.** The derivative is defined as the limit of
  the difference quotient $\dfrac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}$ as $\Delta x$ tends to zero.
  The notation $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ reminds us of this origin: a quotient of
  two infinitely small changes.

## The definition of the derivative

The derivative at a point is the limit of the difference quotient, that is the slope
of a **secant** that is drawn closer and closer to the tangent at the point.

::: formel "The definition of the derivative"
$$
f'(a) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(a+h) - f(a)}{h} \qquad\text{or}\qquad f'(x) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h}
$$
:::

The difference quotient $\dfrac{f(a+h) - f(a)}{h}$ is the slope of the secant through
the points $P$ and $Q$ on the curve. As $h \to 0$, $Q$ slides in towards $P$ and
the secant approaches the curve's tangent at $P$ (dashed). The slope of the secant then tends
to the slope of the tangent, which is the value of the derivative $f'(a)$.

## Rules of differentiation

Below are the rules of differentiation we need. A polynomial (an expression with several
terms) is differentiated **term by term**.

::: formel "Summary: rules of differentiation"
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(x) = x^n &\;\Rightarrow\; f'(x) = nx^{n-1}\\
f(x) = kx^n &\;\Rightarrow\; f'(x) = nkx^{n-1}\\
f(x) = a \ (\text{constant}) &\;\Rightarrow\; f'(x) = 0\\
f(x) = kx &\;\Rightarrow\; f'(x) = k\\
f(x) = \dfrac{g(x)}{a} &\;\Rightarrow\; f'(x) = \dfrac{g'(x)}{a}\\
f(x) = e^x &\;\Rightarrow\; f'(x) = e^x\\
f(x) = e^{kx} &\;\Rightarrow\; f'(x) = ke^{kx}\\
f(x) = a^x &\;\Rightarrow\; f'(x) = a^x\ln a
\end{aligned}
$$
:::

If a function contains a variable **under a root sign or in a denominator**,
we first rewrite the term as a power and then differentiate as usual:
$\sqrt{x} = x^{1/2}$ and $\dfrac{1}{x^2} = x^{-2}$.

To **find the value of the derivative** at a point $x = a$, we first differentiate and
then substitute $x = a$ into the derivative function.

::: exempel "Example 1: The definition of the derivative"
**Find $f'(x)$ for $f(x) = x^2$ using the definition of the derivative.**

::: handskrift
typ: derivatadef
:::

::: textlosning
We set up the definition of the derivative and simplify until $h$ can be
cancelled.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f'(x) &= \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h} = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{(x+h)^2 - x^2}{h}\\
&= \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{x^2 + 2xh + h^2 - x^2}{h} = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{2xh + h^2}{h}\\
&= \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{h(2x + h)}{h} = \lim_{h \to 0} (2x + h) = 2x
\end{aligned}
$$

**Answer:** $f'(x) = 2x$
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 2: Differentiate and find the value of the derivative"
**Find $f'(4)$ when $f(x) = x^3 - 4\sqrt{x}$.**

::: handskrift
typ: derivatavarde
:::

::: textlosning
We first rewrite the root as a power, $\sqrt{x} = x^{1/2}$, and then differentiate
term by term.

$$
f(x) = x^3 - 4x^{1/2}
$$

$$
f'(x) = 3x^2 - 4\cdot \frac{1}{2}x^{-1/2} = 3x^2 - 2x^{-1/2} = 3x^2 - \frac{2}{\sqrt{x}}
$$

Substitute $x = 4$ into the derivative function:

$$
f'(4) = 3\cdot 4^2 - \frac{2}{\sqrt{4}} = 48 - \frac{2}{2} = 48 - 1 = 47
$$

**Answer:** $f'(4) = 47$
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 3: The second derivative"
**Let $y = e^{3x}$. Find $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ and $\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}$.**

::: handskrift
typ: andraderivatan
:::

::: textlosning
We differentiate $y$ with respect to $x$ using the rule $f(x) = e^{kx} \Rightarrow f'(x) = ke^{kx}$, here with $k = 3$:

$$
\frac{dy}{dx} = 3e^{3x}
$$

We get the second derivative $\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}$ by differentiating $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$
once more. The coefficient $3$ comes along:

$$
\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = 3\cdot 3e^{3x} = 9e^{3x}
$$

**Answer:** $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 3e^{3x}$ and $\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2} = 9e^{3x}$
:::
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Leibniz notation"
- $y' = f'(x) = \dfrac{dy}{dx}$ and $y'' = f''(x) = \dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}$.
- Read "the derivative of $y$ with respect to $x$".
- The advantage is that **the variable is visible**: $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ and $\dfrac{dy}{dt}$ mean different things.
- The notation is a reminder of the origin, the difference quotient $\dfrac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}$.
:::

::: sampunkt "The definition of the derivative"
- $f'(a) = \lim_{h \to 0} \dfrac{f(a+h) - f(a)}{h}$.
- The expression is the slope of a **secant**. As $h \to 0$ the secant turns into the tangent.
:::

::: sampunkt "The rules of differentiation"
- $x^n \Rightarrow nx^{n-1}$ and $kx^n \Rightarrow nkx^{n-1}$.
- Constant $\Rightarrow 0$, and $kx \Rightarrow k$.
- $\dfrac{g(x)}{a} \Rightarrow \dfrac{g'(x)}{a}$.
- $e^x \Rightarrow e^x$, $e^{kx} \Rightarrow ke^{kx}$, $a^x \Rightarrow a^x\ln a$.
:::

::: sampunkt "Method"
- A polynomial is differentiated **term by term**.
- Rewrite roots and denominators as powers first: $\sqrt{x} = x^{1/2}$, $\dfrac{1}{x^2} = x^{-2}$.
- The **value** of the derivative at a point: differentiate first, then substitute $x = a$.
- The second derivative is found by differentiating the derivative once more.
:::
