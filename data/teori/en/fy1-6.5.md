# The four laws of thermodynamics

The four laws of thermodynamics are four fundamental principles and among
the most important in physics.

::: formel "The four laws of thermodynamics"
**0.** Two systems in contact with each other will exchange energy until they
are in [[equilibrium|When the resultant force on an object is zero. The object is then at rest or in uniform motion.]].

**1.** Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only converted.

**2.** [[Heat|Energy transferred between objects at different temperatures. Measured in joules (J).]] flows from hot to cold. Heat never flows from cold
to hot by itself.

**3.** All processes stop at [[absolute zero|The lowest possible temperature, 0 K = −273.15 °C. All thermal motion stops there.]].
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "The zeroth law"
- Two systems in contact exchange energy until they are in **equilibrium**, that is
  have the same temperature.
- This is what makes it meaningful to measure temperature at all.
:::

::: sampunkt "The first law"
- **Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only converted.**
- The same thing as the principle of conservation of energy.
:::

::: sampunkt "The second law"
- **Heat flows from hot to cold**, never the other way round by itself.
- A refrigerator does move heat in the other direction, but only by
  supplying energy.
:::

::: sampunkt "The third law"
- All processes **stop at absolute zero**, that is at $0\ \mathrm{K}$.
- There is no kinetic energy left to move there.
:::
:::
