# Voltage

::: tips "Introduction: voltage"
The more you draw a bow, the faster the arrow will fly off.
The tension in the bow determines the [[velocity|Change of position per unit time, with direction (a vector). SI unit m/s.]] of the arrow. The same goes for
the [[voltage|Energy difference per charge between two points. SI unit volt (V).]] in a circuit! The higher the voltage we have in a circuit, the stronger
the [[current|Amount of charge per unit time: *I* = *Q* / *t*. SI unit ampere (A).]] becomes. So the voltage in an electric circuit corresponds to the tension
in the bow, while the current corresponds to the speed of the arrow.
:::

The "[[pressure|Force per unit area: *p* = *F* / *A*. SI unit pascal (Pa) = N/m².]]" that drives the electrons to move between two points in an
electric circuit is called **voltage** (potential difference), written *U*, and has the [[SI unit|The International System of Units, with seven base units (m, kg, s, A, K, mol, cd).]] V
(volt). It can be compared with the pressure in a water pipe. Without pressure the
water stands still. In the same way, a difference in [[electric charge|A fundamental property of matter, can be positive or negative. SI unit coulomb (C).]] between
two points is needed for the electrons to start moving. Voltage is measured
**between two points**.

A voltage can be created, for example, between the positive terminal and the
negative terminal of a battery. A negative charge has a higher [[potential energy|The energy an object has because of its position in a force field, for example *E*ₚ = *m* · *g* · *h* in a gravitational field.]] at the negative terminal than
at the positive terminal, since it is "pulled towards" the positive terminal. How much potential
energy *E* it has at the negative terminal depends on how large the voltage *U* is and
how large the charge *Q* is. This is the definition of voltage.

::: formel "Voltage and electrical energy"
$$
U = \frac{E}{Q} \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad E = Q \cdot U
$$

where

- *U* = voltage between two points (V)
- *E* = difference in electrical (potential) energy between these two points,
  electrical energy (J)
- *Q* = charge (C)
:::

The voltage in an ordinary mains socket in Sweden is 230 V.

::: textlosning
Electrical energy ($E = Q \cdot U$) is converted into [[kinetic energy|The energy an object has because of its motion: *E*ₖ = ½ · *m* · *v*².]]
($E_k = m v^2 / 2$), that is

$$
Q \cdot U = \frac{m \cdot v^2}{2}
$$

We solve for the speed *v* and get

$$
v = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot Q \cdot U}{m}}
$$

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
q \approx 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\ \mathrm{C} \\
U = 1.5\ \mathrm{V} \\
m \approx 9.11 \cdot 10^{-31}\ \mathrm{kg}\ (\text{mass of the electron, looked up in a table})
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
v = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 1.5}{9.11 \cdot 10^{-31}}}
= 726\,327.846\ldots\ \mathrm{m/s}
\approx 730\,000\ \mathrm{m/s} = 0.73\ \mathrm{Mm/s}
$$

**Answer:** 0.73 Mm/s or 730 000 m/s
:::
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Voltage"
- The pressure that drives the electrons between two points in a circuit.
- Written $U$ and has the unit V (volt).
- **Always measured between two points**, never at a single point.
:::

::: sampunkt "The analogies"
- **The bow**: the more you draw it, the faster the arrow flies. The voltage
  corresponds to the tension in the bow, the current to the speed of the arrow.
- **The water pipe**: without pressure the water stands still.
:::

::: sampunkt "The definition"
- $U = \dfrac{E}{Q}$, that is energy per charge.
- Solved for the energy: $E = Q \cdot U$.
- $E$ is the difference in electrical potential energy between the two
  points, in joules.
:::

::: sampunkt "Where the energy is"
- A negative charge has a **higher** potential energy at the negative terminal,
  since it is pulled towards the positive terminal.
- The energy depends both on how large the voltage is and on how large the charge
  is.
:::

::: sampunkt "Accelerated charge"
- A charge that is accelerated through the voltage $U$ gets the kinetic energy
  $E_k = Q \cdot U$.
- Set $\dfrac{m v^{2}}{2} = QU$ and solve for
  $v = \sqrt{\dfrac{2QU}{m}}$.
- An ordinary mains socket in Sweden has a voltage of
  $230\ \mathrm{V}$.
:::
:::
