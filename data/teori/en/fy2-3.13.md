# Eddy currents

::: demo "Demonstration: Eddy currents in a pendulum"
We let an aluminium pendulum swing back and forth. Then we apply
a [[magnetic field|Region around a magnet or current-carrying conductor where other magnetic objects experience a force.]] that the [[pendulum|An object that swings back and forth about an equilibrium position. Simple pendulum: *T* = 2*π* · √(*L* / *g*).]] has to move through. **The pendulum stops!** We
replace the pendulum with another one that has slots. The braking effect becomes smaller.

**Explanation:** When the metal pendulum moves in the magnetic field, currents are induced.
According to **Lenz's law** the currents have a direction that opposes the
pendulum motion.

The eddy currents create magnetic fields that oppose the motion of the plate.
:::

In the figure above, the induced [[current|Amount of charge per unit time: *I* = *Q* / *t*. SI unit ampere (A).]] will give a [[force|An influence that can change an object's motion or shape. SI unit newton (N). A vector quantity.]] **to the left**
(opposing the motion). The currents that are in the magnetic field (which is
directed into the plane) are therefore, by the right-hand rule, directed upwards.
Outside the magnetic field the currents can move freely and form "whirls",
known as **eddy currents**.

When we replace the pendulum with another one that has slots, the eddy currents cannot
swirl as freely, and so the braking effect becomes smaller.
:::

Circulating currents that, according to Lenz's law, oppose the cause of their
origin (that is, oppose motion or a change in the magnetic field) are called
**eddy currents**.

::: tips "Wanted and unwanted eddy currents"
**Wanted eddy currents** are used in:
- **induction hobs** (heat the bottom of the pan through eddy currents),
- **magnetic brakes** on, for example, "[[free fall|When an object falls completely without air resistance, so that only the gravitational force acts on it. All objects then fall equally fast regardless of mass.]]" rides at amusement parks.

**Unwanted eddy currents** occur in AC devices and produce unwanted
**[[heat|Energy transferred between objects at different temperatures. Measured in joules (J).]]**.
:::

::: kuriosa "How an induction hob works"
1. **Alternating current through a copper coil** in the hob creates an alternating
   magnetic field. The 50 Hz from the wall socket is not enough: the [[frequency|Number of oscillations per second. SI unit hertz (Hz) = 1/s.]] is first increased
   almost 1 000 times, to nearly 50 kHz.
2. **A pan is placed on the hob.** The oscillations in the magnetic field induce
   electric eddy currents in the bottom of the pan. Such currents are induced
   in all metals, but for the heating to be strong the pan should be magnetic
   (iron or magnetic stainless steel). A magnetic material gathers and
   strengthens the alternating field.
3. Because of the so-called **skin effect**, the eddy
   currents are concentrated in a thin layer near the surface of the metal, and in a magnetic
   material the layer is especially thin. This increases the electrical
   resistance, which leads to strong **heat generation**. In a pan made of,
   for example, aluminium the heating is too weak.
4. **Magnetic hysteresis**, a kind of magnetic [[inertia|An object's resistance to a change in its motion. Larger mass → greater inertia.]], also contributes. With
   each oscillation of the field the magnetisation of the pan changes. Since
   the material has a certain inertia, this rapid change generates heat.
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Eddy currents"
- Circulating induced currents in a metal that moves in a
  magnetic field, or where the field changes.
- According to **Lenz's law** they always oppose the cause of their origin.
:::

::: sampunkt "The pendulum experiment"
- An aluminium pendulum swinging through a magnetic field **stops**.
- The currents swirl freely in the plate and give a force that brakes
  the motion.
- A pendulum with **slots** is braked much less, since the whirls cannot
  close.
:::

::: sampunkt "Wanted eddy currents"
- **Induction hobs**: eddy currents heat the bottom of the pan directly.
- **Magnetic brakes**, for example in the free fall rides of amusement parks.
  They brake without anything touching anything.
:::

::: sampunkt "Unwanted eddy currents"
- In AC devices and transformers they give **unnecessary heat**,
  that is, energy losses.
- That is why transformer cores are built from thin, insulated sheets instead
  of a solid iron block.
:::

::: sampunkt "The induction hob"
- The frequency is raised from the mains $50\ \mathrm{Hz}$ to nearly
  $50\ \mathrm{kHz}$.
- The rapidly alternating field induces eddy currents in the bottom of the pan.
- The **skin effect** concentrates the currents in the surface layer, which increases
  the resistance and thereby the heat. The effect is greatest in a **magnetic**
  pan, which is why the pan should be magnetic.
- **Magnetic hysteresis**, a kind of magnetic inertia, also contributes.
:::
:::
