# Shifts in the *x*- and *y*-direction

When a graph is moved in a coordinate system without its appearance otherwise
changing, it is called a **shift** (translation). For the sine and cosine curves the
shift depends on two constants: one that moves the curve in the $y$-direction and one
that moves it in the $x$-direction.

## Shift in the *y*-direction

We compare $y = \sin x$ with $y = \sin x + 3$ in a table of values.

| $x$ | $\sin x$ | $\sin x + 3$ |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| $0^\circ$ | $0$ | $3$ |
| $90^\circ$ | $1$ | $4$ |
| $180^\circ$ | $0$ | $3$ |
| $270^\circ$ | $-1$ | $2$ |
| $360^\circ$ | $0$ | $3$ |

Every $y$-coordinate becomes 3 larger, since 3 is added to every value.
Graphically this corresponds to the curve of $y = \sin x + 3$ being shifted 3 units
**up** (in the $y$-direction) compared with $y = \sin x$. In the same way
$y = \sin x - 2$ is shifted two units **down** compared with $y = \sin x$.

## Shift in the *x*-direction

Now we compare $y = \sin x$ with $y = \sin (x - 90^\circ)$.

| $x$ | $\sin x$ | $\sin (x - 90^\circ)$ |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| $0^\circ$ | $0$ | $-1$ |
| $90^\circ$ | $1$ | $0$ |
| $180^\circ$ | $0$ | $1$ |
| $270^\circ$ | $-1$ | $0$ |
| $360^\circ$ | $0$ | $-1$ |

The graph of $y = \sin (x - 90^\circ)$ is shifted $90^\circ$ **to the right**
(in the $x$-direction) compared with $y = \sin x$. Notice the sign: a *minus sign*
inside the argument gives a shift to the *right*. In the same way
$y = \sin (x + 50^\circ)$ is shifted $50^\circ$ **to the left** compared with $y = \sin x$.

::: formel "Shifts in the *x*- and *y*-direction"
The graph of

$$
y = \sin (x + C) + D
$$

is shifted $D$ units in the $y$-direction and $C$ degrees or radians in the $x$-direction,
compared with $y = \sin x$. The direction in the $x$-direction is determined by the sign of $C$:

$$
C > 0 \;\Rightarrow\; \text{to the left} \qquad\qquad C < 0 \;\Rightarrow\; \text{to the right}
$$
:::

it up or down. A positive $C$ pushes the curve to the left.

## General form

The same applies if the curve is also stretched. With all four constants
together we write the general sine function like this.

::: formel "In general for sine and cosine functions"
The graph of

$$
y = A \sin (Bx + C) + D
$$

- has the amplitude $|A|$
- has the period $\dfrac{360^\circ}{B}$ (or $\dfrac{2\pi}{B}$ rad)
- is shifted $-\dfrac{C}{B}$ degrees or radians in the $x$-direction compared with $y = A \sin Bx$
- is shifted $D$ units in the $y$-direction compared with $y = A \sin Bx$

The same also applies to cosine functions.
:::

Since the amplitude is $|A|$, for example $A = -2$ gives the same amplitude as
$A = 2$ (the curve is just reflected in the equilibrium position). So you can write
$A = -2$, but it usually makes the interpretation of the further calculations harder,
so it is best to choose a positive $A$.

::: exempel "Example 1: Describe the shift"
**Describe what the graph of $y = \sin (x + 45^\circ) - 12$ looks like compared with
$y = \sin x$.**

::: handskrift
typ: forskjutning
:::

::: textlosning
The argument contains $(x + 45^\circ)$. Since the constant is positive
($C = 45^\circ > 0$), the curve is shifted $45^\circ$ **to the left**. The term
$-12$ (that is $D = -12$) means that the curve is shifted 12 units **down**.

**Answer:** Compared with $y = \sin x$ the graph is shifted $45^\circ$ to the left
and 12 units down.
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 2: Write the equation of the function"
**A curve has the same shape as $y = \sin x$ but is shifted $30^\circ$ to the
right and 2 units up. Write the equation of the function in the form
$y = \sin (x + C) + D$.**

::: handskrift
typ: skrivforskjuten
:::

::: textlosning
The upward shift gives the $y$-direction directly: $D = 2$.

For the $x$-direction, a shift **to the right** requires a *negative* $C$.
So if we want to move the curve $30^\circ$ to the right we choose $C = -30^\circ$,
which gives the argument $\sin(x - 30^\circ)$.

$$
y = \sin (x + C) + D = \sin \big(x + (-30^\circ)\big) + 2 = \sin (x - 30^\circ) + 2
$$

**Answer:** $y = \sin (x - 30^\circ) + 2$
:::
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Shift"
- A **shift** means that the graph is moved without its shape changing.
- $D$ moves the curve in the $y$-direction: up if $D > 0$, down if $D < 0$.
- $C$ moves the curve in the $x$-direction.
:::

::: sampunkt "The sign sideways"
- A **positive** $C$ in the argument gives a shift **to the left**.
- A **negative** $C$ gives a shift **to the right**.
- So the sign is the opposite of what you first guess. $\sin(x - 90^\circ)$ is moved $90^\circ$ to the right.
:::

::: sampunkt "General form"
$y = A \sin(Bx + C) + D$
- has the amplitude $|A|$,
- has the period $\dfrac{360^\circ}{B}$ or $\dfrac{2\pi}{B}$ rad,
- is shifted $-\dfrac{C}{B}$ in the $x$-direction,
- is shifted $D$ units in the $y$-direction.
- The same applies to cosine functions.
:::

::: sampunkt "Writing the equation"
1. Read off the shift up or down. That gives $D$ directly.
2. Read off the shift sideways and give it the **opposite** sign.
- A shift of $30^\circ$ to the right and 2 up gives $y = \sin(x - 30^\circ) + 2$.
- It is best to choose a positive $A$, even though a negative one gives the same amplitude. It makes the interpretation easier.
:::

:::
