# More on solids of revolution (rotation about the *y*-axis)

We have previously learnt to calculate the volume of a solid that is formed when a
function rotates about the $x$-axis. Then we imagined that the volume consisted of
lots of thin, upright discs stacked in a row along the $x$-axis. We can also
calculate volumes by rotating a curve about the $y$-axis.

The difference is that the curve's $x$-value (instead of the $y$-value) gives the radius of
each disc, that the thickness of the disc corresponds to $dy$ (instead of $dx$)
and that the integration takes place along the $y$-axis (instead of along the $x$-axis).

## Derivation: the disc method for rotation about the *y*-axis

We divide the solid into thin discs perpendicular to the $y$-axis. Each disc can
be seen as a cylinder with radius $r = x$ (the curve's $x$-value) and height
$h = \Delta y$. The volume of one disc is then the base area (a circle) times the height:

$$
V_\text{disc} = \pi\cdot r^2\cdot h = \pi\cdot x^2\cdot \Delta y
$$

The volume of the whole solid $V$ is the sum of the volumes of all the discs:

$$
V = \sum_{y=a}^{b} \pi\cdot x^2\cdot \Delta y
$$

To get the exact volume we let the height $\Delta y$ of each disc become
infinitely small ($dy$) and add up with an integral. This gives:

$$
V = \int_a^b \pi\cdot x^2\, dy = \pi\int_a^b x^2\, dy
$$

::: formel "Volume for rotation about the *y*-axis"
When a function $y = f(x)$ rotates around the $y$-axis in the interval
$a \leq y \leq b$, the volume $V$ is calculated with the formula

$$
V = \pi\int_a^b x^2\, dy
$$
:::

Since we integrate with respect to $y$, $x$ must be expressed in terms of
the variable $y$, which means that we need a function $x = f(y)$. Since
we often have the function in the form $y = \ldots$, this means that we will
need to solve for $x$ in the equation to substitute into the integral. This is called
finding the inverse.

::: exempel "Example 1: Rotation about the *y*-axis"
**Let the region bounded by the curve $y = \sqrt{x - 1}$, the positive
coordinate axes and the line $y = 2$ rotate about the $y$-axis. Calculate
the volume of the solid of revolution.**

::: handskrift
typ: rotationyaxel
:::

::: textlosning
We set up the formula for the volume for rotation about the $y$-axis:

$$
V = \pi\int_a^b x^2\, dy
$$

The boundaries give the limits of integration $a = 0$ and $b = 2$ (remember that
since we integrate along the $y$-axis, it is limits in the $y$-direction that must be
given).

We solve for $x$ in the function $y = \sqrt{x - 1}$. Square both sides to get
rid of the root sign and then solve for $x$:

$$
y^2 = x - 1 \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad x = y^2 + 1
$$

We now substitute $x = y^2 + 1$ and the limits of integration $a = 0$ and $b = 2$ into
the formula for the volume and then expand with the square rule:

$$
V = \pi\int_0^2 (y^2 + 1)^2\, dy = \pi\int_0^2 (y^4 + 2y^2 + 1)\, dy
$$

The fundamental theorem of calculus gives:

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= \pi\left[\frac{y^5}{5} + \frac{2y^3}{3} + y\right]_0^2
   = \pi\left(\frac{2^5}{5} + \frac{2\cdot 2^3}{3} + 2 - 0\right)\\[4pt]
  &= \pi\left(\frac{32}{5} + \frac{16}{3} + 2\right)
   = \pi\left(\frac{96}{15} + \frac{80}{15} + \frac{30}{15}\right)
   = \pi\cdot \frac{206}{15} = \frac{206\pi}{15}
\end{aligned}
$$

This gives $\dfrac{206\pi}{15} \approx 43.1$ volume units.

**Answer:** $V = \dfrac{206\pi}{15}\ \text{v.u.} \approx 43.1\ \text{v.u.}$
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 2: The curve y = x²"
**The region bounded by the curve $y = x^2$, the $y$-axis and the line $y = 4$
rotates about the $y$-axis. Calculate the volume exactly.**

::: handskrift
typ: rotationkvadrat
:::

::: textlosning
The formula requires $x$ expressed in terms of $y$. We solve for $x$ in $y = x^2$, and since
the region lies in the first quadrant we take the positive root:

$$
x = \sqrt{y}
$$

The radius squared is then $x^2 = (\sqrt{y})^2 = y$. With the limits $a = 0$ and
$b = 4$:

$$
V = \pi\int_0^4 x^2\, dy = \pi\int_0^4 y\, dy
= \pi\left[\frac{y^2}{2}\right]_0^4 = \pi\left(\frac{16}{2} - 0\right) = 8\pi
$$

**Answer:** $V = 8\pi\ \text{v.u.} \approx 25.1\ \text{v.u.}$
:::
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Rotation about the vertical axis"
- $V = \pi \int_a^b x^2\,dy$.
- Now the **$x$-value** is the radius and the thickness is $dy$.
- The discs are perpendicular to the $y$-axis, that is lying down instead of standing up.
:::

::: sampunkt "Three differences from the previous case"
- The radius is $x$ instead of $y$.
- The variable of integration is $dy$ instead of $dx$.
- The limits are given in the **$y$-direction**, not in the $x$-direction.
:::

::: sampunkt "Finding the inverse"
- The integral requires $x$ expressed in terms of $y$, that is $x = f(y)$.
- Square away root signs: $y = \sqrt{x - 1}$ gives $x = y^2 + 1$.
- Solving for $x$ is called finding the **inverse**.
- If the region lies in the first quadrant, the positive root is chosen.
:::

::: sampunkt "Method"
1. Read off the limits in the $y$-direction from the boundaries of the region.
2. Solve for $x$ in the function and square the expression.
3. Integrate and substitute the limits.
- $y = x^2$ gives $x^2 = y$, so the simple integral $\pi\int_0^4 y\,dy = 8\pi$.
:::
