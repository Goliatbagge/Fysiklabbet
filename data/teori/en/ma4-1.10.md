# The sine and cosine functions

The function $y = \sin x$ is called the **sine function** and the function
$y = \cos x$ is called the **cosine function**. We investigate what they look like
graphically with the help of the unit circle and the values from the
formula sheet, rounded to two decimal places.

| $x$ | $\sin x$ | $\cos x$ |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| $0$ | $0$ | $1$ |
| $30^\circ$ | $0.5$ | $0.87$ |
| $60^\circ$ | $0.87$ | $0.5$ |
| $90^\circ$ | $1$ | $0$ |
| $120^\circ$ | $0.87$ | $-0.5$ |
| $150^\circ$ | $0.5$ | $-0.87$ |
| $180^\circ$ | $0$ | $-1$ |
| $210^\circ$ | $-0.5$ | $-0.87$ |
| $240^\circ$ | $-0.87$ | $-0.5$ |
| $270^\circ$ | $-1$ | $0$ |
| $300^\circ$ | $-0.87$ | $0.5$ |
| $330^\circ$ | $-0.5$ | $0.87$ |
| $360^\circ$ | $0$ | $1$ |

If we plot the values we get a smoothly waving curve. Drag and zoom in the graph
below. The $x$-axis is in radians, and you can see how $y = \sin x$ oscillates between
$-1$ and $1$.

::: graf
titel: y = \sin x
uttryck: sin(x)
ekvation: y = \sin x
x: -6.5, 6.5
y: -1.6, 1.6
:::

The curves for $y = \sin x$ and $y = \cos x$ look very similar. The curve for
$y = \sin x$ starts at $y = 0$ and the curve for $y = \cos x$ starts at
$y = 1$, but otherwise they have the same shape. The curve for $y = \cos x$ is shifted
$90^\circ$ to the left compared with $y = \sin x$.

Both functions repeat themselves at regular intervals: they are **periodic**.
After a full turn exactly the same values repeat, so the period is $360^\circ$
(corresponding to $2\pi$ radians). Since the sine and cosine values come
from points on the unit circle, they can never be greater than $1$ or less
than $-1$; the range is therefore $-1 \leq y \leq 1$.

::: formel "The sine and cosine functions"
$$
y = \sin x \qquad y = \cos x
$$

Both functions are periodic with the **period** $360^\circ = 2\pi$ and have
the **range**

$$
-1 \leq y \leq 1
$$
:::

Just as with all equations, we can solve trigonometric equations
graphically by drawing both sides and finding the $x$-coordinates of the intersection
points. We draw the equation's left-hand side $y = \text{LHS}$ and
right-hand side $y = \text{RHS}$ in a graphing tool and read off the
$x$-coordinate at each intersection point. These are the solutions.

::: exempel "Example 1: Solve sin x = 0.4 graphically, answer in degrees"
**Solve the equation $\sin x = 0.4$ graphically and answer in degrees.**

::: handskrift
typ: grafisksin
:::

::: textlosning
We draw $y = \sin x$ and the horizontal line $y = 0.4$ in a graphing
tool and find the intersection points. To get the solutions in degrees
we draw the sine function with a degree sign, $y = \sin(x^\circ)$. Within one turn
we get two intersection points, and after that they repeat:

$$
x_1 \approx 23.6^\circ, \quad x_2 \approx 156.4^\circ, \quad
x_3 \approx 383.6^\circ, \quad x_4 \approx 516.4^\circ, \ldots
$$

Every other solution is periodic with the period $360^\circ$: here
$x_3 = x_1 + 360^\circ$ and $x_4 = x_2 + 360^\circ$. To describe
all solutions we give the first two and add a whole number of
periods $n \cdot 360^\circ$, where $n$ is an integer.

**Answer:** $x \approx 23.6^\circ + n \cdot 360^\circ$ and
$x \approx 156.4^\circ + n \cdot 360^\circ$
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 2: Solve cos x = x graphically, answer in radians"
**Solve the equation $\cos x = x$ graphically and answer in radians.**

::: handskrift
typ: grafiskcosx
:::

::: textlosning
We draw $y = \cos x$ and the straight line $y = x$ and find the
$x$-coordinate of the intersection point. Since we want the answer in radians, we draw
the cosine **without** a degree sign. The two curves intersect at a single
point:

$$
x \approx 0.74
$$

Since the answer is in radians we do not write out a unit. Here we should
not add the period $n \cdot 2\pi$ either, since the line $y = x$ grows
without bound and crosses the cosine curve only once. So there is
only one solution.

**Answer:** $x \approx 0.74$
:::
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "What the curves look like"
- $y = \sin x$ starts at $y = 0$ and $y = \cos x$ starts at $y = 1$.
- The shape is the same. The cosine curve is the sine curve shifted $90^\circ$ to the left.
- Both are smoothly waving curves without corners.
:::

::: sampunkt "Period and range"
- Both functions are **periodic** with the period $360^\circ = 2\pi$.
- The range is $-1 \leq y \leq 1$.
- The reason is that the values are coordinates on the unit circle, whose radius is 1.
:::

::: sampunkt "Solving graphically"
1. Draw $y = \text{LHS}$ and $y = \text{RHS}$ in a graphing tool.
2. Read off the $x$-coordinates of the intersection points.
- If you want the answer in **degrees**, write $\sin(x^\circ)$; otherwise $x$ is interpreted as radians.
:::

::: sampunkt "Giving the answer"
- Take the first two solutions and add $n \cdot 360^\circ$.
- $\sin x = 0.4$ gives $x \approx 23.6^\circ + n \cdot 360^\circ$ and $x \approx 156.4^\circ + n \cdot 360^\circ$.
- **Exception:** if the other side crosses the curve only once, like the line $y = x$, there is only one solution and no period term.
:::

:::
