# Curve sketching with the derivative

In curve sketching with the derivative we use the same method for finding
stationary points and their nature that we have gone through before. The difference is that
we now sketch the shape of the whole graph from the stationary points and any
endpoints.

::: formel "Finding stationary points and their nature"
1. Differentiate the original function, set the derivative equal to $0$ and solve
   the equation. We then get the $x$-coordinates of the stationary points.

2. Substitute the $x$-values into **the original function**. We then get
   the $y$-coordinates of the stationary points.

3. Determine the nature of the stationary points using the value of the second derivative. If
   $f'(a) = 0$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
f''(a) &> 0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad \text{local minimum point where } x = a\\
f''(a) &< 0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad \text{local maximum point where } x = a\\
f''(a) &= 0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad \text{stationary point of inflection or local extreme point}
\end{aligned}
$$

If $f''(a) = 0$, a sign study (sign table) is needed to decide
the nature. If the function is a quadratic function, the nature can be read off directly
from the sign in front of the $x^2$ term: a positive $x^2$ term gives a minimum point and a negative one
gives a maximum point.
:::

When we sketch more advanced functions, it is often easiest to use a
**sign table**. In the sign table we mark the zeros of the derivative and
the endpoints of the interval, investigate the sign of the derivative in each subinterval and
read off where the function is increasing ($\nearrow$) and decreasing ($\searrow$). Remember
that a sketch does not have to be exact, but points where the derivative is $0$
(extreme points and stationary points of inflection) and endpoints must be correctly marked.

::: exempel "Example 1: Sketch the graph using a sign table"
**Sketch the graph of $f(x) = (x - 1)^2 e^x$ in the interval $-2 \le x \le 2$.**

**Step 1: Differentiate the function, set it equal to $0$ and find the
$x$-coordinates of the points where the slope is $0$.**

::: handskrift
typ: teckentabell
:::

::: textlosning
The product rule and the chain rule give:

$$
\begin{aligned}
f'(x) &= 2(x - 1)\cdot 1\cdot e^x + (x - 1)^2\cdot e^x\\
f'(x) &= (2x - 2)e^x + (x^2 - 2x + 1)e^x
\end{aligned}
$$

We factorise by taking out $e^x$ so that we can use
the zero product method:

$$
\begin{aligned}
f'(x) &= e^x(2x - 2 + x^2 - 2x + 1)\\
f'(x) &= e^x(x^2 - 1)
\end{aligned}
$$

$f'(x) = 0$ gives $e^x(x^2 - 1) = 0$. The zero product method gives two cases:

$$
\text{Case 1:}\quad e^x = 0 \quad\text{has no solution since } e^x > 0
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Case 2:}\quad x^2 - 1 &= 0\\
x^2 &= 1\\
x &= \pm\sqrt{1}\\
x_1 = -1 \;&\text{and}\; x_2 = 1
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 2: Investigate the sign of the derivative on both sides of the zeros.**

We choose an $x$-value in each subinterval and substitute it into the derivative:

$$
\begin{aligned}
f'(-2) &= e^{-2}\bigl((-2)^2 - 1\bigr) = e^{-2}\cdot 3 = \frac{3}{e^2} \approx 0.406 > 0 \quad(\text{positive})\\
f'(0) &= e^0(0^2 - 1) = 1\cdot(-1) = -1 < 0 \quad(\text{negative})\\
f'(2) &= e^2(2^2 - 1) = e^2\cdot 3 = 3e^2 \approx 22.167 > 0 \quad(\text{positive})
\end{aligned}
$$

We fill in plus and minus signs in the row for the derivative and then draw
"arrow up" ($\nearrow$) where the derivative is positive and "arrow down" ($\searrow$) where
it is negative in the row for the function:

| $x$ | $-2$ | $-2 < x < -1$ | $-1$ | $-1 < x < 1$ | $1$ | $1 < x < 2$ | $2$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $f'(x)$ | | $+$ | $0$ | $-$ | $0$ | $+$ | |
| $f(x)$ | | $\nearrow$ | | $\searrow$ | | $\nearrow$ | |

The sign table shows that $x = -1$ is a local **maximum point** (the derivative goes
from $+$ to $-$) and that $x = 1$ is a local **minimum point** (the derivative goes
from $-$ to $+$).

**Step 3: Substitute the $x$-values of the derivative's zeros and the interval's
endpoints into the original function to find the $y$-coordinates.**

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(-2) &= (-2 - 1)^2\cdot e^{-2} = (-3)^2\cdot e^{-2} = \frac{9}{e^2} \approx 1.22 \quad\Rightarrow\quad (-2,\ 1.22) \text{ an endpoint}\\
f(2) &= (2 - 1)^2\cdot e^2 = (1)^2\cdot e^2 = e^2 \approx 7.39 \quad\Rightarrow\quad (2,\ 7.39) \text{ an endpoint}\\
f(-1) &= (-1 - 1)^2\cdot e^{-1} = (-2)^2\cdot e^{-1} = \frac{4}{e} \approx 1.47 \quad\Rightarrow\quad (-1,\ 1.47) \text{ a maximum point}\\
f(1) &= (1 - 1)^2\cdot e^1 = (0)^2\cdot e = 0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad (1,\ 0) \text{ a minimum point}
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 4: Sketch the graph.**

We draw a coordinate system that covers all the $x$- and $y$-values we
have calculated, mark the endpoints and the extreme points and join them
with a graph according to the sign table: the curve increases slightly up to the maximum point
$(-1,\ 1.47)$, decreases down to the minimum point $(1,\ 0)$ and then rises steeply
to the endpoint $(2,\ 7.39)$.

**Answer:** The curve has a local maximum point at $(-1,\ 1.47)$, a local
minimum point at $(1,\ 0)$ and endpoints at $(-2,\ 1.22)$ and $(2,\ 7.39)$.
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "The method"
1. Differentiate, set $f'(x) = 0$ and solve the equation. That gives the $x$-coordinates.
2. Substitute the $x$-values into the **original** function for the $y$-coordinates.
3. Determine the nature using the sign of the second derivative or with a sign table.
:::

::: sampunkt "Nature"
- $f''(a) > 0$ gives a **minimum point**, $f''(a) < 0$ gives a **maximum point**.
- $f''(a) = 0$ requires a sign study.
- In a sign table: $+$ to $-$ gives a maximum point, $-$ to $+$ gives a minimum point.
:::

::: sampunkt "The sign table"
- Mark the zeros of the derivative **and** the endpoints of the interval.
- Substitute an $x$-value from each subinterval into the derivative and note the sign.
- Draw $\nearrow$ where the derivative is positive and $\searrow$ where it is negative.
:::

::: sampunkt "The sketch"
- Take out a common factor so that you can use the zero product method. $e^x$ is always positive and gives no zeros.
- Also calculate the $y$-values of the **endpoints**.
- Mark extreme points and endpoints exactly and join them according to the sign table. The rest of the sketch does not have to be exact.
:::
