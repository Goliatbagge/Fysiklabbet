# Polynomial equations of higher degree and polynomial division

In Mathematics continuation level 1c we solved polynomial equations by taking out
$x$ as a common factor or by substitution, and we saw that a polynomial can be factorised
using its zeros. The relationship that makes this possible is the factor theorem,
which we revise here. What is new in this section is **polynomial division**:
a way of finding the other factors when we only know one of the
zeros. Then we can break down an equation of higher degree into one of
lower degree, which we can already solve.

::: formel "The factor theorem"
If $x = a$ is a zero of the polynomial $p(x)$, then $(x - a)$ is a factor
of the polynomial. The polynomial can then be written

$$
p(x) = (x - a)\cdot q(x)
$$

where $q(x)$ is a polynomial of lower degree.
:::

When we solve polynomial equations of degree higher than 2, we do it by rewriting
the equation so that it is equal to $0$, factorising and using
the **zero product property**. Sometimes it is hard to find the factors directly. Then
we can find them by dividing polynomials by each other, which is called
**polynomial division**.

Suppose we want to solve the equation $x^3 + 2x^2 - x - 2 = 0$ and that we know that
the polynomial has the zero $x = -1$. Then $(x + 1)$ must be a factor according to
the factor theorem, so we can write

$$
x^3 + 2x^2 - x - 2 = (x + 1)(\ldots)
$$

To find the other factor we divide $x^3 + 2x^2 - x - 2$ by
$x + 1$. The polynomial is written with a line above it and the divisor $x + 1$ in
the step to the right. The quotient is built up term by term above the line, and
at each step we subtract what we have multiplied out:

$$
\def\arraystretch{1.45}
\begin{array}{l}
\phantom{-\,(}x^2 + x - 2 \\
\phantom{-\,(}\overline{x^3 + 2x^2 - x - 2\;}\rule[-0.24em]{0.04em}{1.12em}\underline{\;x + 1\;} \\
\phantom{-\,(}\mkern-24mu{-}\,\underline{(\mathrlap{x^3 + x^2)}\phantom{x^3 + 2x^2 - x - 2}} \\
\phantom{-\,(x^3 {}+{}}x^2 - x - 2 \\
\phantom{-\,(x^3 {}+{}}\mkern-24mu{-}\,\underline{(\mathrlap{x^2 + x)}\phantom{x^2 - x - 2}} \\
\phantom{-\,(x^3 {}+{} x^2}\:{-}2x - 2 \\
\phantom{-\,(x^3 {}+{} x^2}\:\mkern-24mu{-}\,\underline{({-}2x - 2)} \\
\phantom{-\,(x^3 {}+{} x^2}\:\phantom{{-}2x {}-{}}0
\end{array}
$$

First $x^3 : x = x^2$, which is written in the quotient. The product
$x^2(x + 1) = x^3 + x^2$ is written in brackets with a minus sign in front,
since the whole product is to be subtracted, and below the line
$x^2 - x - 2$ remains. Then $x^2 : x = x$, and when
$x(x + 1) = x^2 + x$ is subtracted, $-2x - 2$ remains. Finally
$-2x : x = -2$, and when $-2(x + 1) = -2x - 2$ is subtracted, the remainder is $0$.
So the quotient is $x^2 + x - 2$, and

$$
x^3 + 2x^2 - x - 2 = (x + 1)(x^2 + x - 2)
$$

Now we can go back to the equation, factorise and solve it with
the zero product property. We solve the quadratic factor by completing the square (or
the $pq$ formula):

$$
(x + 1)(x^2 + x - 2) = 0
$$

The factor $x + 1 = 0$ gives the root we already know, $x_1 = -1$. The factor
$x^2 + x - 2 = 0$ gives

$$
x = -\frac{1}{2} \pm \sqrt{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^2 + 2}
= -\frac{1}{2} \pm \sqrt{\frac{9}{4}}
= -\frac{1}{2} \pm \frac{3}{2}
$$

so $x_2 = -\dfrac{1}{2} - \dfrac{3}{2} = -2$ and
$x_3 = -\dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{3}{2} = 1$. So the equation
$x^3 + 2x^2 - x - 2 = 0$ has the solutions $x_1 = -1$, $x_2 = -2$ and
$x_3 = 1$.

::: exempel "Example 1: an already factorised polynomial"
**Find the zeros of $p(x) = x(x + 4)(x - 10)$.**

::: handskrift
typ: faktoriseratpolynom
:::

::: textlosning
The zeros are obtained when $p(x) = 0$, that is

$$
x(x + 4)(x - 10) = 0
$$

The zero product property gives three possibilities, one per factor:

$$
x_1 = 0, \qquad x + 4 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; x_2 = -4, \qquad
x - 10 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; x_3 = 10
$$

**Answer:** $x_1 = 0$, $x_2 = -4$ and $x_3 = 10$
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 3: take out x"
**Solve the equation $x^3 - 7x^2 + 10x = 0$.**

::: handskrift
typ: brytutxrot
:::

::: textlosning
We factorise the left-hand side by taking out $x$:

$$
x(x^2 - 7x + 10) = 0
$$

The zero product property gives $x_1 = 0$ and the quadratic factor $x^2 - 7x + 10 = 0$:

$$
x = \frac{7}{2} \pm \sqrt{\left(\frac{7}{2}\right)^2 - 10}
= \frac{7}{2} \pm \sqrt{\frac{49}{4} - \frac{40}{4}}
= \frac{7}{2} \pm \sqrt{\frac{9}{4}}
= \frac{7}{2} \pm \frac{3}{2}
$$

This gives $x_2 = \dfrac{7}{2} - \dfrac{3}{2} = 2$ and
$x_3 = \dfrac{7}{2} + \dfrac{3}{2} = 5$.

**Answer:** $x_1 = 0$, $x_2 = 2$ and $x_3 = 5$
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 4: polynomial division with a given root"
**The equation $x^3 + 4x^2 - 7x - 10 = 0$ has the root $x = 2$. Find the other
roots of the equation.**

::: handskrift
typ: polynomdivision
:::

::: textlosning
Since $x = 2$ is a root, $(x - 2)$ is a factor according to the factor theorem. We write the polynomial with a line above it and the divisor
$x - 2$ in the step to the right. The quotient is built up term by term above
the line:

$$
\def\arraystretch{1.45}
\begin{array}{l}
\phantom{-\,(}x^2 + 6x + 5 \\
\phantom{-\,(}\overline{x^3 + 4x^2 - 7x - 10\;}\rule[-0.24em]{0.04em}{1.12em}\underline{\;x - 2\;} \\
\phantom{-\,(}\mkern-24mu{-}\,\underline{(\mathrlap{x^3 - 2x^2)}\phantom{x^3 + 4x^2 - 7x - 10}} \\
\phantom{-\,(x^3 {}+{}}6x^2 - 7x - 10 \\
\phantom{-\,(x^3 {}+{}}\mkern-24mu{-}\,\underline{(\mathrlap{6x^2 - 12x)}\phantom{6x^2 - 7x - 10}} \\
\phantom{-\,(x^3 {}+{} 6x^2 {}-{}}5x - 10 \\
\phantom{-\,(x^3 {}+{} 6x^2 {}-{}}\mkern-24mu{-}\,\underline{(5x - 10)} \\
\phantom{-\,(x^3 {}+{} 6x^2 {}-{} 5x {}-{} 1}0
\end{array}
$$

Each round works the same way. The first term $x^3$ divided by
the divisor's $x$ gives $x^2$, which is written in the quotient. The product $x^2(x - 2) = x^3 - 2x^2$ is written below
the terms of the same degree, in brackets with a minus sign in front, since
the whole product is to be subtracted. Below the line everything that remains is written,
$6x^2 - 7x - 10$. Then $6x^2 : x = 6x$, and when
$6x(x - 2) = 6x^2 - 12x$ is subtracted, $5x - 10$ remains. Finally
$5x : x = 5$, and when $5(x - 2) = 5x - 10$ is subtracted, the remainder is $0$, so
$x - 2$ divides exactly. The quotient is at the top: $x^2 + 6x + 5$. It is the other
factor, so the equation can be written

$$
(x - 2)(x^2 + 6x + 5) = 0
$$

The factor $x - 2$ gives the root we already know, $x = 2$. The other roots are obtained from
$x^2 + 6x + 5 = 0$:

$$
x = -3 \pm \sqrt{3^2 - 5} = -3 \pm \sqrt{4} = -3 \pm 2
$$

which gives $x_1 = -3 - 2 = -5$ and $x_2 = -3 + 2 = -1$.

**Answer:** $x_1 = -5$ and $x_2 = -1$
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "The factor theorem"
- If $x = a$ is a zero of $p(x)$, then $(x - a)$ is a **factor**.
- The polynomial can then be written $p(x) = (x - a) \cdot q(x)$, where $q(x)$ has a lower degree.
- This is how an equation of high degree is broken down into one that can be solved.
:::

::: sampunkt "Method"
1. Rewrite the equation so that one side is 0.
2. Factorise, by taking out a common factor or with polynomial division.
3. Use the **zero product property**: each factor is set equal to zero.
- If $x$ can be taken out directly, that is always quickest.
:::

::: sampunkt "Polynomial division"
- Used when the factor cannot be seen directly but a root is known.
- Divide term by term: take the highest-degree term, multiply out and subtract.
- Repeat until the remainder is 0. The quotient is the other factor.
:::

::: sampunkt "Graphical solution"
- The roots are the **zeros** of the graph, that is where the curve crosses the $x$-axis.
- A cubic equation has at most three real roots.
- Read them off the graph and preferably check by substitution.
:::

:::
