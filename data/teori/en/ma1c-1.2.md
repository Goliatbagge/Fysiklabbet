is called the **denominator**. A way to remember it: the **d**enominator is
**d**own below.

in size.

## Expanding and simplifying

::: formel "Expanding and simplifying"
When we rewrite a fraction without changing its size, we **expand**
or **simplify** the fraction. Expanding and simplifying do not change
the size of the fraction. When we

- **multiply** the numerator and denominator by the same number, we **expand** the fraction
- **divide** the numerator and denominator by the same number, we **simplify** the fraction.
:::

::: exempel "Example 1: Simplify a fraction"
**Simplify $\dfrac{6}{15}$ by 3.**

::: handskrift
typ: forkorta
:::

::: textlosning
We divide the numerator and denominator by 3:

$$
\frac{6}{15} = \frac{6/3}{15/3} = \frac{2}{5}
$$

**Answer:** $\dfrac{2}{5}$
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 2: Expand a fraction"
**Expand $\dfrac{2}{3}$ so that the denominator becomes 12.**

::: handskrift
typ: forlanga
:::

::: textlosning
Think: "What should I multiply the denominator 3 by to make it 12? By
4, of course. So I should multiply the numerator and denominator by 4!"

$$
\frac{2}{3} = \frac{2 \cdot 4}{3 \cdot 4} = \frac{8}{12}
$$

**Answer:** $\dfrac{8}{12}$
:::
:::

::: härledning "NOTE: Cancelling identical numbers"
Simplifying a fraction by "cancelling identical numbers" in the numerator and denominator
can only be done when there is a **multiplication sign** between them. So you can
*not* cancel identical numbers that have a plus or minus sign between them.

If we write $2 = \dfrac{8}{4} = \dfrac{4 \cdot 2}{4 \cdot 1}$ and "cancel"
the 4s (multiplication sign between), we get $\dfrac{2}{1} = 2$. Correct!

If we instead write $2 = \dfrac{8}{4} = \dfrac{6 + 2}{2 + 2}$ and
"cancel" the 2s (plus sign between), we get $\dfrac{6}{2} = 3 \neq 2$. Not
correct!
:::

## Comparing fractions

::: formel "Comparing fractions"
Comparing fractions is easiest if we rewrite the fractions with the **same
denominator**. For example, we easily see that $\dfrac{5}{7}$ is greater than $\dfrac{4}{7}$.
:::

::: exempel "Example 3: Which fraction is the largest?"
**Which fraction is the largest: $\dfrac{4}{9}$ or $\dfrac{3}{7}$?**

::: handskrift
typ: jamfora
:::

::: textlosning
We rewrite the fractions with the same denominator! One way is to expand each
fraction by the number in the denominator *of the other fraction*.

We expand $\dfrac{4}{9}$ by 7, since that is in the denominator of the other
fraction. We expand $\dfrac{3}{7}$ by 9, since that is in the denominator of the
first fraction.

$$
\frac{4}{9} = \frac{4 \cdot 7}{9 \cdot 7} = \frac{28}{63}
\qquad\qquad
\frac{3}{7} = \frac{3 \cdot 9}{7 \cdot 9} = \frac{27}{63}
$$

We now easily see that $\dfrac{28}{63}$ is greater than $\dfrac{27}{63}$, and
therefore $\dfrac{4}{9}$ must be the largest.

**Answer:** $\dfrac{4}{9}$
:::
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Numerator and denominator"
- The number **above** the fraction bar is the **numerator**, the number **below** is
  the **denominator**.
- Memory rule: the **d**enominator is **d**own below.
:::

::: sampunkt "Different fractions can be equal"
- $\dfrac{1}{4} = \dfrac{3}{12}$, since they correspond to equal
  shares.
:::

::: sampunkt "Expanding and simplifying"
- **Expand**: multiply the numerator **and** denominator by the same number.
- **Simplify**: divide the numerator **and** denominator by the same number.
- **The size of the fraction does not change** with either of them.
:::

::: sampunkt "Common trap"
- You may only cancel equal numbers when there is a **multiplication sign** between them.
- $\dfrac{4 \cdot 2}{4 \cdot 1}$ may be simplified to
  $\dfrac{2}{1}$.
- $\dfrac{6 + 2}{2 + 2}$ may **not** be simplified by cancelling
  the twos. That gives 3 instead of the correct answer 2.
:::

::: sampunkt "Comparing fractions"
- Rewrite with the **same denominator**, then the numerator decides.
- $\dfrac{5}{7}$ is greater than $\dfrac{4}{7}$ since the denominators are equal.
:::
:::
