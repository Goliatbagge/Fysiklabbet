# The inscribed angle theorem

The edge of a circle is called the **circumference** and an angle with its vertex on the circumference
is called an **inscribed angle**. The middle point of a circle is called the **centre**,
and an angle with its vertex at the centre is called a **central angle**.
Inscribed and central angles have arms that stand on an
**arc**, see the figure below.

::: formel "The inscribed angle theorem"
The central angle is twice as large as the inscribed angle on the same
arc.

$$
y = 2x
$$

where $x$ is the inscribed angle and $y$ is the central angle.
:::

In tasks where you use the inscribed angle theorem, it can be justified with
(the inscribed angle theorem). Inscribed angles can look a little different depending on where
on the circumference they lie. The theorem $y = 2x$ applies as long as the angles stand on
the same arc.

A theorem that follows directly from an earlier theorem is called a **corollary**.

::: tips "Corollaries of the inscribed angle theorem"
1. Inscribed angles on the same arc are equal. Justified by
   (inscribed angles on the same arc).
2. Inscribed angles on a semicircular arc are 90°. Justified by (inscribed angle on
   a semicircular arc).
:::

::: tips "Angles in circles: special cases"
**Tangents.** The angle formed between a tangent and the radius to
the point of contact is always 90°. Justified by (tangent against radius).

**Isosceles triangles.** Two radii that form a triangle always give an
isosceles triangle. The base angles in an isosceles triangle are always
equal. Justified by (isosceles triangle).
:::

::: textlosning
**a)** The inscribed angle $x$ and the central angle 120° stand on the same
arc. The inscribed angle theorem gives

$$
x = \frac{120°}{2} = 60° \quad \text{(the inscribed angle theorem)}
$$

**Answer:** $x = 60°$

**b)** The angles $x$ and 32° are inscribed angles that stand on the same
arc:

$$
x = 32° \quad \text{(inscribed angles on the same arc)}
$$

**Answer:** $x = 32°$
:::

::: handskrift
typ: likbentradier
:::

::: textlosning
Together with the radius, the chord and the diameter form a triangle where two
sides are radii: an isosceles triangle. The base angles are then equal:

$$
x = 23° \quad \text{(base angles in an isosceles triangle: both sides are radii)}
$$

$x$ is an inscribed angle and $y$ is the central angle on the same arc:

$$
y = 2x = 2 \cdot 23° = 46° \quad \text{(the inscribed angle theorem)}
$$

**Answer:** $y = 46°$
:::

::: textlosning
$$
\angle ABP = 90° \quad \text{(tangent against radius)}
$$

The angle sum in the triangle $ABP$ gives

$$
v = 180° - 90° - 50° = 40° \quad \text{(angle sum of a triangle)}
$$

**Answer:** $v = 40°$

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "The concepts"
- **Circumference**: the edge of the circle. **Centre**: its middle point.
- **Inscribed angle**: has its vertex on the circumference.
- **Central angle**: has its vertex at the centre.
- Both stand on an **arc**.
:::

::: sampunkt "The inscribed angle theorem"
- The central angle is **twice as large** as the inscribed angle on the same
  arc.
- $y = 2x$
- Applies **as long as the angles stand on the same arc**.
:::

::: sampunkt "Two corollaries"
- Inscribed angles on **the same** arc are **equal**.
- An inscribed angle on a **semicircular arc** is always $90^\circ$.
:::

::: sampunkt "Two special cases"
- **Tangent against radius**: the angle between a tangent and the radius to
  the point of contact is always $90^\circ$.
- **Two radii** always form an **isosceles** triangle, so the base angles
  are equal.
:::
