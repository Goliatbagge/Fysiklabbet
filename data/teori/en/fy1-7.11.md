# Electric field strength between two plates

If we place two plates parallel to each other and charge one plate positively
and the other plate negatively, a field arises that has the same strength everywhere
between the plates. A field that has the same strength everywhere is called a **uniform
field**.

The electric field is directed from the positive plate to
the negative one.

The strength of the field is determined by the [[voltage|Energy difference per charge between two points. SI unit volt (V).]] between the plates and the distance between
them.

::: formel "Electric field strength between two plates"
$$
\mathbb{E} = \frac{U}{d}
$$

where

- $\mathbb{E}$ = electric field strength (N/C or V/m)
- *U* = voltage between the plates (V)
- *d* = plate separation (m)

::: härledning "Derivation: Electric field strength between two plates"
We place a small positive charge *Q* in a uniform [[electric field|The region around a charge where another charge is affected by an electric force.]]
between two plates. The voltage between the plates is *U* and the distance between
them is *d*.

A downward electric [[force|An influence that can change an object's motion or shape. SI unit newton (N). Vector quantity.]] *F* will act on the charge. If we
move the charge from the negative plate to the positive plate,
energy *E* is required. We do [[work|Energy transferred when a force acts over a distance: *W* = *F* · *s* · cos *α*. SI unit joule (J).]] *W*, where we must push with a force
corresponding to the electric force *F* over the [[distance|The total length of path an object has moved. A scalar quantity, measured in metres.]] *d*.

According to the definition of voltage,

$$
U = \frac{E}{Q} \qquad (1)
$$

The work *W* done gives this increased electric [[potential energy|The energy an object has because of its position in a force field, for example *E*ₚ = *m* · *g* · *h* in a gravitational field.]] *E*. This
gives $E = W$.

We have previously defined work as $W = F \cdot s$, where the distance
*s* in our example corresponds to the plate separation *d*. Substituting into the formula above
gives

$$
E = F \cdot d
$$

According to the definition of electric field strength $\mathbb{E} = F/Q$,
the electric force can be written $F = \mathbb{E} \cdot Q$. Putting this into
the formula above gives

$$
E = \mathbb{E} \cdot Q \cdot d
$$

Substituting into (1) gives

$$
U = \frac{\mathbb{E} \cdot Q \cdot d}{Q} \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad U = \mathbb{E} \cdot d
$$

We solve for the electric field strength $\mathbb{E}$ and get

$$
\mathbb{E} = \frac{U}{d} \qquad \text{q.e.d.}
$$
:::

::: härledning "Unit analysis: Electric field strength"
We want to show that 1 V/m is the same as 1 N/C. Since voltage is defined
as $U = E/Q$,

$$
[1\ \mathrm{V}] = [1\ \mathrm{J/C}] \qquad (1)
$$

From the earlier formula for energy and work $\Delta E = F \cdot s$
we have

$$
[1\ \mathrm{J}] = [1\ \mathrm{N}] \cdot [1\ \mathrm{m}] = [1\ \mathrm{N \cdot m}]
$$

Substituting into (1) gives

$$
[1\ \mathrm{V}] = [1\ \mathrm{N \cdot m / C}]
$$

To get V/m on the left-hand side, we divide both sides by m.

$$
[1\ \mathrm{V/m}] = [1\ \mathrm{N \cdot m / (C \cdot m)}] = [1\ \mathrm{N/C}] \qquad \text{q.e.d.}
$$
:::
:::

From this formula we can see that another unit for electric
field strength is V/m.

::: textlosning
We set up the formula for the electric field strength between two plates and
solve for the voltage *U*.

$$
\mathbb{E} = \frac{U}{d} \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad U = \mathbb{E} \cdot d
$$

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
\mathbb{E} = 5.0 \cdot 10^4\ \mathrm{V/m} \\
d = 8.0\ \mathrm{cm} = 0.080\ \mathrm{m}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
U = 5.0 \cdot 10^4 \cdot 0.080 = 4\,000\ \mathrm{V} = 4.0\ \mathrm{kV}
$$

**Answer:** 4.0 kV or 4 000 V
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Uniform field"
- Two parallel plates, one positive and one negative, give a field that is
  **equally strong everywhere** between them.
- The direction is from the **positive** plate to the **negative** one,
  perpendicular to the plates.
:::

::: sampunkt "The formula"
- $\mathbb{E} = \dfrac{U}{d}$
- $U$ is the voltage between the plates and $d$ the plate separation in metres.
- Solve for: $U = \mathbb{E} \cdot d$ and
  $d = \dfrac{U}{\mathbb{E}}$.
:::

::: sampunkt "Where the formula comes from"
- The work to move the charge between the plates is
  $E = F \cdot d$ with $F = \mathbb{E} \cdot Q$.
- Substitute into $U = \dfrac{E}{Q}$; $Q$ cancels and what remains is
  $U = \mathbb{E} \cdot d$.
:::

::: sampunkt "Two units, the same thing"
- $1\ \mathrm{V/m} = 1\ \mathrm{N/C}$
- Follows from $1\ \mathrm{V} = 1\ \mathrm{J/C}$ and
  $1\ \mathrm{J} = 1\ \mathrm{N \cdot m}$.
:::

::: sampunkt "Values to keep in mind"
- Charge of the electron:
  $q_e \approx 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\ \mathrm{C}$.
- Mass of the electron:
  $m_e \approx 9.11 \cdot 10^{-31}\ \mathrm{kg}$.
- Field strength: definition $\dfrac{F}{Q}$, sphere
  $k \dfrac{Q}{r^{2}}$, plates $\dfrac{U}{d}$.
:::
