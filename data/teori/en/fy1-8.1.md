# Theory of relativity

**The speed of light in a vacuum is always the same** ($\approx 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\ \mathrm{m/s}$),
regardless of how the observer and the light source move relative to each other.

::: kuriosa "Michelson and Morley's experiment"
That the [[speed|Change of position per unit time, with direction (a vector). SI unit m/s.]] of light in a medium is [[constant|A value that does not change. For example the acceleration due to gravity *g* or the speed of light *c*.]] regardless of how the light source moves
was proved by Michelson and Morley in an experiment in 1887 that involved
the motion of the Earth and mirrors.

The Earth moves through space at a [[speed|The magnitude of velocity, without direction (a scalar). Measured in m/s or km/h.]] of $v \approx 30\ \mathrm{km/s}$. The speed of light in
a vacuum is $c \approx 300\,000\ \mathrm{km/s}$. If light had behaved according to classical
physics, light sent in the direction of the Earth's motion would have the speed
$c + v \approx 300\,030\ \mathrm{km/s}$. Light sent against the Earth's motion would have
the speed $c - v \approx 299\,970\ \mathrm{km/s}$. Neither of these alternatives matched in
the experiment. In both cases the total speed of the light was unchanged,
that is $c \approx 300\,000\ \mathrm{km/s}$! This went against the expected result
and has been called "the most famous failed experiment of all time".
:::

## Relativistic effects

Three effects that the constant speed of light brings about are that the closer
an object comes to the speed of light, the

- **slower** time passes for the object (**time dilation**): "the faster
  you move through space, the slower you move through time"
- **shorter** the object becomes in the direction of motion (**length contraction**)
- **greater** the object's mass becomes (**relativistic mass**).

::: formel "Time dilation"
$$
t = \frac{t_0}{\sqrt{1 - \dfrac{v^2}{c^2}}}
$$

where

- $t_0$ = the time of an event measured inside a moving frame, **proper time** (s)
- *t* = the time of the same event seen from outside the moving frame (s)
- *v* = the speed of the moving frame (m/s)
- *c* = the speed of light ($\approx 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\ \mathrm{m/s}$)

::: härledning "Derivation: Time dilation"
Imagine a lamp that sends out light (a [[photon|A quantum of electromagnetic radiation. Carries energy *E* = *h* · *f*, where *h* is Planck's constant.]]) from the ceiling of a
bus moving at constant speed *v*. Alice sees the event from inside
the bus. Bianca sees the event from outside the bus.

The [[distance|The total length of path an object has moved. A scalar quantity, measured in metres.]] *x* (Alice's view) is the speed *c* multiplied by the time that
Alice experiences, $t_0$, for the photon to leave the lamp and reach the floor,
that is $x = c \cdot t_0$.

The distance *y* (Bianca's view) is the speed *c* multiplied by the time that
Bianca experiences, *t*, for the photon to leave the lamp and reach the floor,
that is $y = c \cdot t$. Since the bus moved at the speed *v*
during the time *t*, the photon lands $v \cdot t$ further ahead than where the lamp was.

These distances give a right-angled triangle with the legs *x* and $v \cdot t$
and the hypotenuse *y*. Pythagoras' theorem gives

$$
y^2 = x^2 + (v t)^2
$$

$$
(c \cdot t)^2 = (c \cdot t_0)^2 + (v \cdot t)^2
$$

$$
c^2 \cdot t^2 - v^2 \cdot t^2 = c^2 \cdot t_0^2
$$

$$
t^2 (c^2 - v^2) = c^2 \cdot t_0^2
$$

$$
t^2 = \frac{c^2 \cdot t_0^2}{c^2 - v^2}
$$

Dividing the right-hand side through by $c^2$:

$$
t^2 = \frac{t_0^2}{1 - \dfrac{v^2}{c^2}}
\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad
t = \frac{t_0}{\sqrt{1 - \dfrac{v^2}{c^2}}} \qquad \text{q.e.d.}
$$
:::
:::

Time dilation is a real effect that must be taken into account, for
example when synchronising the clocks of GPS satellites with our clocks at
the Earth's surface.

::: formel "Length contraction"
$$
l = l_0 \cdot \sqrt{1 - \frac{v^2}{c^2}}
$$

where

- $l_0$ = the length in the direction of motion measured inside a moving frame (m)
- *l* = the length in the direction of motion seen from outside the moving frame (m)
- *v* = the speed of the moving frame (m/s)
- *c* = the speed of light in a vacuum (2.998 · 10⁸ m/s)
:::

::: formel "Relativistic mass"
$$
m = \frac{m_0}{\sqrt{1 - \dfrac{v^2}{c^2}}}
$$

where

- $m_0$ = the mass measured inside a moving frame (kg)
- *m* = the mass seen from outside the moving frame (kg)
- *v* = the speed of the moving frame (m/s)
- *c* = the speed of light (2.998 · 10⁸ m/s)
:::

That mass increases at high speeds has been proved with
particle accelerators.

Einstein also showed that "mass is energy" in one of the world's most famous
formulas.

::: formel "Energy of mass"
$$
E = m \cdot c^2
$$

where

- *E* = energy (J)
- *m* = mass (kg)
- *c* = the speed of light ($\approx 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\ \mathrm{m/s}$)
:::

Since the speed of light is a very large number, this means that something
with a small mass contains large amounts of energy.

::: exempel "Example 1: Spaceship with headlights"
**A spaceship moves at 70 % of the speed of light while it switches
on its headlights (which are on both the front and the back of the spaceship).
At what speed will the light hit you if the spaceship is moving**

**a) towards you?**
**b) away from you?**

::: handskrift
typ: rymdskepp
:::

::: textlosning
**a)** The speed of light is constant. The light will therefore hit you
at the speed of light *c*.

**Answer:** *c*

**b)** Again, the motion of the spaceship does not matter. The light will
hit you at the speed of light *c*.

**Answer:** *c*
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 2: Bus at 87 % of the speed of light"
**A bus moves at 87 % of the speed of light. What is the relationship between
the time for a passenger inside the bus and the time for an observer outside
the bus?**

::: handskrift
typ: tidsdilatation
:::

::: textlosning
We set up the formula for time dilation

$$
t = \frac{t_0}{\sqrt{1 - \dfrac{v^2}{c^2}}}
$$

Remember: $t_0$ = "the passenger's clock" and $t$ = "the observer's clock".

Substituting $v = 0.87 c$ gives

$$
t = \frac{t_0}{\sqrt{1 - \dfrac{(0.87 c)^2}{c^2}}}
= \frac{t_0}{\sqrt{1 - 0.87^2}}
= \frac{t_0}{\sqrt{0.2431}}
= \frac{t_0}{0.493\ldots}
= 2.028\ldots \cdot t_0
$$

That is, $t \approx 2 \cdot t_0$.

This means that time ticks twice as fast for the observer compared with
the passenger. The observer will age two years during one year for
the passenger!

**Answer:** $t \approx 2 \cdot t_0$
:::
:::

::: kuriosa "Time in gravitational fields"
Your feet age a tiny bit more slowly than your head. This is because your head,
when you stand or sit, is 1–1.5 metres higher up in the Earth's
gravitational field and therefore experiences a gravity that is slightly weaker.

As Einstein showed theoretically in his general theory of relativity, and as
we have since been able to establish experimentally by studying the atomic clocks of GPS satellites,
time flows more slowly in a strong gravitational field than in
a weak one.

In practice the effect is small down here at the Earth's surface, since your head
ages about 90 billionths of a second more than your feet during a 79-year
lifetime.
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "The basic postulate"
- **The speed of light in a vacuum is always the same**,
  $c \approx 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\ \mathrm{m/s}$.
- It does not depend on how the light source or the observer moves.
- Light from a spaceship moving away from you hits you at exactly the same
  speed as light from one coming towards you.
:::

::: sampunkt "Michelson and Morley, 1887"
- The Earth moves at $30\ \mathrm{km/s}$, so classical physics predicted
  $c + v$ in one direction and $c - v$ in the other.
- The measurement gave the **same** speed in both directions.
- Called "the most famous failed experiment of all time".
:::

::: sampunkt "Three relativistic effects"
- **Time dilation**: time passes more slowly. The faster you move through
  space, the slower you move through time.
- **Length contraction**: the object becomes shorter in the direction of motion.
- **Relativistic mass**: the mass becomes greater.
- All three grow as the speed approaches $c$.
:::

::: sampunkt "The formulas"
- $t = \dfrac{t_0}{\sqrt{1 - \dfrac{v^{2}}{c^{2}}}}$
- $l = l_0 \cdot \sqrt{1 - \dfrac{v^{2}}{c^{2}}}$
- $m = \dfrac{m_0}{\sqrt{1 - \dfrac{v^{2}}{c^{2}}}}$
- Index 0 means **inside** the moving frame. $t_0$ is called **proper time**.
:::

::: sampunkt "Mass is energy"
- $E = m \cdot c^{2}$
- Since $c$ is so large, even a very small mass holds enormous
  amounts of energy.
:::

::: sampunkt "The effects are real"
- The clocks of **GPS satellites** must be corrected for time dilation.
- The increase in mass has been measured in **particle accelerators**.
- Time also passes more slowly in a **strong gravitational field**: your
  feet age marginally more slowly than your head.
:::
:::
