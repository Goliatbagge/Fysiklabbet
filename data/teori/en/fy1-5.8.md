# Pascal's principle

An external [[force|An influence that can change the motion or shape of an object. SI unit newton (N). A vector quantity.]] on a liquid causes a [[pressure|Force per unit area: *p* = *F* / *A*. SI unit pascal (Pa) = N/m².]] increase that is **transmitted throughout the
liquid** (because it is hard to compress a liquid). This
makes it possible to amplify forces and is used in **hydraulic jacks**.

A pressure increase $p_1$ at one end of a hydraulic jack is transmitted throughout the
liquid, so that the pressure increase $p_2$ at the other end is equally large.

$$
p_1 = p_2 \qquad\text{so}\qquad \frac{F_1}{A_1} = \frac{F_2}{A_2}
$$

::: formel "Pascal's principle"
$$
\frac{F_1}{A_1} = \frac{F_2}{A_2}
$$

where

- $F_1$, $F_2$ = the forces on each piston (N)
- $A_1$, $A_2$ = the areas of each piston (same unit)
:::

::: exempel "Example 1: Hydraulic jack"
**You push with a force of 0.10 kN on a 0.50 dm² plate in a hydraulic
jack. How large a weight can the jack lift at the other end if
the plate there is 100 dm²?**

::: handskrift
typ: domkraft
:::

::: textlosning
Pascal's principle says

$$
\frac{F_1}{A_1} = \frac{F_2}{A_2}
$$

We solve for $F_2$.

$$
F_2 = \frac{F_1 \cdot A_2}{A_1}
$$

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
F_1 = 0.10\ \mathrm{kN} = 100\ \mathrm{N} \\
A_1 = 0.50\ \mathrm{dm^2} \\
A_2 = 100\ \mathrm{dm^2}
\end{array} \right]
$$

In this case we do not need to give the areas in [[SI units|The International System of Units, with seven base units (m, kg, s, A, K, mol, cd).]]; it is enough that
they have **the same unit** for the answer to come out in newtons.

$$
F_2 = \frac{100 \cdot 100}{0.50} = 20\,000\ \mathrm{N} = 20\ \mathrm{kN}
$$

**Answer:** 20 kN. (You push with a force corresponding to the weight of 10 kg and
can lift with a force corresponding to the weight of 2 tonnes!)
:::
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Pascal's principle"
- An external force on a liquid gives a pressure increase that is **transmitted throughout the
  liquid**, equally large everywhere.
- The cause: a liquid can hardly be compressed.
:::

::: sampunkt "The formula"
- $\dfrac{F_1}{A_1} = \dfrac{F_2}{A_2}$
- Comes from the pressure being equally large at both ends: $p_1 = p_2$.
:::

::: sampunkt "Force amplification"
- A small force on a **small** piston gives a large force on a **large**
  piston.
- The amplification is the ratio of the areas:
  $F_2 = \dfrac{F_1 \cdot A_2}{A_1}$.
- Used in **hydraulic jacks** and brakes.
:::

::: sampunkt "The units"
- The areas do not need to be in SI units, only in **the same** unit.
- Then the answer is automatically in the same force unit as the force inserted.
:::

::: sampunkt "Order of magnitude"
- $100\ \mathrm{N}$ on $0.50\ \mathrm{dm^2}$ against
  $100\ \mathrm{dm^2}$ gives $20\ \mathrm{kN}$.
- You push with the weight of ten kilograms and lift two tonnes.
:::
:::
