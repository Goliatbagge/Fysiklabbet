# Acceleration

A change of [[velocity|Change of position per unit time, with direction (a vector). SI unit m/s.]] is called **[[acceleration|How quickly the velocity changes. SI unit m/s². A vector quantity.]]**, is written *a*, has the [[SI unit|The International System of Units, with seven base units (m, kg, s, A, K, mol, cd).]]
m/s² and is a [[vector|A quantity with both magnitude and direction, for example velocity, force or acceleration. Differs from a scalar, which has only magnitude.]]. Something with an acceleration of 1 m/s² increases its velocity by
1 m/s every second.

::: formel "The sign of the acceleration"
If the acceleration is

- **positive**, the velocity increases.
- **negative**, the velocity decreases.
- **zero**, the velocity is [[constant|A value that does not change. For example the acceleration due to gravity *g* or the speed of light *c*.]].
:::

So braking is also an acceleration (!), even though in everyday language the word
is used almost exclusively for an increase in speed. A negative acceleration,
or braking, is also called **[[deceleration|Negative acceleration: when the velocity of an object decreases (braking).]]**.

The mean acceleration over a certain time interval is called the
**[[average acceleration|The mean acceleration over a time interval: the change in velocity Δ*v* divided by the time interval Δ*t*.]]**.

::: formel "Average acceleration"
$$
a_m = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t} = \frac{v_2 - v_1}{t_2 - t_1}
$$

where

- *a*ₘ = acceleration (m/s²)
- Δ*v* = change in velocity (m/s)
- Δ*t* = time interval (s)
:::

Constant acceleration is calculated in the same way as average acceleration.

::: formel "Velocity at constant acceleration"
$$
v = v_0 + a \cdot t
$$

where

- *v* = velocity (m/s)
- *v*₀ = [[initial velocity|The velocity at the time *t* = 0 (when the motion starts). Written *v*₀.]] / starting velocity (m/s)
- *a* = acceleration (m/s²)
- *t* = time (s)
:::

::: härledning "Note: Not v = s/t for accelerated motion"
When calculating [[speed|The magnitude of velocity, without direction (a scalar). Measured in m/s or km/h.]]/velocity for accelerated motion, the formula
$v = \dfrac{s}{t}$ can **not** be used!
:::

## Acceleration due to gravity and free fall

When an object falls without [[air resistance|The force from the air that slows down objects moving through the air. Depends on the shape, size and speed of the object.]], that is when it is affected only by the
[[gravitational force|The force with which the Earth pulls all objects towards its centre. The gravitational force gives an object its weight and is directed straight down towards the centre of the Earth.]] (or [[gravitation|The force that acts between all masses. At the Earth's surface it is called the gravitational force (weight).]]), it is in **[[free fall|When an object falls completely without air resistance, so that only the gravitational force acts on it. All objects then fall equally fast, regardless of mass.]]** with
a certain acceleration. This acceleration is called the **[[acceleration due to gravity|The acceleration that a freely falling object gets from the gravitational force, about 9.82 m/s² at the Earth's surface. Written *g*.]]** and
is written *g*.

The acceleration due to gravity at the Earth's surface is more or less constant: about 9.8 m/s².
The acceleration due to gravity is larger the closer you are to the centre of the Earth.
Since the Earth is slightly flattened at the poles, we are a little closer to the Earth's
centre if we are at the North or South Pole than if we are at the
equator.

| Place | Acceleration due to gravity (m/s²) |
| --- | --- |
| At the poles | 9.83 |
| At the equator | 9.78 |
| In Sweden | 9.82 |

::: demo "The flattening of the Earth"
We spin a "metal globe" and see that it becomes flattened at the poles. It is
the same kind of [[forces|An influence that can change the motion or shape of an object. SI unit newton (N). A vector quantity.]] that make the Earth flattened as it rotates. We
will talk more about forces in circular motion in Physics level 2.
:::

::: formel "The acceleration due to gravity in tasks"
When we do physics tasks we assume that we are in Sweden,
unless stated otherwise, and use the acceleration due to gravity *g* = 9.82 m/s².
:::

The acceleration due to gravity is always directed towards the centre of the Earth. In free fall
all objects fall equally fast, regardless of shape or mass. Here on Earth there is
air resistance, which prevents free fall (a feather does not fall to the
ground as fast as a hammer). But for small objects and/or short times the formulas
for free fall can be used on Earth too.

::: demo "Free fall in a vacuum tube"
We place a feather and a metal washer in a glass tube. When we turn the
glass tube over, the metal washer falls faster than the feather. We pump the air out of the tube
and create a vacuum. When we now turn the glass tube over, the feather and the metal washer fall
equally fast. So without air resistance all objects fall equally fast!
:::

The same experiment has been done at full scale, on the Moon, where there is no
air at all. At the end of the Apollo 15 mission in 1971 the commander
David Scott stood in front of the TV camera with a geologist's hammer in one hand and a
falcon feather in the other, and let go of them at the same time. With no air to slow down
the feather, both landed in the lunar dust at the same instant:

::: exempel "Example 1: A stone in free fall"
**A stone is released from rest and falls freely for 2.0 seconds. What velocity
does it have then?**

::: handskrift
typ: frittfall
:::

::: textlosning
We write down the formula for constant acceleration and solve for
the change in velocity Δ*v*.

$$
a = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t} \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad \Delta v = a \cdot \Delta t
$$

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
a = g \approx -9.82\ \mathrm{m/s^2} \\
\Delta t = 2.0\ \mathrm{s}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
\Delta v = (-9.82) \cdot 2.0 = -19.64\ \mathrm{m/s}
$$

Since the stone is released from rest (*v* = 0) and its velocity decreases by
19.64 m/s, the final velocity is

$$
0 + (-19.64)\ \mathrm{m/s} = -19.64\ \mathrm{m/s} \approx -20\ \mathrm{m/s}
$$

**Answer:** −20 m/s, or alternatively 20 m/s downwards.
:::
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Acceleration"
- **Acceleration** ($a$) is how quickly the velocity changes. The SI unit is
  m/s² and the quantity is a **vector**.
- $a = 1\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$ means that the velocity increases by $1\ \mathrm{m/s}$ every
  second.
- Positive acceleration: the velocity increases. Negative: the velocity decreases.
  Zero: the velocity is constant.
- Braking is also an acceleration. It is called **deceleration**.
:::

::: sampunkt "Formulas"
- Average acceleration:
  $a_m = \dfrac{\Delta v}{\Delta t} = \dfrac{v_2 - v_1}{t_2 - t_1}$.
- Constant acceleration is calculated in the same way.
- Velocity at constant acceleration: $v = v_0 + a \cdot t$, where $v_0$ is
  the initial velocity.
:::

::: sampunkt "Warning"
- $v = \dfrac{s}{t}$ must **not** be used for accelerated motion. It
  only applies to constant velocity.
:::

::: sampunkt "Acceleration due to gravity"
- **The acceleration due to gravity** $g$ is the acceleration of a freely falling
  object, directed towards the centre of the Earth.
- In tasks we use $g = 9.82\ \mathrm{N/kg}$ unless stated
  otherwise, that is the value for Sweden.
- $g$ is larger closer to the centre of the Earth: $9.83\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$ at the poles and
  $9.78\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$ at the equator, since the Earth is flattened at the poles.
:::

::: sampunkt "Free fall"
- **Free fall** occurs when only the gravitational force acts, that is without
  air resistance.
- Then **all objects fall equally fast**, regardless of mass and shape.
- The vacuum tube and Apollo 15's hammer and feather on the Moon show this.
- On Earth air resistance slows things down, but for small objects and short times
  the formulas still work.
:::
:::
