# Proportionality

A relationship where the ratio between the variables is constant is called
**proportionality**. A proportionality is a special case of a linear
relationship that can be described by the formula $y = kx$ and where the graph is always *a
straight line that passes through the origin*. The ratio $k$ between $y$ and $x$ is called
the **constant of proportionality**.

Proportionalities arise, for example, in linear models where the starting value is 0.
Example: When you buy pick-and-mix sweets you pay a certain price per kilo. The price
depends on the weight. If you buy 0 kg of sweets you pay 0 kr (the starting value is 0).
We say that "the price is proportional to the weight".
The constant of proportionality $k$ corresponds to the price per kilo.

::: formel "Proportionality as a formula"
All proportionalities can be written

$$
y = kx
$$

where $k$ is called the constant of proportionality.
:::

::: formel "Proportionality as a graph"
The graph of a proportionality is always a straight line that passes through the origin.
:::

From the formula it follows that the constant of proportionality can be calculated as

$$
k = \frac{y}{x}
$$

Investigate for yourself: drag the slider for $k$ and see how the graph of the proportionality
changes. Notice that the line always passes through the origin, whatever the value
of $k$.

::: graf
titel: y = kx
uttryck: k*x
ekvation: y = {k}x
lutningstriangel: ja
k: 2, -4, 4, 0.5
x: -6, 6
y: -6, 6
:::

::: textlosning
**a)** Since the price per litre 20.00 kr is to be multiplied by each litre $x$
he fills up to get the cost, the term $20.00x$ must be there. We have
no starting value/no starting fee or similar, so there must be no constant term
in the formula for the cost.

**Answer:** $y = 20.00x$ and the constant of proportionality is 20.00.

for the number of litres of petrol $x$ and one for the corresponding cost $y$ and fill

::: textlosning
For the graph to correspond to a proportionality it must be a *straight
line* AND *pass through the origin*. Graph B is a straight line, but does not pass through
the origin and is therefore not a proportionality. Graph C does pass through
the origin, but is not a straight line and is therefore not a proportionality.
Only graph A meets the criteria for a proportionality.

**Answer:** A
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Proportionality"
- A relationship where the **ratio** between the variables is constant.
- A special case of a linear relationship, where the starting value is **0**.
- $y = kx$
:::

::: sampunkt "The constant of proportionality"
- $k = \dfrac{y}{x}$
- For pick-and-mix sweets, $k$ is the **price per kilo**.
:::

::: sampunkt "The graph"
- **A straight line that always passes through the origin.**
- If $m \neq 0$ the relationship is linear but **not** proportional.
- A larger $k$ gives a steeper line. A negative $k$ gives a line that slopes
  downwards.

- If you buy 0 of something and pay 0 kr: proportional.
- If there is a fixed starting fee: linear, but not proportional.
