# Electric fields and electric field strength

## Electric fields

A property of space that causes charges to be affected by an electric [[force|An influence that can change an object's motion or shape. SI unit newton (N). Vector quantity.]]
is called an **[[electric field|The region around a charge where another charge is affected by an electric force.]]**. The field is drawn with field lines and points in the same
direction as the force on a small positive charge. ("Small", so that it
does not affect the field. "Positive charge", because the original
experiments were carried out with positive hydrogen nuclei, and the field was then defined in
the same direction as the force on them.)

This means that field lines are always drawn **"out of plus"** and **"into
minus"**.

::: formel "Motion of charges"
| Charges that are … | … move … |
| --- | --- |
| positive | **along** the direction of the field |
| negative | **against** the direction of the field |
:::

The closer together the field lines are, the stronger the field. Field lines
always point perpendicularly into or out of the surface of the charge.

## Electric field strength

How strong an electric field is, is called the **electric field strength**. Electric
field strength is denoted $\mathbb{E}$ and has the unit N/C (newtons per coulomb).
**NOTE!** Distinguish between $\mathbb{E}$ (field strength) and *E* (energy). Electric
field strength is defined as the force acting per unit charge.

::: formel "Electric field strength (definition)"
$$
\mathbb{E} = \frac{F}{Q}
$$

where

- $\mathbb{E}$ = electric field strength (N/C)
- *F* = electric force on a charge in an electric field (N)
- *Q* = magnitude of the charge (C)
:::

::: demo "Field strength from a sphere"
We charge the metal sphere of the Van de Graaff generator. When we get close enough
with the small metal sphere, a discharge occurs. The electric field is
stronger the closer we are to the charged sphere!
:::

The electric field gets weaker the further away from a charged sphere you
are.

::: formel "Electric field strength from a charged sphere"
$$
\mathbb{E} = k \cdot \frac{Q}{r^2}
$$

where

- $\mathbb{E}$ = electric field strength (N/C or V/m)
- *k* = 8.99 · 10⁹ N·m²/C² (Coulomb's [[constant|A value that does not change. For example the acceleration due to gravity *g* or the speed of light *c*.]])
- *Q* = charge of the sphere (C), given without sign
- *r* = distance to the centre of the sphere (m)

::: härledning "Derivation: Electric field strength from a charged sphere"
Imagine a small charge *q* located in an electric field
from a larger charge *Q*. Because the charge is small, its effect
on the field is negligible.

The electric field strength at the particle's position is, by
definition,

$$
\mathbb{E} = \frac{F}{q} \qquad (1)
$$

The force *F* between these charges can be expressed with [[Coulomb's law|The force between two point charges: *F* = *k* · *Q*₁ · *Q*₂ / *r*². Decreases with the square of the distance.]]
$F = k \cdot Q_1 Q_2 / r^2$, so

$$
F = k \cdot \frac{q \cdot Q}{r^2}
$$

Substituting this into (1) gives

$$
\mathbb{E} = \frac{k \cdot \frac{q \cdot Q}{r^2}}{q}
= \frac{k \cdot q \cdot Q}{q \cdot r^2}
= \frac{k \cdot Q}{r^2}
= k \cdot \frac{Q}{r^2} \qquad \text{q.e.d.}
$$
:::
:::

::: textlosning
We set up the formula for the electric field strength from a charged sphere.

$$
\mathbb{E} = k \cdot \frac{Q}{r^2}
$$

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
k \approx 8.99 \cdot 10^9\ \mathrm{N \cdot m^2 / C^2} \\
Q = 20\ \mathrm{nC} = 20 \cdot 10^{-9}\ \mathrm{C} \\
r = 12\ \mathrm{cm} = 0.12\ \mathrm{m}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
\mathbb{E} = 8.99 \cdot 10^9 \cdot \frac{20 \cdot 10^{-9}}{0.12^2}
= 12\,486.111\ldots\ \mathrm{N/C}
\approx 12\,000\ \mathrm{N/C} = 12\ \mathrm{kN/C}
$$

**Answer:** 12 kN/C or 12 000 N/C
:::
:::

::: textlosning
There is a field from the charge on the left and a field from the
charge on the right. The resultant field is obtained by adding
these fields.

The field from the charge on the left is

$$
\mathbb{E}_1 = k \cdot \frac{Q_1}{r_1^2}
$$

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
k \approx 8.99 \cdot 10^9\ \mathrm{N \cdot m^2 / C^2} \\
Q_1 = 8.0\ \mathrm{nC} = 8.0 \cdot 10^{-9}\ \mathrm{C} \\
r_1 = 2.0\ \mathrm{cm} = 0.020\ \mathrm{m}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
\mathbb{E}_1 = 8.99 \cdot 10^9 \cdot \frac{8.0 \cdot 10^{-9}}{0.020^2}
= 179\,800\ \mathrm{N/C}\ (\text{to the left at P, since fields go INTO minus})
$$

In the same way we calculate the field from the charge on the right as

$$
\mathbb{E}_2 = k \cdot \frac{Q_2}{r_2^2}
$$

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
k \approx 8.99 \cdot 10^9\ \mathrm{N \cdot m^2 / C^2} \\
Q_2 = 6.0\ \mathrm{nC} = 6.0 \cdot 10^{-9}\ \mathrm{C} \\
r_2 = 5.0\ \mathrm{cm} = 0.050\ \mathrm{m}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
\mathbb{E}_2 = 8.99 \cdot 10^9 \cdot \frac{6.0 \cdot 10^{-9}}{0.050^2}
= 21\,576\ \mathrm{N/C}\ (\text{to the left at P, since fields go OUT OF plus})
$$

Both fields act in the same direction (to the left), so the total field
$\mathbb{E}$ is obtained by adding them.

$$
\mathbb{E} = \mathbb{E}_1 + \mathbb{E}_2 = 179\,800 + 21\,576
= 201\,376\ \mathrm{N/C}
\approx 200\,000\ \mathrm{N/C} = 0.20\ \mathrm{MN/C}\ \text{to the left}
$$

**Answer:** 0.20 MN/C or 2.0 · 10⁵ N/C to the left.
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Electric field"
- A property of space that causes charges to be affected by an electric
  force.
- Drawn with **field lines**, in the same direction as the force on a small
  **positive** charge.
- The lines go **out of plus** and **into minus**, always perpendicular to
  the surface of the charge.
- **Denser** lines mean a **stronger** field.
:::

::: sampunkt "Motion of the charges"
- **Positive** charges move **along** the field.
- **Negative** charges move **against** the field.
:::

::: sampunkt "Electric field strength"
- The force **per unit charge**:
  $\mathbb{E} = \dfrac{F}{Q}$.
- The SI unit is N/C, which can also be written V/m.
- Distinguish between $\mathbb{E}$ (field strength) and $E$ (energy).
:::

::: sampunkt "The field from a charged sphere"
- $\mathbb{E} = k \cdot \dfrac{Q}{r^{2}}$
- Derived from Coulomb's law: the small test charge $q$ cancels.
- Decreases with the **square** of the distance, just like gravity.
- Insert the charge without sign and determine the direction by
  reasoning.
:::

::: sampunkt "Several charges"
- The field strength is a **vector**. Calculate each contribution separately.
- Add with signs according to direction, and answer with both magnitude and
  direction.
:::
:::
