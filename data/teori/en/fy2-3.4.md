# Charged particles in magnetic fields

We saw earlier from Ørsted's experiment that **moving charges**
(current-carrying conductors) affect magnets, for example compass needles, with a
[[force|An influence that can change an object's motion or shape. SI unit newton (N). A vector quantity.]]. According to **[[Newton's third law|For every force (action) there is an equal and opposite force (reaction).]]** on action and reaction, the
reverse must also hold: a **[[magnetic field|A region around a magnet or current-carrying conductor where other magnetic objects are affected by a force.]] affects moving charges with
an equal but opposite force**.

Magnetic fields affect moving charges with a **magnetic force**.
The direction of the force is determined with the **right-hand rule**. If you hold up
your right hand and form 90° between the thumb, the index finger and the middle finger,
then:

- **the thumb** corresponds to the direction of the current *I*
- **the index finger** corresponds to the direction of the magnetic field *B*
- **the middle finger** corresponds to the direction of the force *F*

::: tips "Memory rule: three fingers"
- The **t**humb is the current (in Swedish *tummen* and *strömmen* almost rhyme)
- The "**b**ang finger" is **B** (the magnetic field)
- The rude **f**inger is **F** (the force)

If the moving charge is

- **negative**, the direction of the current is **opposite** to the direction of the charge
- **positive**, the direction of the current is **the same** as the direction of the charge.
:::

::: demo "Demonstration: Deflection of electrons in an oscilloscope"
We start an oscilloscope that fires electrons at a fluorescent
screen and investigate how the electron beam is **deflected** (bent) when we
bring a magnet close to it. Does the right-hand rule work?
:::

The larger the charge, the faster the charge moves and the stronger the
magnetic field perpendicular to the motion of the charge, the stronger the
force on each individual charge. Remember: the elementary charge of
electrons and protons is $q \approx 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}$ C.

::: formel "Magnetic force on a charged particle in a magnetic field"
$$
F = q \cdot v \cdot B
$$

where

- *F* = magnetic force on the charge (N)
- *q* = the size of the charge (C)
- *v* = the [[velocity|Change of position per unit time, with direction (a vector). SI unit m/s.]] of the charge (m/s)
- *B* = magnetic flux density perpendicular to the motion of the charge (T)
:::

The derivation of the formula for the magnetic force on a charged particle in a
magnetic field requires **the theory of relativity** and **Lorentz transformations**,
which is beyond the scope of this subject.

::: textlosning
We set up the formula for the magnetic force on a charge.

$$
F = q \cdot v \cdot B
$$

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
q \approx 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\ \mathrm{C} \\
v = 2.4\ \mathrm{Mm/s} = 2.4 \cdot 10^{6}\ \mathrm{m/s} \\
B = 0.080\ \mathrm{T}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
F = 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 2.4 \cdot 10^{6} \cdot 0.080 = 3.076 \cdot 10^{-14}\ \mathrm{N} \approx 3.1 \cdot 10^{-14}\ \mathrm{N} = 31\ \mathrm{fN}
$$

The direction is determined by the right-hand rule. The thumb is the direction of the current,
the index finger the direction of the magnetic field, the middle finger the direction of the force. Since
the electron (negative) is travelling **downwards**, the direction of the current is **opposite**:
**upwards**. The direction of the magnetic field is **into the plane**. The right-hand rule
then gives that the force is **directed to the left**.

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Magnetic fields affect moving charges"
- Ørsted showed that moving charges affect magnets.
- According to Newton's third law the reverse must hold: a **magnetic field
  affects moving charges** with an equal and opposite force.
- The charge must **be moving**. A stationary charge is not affected
  at all.
:::

::: sampunkt "The formula"
- $F = q \cdot v \cdot B$
- $q$ is the size of the charge, $v$ its speed and $B$ the magnetic
  flux density **perpendicular** to the motion.
- The elementary charge is
  $1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\ \mathrm{C}$.
:::

::: sampunkt "The right-hand rule with three fingers"
- Hold the thumb, index finger and middle finger at right angles to each other.
- **The thumb**: the direction of the current $I$.
- **The index finger**: the magnetic field $B$.
- **The middle finger**: the force $F$.
:::

::: sampunkt "The sign of the charge"
- **Positive** charge: the direction of the current is **the same** as the direction of motion.
- **Negative** charge, for example an electron: the direction of the current is
  **opposite** to the direction of motion.
- This is the most common mistake in the problems.
:::

::: sampunkt "The consequence"
- The force is perpendicular to both the velocity and the field.
- That is why **the path is bent** without the speed changing, just as in
  circular motion.
- This can be seen in the oscilloscope, where the electron beam is deflected by a magnet.
:::
:::
