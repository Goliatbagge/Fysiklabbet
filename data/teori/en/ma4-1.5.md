# Radians

To make differentiating trigonometric functions easier, we need a new
measure of angle. Instead of measuring angles in degrees we can measure them in
**radians**. The unit for radians is sometimes written "rad".

::: formel "Definition: radian"
An angle that subtends a circular arc whose length is equal to the radius of the
circle is **1 radian**. Put differently: the ratio between arc length
and radius gives a measure of angle in radians.

Since radians are the ratio between arc length and radius (both in, for example,
metres), the radian is really a dimensionless unit. In mathematics, angles are
sometimes given without a unit, and it is then understood that they are measured in radians.

A full turn corresponds to the whole circumference of the circle, which is $2\pi$ radii. So
$360^\circ = 2\pi$ rad. From this we can find the
relationship between radians and degrees.

::: formel "Relationship between degrees and radians"
$$
360^\circ = 2\pi \text{ rad} \qquad 180^\circ = \pi \text{ rad}
$$

If we divide the second relationship by 180 and by $\pi$ respectively, we get
the conversion factors in both directions:

$$
1^\circ = \frac{\pi}{180}\ \text{rad} \qquad 1\ \text{rad} = \frac{180^\circ}{\pi} \approx 57.3^\circ
$$

Learn $\pi$ rad $= 180^\circ$ and $2\pi$ rad $= 360^\circ$. These
come up often.
:::

On your calculator you switch between degrees and radians in the settings.
Find out how your particular calculator does it. In GeoGebra an angle given
as a plain number or with the unit $^\circ$ is interpreted as degrees, while an angle that
contains $\pi$ or the unit rad is interpreted as radians.

::: exempel "Example 1: Convert to radians"
**Convert to radians.<br>a)&nbsp;$30^\circ$&emsp;&emsp;b)&nbsp;$90^\circ$**

::: handskrift
typ: radianomv
:::

::: textlosning
**a)** We use the fact that $1^\circ = \dfrac{\pi}{180}$ rad. This gives

$$
30^\circ = 30 \cdot \frac{\pi}{180}\ \text{rad} = \frac{\pi}{6}\ \text{rad}
$$

**Answer:** $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ rad

**b)** In the same way we get

$$
90^\circ = 90 \cdot \frac{\pi}{180}\ \text{rad} = \frac{\pi}{2}\ \text{rad}
$$

Alternatively we can use the fact that $\pi$ rad $= 180^\circ$. Then $90^\circ$ must
be half of that, so $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ rad.

**Answer:** $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ rad
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 2: Convert to degrees"
**Convert to degrees.<br>a)&nbsp;$\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ rad&emsp;&emsp;b)&nbsp;$\dfrac{2\pi}{5}$ rad&emsp;&emsp;c)&nbsp;$0.72$ rad**

::: handskrift
typ: gradomv
:::

::: textlosning
**a)** Since $\pi$ rad $= 180^\circ$,

$$
\frac{\pi}{4}\ \text{rad} = \frac{180^\circ}{4} = 45^\circ
$$

**Answer:** $45^\circ$

**b)** Again we use the fact that $\pi$ rad $= 180^\circ$ and get

$$
\frac{2\pi}{5}\ \text{rad} = \frac{2 \cdot 180^\circ}{5} = \frac{360^\circ}{5} = 72^\circ
$$

**Answer:** $72^\circ$

**c)** Here there is no $\pi$, but since $\pi$ rad $= 180^\circ$ we have
$1\ \text{rad} = \dfrac{180^\circ}{\pi}$. This gives

$$
0.72\ \text{rad} = 0.72 \cdot \frac{180^\circ}{\pi} = 41.252\ldots^\circ \approx 41^\circ
$$

**Answer:** $41^\circ$
:::
:::

When we solve a trigonometric equation and want the answer in radians, we
first set the calculator to radians. The solutions are then given with the period
$2\pi$ (for sine and cosine) or $\pi$ (for tangent) instead of
$360^\circ$ and $180^\circ$ respectively.

::: exempel "Example 3: Solve an equation, answer in radians"
**Solve the equation $\sin x = 0.7$. Answer in radians to two decimal places.**

::: handskrift
typ: sinradianer
:::

::: textlosning
First make sure to change the setting on your calculator to radians. With
$\sin^{-1}(0.7)$ we get an angle in the first quadrant, and the second
solution is reflected in $\pi$ (the counterpart of $180^\circ$):

$$
x = 0.775\ldots + n \cdot 2\pi \approx 0.78 + n \cdot 2\pi
$$

$$
x = \pi - 0.775\ldots + n \cdot 2\pi = 2.366\ldots + n \cdot 2\pi \approx 2.37 + n \cdot 2\pi
$$

Remember: $2\pi = 360^\circ$ and $\pi = 180^\circ$.

**Answer:** $x \approx 0.78 + n \cdot 2\pi$ and $x \approx 2.37 + n \cdot 2\pi$
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 4: Exact solution in radians"
**Solve the equation $\tan x = -\sqrt{3}$. Give an exact answer in radians.**

::: handskrift
typ: exakttan
:::

::: textlosning
We look in the formula sheet for which angle gives $-\sqrt{3}$ for tangent. There
we see that the angle $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$ rad (corresponding to $120^\circ$) is a
solution. Since tangent has the period $\pi$, the solution repeats every half
turn:

$$
x = \frac{2\pi}{3} + n \cdot \pi
$$

**Answer:** $x = \dfrac{2\pi}{3} + n \cdot \pi$
:::
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Radian"
- **1 radian** is the angle that subtends a circular arc as long as the radius.
- Radians are the ratio between arc length and radius, so dimensionless.
- If an angle is given without a unit in mathematics, radians are meant.
:::

::: sampunkt "Relationships"
- $360^\circ = 2\pi$ rad and $180^\circ = \pi$ rad. Learn these by heart.
- $1^\circ = \dfrac{\pi}{180}$ rad.
- $1$ rad $= \dfrac{180^\circ}{\pi} \approx 57.3^\circ$.
:::

::: sampunkt "Converting"
- Degrees to radians: multiply by $\dfrac{\pi}{180}$. $30^\circ = \dfrac{\pi}{6}$ rad.
- Radians to degrees: replace $\pi$ with $180^\circ$. $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ rad $= 45^\circ$.
- If there is no $\pi$, multiply by $\dfrac{180^\circ}{\pi}$ instead: $0.72$ rad $\approx 41^\circ$.
:::

::: sampunkt "Equations in radians"
- First set the **calculator** to radians.
- The period is $2\pi$ for sine and cosine, and $\pi$ for tangent.
- The second sine solution is reflected in $\pi$ instead of in $180^\circ$.
:::
