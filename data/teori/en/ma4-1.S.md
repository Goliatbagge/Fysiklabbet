# Summary: Trigonometry

This chapter was about trigonometry: how sine, cosine and tangent are
defined both in a right-angled triangle and on the unit circle, how
trigonometric equations are solved completely (with a period term) and in given
intervals, and how the graphs of the sine and cosine functions are shaped by
amplitude, period and shift. After the chapter you should be able to work with
degrees and radians, use trigonometric relationships (the Pythagorean identity, the addition and
subtraction formulas, the double angle) to simplify expressions, prove
identities and solve equations, and interpret and set up sine models for
periodic processes.

## Concepts to know

- **hypotenuse**: the side opposite the right angle in a right-angled
  triangle; always the longest side of the triangle.
- **leg**: the two shorter sides in a right-angled triangle; the **opposite
  leg** is opposite the angle *v*, the **adjacent leg** forms
  the angle *v* together with the hypotenuse.
- **inverse trigonometric functions ($\tan^{-1}$, $\sin^{-1}$,
  $\cos^{-1}$)**: give the angle when a ratio of sides is known; also called
  arctan, arcsin and arccos.
- **the unit circle**: a circle with radius 1 and its centre at the origin; an
  angle *v* measured anticlockwise from the positive *x*-axis gives the point
  $(\cos v,\ \sin v)$ on the edge.
- **radian**: a measure of angle where 1 radian subtends a circular arc as long
  as the radius; $\pi$ rad $= 180^\circ$.
- **complementary angles**: two angles whose sum is $90^\circ$; the sine of
  one is the cosine of the other.
- **the Pythagorean identity**: the relationship $\sin^2 v + \cos^2 v = 1$, which
  holds for all angles *v*.
- **addition and subtraction formulas**: expressions for $\sin(u \pm v)$ and
  $\cos(u \pm v)$ in terms of the sine and cosine of *u* and *v* separately.
- **the double angle**: formulas for $\sin 2v$ and $\cos 2v$, obtained by
  setting $u = v$ in the addition formulas.
- **amplitude**: the distance from the equilibrium position to the top (or
  bottom) of the curve; half the difference between the largest and smallest value.
- **period**: the smallest angle (or distance in the *x*-direction) after which the graph
  repeats itself; often called the **period** ($T$, periodic time) in applications.
- **shift**: when a graph is moved in the *x*-direction and/or *y*-direction without
  changing shape; the shift in the *x*-direction is often called the **phase shift**
  and the shift in the *y*-direction determines the **equilibrium position** in applications.
- **asymptote**: a line that a graph approaches but never reaches; the graph
  of $y = \tan x$ has vertical asymptotes where $\cos x = 0$.
- **harmonic oscillation**: periodic motion (for example sound or
  alternating current) that can be described by a sine function.

## Formulas

::: formel "The chapter's formulas"
**Trigonometric functions in a right-angled triangle**

$$
\tan v = \frac{a}{b}, \qquad \sin v = \frac{a}{c}, \qquad \cos v = \frac{b}{c}
$$

where *a* is the opposite leg, *b* is the adjacent leg and *c* is
the hypotenuse for the angle *v*. If the sides are known instead of the angle,
an inverse function is used, for example

$$
v = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{a}{b}\right) = \arctan\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)
$$

**Sine, cosine and tangent on the unit circle**

$$
\sin v = y, \qquad \cos v = x, \qquad \tan v = \frac{\sin v}{\cos v}\ (\cos v \neq 0)
$$

where $(x, y)$ is the point on the unit circle (radius 1, centre at the origin)
that a radius at the angle *v*, measured anticlockwise from the positive *x*-axis, meets.

**Exact values for the standard angles**

$$
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
v & 0^\circ & 30^\circ & 45^\circ & 60^\circ & 90^\circ \\
\hline
\sin v & 0 & \dfrac{1}{2} & \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} & \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} & 1 \\[2mm]
\cos v & 1 & \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} & \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} & \dfrac{1}{2} & 0 \\[2mm]
\tan v & 0 & \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3} & 1 & \sqrt{3} & \text{undef.}
\end{array}
$$

**All solutions to a trigonometric equation**

If *v* is a solution, then, where *n* is an arbitrary integer:

$$
\sin x = a: \quad x = v + n \cdot 360^\circ \quad \text{and} \quad x = 180^\circ - v + n \cdot 360^\circ
$$

$$
\cos x = a: \quad x = \pm v + n \cdot 360^\circ
$$

$$
\tan x = a: \quad x = v + n \cdot 180^\circ
$$

**Relationship between degrees and radians**

$$
360^\circ = 2\pi \text{ rad} \qquad 180^\circ = \pi \text{ rad}
$$

$$
1^\circ = \frac{\pi}{180}\ \text{rad} \qquad 1\ \text{rad} = \frac{180^\circ}{\pi} \approx 57.3^\circ
$$

**Complementary angles and other relationships on the unit circle**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
\sin(90^\circ - v) = \cos v & \qquad \cos(90^\circ - v) = \sin v \\[2pt]
\sin(-v) = -\sin v & \qquad \cos(-v) = \cos v \\[2pt]
\sin(180^\circ - v) = \sin v & \qquad \cos(180^\circ - v) = -\cos v \\[2pt]
\sin(v + 90^\circ) = \cos v & \qquad \cos(v + 90^\circ) = -\sin v \\[2pt]
\sin(v + 180^\circ) = -\sin v & \qquad \cos(v + 180^\circ) = -\cos v
\end{array}
$$

These relationships are not on the formula sheet. Try to understand them from
the symmetries of the unit circle instead of just memorising them.

**The Pythagorean identity**

$$
\sin^2 v + \cos^2 v = 1
$$

$$
\sin^2 v = 1 - \cos^2 v \qquad \cos^2 v = 1 - \sin^2 v
$$

**Addition and subtraction formulas**

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sin(u + v) &= \sin u\cos v + \cos u\sin v\\
\sin(u - v) &= \sin u\cos v - \cos u\sin v\\
\cos(u + v) &= \cos u\cos v - \sin u\sin v\\
\cos(u - v) &= \cos u\cos v + \sin u\sin v
\end{aligned}
$$

**Double angle formulas**

$$
\sin 2v = 2\sin v\cos v
$$

$$
\cos 2v = \cos^2 v - \sin^2 v = 2\cos^2 v - 1 = 1 - 2\sin^2 v
$$

**Amplitude and period of the sine and cosine functions**

$$
y = A \sin(Bx + C) + D
$$

- amplitude $|A|$
- period $\dfrac{360^\circ}{B}$ (or $\dfrac{2\pi}{B}$ rad), often called the
  *periodic time* $T$ in applications
- shift $-\dfrac{C}{B}$ in the *x*-direction, often called the *phase shift* in
  applications
- shift $D$ in the *y*-direction, which determines the *equilibrium position* in applications

The basic functions $y = \sin x$ and $y = \cos x$ have the period $360^\circ$
($2\pi$ rad) and the range $-1 \leq y \leq 1$.

**Period of the tangent function**

$$
y = \tan(Bx + C) + D
$$

has the period $\dfrac{180^\circ}{B}$ (or $\dfrac{\pi}{B}$ rad) and has no
amplitude. There are vertical asymptotes where $\cos(Bx + C) = 0$.

**Combining into a sine curve**

$$
y = a \sin x + b \cos x = c \sin(x + v)
$$

where

$$
c = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2} \qquad \text{and} \qquad \tan v = \frac{b}{a}
$$

The largest value is *c* and the smallest value is $-c$.
:::

## Important relationships and methods

- Choose the trigonometric function based on which sides you know and which you
  are looking for: sine (opposite/hypotenuse), cosine (adjacent/hypotenuse),
  tangent (opposite/adjacent). If you are instead looking for the angle, use the
  inverse function.
- Sine is the *y*-coordinate and cosine is the *x*-coordinate of the point where
  the angle's radius meets the unit circle. This holds for **all** angles,
  not just the acute ones in a right-angled triangle.
- An angle over $360^\circ$ (or under $0^\circ$) gives the same
  trigonometric values as the angle minus (or plus) a whole number of
  turns. Subtract or add $n \cdot 360^\circ$ before you interpret it on the
  unit circle.
- A trigonometric equation has infinitely many solutions. First give
  **all** solutions with the right period term, then test which integers *n*
  give solutions in the interval the task asks for.
- The zero product method also works for trigonometric equations: if a
  product of trigonometric expressions equals zero, set each factor
  equal to zero separately.
- Use $\dfrac{\sin x}{\cos x} = \tan x$ to eliminate a mixed
  term, and the Pythagorean identity to replace $\sin^2$ with $\cos^2$
  (or the other way round) so that the equation contains only one and the same function.
- If the equation contains both an angle and its double: rewrite using
  the double angle formulas so that the whole equation is in a single
  angle. Choose the version of $\cos 2v$ that matches the other terms.
- If a substitution (for example $\sin x = t$) gives a quadratic equation: solve it
  with the *pq* formula and check that the solutions lie in the interval
  $-1 \leq t \leq 1$ before you substitute back to *x*.
- The Pythagorean identity determines the magnitude of $\sin v$ or $\cos v$ when
  the other is given, but the **quadrant** decides which sign (plus or
  minus) is the correct answer.
- The coefficient *in front of* the trigonometric function controls the amplitude;
  the coefficient *inside* the argument (in front of *x*) controls the period. Never
  mix them up.
- A minus sign inside the argument ($x - 90^\circ$) pushes the curve to the
  **right**, a plus sign to the **left**. Easy to mix up.
- A sum $a \sin x + b \cos x$ can always be rewritten as a single
  sine curve $c \sin(x + v)$, which makes it easy to read off the
  largest and smallest value directly as $\pm c$.

## Before the test

- Can you define sine, cosine and tangent both in a right-angled triangle
  and on the unit circle?
- Can you use inverse trigonometric functions to find an
  angle from a known ratio of sides?
- Do you know the exact values for $0^\circ$, $30^\circ$, $45^\circ$, $60^\circ$
  and $90^\circ$ (or the memory trick for working them out)?
- Do you know how to convert between degrees and radians, and that $\pi$ rad
  $= 180^\circ$?
- Can you give all solutions to a trigonometric equation with the right
  period term, for sine, cosine and tangent?
- Can you pick out the solutions that lie in a given interval by
  testing different integers *n*?
- Do you know the complementary angle relationship and the other relationships between angles on
  the unit circle (negative angle, half turn, quarter turn)?
- Do you know the Pythagorean identity, and can you use it to find $\sin v$
  or $\cos v$ when the other is given, with the right sign based on the
  quadrant?
- Do you know the addition and subtraction formulas and the double angle
  formulas, and can you use them to rewrite equations and prove
  identities?
- Can you read off the amplitude and period from a function of the form
  $y = A \sin(Bx + C) + D$, tell apart what *A*, *B*, *C* and *D* do to
  the graph, and explain why the graph of $y = \tan x$ instead has
  vertical asymptotes?
- Can you rewrite $y = a \sin x + b \cos x$ as a single sine curve
  $c \sin(x + v)$ and use it to find the largest and smallest value?
- Can you set up a sine model (amplitude, period, phase shift,
  equilibrium position) for a periodic application from a word problem?
