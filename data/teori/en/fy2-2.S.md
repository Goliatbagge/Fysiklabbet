# Summary: Mechanical waves

The chapter was about oscillations and waves: from a mass on a spring via
harmonic oscillation and pendulums to how pulses become waves that are reflected,
transmitted and superposed, and form standing waves in strings and pipes.
Then we moved on to sound (intensity, sound level, the Doppler effect) and
water waves (refraction, diffraction, interference). After the chapter you should
be able to calculate spring oscillations, periods, the propagation speed of waves,
standing waves, sound level in decibels, and refraction and interference patterns.

## Concepts to know

- **Spring constant *k***: a measure of how "stiff" a spring is; appears in Hooke's
  law $F = k \cdot \Delta l$.
- **Displacement *y***: the position of an oscillating object measured from
  the equilibrium position.
- **Amplitude *A***: the maximum displacement from the equilibrium position (in a
  spring oscillation, pendulum or wave).
- **Harmonic oscillation**: an undamped oscillation in which the restoring
  force is proportional to the displacement, $F_R = -k \cdot y$.
- **Period *T* and (natural) frequency *f***: the time for one complete oscillation
  and the number of oscillations per second respectively, $f = 1/T$.
- **Resonance**: a large increase in the amplitude of the oscillation when energy
  is supplied at the same frequency as the natural frequency of the system.
- **Transverse and longitudinal wave**: the displacement is perpendicular to
  or along the direction of propagation of the wave respectively.
- **Reflection, transmission and superposition**: a wave that bounces
  back, a wave passed on to a new medium, and two waves whose
  displacements add when they meet, respectively.
- **Standing wave, node and antinode**: points with amplitude 0 (nodes) and
  points midway between them with maximum amplitude (antinodes); the energy
  is not transported through the medium but is concentrated at the antinodes.
- **Sound intensity *I* and sound level *L***: energy transport per
  cross-sectional area (W/m²) and the same quantity converted to a
  logarithmic scale in decibels (dB) respectively.
- **The Doppler effect**: the apparent change in frequency that occurs when
  the source and the receiver move relative to each other.
- **Diffraction and interference**: a wave that bends at the edge of an
  opening, and two waves that meet and form antinodal lines
  (constructive interference) and nodal lines (destructive interference), respectively.

## Formulas

::: formel "Spring oscillation"
**Hooke's law**

$$
F = k \cdot \Delta l
$$

where

- *F* = force loading/exerted by the spring (N)
- *k* = spring constant (N/m)
- Δ*l* = extension of the spring (m)

**Resultant force on a mass on a spring**

$$
F_R = -k \cdot y
$$

**Oscillation energy in a spring**

$$
E = \frac{k \cdot A^{2}}{2}
$$

where

- *E* = oscillation energy (J)
- *A* = amplitude of the oscillation (m)

**Harmonic oscillation**

$$
y = A \cdot \sin(\omega \cdot t) \qquad v = \omega \cdot A \cdot \cos(\omega \cdot t) \qquad a = -\omega^{2} \cdot A \cdot \sin(\omega \cdot t)
$$

$$
\omega = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}} \qquad\qquad T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{m}{k}}
$$

where

- *ω* = angular velocity (rad/s)
- *t* = time after passing the equilibrium position upwards (s)
- *m* = mass (kg)
- *T* = period (s)
:::

::: formel "Simple pendulum and natural frequency"
**Period of a simple pendulum at small displacements**

$$
T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{l}{g}}
$$

where

- *l* = length of the pendulum (m)
- *g* = acceleration due to gravity (m/s²)

**Natural frequency**

$$
f = \frac{1}{T}
$$
:::

::: formel "Waves: propagation speed and standing waves"
**Propagation speed of a wave**

$$
v = \frac{\lambda}{T} = f \cdot \lambda
$$

where

- *v* = propagation speed of the wave (m/s)
- *λ* = wavelength (m)

**Standing wave in a string or pipe (node to node / antinode to antinode)**

$$
l = \frac{n \cdot \lambda}{2}
$$

**Standing wave in a half-open pipe (node to antinode)**

$$
l = \frac{(2n - 1) \cdot \lambda}{4}
$$

where

- *l* = length of the string/pipe (m)
- *n* = order number of the tone (*n* = 1 the fundamental, *n* = 2 the first
  overtone, …)
:::

::: formel "Sound intensity and sound level"
$$
I = \frac{P}{A} \qquad\qquad I = \frac{P}{4\pi \cdot r^{2}}
$$

where

- *I* = sound intensity (W/m²)
- *P* = power (W)
- *A* = area (m²), *r* = distance to a point source (m)

$$
L = 10 \cdot \lg\!\left(\frac{I}{I_0}\right)
$$

where

- *L* = sound level (dB)
- $I_0 = 10^{-12}$ W/m² (the threshold of hearing)
:::

::: formel "Refraction and interference of water waves"
**The law of refraction**

$$
\frac{\sin i}{\sin b} = \frac{v_1}{v_2} = \frac{\lambda_1}{\lambda_2}
$$

where

- *i*, *b* = angle of incidence and angle of refraction respectively
- $v_1, v_2$ = speed in medium 1 and medium 2 respectively

**Interference**

$$
\Delta s = n \cdot \lambda \qquad (\text{antinodal lines, constructive})
$$

$$
\Delta s = \left(n + \tfrac{1}{2}\right) \cdot \lambda \qquad (\text{nodal lines, destructive})
$$

where

- Δ*s* = path difference to the two wave sources (m)
- *n* = order number of the line (0, 1, 2, …)
:::

## Important relationships and methods

- At the equilibrium position **the speed is at its maximum** and **the acceleration is 0**; at
  the turning points **the speed is 0** and **the acceleration is at its maximum**. The resultant
  always points towards the equilibrium position.
- The period of a spring oscillation ($T = 2\pi\sqrt{m/k}$) and of a
  pendulum ($T = 2\pi\sqrt{l/g}$) does **not** depend on the amplitude, only on
  the mass/spring constant and the pendulum length respectively.
- The pendulum formula only applies at **small angles of displacement** (otherwise the
  motion stops being harmonic) and when the mass of the string is negligible.
- A transverse wave that is reflected at a **fixed end** is inverted
  (crest → trough); at a **free end** it keeps its shape.
- In transmission between different media: from a denser to a less dense medium
  the pulse is transmitted **lengthened**, from a less dense to a denser medium
  **shortened**. The reflection is upright and inverted respectively.
- In a standing wave the distance between two adjacent nodes (or two
  adjacent antinodes) is always **half a wavelength**.
- The speed of sound depends on the temperature and the material:
  $v_\text{solid} > v_\text{liquid} > v_\text{gas}$; in air you use
  about 340 m/s unless otherwise stated.
- The decibel scale is **logarithmic**: an increase of about 3 dB corresponds to a
  doubling of the sound intensity, not a linear increase.
- When water waves are refracted, **the frequency is always constant**: it is
  the wavelength and the speed that change (decrease towards shallower water,
  increase towards deeper).
- On an **antinodal line** the path difference to the two wave sources is a whole
  number of wavelengths (the waves meet in phase); on a **nodal line** it is a half-integer
  number of wavelengths (the waves meet in antiphase).

## Before the test

- Do you know Hooke's law, and can you calculate the spring constant from the extension and
  the force?
- Can you determine the oscillation energy from the amplitude and explain where
  the energy is in different positions?
- Do you know the formulas for displacement, velocity and acceleration in a
  harmonic oscillation, and where in the motion they are greatest?
- Do you know the formula for the period of a spring oscillation and of a simple
  pendulum, and that neither of them depends on the amplitude?
- Can you explain what resonance is and give examples of resonance phenomena?
- Can you tell a transverse wave from a longitudinal one, and do you know how a pulse
  is reflected at a fixed/free end?
- Can you use the principle of superposition to draw what two pulses look like
  when they meet?
- Can you determine the number of antinodes/nodes in a standing wave and link it
  to the length of the string or pipe (open, closed, half-open)?
- Can you calculate with sound intensity and sound level in decibels, and understand why
  the decibel scale is logarithmic?
- Do you know the law of refraction for water waves, and what changes
  (and what stays constant) on refraction?
- Can you decide whether a point lies on an antinodal line or a nodal line
  from the path difference to two wave sources?
