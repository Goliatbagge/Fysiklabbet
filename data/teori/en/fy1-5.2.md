# Pressure and compressive force

A [[force|An influence that can change an object's motion or shape. SI unit newton (N). A vector quantity.]] that arises when two objects are in contact with each other is called a
**compressive force** (pressing force), written *F*, and its unit is N (newton). The compressive force per
unit area is called **[[pressure|Force per unit area: *p* = *F* / *A*. SI unit pascal (Pa) = N/m².]]**, written *p*, and
its unit is Pa (pascal) or N/m² (newtons per square metre).

Compressive force and pressure are therefore **different** things! When you walk in deep powder snow
you act on the snow with a compressive force corresponding to your weight, and you sink
into the snow. If you put on skis instead, you do not sink into the snow
even though your weight and compressive force are the same. That is because the compressive force
is spread over a larger area, so the *pressure* becomes lower.

::: formel "Pressure between solid bodies"
$$
p = \frac{F}{A}
$$

where

- *p* = pressure (Pa or N/m²)
- *F* = compressive force (N)
- *A* = the area the compressive force acts on (m²)
:::

::: exempel "Example 1: The lady vs the tank"
**What causes the greatest pressure: a 60-kilogram lady stepping with her weight on a
stiletto heel with an area of 1.0 cm², or a 60-tonne tank with a total
caterpillar track area of 6.0 m²? Use the gravitational field strength $g \approx 10\ \mathrm{N/kg}$.**

::: handskrift
typ: damenpansar
:::

::: textlosning
We first calculate the pressure from the lady.

$$
p_\text{lady} = \frac{F_\text{lady}}{A_\text{lady}}
$$

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
F_\text{lady} = m_\text{lady} \cdot g = 60 \cdot 10 = 600\ \mathrm{N} \\
A_\text{lady} = 1.0\ \mathrm{cm^2} = 1.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\ \mathrm{m^2}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
p_\text{lady} = \frac{600}{1.0 \cdot 10^{-4}} = 6\,000\,000\ \mathrm{Pa} = 6.0\ \mathrm{MPa}
$$

We then calculate the pressure from the tank with the mass *m* = 60 tonnes = 60 000 kg.

$$
p_\text{tank} = \frac{F_\text{tank}}{A_\text{tank}}
$$

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
F_\text{tank} = m_\text{tank} \cdot g = 60\,000 \cdot 10 = 600\,000\ \mathrm{N} \\
A_\text{tank} = 6.0\ \mathrm{m^2}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
p_\text{tank} = \frac{600\,000}{6.0} = 100\,000\ \mathrm{Pa} = 0.10\ \mathrm{MPa}
$$

**Answer:** The lady. The pressure from the heel is about 60 times greater!
:::
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Compressive force and pressure are different things"
- **Compressive force** ($F$, N) arises when two objects are in contact.
- **Pressure** ($p$) is the compressive force **per unit area**. The SI unit is Pa
  (pascal) or N/m².
:::

::: sampunkt "The formula"
- $p = \dfrac{F}{A}$
- $A$ is the area the force acts on.
- Solving for: $F = p \cdot A$ and $A = \dfrac{F}{p}$.
:::

::: sampunkt "Same force, different pressure"
- In powder snow you sink in. With skis you do not, despite exactly the same
  weight.
- The force is spread over a **larger area**, so the pressure becomes lower.
- A small area gives a large pressure: a stiletto heel gives about 60 times greater
  pressure than a 60-tonne tank.
:::

::: sampunkt "Common trap"
- Convert the area to **square metres** before substituting.
- $1\ \mathrm{cm^2} = 10^{-4}\ \mathrm{m^2}$ and
  $1\ \mathrm{mm^2} = 10^{-6}\ \mathrm{m^2}$.
:::
:::
