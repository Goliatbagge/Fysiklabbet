# Summary: Motion and forces

This chapter was about forces that turn and forces that curve a
path. You have learnt to calculate with moments and rotational equilibrium, to describe
circular motion with period, frequency, radians and angular velocity, and to
use centripetal force to analyse everything from a conical pendulum to
a vertical circular path. The chapter ended with projectile motion, where the motion
is split into an *x*-direction and a *y*-direction.

## Concepts to know

- **Pivot**: the axis or point an object turns about.
- **Lever arm** ($l$): the perpendicular (shortest) distance between the force's
  line of action and the pivot.
- **Moment** ($M$): the turning effect of a force, $M = F \cdot l$; positive
  anticlockwise, negative clockwise.
- **Rotational equilibrium**: the anticlockwise moment is as large as the clockwise
  moment, so that the object does not turn; the pivot can then be chosen
  freely.
- **Period** ($T$): the time it takes for a motion to repeat itself (for example one
  revolution or one oscillation).
- **Frequency** ($f$): the number of repetitions per second.
- **Radian**: a unit of angle where 1 rad is the angle whose circular arc is as long
  as the radius; $360^\circ = 2\pi$ rad.
- **Angular velocity** (ω): how large an angle something turns through per unit of time.
- **Centripetal force** ($F_C$): the resultant force, directed towards the centre
  of the circle, that is needed to keep an object in circular motion. It is not a
  separate force, but a resultant of the actual forces.
- **Centripetal acceleration** ($a_C$): the acceleration that belongs to
  the centripetal force, directed towards the centre of the circle.
- **Conical pendulum**: a pendulum whose bob sweeps round in a horizontal circle while
  the string forms a cone.
- **Projectile motion**: motion along a curved path (a parabolic trajectory) that can be split
  into an *x*-direction (constant velocity) and a *y*-direction (constant acceleration
  $-g$); the angle of elevation α, the range and the maximum height describe the path.
- **Horizontal throw**: projectile motion with angle of elevation $0^{\circ}$, for
  example a ball rolling off a table. In the *y*-direction it is a free fall from rest,
  so the time of fall depends only on the height.

## Formulas

::: formel "The chapter's formulas"
**Moment**

$$
M = F \cdot l
$$

where

- *M* = moment (Nm)
- *F* = force (N)
- *l* = lever arm (m)

**The principle of moments (rotational equilibrium)**

$$
M_1 = M_2
$$

where

- $M_1$ = the anticlockwise moment(s)
- $M_2$ = the clockwise moment(s)

**Period and frequency**

$$
T = \frac{1}{f} \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad f = \frac{1}{T}
$$

where

- *T* = period (s)
- *f* = frequency (Hz or s⁻¹)

**Degrees and radians**

$$
360^{\circ} = 2\pi \qquad 180^{\circ} = \pi \qquad 90^{\circ} = \frac{\pi}{2}
$$

**Angular velocity**

$$
\omega = \frac{2\pi}{T} = 2\pi \cdot f
$$

where

- *ω* = angular velocity (rad/s)
- *T* = period (s)
- *f* = frequency (Hz)

**Speed in a circular path**

$$
v = \omega \cdot r = \frac{2\pi \cdot r}{T}
$$

where

- *v* = speed in the direction of motion (m/s)
- *ω* = angular velocity (rad/s)
- *r* = radius of the path (m)
- *T* = period (s)

**Centripetal acceleration**

$$
a_C = \frac{v^{2}}{r} = \omega^{2} \cdot r
$$

**Centripetal force**

$$
F_C = \frac{m \cdot v^{2}}{r} = \frac{4\pi^{2} \cdot m \cdot r}{T^{2}}
$$

where

- $F_C$ = centripetal force (N)
- $a_C$ = centripetal acceleration (m/s²)
- *m* = the mass of the object performing the motion (kg)
- *v* = speed in the direction of motion (m/s)
- *r* = radius of the path (m)
- *T* = period (s)

**Period of a conical pendulum**

$$
T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{l \cdot \cos \alpha}{g}}
$$

where

- *T* = period (s)
- *l* = length of the pendulum (m)
- *α* = angle of deflection from the vertical
- *g* = acceleration due to gravity (m/s²)

**Projectile motion in the *x*- and *y*-directions**

$$
v_x = v_0 \cdot \cos \alpha \qquad x = v_0 \cdot \cos \alpha \cdot t
$$

$$
v_y = v_0 \cdot \sin \alpha - g \cdot t \qquad y = v_0 \cdot \sin \alpha \cdot t - \frac{g \cdot t^{2}}{2}
$$

$$
v = \sqrt{v_x^{2} + v_y^{2}}
$$

where

- $v_{x,y}$ = velocity in the *x*-/*y*-direction (m/s)
- *x*, *y* = position in the *x*-/*y*-direction (m)
- $v_0$ = initial velocity (m/s)
- *α* = angle to the horizontal (angle of elevation)
- *t* = time (s)
- *v* = speed in projectile motion (m/s)

**Range and maximum height**

$$
x_{\max} = \frac{v_0^{2} \cdot \sin(2\alpha)}{g} \qquad y_{\max} = \frac{v_0^{2} \cdot \sin^{2}(\alpha)}{2g}
$$

## Important relationships and methods

- In rotational equilibrium the pivot can be chosen freely. A force whose
  line of action passes through the chosen pivot gets the lever arm 0 and
  then gives no moment. A smart choice makes an unknown force disappear from
  the equation.
- The closer to the centre of gravity a supporting or carrying force acts, the
  larger the share of the weight it carries.
- An object topples when the vertical line through the centre of gravity falls outside
  the base of support.
- The centripetal force is not a separate force, but the resultant of the real
  forces that keep the body in the circular path (tension, normal force,
  friction, gravitational force, …).
- In circular motion with constant speed, the speed is constant, but
  the velocity (a vector) is not: the direction changes all the time.
- In limiting cases (the top of a loop, the upper position in a vertical circle)
  the tension/normal force is zero, and the gravitational force alone provides
  the centripetal force. This gives the minimum allowed speed.
- In projectile motion the *x*-direction and the *y*-direction are treated separately: constant
  velocity in the *x*-direction (no force acts), constant acceleration $-g$ in
  the *y*-direction (only the gravitational force acts).
- The formulas for range and maximum height only apply when the throw starts and
  ends in the same horizontal plane. Otherwise the equations for *x* and *y*
  must be solved separately (as in "throw from a height").
- In a horizontal throw ($\alpha = 0^{\circ}$), the motion in the *y*-direction is a
  free fall from rest: the time of fall is found from $y = -h$ and depends only on
  the height, and the range is $x = v_0 \cdot t$. A ball launched horizontally and
  one dropped at the same time hit the floor at the same moment.
- Combined problems such as "The fatal swing" combine the principle of conservation of energy
  ($E_p = E_k$) with centripetal force to determine an unknown speed.
- The conical pendulum as a measuring method ("The flying cow" in 1.7): the string's
  vertical part is the height $h$ of the path below the attachment point, so $g = \dfrac{4\pi^{2} h}{T^{2}}$.
  Only the height and the period need to be measured.

- Can you choose the pivot freely in rotational equilibrium and solve for an unknown
  force with the principle of moments $M_1 = M_2$?
- Can you switch between period $T$ and frequency $f$, and between degrees and
  radians?
- Do you know the formula for angular velocity $\omega = 2\pi/T = 2\pi \cdot f$ and for
  the speed along the path $v = \omega \cdot r$?
- Do you know the formulas for centripetal acceleration and centripetal force, and can you
  explain why the centripetal force is always a resultant of other
  forces?
- Can you analyse a vertical circular motion (for example a weight on a string) in
  both the upper and the lower turning position, including the limiting case where the tension becomes
  zero?
- Do you know the formula for the period of a conical pendulum?
- Can you split a projectile motion into the *x*- and *y*-directions and calculate velocity
  and position at a given time?
- Do you know the formulas for range and maximum height, and when they apply?
- Can you calculate a horizontal throw: the time of fall from the height and the range
  from the constant velocity in the *x*-direction?
- Can you combine the principle of conservation of energy with centripetal force in a combined
  problem, as in "The fatal swing"?
