# Magnetic flux and the law of induction

We have noted earlier that a [[voltage|Energy difference per charge between two points. SI unit volt (V).]] can be induced in **two ways**:
either when a conductor moves in a [[magnetic field|Region around a magnet or current-carrying conductor where other magnetic objects are acted on by a force.]] ($e = l \cdot v \cdot B$),
or by **varying a magnetic field** near a conductor (for which
we do not yet have a formula).

To calculate the induced voltage when we vary a magnetic field
we need to introduce a new [[quantity|A property that can be measured with a number and a unit, for example distance, time, force.]]: **[[magnetic flux|The amount of magnetic field passing through a surface: *Φ* = *B* · *A* · cos *α*. SI unit weber (Wb).]]**. The number of magnetic
flux lines passing through an area is called the **magnetic flux**, is written
*Φ* (the capital Greek letter "phi") and has the [[SI unit|The International System of Units, with seven base units (m, kg, s, A, K, mol, cd).]] **Wb (weber)**.

::: tips "Flux versus flux density"
**Note!** Magnetic flux must not be confused with magnetic flux density.

- **Magnetic flux** *Φ* = the number of flux lines passing through an area.
- **Magnetic flux density** *B* = how closely packed (the "[[density|Mass per unit volume: *ρ* = *m* / *V*. SI unit kg/m³. Decides whether an object floats in a medium.]]" of) the
  flux lines are.
:::

The number of flux lines through the area is the magnetic flux Φ.
:::

The magnetic flux can be increased by increasing the **area** *A* (more
flux lines pass through) or by increasing the **flux density** *B* (the flux lines
are closer together).

::: formel "Magnetic flux"
$$
\Phi = B \cdot A_\perp
$$

where

- *Φ* = magnetic flux (Wb)
- *B* = magnetic flux density (T)
- $A_\perp$ = area perpendicular to the flux lines (m²)
:::

## The law of induction

The relationship for how large an induced voltage is when a magnetic
flux is varied is called the **law of induction**.

::: formel "The law of induction"
Average voltage, or voltage for a [[constant|A value that does not change. For example the acceleration due to gravity *g* or the speed of light *c*.]]/uniform change
in flux:

$$
e = -\frac{\Delta \Phi}{\Delta t}
$$

Instantaneous voltage:

$$
e = -\frac{d\Phi}{dt} = -\Phi'(t)
$$

where

- *e* = induced voltage/emf (V)
- Δ*Φ* = change in magnetic flux (Wb)
- Δ*t* = time taken for the change in magnetic flux (s)
- $\dfrac{d\Phi}{dt} = \Phi'(t)$ = time derivative of the magnetic flux
  (Wb/s)

::: härledning "Derivation: The law of induction"
We imagine moving a conductor at constant [[velocity|Displacement per unit time, with direction (a vector). SI unit m/s.]] in a
magnetic field.

Between the times $t_1$ and $t_2$ the magnetic flux has changed by

$$
\Delta \Phi = B \cdot \Delta A \qquad (1)
$$

where Δ*A* corresponds to the shaded region:

$$
\Delta A = l \cdot \Delta s
$$

The [[displacement|The vector between start and end position. Differs from distance, which is the total length of path.]] is $\Delta s = v \cdot \Delta t$. This gives

$$
\Delta A = l \cdot v \cdot \Delta t
$$

Substituting into (1) gives

$$
\Delta \Phi = B \cdot l \cdot v \cdot \Delta t
$$

From before we have $e = l \cdot v \cdot B$, so $B \cdot l \cdot v = e$:

$$
\Delta \Phi = e \cdot \Delta t
$$

We solve for the induced voltage and get

$$
e = \frac{\Delta \Phi}{\Delta t}
$$

Q.E.D. (Lenz's law adds the minus sign.)
:::
:::

The **minus sign** in the law of induction comes from **Lenz's law**: the
induced [[current|Amount of charge per unit time: *I* = *Q* / *t*. SI unit ampere (A).]] opposes the change that causes it.

## Induction in a coil

If the magnetic flux changes in a coil with *N* turns, the
induced voltage is *N* times as large.

::: formel "The law of induction for a coil"
Average voltage, or voltage for a constant/uniform change
in flux:

$$
e = -N \cdot \frac{\Delta \Phi}{\Delta t}
$$

Instantaneous voltage:

$$
e = -N \cdot \frac{d\Phi}{dt} = -N \cdot \Phi'(t)
$$

where

- *e* = induced voltage (V)
- *N* = number of turns in the coil
- Δ*Φ* = change in magnetic flux (Wb)
- Δ*t* = time taken for the change in magnetic flux (s)
- $\dfrac{d\Phi}{dt} = \Phi'(t)$ = time derivative of the magnetic flux
  (Wb/s)
:::

::: tips "Induction and graphs"
The [[slope|How steeply a line rises in a graph: Δ*y* divided by Δ*x*. In an *s-t* graph the slope is the velocity, in a *v-t* graph the acceleration.]] of a *Φ*–*t* graph corresponds to the induced voltage/emf, since
$e = -\Phi'(t)$ (the derivative corresponds to the slope).
:::

::: exempel "Example 1: Uniform change in flux in a coil"
**The magnetic flux in a coil with 370 turns increases uniformly from 0
to 0.17 mWb in 0.23 s. How large is the induced voltage?**

::: handskrift
typ: flodesandring
:::

::: textlosning
We set up the formula for the induced voltage in a coil for a uniform
change in flux.

$$
e = -N \cdot \frac{\Delta \Phi}{\Delta t} \qquad (1)
$$

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
N = 370 \\
\Delta \Phi = (0.17 - 0)\ \mathrm{mWb} = 0.17 \cdot 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{Wb} \\
\Delta t = 0.23\ \mathrm{s}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
e = -370 \cdot \frac{0.17 \cdot 10^{-3}}{0.23} = -0.273\ldots\ \mathrm{V} \approx -0.27\ \mathrm{V}
$$

**Answer:** −0.27 V
:::
:::

::: textlosning
**a)** The induced voltage at a particular time corresponds to the
**instantaneous voltage**, which is given by the slope of the graph. We determine the slope
at *t* = 1 s by looking at the rate of change between *t* = 0 and
*t* = 2 s.

$$
e = -\frac{\Delta \Phi}{\Delta t} = -\frac{10\ \mathrm{mWb}}{2\ \mathrm{s}} = -\frac{10 \cdot 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{Wb}}{2\ \mathrm{s}} = -0.005\ \mathrm{V} = -5.0\ \mathrm{mV}
$$

**Answer:** −5.0 mV

**b)** At *t* = 4 s there is **no change** in the magnetic flux (it
stays constant at 10 mWb). Δ*Φ* = 0, which means that no voltage
is induced.

**Answer:** 0 V
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Magnetic flux"
- The number of flux lines passing through an area. Written $\Phi$, with the unit
  **Wb (weber)**.
- $\Phi = B \cdot A_\perp$
- $A_\perp$ is the area **perpendicular** to the flux lines.
:::

::: sampunkt "Flux or flux density"
- **Flux** $\Phi$: how many lines pass through the area.
- **Flux density** $B$: how closely the lines are packed.
- The flux increases if the area gets larger **or** if the field gets denser.
:::

::: sampunkt "The law of induction"
- $e = -\dfrac{\Delta \Phi}{\Delta t}$ for a uniform change.
- $e = -\dfrac{d\Phi}{dt} = -\Phi'(t)$ for the instantaneous value.
- **The minus sign comes from Lenz's law.**
:::

::: sampunkt "Coil with N turns"
- $e = -N \cdot \dfrac{\Delta \Phi}{\Delta t}$
- So the voltage is $N$ times as large.
:::

::: sampunkt "From a graph"
- The **slope** of a $\Phi$-$t$ graph gives the induced voltage,
  since $e = -\Phi'(t)$.
- A horizontal graph means that nothing at all is induced.
- A steeper slope gives a larger voltage.
:::
:::
