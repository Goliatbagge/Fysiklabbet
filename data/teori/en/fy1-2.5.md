# Velocity-time graphs

A graph with [[velocity|Change of position per unit time, with direction (a vector). SI unit m/s.]] on the *y*-axis and time on the *x*-axis is called a
**velocity-time graph** or a ***v-t* graph**.

At **[[constant|A value that does not change. For example the acceleration due to gravity *g* or the speed of light *c*.]] [[acceleration|How quickly the velocity changes. SI unit m/s². A vector quantity.]]** the graph in a *v-t* graph is a straight line where
the line's [[slope|How steeply a line rises in a graph: Δ*y* divided by Δ*x*. In an *s-t* graph the slope is the velocity, in a *v-t* graph the acceleration.]] corresponds to the acceleration. The steeper the slope, the larger the
acceleration. In the same way as for [[average velocity|The mean velocity over a time interval: the displacement Δ*s* divided by the time Δ*t*.]] and instantaneous velocity,
the [[average acceleration|The mean acceleration over a time interval: the change in velocity Δ*v* divided by the time interval Δ*t*.]] between two times corresponds to the
**slope of the secant**, and the instantaneous acceleration to the **slope of the tangent**.

Whatever type of acceleration we are calculating, the slope of the relevant
line is given by $\dfrac{\Delta y}{\Delta x} = \dfrac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}$.

::: formel "Acceleration"
$$
a = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t} = \frac{v_2 - v_1}{t_2 - t_1}
$$

where

- *a* = constant acceleration or average acceleration (m/s²)
- Δ*v* = change in velocity (m/s)
- Δ*t* = time interval (s)
- *t*₁, *t*₂ = time 1 and time 2 respectively (s)
- *v*₁, *v*₂ = velocity at time 1 and at time 2 respectively (m/s)
:::

We can also easily calculate the [[displacement|The vector from the start position to the end position. Differs from distance, which is the total length of path.]] from a *v-t* graph.

::: formel "The area in a v-t graph"
In a *v-t* graph the **area** between the graph and the *x*-axis corresponds to
the **displacement**. We interpret an area above the *x*-axis as positive and an area
below the *x*-axis as negative. **[[The distance|The total length of path an object has moved. A scalar quantity, measured in metres.]]** is calculated in the same way, but then
areas below the *x*-axis are also counted as positive.

::: härledning "Derivation: The area in a v-t graph"
In "ordinary" areas we multiply lengths by each other, and units too!
For example

$$
10\ \mathrm{m} \cdot 5\ \mathrm{m} = 10 \cdot 5 \cdot \mathrm{m} \cdot \mathrm{m} = 50\ \mathrm{m^2}
$$

In a *v-t* graph the area corresponds to a multiplication of a velocity *v*
and a time Δ*t*. But we have already shown that Δ*s* = *v* · Δ*t*, that
is, the area corresponds to the displacement!
:::
:::

The displacement gets the same length unit as the one in the velocity, provided that the
time unit is the same as in the velocity. If, for example, we have the units **m**/s
and s on the axes, the displacement will have the unit **m**.

**a) How fast is the car going at the time 9 seconds?**
**b) What is the acceleration at the time 7 seconds?**
**c) How far has the car driven from when it rolls into the car park until it has parked?**

::: textlosning
**a)** Reading off at *t* = 9 s gives *v* = 11 m/s.

**Answer:** 11 m/s

**b)** Since the line is straight at *t* = 7, we have a constant acceleration.
We choose two points on this straight line, for example (5, 16) and (13, 6).

Substituting into $a = \dfrac{v_2 - v_1}{t_2 - t_1}$ gives

$$
a = \frac{6 - 16}{13 - 5} = \frac{-10}{8} = -1.25\ \mathrm{m/s^2}
\quad (\text{the minus sign means that the car is braking})
$$

**Answer:** −1.25 m/s²

**c)** The displacement corresponds to the area under the graph. We divide the area under
the graph into four parts: *A* (rectangle under the flat part), *B* (rectangle
under the lower flat part), *C* (triangle where the velocity drops from 16
to 6) and *D* (triangle where the velocity drops from 6 to 0):

$$
s = A + B + C + D
$$

The areas of the rectangles are the base multiplied by the height, and the areas of the triangles
are the base multiplied by the height divided by 2.

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
A = 5 \cdot 16 = 80\ \mathrm{m} \\
B = (17 - 5) \cdot 6 = 12 \cdot 6 = 72\ \mathrm{m} \\
C = \dfrac{(13 - 5)\cdot(16 - 6)}{2} = \dfrac{8 \cdot 10}{2} = \dfrac{80}{2}\ \mathrm{m} = 40\ \mathrm{m} \\
D = \dfrac{(21 - 17)\cdot 6}{2} = \dfrac{4 \cdot 6}{2} = \dfrac{24}{2}\ \mathrm{m} = 12\ \mathrm{m}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
s = 80 + 72 + 40 + 12 = 204\ \mathrm{m}
$$

**Answer:** 204 m
:::

::: textlosning
**a)** For the displacement Δ*s* we have

$$
\Delta s = A_1 + A_2
$$

Note that *A*₁ lies above the *x*-axis (positive displacement) and *A*₂
below the *x*-axis (negative displacement).

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
A_1 = \dfrac{\text{base} \cdot \text{height}}{2} = \dfrac{5 \cdot 10}{2} = 25\ \mathrm{m} \\
A_2 = -\dfrac{\text{base} \cdot \text{height}}{2} = -\dfrac{5 \cdot 10}{2} = -25\ \mathrm{m}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
\Delta s = 25 + (-25) = 0
$$

So the displacement is 0 (the object ends up in the same position as it
started).

**Answer:** The displacement is 0.

**b)** The distance travelled *s* corresponds to the total area
(whether it lies above or below the *x*-axis).

$$
s = |A_1| + |A_2| = 25 + 25 = 50\ \mathrm{m}
$$

**Answer:** 50 m

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Velocity-time graphs"
- Velocity $v$ on the $y$-axis, time $t$ on the $x$-axis. Called a
  *v-t* graph.
- **The slope is the acceleration.** A steeper line means a larger
  acceleration.
- Constant acceleration gives a straight line.
:::

::: sampunkt "Calculating the slope"
- $a = \dfrac{\Delta v}{\Delta t} = \dfrac{v_2 - v_1}{t_2 - t_1}$.
- **Average acceleration**: the slope of the secant between two times.
- **Instantaneous acceleration**: the slope of the tangent at a point.
:::

::: sampunkt "The area is the displacement"
- The **area** between the graph and the $x$-axis corresponds to the **displacement**.
- Area **above** the axis counts as positive, area **below** the axis as negative.
- Why: the area is $v \cdot \Delta t$, and $\Delta s = v \cdot \Delta t$.
:::

::: sampunkt "Displacement or distance"
- **Displacement**: add the areas with their signs.
- **Distance**: count all areas as positive.
- A graph from $+10$ to $-10$ m/s gives the displacement 0 but the distance
  $50\ \mathrm{m}$.
:::

::: sampunkt "Calculating the area"
- Divide the region into rectangles and triangles.
- Rectangle: base times height. Triangle:
  $\dfrac{\text{base} \cdot \text{height}}{2}$.
- The unit becomes the length unit of the velocity, if the time unit is the same on both
  axes.
:::
:::
