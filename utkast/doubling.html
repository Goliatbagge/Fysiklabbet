How long does it take to double your money?

You put money in a savings account, and it grows with compound interest. Find out how long it takes before the amount has doubled, and whether there is a rule that lets you work it out in your head.

Part 2 · Explain

Why doesn't the deposit matter?

Look at the box marked in green in the Interest Lab, How many times larger? Every year the amount is multiplied by the growth factor 1.06. After 12 years it has been multiplied by 1.06 twelve times, and the box shows what that gives.

Now change the deposit in the Interest Lab several times. Look at the box after each change:

  • Does the number 2.0122 change, that is, how many times larger the amount has become?
  • Do the kronor on the line Amount at the bottom of the box change?
  • Has the amount doubled after 12 years, no matter how much you deposited?
The Interest Lab is set up: 6 % and 12 years

5What do you notice? Use the growth factor to explain why.

Hint
If you deposit K kronor, what is the amount after n years? What should it equal when the amount has doubled?
Compare with an explanation
After n years the amount is K · 1.06n. The amount has doubled when K · 1.06n = 2K. Divide both sides by K: 1.06n = 2. The deposit K has disappeared from the equation, so the doubling time depends only on the growth factor, that is, on the interest rate.

Part 4 · Formulate a rule · 10 min

Your own rule

This is what you found on the previous page

rate · doubling time ≈ 72

But you want to be able to work out the doubling time. How do you get the doubling time on its own? What should you do with the interest rate?

10Write your rule.

Doubling time ≈/ interest rate
Hint
Think of an example. If 6 · doubling time = 72, how do you work out the doubling time? Do the same with any interest rate.
Why 72?
Your values for rate · time lie between about 70 and 73. People usually choose 72 because it can be divided evenly by 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 9 and 12. That makes it easy to calculate in your head: 72 / 6 = 12 years.

11Test your rule at 24 % interest. You need the time to one decimal place, just like in Part 2.

  1. Set 24 % in the Interest Lab, for example with the button High rate: 24 %.
  2. Choose Spreadsheet and find the first whole year in which the amount reaches the target. Click that row.
  3. Choose the step 0.1 years and find the first tenth in which the target is reached.
The rule says–years. years (one decimal)
Compare
The rule works well for low rates, about 2 to 10 %. There rate · time lies between 70 and 73. At 24 % the rule says 3 years, but it takes 3.2 years, so rate · time is 77. The higher the rate, the worse the rule fits.

Part 5 · From interest to equations · 15 min

Hard equations in your head

The equation 1.06x = 2 asks the same question that you have explored: how many times x do you have to multiply by the growth factor 1.06 to get 2?

12First try to solve 1.06x = 2 with the methods you have learned so far. What happens?

A tool you already have

1.06x = 2  ⇒  x ≈ 72 / 6 = 12

The growth factor 1.06 means the interest rate 6 %, and = 2 means doubling. So the rule of 72 gives the answer in your head. The Interest Lab shows 11.9, so mental arithmetic gets very close.
