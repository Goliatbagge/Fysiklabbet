# Normal distribution

Much statistical data is spread evenly around the mean. Such a
distribution is called a **normal distribution**. In normally distributed data
the values are distributed with the same percentages within certain given intervals that
are based on the mean $\mu$ and the standard deviation $\sigma$, according to
the normal distribution curve below:

where $\mu$ = mean and $\sigma$ = standard deviation.

::: demo "Try it yourself: the Galton board"
Why are so many things distributed in exactly this way? You can see it with a
**Galton board**, which the British scientist Francis Galton built in
the 1870s. Balls are dropped through rows of pins. At each pin the
ball bounces to the left or to the right with equal probability, completely at random.
The number of steps to the right decides which slot the ball ends up in.

A single ball can end up almost anywhere, but when many balls have
fallen they form a bell shape together. Tick *Show the normal
distribution curve* and the curve is drawn on the glass. Even though each ball only
bounces at random, the whole follows the normal distribution curve. When many small
random contributions are added together, the result is normally distributed, and that is why
the heights of people, for example, are approximately normally distributed.

Tick *Show the 68–95 rule* to test the percentages in the figure above.
With many balls, roughly two thirds of the balls end up within one
standard deviation of the mean and roughly 95 % within two, close to the
percentages in the normal distribution curve. It is not completely exact,
because the board only has a limited number of rows.

::: minisim
typ: galton
:::
:::

To find proportions that do not lie at whole
standard deviations you need help from digital tools, for example
GeoGebra or Fysiklabbet's
[probability calculator](sannolikhetskalkylator.html), where decimal numbers
are written with a comma and the question is asked in words.

To calculate percentages for normal distributions in GeoGebra we click
the "Probability Calculator" button, fill in the mean
($\mu$) and standard deviation ($\sigma$), click the button that
corresponds to the interval we are interested in and fill in the values.

| Interval type | Example wording |
| --- | --- |
| Open to the left | "What percentage is less than $x$?" |
| Interval | "What percentage lies between $x$ and $y$?" |
| Interval complement | "What percentage is less than $x$ or greater than $y$?" |
| Open to the right | "What percentage is greater than $x$?" |

How tall the normal distribution curve is depends on the spread. With a small
spread the values are gathered closely and the curve becomes taller and narrower. With a
large spread the values are more spread out and the curve becomes lower and
wider.

**What proportion is<br>a) between 173 cm and 189 cm?&emsp;&emsp;b) taller than 197 cm?**

**a)** We then see that 34.1 % lie between 173 cm and 181 cm. Between 181 cm
and 189 cm there is also 34.1 %. So between 173 cm and 189 cm there is

$$
34.1\ \% + 34.1\ \% = 68.2\ \%
$$

**Answer:** 68.2 %

**b)** We mark the area above 197 cm and compare with the
normal distribution curve. 197 cm corresponds to "the mean + two
standard deviations" and above this lies 2.3 %.

**Answer:** 2.3 %

::: exempel "Example 2: The height of adult women (GeoGebra)"
**Adult women have a mean height of 165.5 cm with standard deviation
6.15 cm.<br>a) What proportion of the women are between 160 and 170 cm?<br>b) How tall must a woman be to be taller than 99 % of all women?**

::: handskrift
typ: normalgeogebra
:::

::: textlosning
**a)** We solve the task with GeoGebra.

1. Click the "Probability Calculator" button in the top
   menu bar.
2. We enter the mean 165.5 at $\mu$ and the standard deviation
   6.15 at $\sigma$. NB! Remember that decimal numbers are written with a point
   rather than a comma: 165,5 → 165.5.
3. We now have a "closed interval" from 160 to 170 cm, which we
   enter in the fields: $P(160 \leq X \leq 170) = 0.582\ldots$

We see that the proportion is about 0.58 = 58 %, while the area is marked in

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Normal distribution"
- **Normal distribution:** the values are distributed evenly and symmetrically around the mean.
- The curve is completely determined by the mean $\mu$ and the standard deviation $\sigma$.
- A small spread gives a tall, narrow curve, a large spread gives a low, wide curve. The area under the curve is always the same.
:::

::: sampunkt "The percentages"
- Between $\mu$ and $\mu \pm \sigma$: 34.1 % in each direction.
- Between $\mu \pm \sigma$ and $\mu \pm 2\sigma$: 13.6 % in each direction.
- Beyond $\mu \pm 2\sigma$: 2.3 % in each direction.
- So 68.2 % of the values lie within one standard deviation.
:::

::: sampunkt "Method by hand"
1. Draw the curve and write $\mu$ directly below the peak.
2. Fill in $\mu \pm \sigma$ and $\mu \pm 2\sigma$ step by step in both directions.
3. Mark the requested area and add the percentages.
- With $\mu = 181$ and $\sigma = 8$, 68.2 % lie between 173 and 189, and 2.3 % above 197.
:::

::: sampunkt "The probability calculator"
- Uneven limits require a digital tool: the "Probability Calculator" button.
- Fill in $\mu$ and $\sigma$, choose the interval type and enter the limits. Decimal numbers with a point.
- Four interval types: open to the left, closed interval, interval complement and open to the right.
- If a **limit** is wanted instead of a proportion, the proportion is entered, for example 0.99 to find the height that 99 % lie below.
- Always show which values you entered and which interval you chose.
:::
