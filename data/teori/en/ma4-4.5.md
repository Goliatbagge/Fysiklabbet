# Complex numbers as points and arrows

All **real** numbers can be placed on the ordinary number line. Imaginary numbers,
on the other hand, cannot be placed there. To illustrate imaginary numbers we introduce an
imaginary number line perpendicular to the real one. Together they form the
**complex plane**.

The complex plane works like a coordinate system: the real part is read off
on the horizontal **Re-axis** and the imaginary part on the vertical **Im-axis**.
The origin corresponds to the number $0$. A complex number $z = a + bi$ then corresponds to the point
with real part $a$ on the Re-axis and imaginary part $b$ on the Im-axis.

The number can be illustrated either as a **point** or as an **arrow**: an
arrow starting at the origin with its tip at the point of the complex number.

::: formel "The complex number in the complex plane"
$$
z = a + bi \quad\longleftrightarrow\quad \text{the point } (a,\ b)
$$

The real part $a$ gives the position along the Re-axis, the imaginary part $b$ the position along the Im-axis.
:::

Real numbers (where $b = 0$) end up on the Re-axis and purely imaginary numbers (where
$a = 0$) on the Im-axis.

## Addition and subtraction: geometrically

Addition and subtraction of complex numbers are calculated in the same way as when we
add and subtract vectors: the real parts separately and the imaginary parts
separately.

::: formel "Addition and subtraction"
$$
(a + bi) \pm (c + di) = (a \pm c) + (b \pm d)i
$$
:::

Geometrically this means that the arrows are added together. If we translate
the arrow for $w$ so that it starts where the arrow for $z$ ends, then
the sum $z + w$ corresponds to the arrow from the origin to the tip of the translated arrow.

::: exempel "Example 1: Points in the complex plane"
**Give the real part and imaginary part of $z = 3 + 2i$ and $w = -2 - 4i$, and describe where the points end up.**

::: handskrift
typ: talplanpunkter
:::

::: textlosning
For $z = 3 + 2i$ the real part is $3$ and the imaginary part $2$, so the point lies
$3$ steps to the right and $2$ steps up, in the first quadrant.

For $w = -2 - 4i$ the real part is $-2$ and the imaginary part $-4$, so the point lies
$2$ steps to the left and $4$ steps down, in the third quadrant.

**Answer:** $z$ has $\mathrm{Re}(z) = 3$, $\mathrm{Im}(z) = 2$; $w$ has
$\mathrm{Re}(w) = -2$, $\mathrm{Im}(w) = -4$.
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 2: Add two complex numbers"
**Calculate $z + w$ when $z = 3 + 2i$ and $w = -2 - 4i$, and interpret the result geometrically.**

::: handskrift
typ: komplexaddition
:::

::: textlosning
We add the real parts separately and the imaginary parts separately:

$$
\begin{aligned}
z + w &= (3 + 2i) + (-2 - 4i)\\
&= (3 + (-2)) + (2 + (-4))i\\
&= 1 - 2i
\end{aligned}
$$

Geometrically: if we place the arrow for $w$ as a continuation of the arrow for $z$, then
the combined arrow goes from the origin to the point $1 - 2i$.

**Answer:** $z + w = 1 - 2i$
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 3: Subtract two complex numbers"
**Calculate $z - w$ when $z = 3 + 2i$ and $w = -2 - 4i$.**

::: handskrift
typ: komplexsubtraktion
:::

::: textlosning
We subtract the real parts separately and the imaginary parts separately. Watch
the signs: we are subtracting a negative number:

$$
\begin{aligned}
z - w &= (3 + 2i) - (-2 - 4i)\\
&= (3 - (-2)) + (2 - (-4))i\\
&= 5 + 6i
\end{aligned}
$$

**Answer:** $z - w = 5 + 6i$
:::
:::

## The conjugate as a reflection

The conjugate of a complex number, $\bar{z}$, corresponds to a **reflection in the
real axis** (the Re-axis). The number $z = a + bi$ and its conjugate
$\bar{z} = a - bi$ have the same real part $a$ but opposite imaginary parts, so
the points lie equally far above and below the Re-axis.

Multiplication and division of complex numbers, on the other hand, do **not** work in the same
way as for vectors. More about that later.

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "The complex plane"
- The horizontal **Re-axis** shows the real part, the vertical **Im-axis** the imaginary part.
- The number $z = a + bi$ corresponds to the point $(a,\ b)$.
- Real numbers end up on the Re-axis, purely imaginary numbers on the Im-axis.
:::

::: sampunkt "Point or arrow"
- The number can be drawn as a **point** or as an **arrow**, that is an arrow from the origin to the point.
- The origin corresponds to the number 0.
:::

::: sampunkt "Addition and subtraction"
- $(a + bi) \pm (c + di) = (a \pm c) + (b \pm d)i$. Calculated exactly as with vectors.
- Geometrically the arrows are added: move the arrow for $w$ to the tip of the arrow for $z$.
- The sum is the arrow from the origin to the new tip.
:::

::: sampunkt "The conjugate geometrically"
- The conjugate $\bar{z}$ is a **reflection in the Re-axis**.
- $z$ and $\bar{z}$ have the same real part but opposite imaginary parts.
- Multiplication and division, on the other hand, do **not** work as for vectors.
:::

:::
