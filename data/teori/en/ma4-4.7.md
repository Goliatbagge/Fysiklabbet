# Multiplication and division in polar form

In rectangular form, multiplying complex numbers is a fairly laborious
calculation. In polar form, on the other hand, it becomes surprisingly simple. We investigate
what happens when we multiply two complex numbers in polar form, for example
$z = 4(\cos 40^\circ + i\sin 40^\circ)$ and $w = 2(\cos 80^\circ + i\sin 80^\circ)$.

We multiply them together and collect the real and imaginary parts. With
the addition formulas for sine and cosine ($\cos u\cos v - \sin u\sin v = \cos(u + v)$
and $\sin u\cos v + \cos u\sin v = \sin(u + v)$) the brackets can be written as
the cosine and sine of a single angle:

$$
\begin{aligned}
z\cdot w &= 4(\cos 40^\circ + i\sin 40^\circ)\cdot 2(\cos 80^\circ + i\sin 80^\circ)\\
&= 8(\cos 40^\circ\cos 80^\circ + i\cos 40^\circ\sin 80^\circ + i\sin 40^\circ\cos 80^\circ + i^2\sin 40^\circ\sin 80^\circ)\\
&= 8\big((\cos 40^\circ\cos 80^\circ - \sin 40^\circ\sin 80^\circ) + i(\cos 40^\circ\sin 80^\circ + \sin 40^\circ\cos 80^\circ)\big)\\
&= 8\big(\cos(40^\circ + 80^\circ) + i\sin(40^\circ + 80^\circ)\big)\\
&= 8(\cos 120^\circ + i\sin 120^\circ)
\end{aligned}
$$

Notice the pattern: the modulus $8$ is the product of the moduli
($4$ and $2$), and the argument $120^\circ$ is the sum of the arguments ($40^\circ$
and $80^\circ$). This gives a simple rule, and there is an equally simple rule for
division, where the moduli are divided instead and the arguments subtracted.

::: formel "Multiplication and division of complex numbers in polar form"
For $z = |z|(\cos u + i\sin u)$ and $w = |w|(\cos v + i\sin v)$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
z\cdot w &= |z|\cdot |w|\,\big(\cos(u + v) + i\sin(u + v)\big)\\[4pt]
\frac{z}{w} &= \frac{|z|}{|w|}\,\big(\cos(u - v) + i\sin(u - v)\big)
\end{aligned}
$$

::: härledning "Derivation: multiplication in polar form"
We do the same calculation as in the example above, but with arbitrary angles $u$ and $v$. The moduli $|z|$ and $|w|$ are real numbers and can be moved to the front, and we multiply out the brackets term by term:

$$
\begin{aligned}
z\cdot w &= |z|(\cos u + i\sin u)\\
&\qquad\cdot|w|(\cos v + i\sin v)\\
&= |z|\cdot|w|\,(\cos u + i\sin u)\\
&\qquad\cdot(\cos v + i\sin v)\\
&= |z|\cdot|w|\,(\cos u\cos v + i\cos u\sin v\\
&\qquad + i\sin u\cos v + i^2\sin u\sin v)
\end{aligned}
$$

With $i^2 = -1$ the last term becomes $-\sin u\sin v$, and it belongs to the real part. We collect the real part and the imaginary part separately:

$$
\begin{aligned}
z\cdot w &= |z|\cdot|w|\,\big((\cos u\cos v - \sin u\sin v)\\
&\qquad + i(\sin u\cos v + \cos u\sin v)\big)
\end{aligned}
$$

The two inner brackets are the addition formulas for cosine and sine:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\cos u\cos v - \sin u\sin v &= \cos(u + v)\\
\sin u\cos v + \cos u\sin v &= \sin(u + v)
\end{aligned}
$$

Substituting them we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
z\cdot w &= |z|\cdot|w|\,\big(\cos(u + v) + i\sin(u + v)\big)
\end{aligned}
$$

The moduli are multiplied and the arguments added, just as when we multiplied the numbers with the arguments $40^\circ$ and $80^\circ$.
:::

::: härledning "Derivation: division in polar form"
We write the moduli as a separate factor in front. Then we divide in the same way as in rectangular form: we multiply the numerator and denominator by the conjugate of the denominator. The conjugate of $\cos v + i\sin v$ is $\cos v - i\sin v$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{z}{w} &= \frac{|z|(\cos u + i\sin u)}{|w|(\cos v + i\sin v)}\\[4pt]
&= \frac{|z|}{|w|}\cdot\frac{\cos u + i\sin u}{\cos v + i\sin v}\\[4pt]
&= \frac{|z|}{|w|}\cdot\frac{(\cos u + i\sin u)(\cos v - i\sin v)}{(\cos v + i\sin v)(\cos v - i\sin v)}
\end{aligned}
$$

We multiply out in the numerator and the denominator. The denominator is a product of a number and its conjugate, so there the difference of two squares gives only two terms:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{z}{w} &= \frac{|z|}{|w|}\cdot\frac{\begin{aligned}&\cos u\cos v - i\cos u\sin v\\ &+ i\sin u\cos v - i^2\sin u\sin v\end{aligned}}{\cos^2 v - i^2\sin^2 v}
\end{aligned}
$$

With $i^2 = -1$ we collect the real part and the imaginary part separately in the numerator, and the denominator becomes $\cos^2 v + \sin^2 v$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{z}{w} &= \frac{|z|}{|w|}\cdot\frac{\begin{aligned}&(\cos u\cos v + \sin u\sin v)\\ &+ i(\sin u\cos v - \cos u\sin v)\end{aligned}}{\cos^2 v + \sin^2 v}
\end{aligned}
$$

In the denominator, $\cos^2 v + \sin^2 v = 1$ by the Pythagorean identity. In the numerator the two inner brackets are the subtraction formulas for cosine and sine:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\cos u\cos v + \sin u\sin v &= \cos(u - v)\\
\sin u\cos v - \cos u\sin v &= \sin(u - v)
\end{aligned}
$$

Substituting them we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{z}{w} &= \frac{|z|}{|w|}\cdot\frac{\cos(u - v) + i\sin(u - v)}{1}\\[4pt]
&= \frac{|z|}{|w|}\,\big(\cos(u - v) + i\sin(u - v)\big)
\end{aligned}
$$

The moduli are divided and the arguments subtracted. The rule agrees with the multiplication rule: if we multiply the quotient by $w$, the moduli are multiplied to $\dfrac{|z|}{|w|} \cdot |w| = |z|$ and the arguments added to $(u - v) + v = u$, so that we get back $z$.
:::
:::

Geometrically this means that a multiplication is a **rotation** (the arguments
are added) combined with a **scaling** (the moduli are multiplied). If you want to do
the opposite, divide, you rotate back and scale down.

## Multiplication and division by i

A particularly important special case is multiplying by $i$. If we take the number $3$
and multiply by $i$ we get $3i$. If we multiply $3i$ by $i$ we get
$3i^2 = -3$. If we multiply $-3$ by $i$ we get $-3i$, and if we multiply
$-3i$ by $i$ we get $-3i^2 = 3$, and we are back where we started. If we draw
the numbers as arrows in the complex plane, we see that each multiplication by
$i$ rotates the arrow $90^\circ$ **anticlockwise**.

This also applies to numbers with both a real part and an imaginary part. The number $3 + 2i$ gives
$$
i(3 + 2i) = 3i + 2i^2 = -2 + 3i,
$$
and the arrow for $-2 + 3i$ is exactly the arrow for $3 + 2i$ rotated $90^\circ$ anticlockwise.
After all, the number $i$ has modulus $1$ (no scaling) and argument $90^\circ$
(the rotation).

::: formel "Multiplication and division by i"
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{multiplication by } i &\;\Longleftrightarrow\; \text{rotation } 90^\circ \text{ anticlockwise}\\[2pt]
\text{division by } i &\;\Longleftrightarrow\; \text{rotation } 90^\circ \text{ clockwise}
\end{aligned}
$$
:::

::: exempel "Example 1: multiply and divide in polar form"
**Let $z_1 = 3(\cos 120^\circ + i\sin 120^\circ)$ and $z_2 = 6(\cos 30^\circ + i\sin 30^\circ)$. Find<br>a)&nbsp;$z_1\cdot z_2$&emsp;&emsp;b)&nbsp;$\dfrac{z_2}{z_1}$**

::: handskrift
typ: polarmultdiv
:::

::: textlosning
a) In multiplication we multiply the moduli and add the arguments:

$$
z_1\cdot z_2 = 3\cdot 6\big(\cos(120^\circ + 30^\circ) + i\sin(120^\circ + 30^\circ)\big) = 18(\cos 150^\circ + i\sin 150^\circ)
$$

b) In division we divide the moduli and subtract the arguments:

$$
\frac{z_2}{z_1} = \frac{6}{3}\big(\cos(30^\circ - 120^\circ) + i\sin(30^\circ - 120^\circ)\big) = 2\big(\cos(-90^\circ) + i\sin(-90^\circ)\big) = 2(0 + i\cdot(-1)) = -2i
$$

**Answer:** a) $18(\cos 150^\circ + i\sin 150^\circ)$&emsp;&emsp;b) $2(\cos(-90^\circ) + i\sin(-90^\circ)) = -2i$
:::
:::

**Mark in the same complex plane<br>a)&nbsp;$\dfrac{z}{i}$&emsp;&emsp;b)&nbsp;$z\cdot i^2$**

::: handskrift
typ: vridtalplan
:::

::: textlosning
From the figure we read off that $z = -3 + 2i$.

a) A division by $i$ corresponds to a rotation of $90^\circ$ **clockwise**. So the number
$\dfrac{z}{i}$ has the same modulus as $z$ but an argument that is
$90^\circ$ smaller. If we calculate it (with $\dfrac{1}{i} = -i$) we get

$$
\frac{z}{i} = z\cdot(-i) = (-3 + 2i)(-i) = 3i - 2i^2 = 2 + 3i
$$

b) A multiplication by $i^2 = i\cdot i$ is two rotations of $90^\circ$ anticlockwise,
that is a rotation of $180^\circ$. Since $i^2 = -1$,

$$
z\cdot i^2 = z\cdot(-1) = -(-3 + 2i) = 3 - 2i
$$

**Answer:** a) $\dfrac{z}{i} = 2 + 3i$ ($90^\circ$ clockwise)&emsp;&emsp;b) $z\cdot i^2 = 3 - 2i$ ($180^\circ$)
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "The rules"
- $z \cdot w = |z| \cdot |w|\big(\cos(u + v) + i\sin(u + v)\big)$.
- $\dfrac{z}{w} = \dfrac{|z|}{|w|}\big(\cos(u - v) + i\sin(u - v)\big)$.
- In words: **multiply the moduli and add the arguments**. In division: divide and subtract.
:::

::: sampunkt "Why it works"
- Multiply the polar forms together and collect the real and imaginary parts.
- The **addition formulas** for sine and cosine combine the brackets into a single angle.
:::

::: sampunkt "Geometrically"
- A multiplication is a **rotation** (the arguments are added) and a **scaling** (the moduli are multiplied).
- Division rotates back and scales down.
:::

::: sampunkt "Multiplication by the imaginary unit"
- The number $i$ has modulus 1 and argument $90^\circ$, so no scaling, only rotation.
- Multiplication by $i$ rotates the arrow $90^\circ$ **anticlockwise**.
- Division by $i$ rotates $90^\circ$ **clockwise**. Multiplication by $i^2$ rotates $180^\circ$.
:::
