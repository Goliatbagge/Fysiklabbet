# Solving systems of linear equations graphically

When we have several equations that are to have common (identical) solutions,
we form a **system of equations**. When we solve a system of equations with
the help of its graphs, we carry out a **graphical solution**.

Consider the equation

$$
y = 2x - 4
$$

It consists of **two different** variables, $x$ and $y$. It has many
(infinitely many) pairs of solutions, for example

| $x$ | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $y$ | −4 | −2 | 0 | 2 |

and so on. For every value we substitute for $x$, we get a definite
value of $y$.

The same holds for the equation $y = -x + 5$. In the same way, it
also has infinitely many solutions.

If we want to find a **common** solution to both equations, we form
a system of equations, which is marked with a curly bracket as follows

$$
\begin{cases}
y = 2x - 4 \\
y = -x + 5
\end{cases}
$$

::: tips "Solving a system of equations graphically"
1. Draw the graphs of the equations by entering them in Geogebra.
2. Find the coordinates of any points of intersection.
3. The $x$-coordinate is the solution for $x$ and the $y$-coordinate is
   the solution for $y$.
:::

:::

::: exempel "Example 2: Find k and m"
**Find $k$ and $m$ so that the system of equations**

::: handskrift
typ: bestamkm
:::

::: textlosning
$$
\begin{cases}
y = kx + m \\
y = 2x + 5
\end{cases}
$$

**has no solutions.**

For the system of equations to have no solution, the lines must have
**the same $k$-value** but **different $m$-values**. For the lower equation
we have $k = 2$ and $m = 5$.

So the upper equation must have the same $k$-value, so $k = 2$.

The $m$-values must be different, so $m$ can be anything except 5, that is
$m \neq 5$.

**Answer:** $k = 2$ and $m \neq 5$
:::
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Systems of equations"
- Several equations that are to have **common** solutions.
- Marked with a **curly bracket**.
- A single equation in two variables has infinitely many pairs of solutions. It
  is the requirement of a **common** solution that gives a definite answer.
:::

::: sampunkt "Graphical solution"
1. Draw the graphs of both equations.
2. Find the coordinates of the **point of intersection**.
3. The $x$-coordinate is the solution for $x$, the $y$-coordinate for $y$.
:::

::: sampunkt "Number of solutions"
- **Different $k$-values**: the lines cross each other, so **one** solution.
  $m$ does not matter.
- **Same $k$, different $m$**: parallel lines, **no** solution.
- **Same $k$, same $m$**: the same line, **infinitely many** solutions.
:::

::: sampunkt "How to decide"
- Rewrite both equations in the form $y = kx + m$.
- Then compare $k$ first and $m$ second.
:::

::: sampunkt "The form of the answer"
- Write the answer with a bracket:
  $\begin{cases} x = 3 \\ y = 2 \end{cases}$
- **Both** variables belong to the answer.
:::
:::
