# Loudness

How strong a sound is, its "volume", is called **loudness** and corresponds
to the **[[amplitude|The maximum displacement of a wave from the equilibrium position.]]** of the oscillation. Loudness can be defined in two ways:
**sound intensity** and **sound level**.

The energy that the wave transports per second through a cross-sectional area of 1 m²
is called **sound intensity**, is denoted *I* and has the [[SI unit|The International System of Units, with seven base units (m, kg, s, A, K, mol, cd).]] W/m².

::: formel "Sound intensity"
$$
I = \frac{P}{A}
$$

where

- *I* = sound intensity (W/m²)
- *P* = [[power|Work or energy per unit time: *P* = *W* / *t*. SI unit watt (W).]] (W)
- *A* = area (m²)
:::

If the sound is emitted uniformly in all directions, the area *A* corresponds to
the surface of a sphere (a ball). The area of the sphere is calculated with the formula
$A = 4\pi \cdot r^{2}$, where *r* is the radius of the sphere.

::: formel "Sound intensity from a point source"
$$
I = \frac{P}{4\pi \cdot r^{2}}
$$

where

- *I* = intensity (W/m²)
- *P* = emitted power (W)
- *r* = distance to the point source (m)
:::

The human ear can hear down to $10^{-12}$ W/m², and the **threshold of pain** (where
sound turns into pain) is at 1 W/m².

So that the values of loudness do not consist of such small numbers, and so
that it better matches how loud the sound is perceived to be, the
concept of **sound level** was introduced. It is denoted *L* and has the unit **dB** (decibel),
where 0 dB corresponds to the threshold of hearing.

::: formel "Sound level"
$$
L = 10 \cdot \lg\!\left(\frac{I}{I_0}\right)
$$

where

- *L* = sound level (dB)
- *I* = sound intensity (W/m²)
- $I_0 = 10^{-12}$ W/m² (the threshold of hearing)
- lg stands for the "base-10 logarithm", log₁₀ (mathematics)
:::

::: tips "The decibel scale is logarithmic"
An increase of about **3 dB** ($10 \cdot \lg 2 \approx 3$) corresponds to a
**doubling** of the sound intensity. A sound level of 13 dB is therefore twice as
strong/energetic as 10 dB.

A difference of **30 dB** corresponds to 10 doublings, that is, an increase
by a factor of $2^{10} = 1\,024$. A sound level of 40 dB is therefore not 4
times as strong as 10 dB, but **more than 1 000 times** stronger!
:::

The human ear can hear down to 0 dB and the threshold of pain is at 120 dB.

**a) the sound intensity**
**b) the sound level**


::: handskrift
typ: ljudintensitet

::: textlosning
**a)** We set up the formula for sound intensity.

$$
I = \frac{P}{A}
$$

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
P = 5.0\ \mathrm{W} \\
A = 4\pi \cdot r^{2} = 4\pi \cdot (10\ \mathrm{m})^{2} = 1\,256.637\ldots\ \mathrm{m^{2}}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
I = \frac{5.0}{1\,256.637\ldots} = 0.00397\ldots\ \mathrm{W/m^{2}} \approx 0.0040\ \mathrm{W/m^{2}} = 4.0\ \mathrm{mW/m^{2}}
$$

**Answer:** 4.0 mW/m²

**b)** We set up the formula for sound level.

$$
L = 10 \cdot \lg\!\left(\frac{I}{I_0}\right)
$$

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
I = 0.00397\ldots\ \mathrm{W/m^{2}}\ (\text{from part a}) \\
I_0 = 10^{-12}\ \mathrm{W/m^{2}}\ (\text{constant})
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
L = 10 \cdot \lg\!\left(\frac{0.00397\ldots}{10^{-12}}\right) = 95.997\ldots\ \mathrm{dB} \approx 96\ \mathrm{dB}
$$

**Answer:** 96 dB
:::

::: exempel "Example 2: Sound intensity at 93 dB"
**The world's loudest snore was measured at 93 dB. What
sound intensity does that correspond to?**

::: handskrift
typ: ljudniva
:::

::: textlosning
We set up the formula for sound level and solve for the sound intensity *I*.

$$
L = 10 \cdot \lg\!\left(\frac{I}{I_0}\right)
$$

Divide both sides by 10:

$$
\frac{L}{10} = \lg\!\left(\frac{I}{I_0}\right)
$$

Rewrite in power form and solve for *I*:

$$
10^{L/10} = \frac{I}{I_0} \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad I = I_0 \cdot 10^{L/10}
$$

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
I_0 = 10^{-12}\ \mathrm{W/m^{2}} \\
L = 93\ \mathrm{dB}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
I = 10^{-12} \cdot 10^{93/10} = 0.00199\ldots\ \mathrm{W/m^{2}} \approx 0.0020\ \mathrm{W/m^{2}} = 2.0\ \mathrm{mW/m^{2}}
$$

**Answer:** 2.0 mW/m²
:::
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Loudness"
- How strong the sound is, that is, the volume. Corresponds to the
  **amplitude** of the oscillation.
- Can be given in two ways: **sound intensity** and **sound level**.
:::

::: sampunkt "Sound intensity"
- The energy the wave transports per second through
  $1\ \mathrm{m^2}$. The SI unit is W/m².
- $I = \dfrac{P}{A}$
- If the sound is emitted equally in all directions, the area is a sphere:
  $I = \dfrac{P}{4\pi r^{2}}$.
- The intensity therefore decreases with the **square** of the distance.
:::

::: sampunkt "Sound level"
- $L = 10 \lg\!\left(\dfrac{I}{I_0}\right)$, with the unit dB.
- $I_0 = 10^{-12}\ \mathrm{W/m^2}$ is the threshold of hearing, that is, 0 dB.
- Solve for the intensity:
  $I = I_0 \cdot 10^{\,L/10}$.
:::

::: sampunkt "The scale is logarithmic"
- **3 dB** more means **double** the intensity.
- **10 dB** more means **ten times** the intensity.
- 30 dB more is therefore not three times, but more than **a thousand times**
  stronger.
:::

::: sampunkt "Limits to know"
- Threshold of hearing: 0 dB, that is,
  $10^{-12}\ \mathrm{W/m^2}$.
- Risk of hearing damage with prolonged exposure: 85 dB.
- Threshold of pain: 120 dB, which corresponds to
  $1\ \mathrm{W/m^2}$.
:::
:::
