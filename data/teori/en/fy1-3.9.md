# Example: The direction of acceleration

Deciding the direction of the acceleration, that is whether it is positive or
negative, can be tricky and go against our gut feeling. The key to determining
the sign of the acceleration is to give [[velocities|Change of position per unit time, with direction (a vector). SI unit m/s.]] as **[[vectors|A quantity with both magnitude and direction, for example velocity, force or acceleration. Differs from a scalar, which only has magnitude.]] (with signs)**.

direction as upwards.**

**Determine<br>a) the sign of the acceleration&emsp;&emsp;b) the [[acceleration|How quickly the velocity changes. SI unit m/s². A vector quantity.]].**

::: handskrift
typ: bollrakt
:::

::: textlosning
**a)** During **the whole throw** (on the way up, at the turning point and on the way down),
the velocity decreases by the same amount every unit of time. The acceleration is
therefore [[constant|A value that does not change. For example the acceleration due to gravity *g* or the speed of light *c*.]] and the same at every moment. Since the velocity
**decreases** (from 6 m/s to 0 to −6 m/s), the acceleration is **negative**.

**Answer:** Negative

**b)** The acceleration is the [[acceleration due to gravity|The acceleration a freely falling object gets from the gravitational force, about 9.82 m/s² at the Earth's surface. Written *g*.]], which we know has the magnitude
9.82 m/s² in Sweden. Since we have defined the positive direction as upwards, it
is therefore negative: $a = -g = -9.82\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$.

**Answer:** −9.82 m/s²
:::
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "The basic rule"
- The sign of the acceleration is decided by the **velocity as a vector**, that is with
  its sign, never by the speed.
- Increasing velocity gives positive acceleration, decreasing velocity negative.
- Always decide the **positive direction** first and stick to it.
:::

::: sampunkt "Speed and velocity are different things"
- The **speed** is the magnitude of the velocity without a sign.
- The speed can increase while the velocity decreases, and the other way round.
- This is the most common trap in the whole section.
:::

::: sampunkt "The ball straight up"
- The velocity goes from $+6$ via 0 at the turning point to $-6$ m/s.
- It **decreases the whole time**, so the acceleration is negative during the whole
  throw, even at the turning point.
- The acceleration is $-9.82\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$ with the positive direction upwards.
:::

::: sampunkt "The lift, all four cases"
- Speeding up **upwards**: the velocity increases, so **positive**.
- Slowing down **upwards**: the velocity decreases, so **negative**.
- Speeding up **downwards**: the velocity goes from 0 towards $-2$ m/s and
  **decreases**, so **negative**.
- Slowing down **downwards**: the velocity goes from $-2$ m/s towards 0 and **increases**,
  so **positive**.
:::

::: sampunkt "How to do it"
1. Mark the positive direction.
2. Write the velocity with its sign at the beginning and at the end.
3. Did it get larger or smaller? That gives the sign.
:::
:::
