# Force and Newton's first law

The ability to deform a body or change its motion is called **[[force|An influence that can change the motion or shape of an object. SI unit newton (N). A vector quantity.]]**, is written
*F*, has the [[SI unit|The International System of Units, with seven base units (m, kg, s, A, K, mol, cd).]] N (newton) and is a [[vector|A quantity with both magnitude and direction, for example velocity, force or acceleration. Differs from a scalar, which has only magnitude.]].

There are several kinds of forces, for example magnetic force (a bar magnet),
electric force (a balloon on the ceiling), pressure force (diving) and [[gravitational force|The force that acts between all masses. At the Earth's surface it is called the gravitational force (weight).]]
(the Earth's pull). To measure force you can use a
**newton meter** (dynamometer).

An object can be acted on by two or more forces. A force that corresponds to
the combined effect of several forces is called the **resultant**, or
**[[resultant force|The sum of all the forces acting on an object (a vector sum). Gives the object its acceleration.]]**, and is written $F_R$. If the resultant force is zero we say
that there is **equilibrium of forces**.

A force can also be split into several forces with specific directions, and these
are then called **components**.

::: demo "Introduction: Newton's first law"
If a car is driving at 90 km/h and we take our foot off the accelerator, it will slow down.
Why? Because of [[air resistance|The force from the air that slows down objects moving through the air. Depends on the shape, size and speed of the object.]] and frictional forces. But if there
were no forces acting on the car, what would happen then? It would
continue its motion in the same direction.

This is the heart of [[Newton's first law|A body remains at rest or in uniform motion if the resultant force is zero.]]! If the resultant force on an object is 0,
its motion will not change. So it will remain at rest or
remain in motion with [[constant|A value that does not change. For example the acceleration due to gravity *g* or the speed of light *c*.]] [[velocity|Change of position per unit time, with direction (a vector). SI unit m/s.]].
:::

::: formel "Newton's first law (the law of inertia)"
If the resultant force on an object is zero, the object is either
**at rest** or moving with **constant velocity** (in a straight line).
:::

::: demo "Think about it: How can the resultant force be 0 when something is moving?"
Imagine the following: a skydiver who jumps out of an aeroplane
is at first acted on almost only by the [[gravitational force|The force with which the Earth pulls all objects towards its centre. The gravitational force gives an object its weight and is directed straight down towards the centre of the Earth.]]. There is then a resultant
force downwards and they accelerate down towards the ground. The higher the velocity becomes,
however, the more the air resistance increases. In the end the velocity is so high that
the air resistance is as large as the gravitational force (but in the opposite direction). There is then a
resultant force of 0 N. But is the skydiver at rest?
Hardly. But they are moving at **constant velocity**! (A skydiver rarely reaches more
than just over 200 km/h, however far they fall.)
:::

::: exempel "Example 1: Resultant force in three cases"
**Two forces have the magnitudes $F_1 = 15\ \mathrm{N}$ and $F_2 = 25\ \mathrm{N}$. Determine the resultant if
they act<br>a) in the same direction&emsp;&emsp;b) in opposite directions&emsp;&emsp;c) perpendicular to each other.**

::: handskrift
typ: resultantkraft
:::

::: textlosning
**a)** The forces work together, so the resultant force *F*ᴿ is

$$
F_R = F_1 + F_2 = 15 + 25 = 40\ \mathrm{N}
$$

**Answer:** 40 N in the same direction as *F*₁ and *F*₂.

**b)** The forces work against each other, so the resultant force *F*ᴿ is

$$
F_R = F_2 - F_1 = 25 - 15 = 10\ \mathrm{N}
$$

**Answer:** 10 N in the same direction as the larger force.

**c)** The resultant *F*ᴿ forms the hypotenuse of a right-angled force triangle where
*F*₁ and *F*₂ are the legs. It can then be calculated with Pythagoras' theorem.

$$
F_R^{2} = F_1^{2} + F_2^{2} \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad F_R = \sqrt{F_1^{2} + F_2^{2}}
$$

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
F_1 = 15\ \mathrm{N} \\
F_2 = 25\ \mathrm{N}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
F_R = \sqrt{15^{2} + 25^{2}} = \sqrt{850} = 29.154\ldots \approx 29\ \mathrm{N}
$$

**Answer:** 29 N
:::
:::

typ: komposanty

::: textlosning
Once again we have a right-angled force triangle, but this time we only know the magnitude of
one of the forces. We cannot then use Pythagoras' theorem but have to
use trigonometry.

::: formel "Remember: trigonometry in a right-angled triangle"
$$
\sin v = \frac{a}{c}, \qquad \cos v = \frac{b}{c}, \qquad \tan v = \frac{a}{b}
$$

where *a* is the opposite side, *b* is the adjacent side and *c* is the hypotenuse
for the angle *v*.
:::

If we look at the figure in the task, we have the hypotenuse and need to calculate the
opposite side, $F_y$. So we use the sine of the angle.

$$
\sin 25^{\circ} = \frac{F_y}{20} \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad
F_y = 20 \sin 25^{\circ} = 8.452\ldots \approx 8.5\ \mathrm{N}
$$

**Answer:** 8.5 N
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Force"
- **Force** ($F$) is the ability to deform a body or change its
  motion.
- The SI unit is N (newton) and force is a **vector**.
- Kinds of forces: magnetic, electric, pressure force, gravitational force.
- Force is measured with a **newton meter**.
:::

::: sampunkt "Resultant and equilibrium"
- The **resultant force** $F_R$ is the combined effect of all the forces.
- If $F_R = 0$ there is **equilibrium of forces**.
- **Components**: a force split into partial forces along chosen directions,
  usually $F_x$ and $F_y$.
:::

::: sampunkt "Newton's first law"
- If the resultant force is zero, the object is **either at rest or moving
  with constant velocity** along a straight line.
- Also called **the law of inertia**.
- The skydiver: when the air resistance has become as large as the
  gravitational force, $F_R = 0$, and they keep falling at constant velocity.
  So equilibrium does not mean standing still.
:::

::: sampunkt "Calculating the resultant"
- **Same direction**: add, $F_R = F_1 + F_2$.
- **Opposite directions**: subtract, $F_R = F_2 - F_1$, directed the way of the larger
  force.
- **Perpendicular**: Pythagoras' theorem,
  $F_R = \sqrt{F_1^{2} + F_2^{2}}$.
:::

::: sampunkt "Components with trigonometry"
- In a right-angled force triangle:
  $\sin v = \dfrac{a}{c}$, $\cos v = \dfrac{b}{c}$,
  $\tan v = \dfrac{a}{b}$.
- The force $F$ at the angle $v$ to the horizontal gives
  $F_y = F \sin v$ and $F_x = F \cos v$.
:::
:::
