# Acceleration-time graphs

A graph with [[acceleration|How quickly the velocity changes. SI unit m/s². A vector quantity.]] on the *y*-axis and time on the *x*-axis is called an
**acceleration-time graph** or an ***a-t* graph**.

::: formel "The area in an a-t graph"
In an *a-t* graph the **area** between the graph and the *x*-axis corresponds to
the **change in velocity**. We interpret an area above the *x*-axis as positive
(an increase in velocity) and an area below the *x*-axis as negative
(a decrease in velocity).

::: härledning "Derivation: The area in an a-t graph"
In an *a-t* graph the area corresponds to a multiplication of an acceleration
*a* and a time Δ*t*. From the definition of acceleration we have

$$
a = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t} \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad \Delta v = a \cdot \Delta t
$$

So the area in an *a-t* graph corresponds to the change in velocity over the interval.
:::
:::

::: textlosning
The change in velocity corresponds to the area under the graph up to 4.0 s, as follows

$$
\Delta v = A = 4 \cdot 5 = 20\ \mathrm{m/s} \quad (\text{an increase in velocity of 20 m/s})
$$

Since the body had an initial velocity of 8.0 m/s, the final velocity is

$$
8.0 + 20 = 28\ \mathrm{m/s}
$$

**Answer:** 28 m/s
:::
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Acceleration-time graphs"
- Acceleration $a$ on the $y$-axis, time $t$ on the $x$-axis. Called an
  *a-t* graph.
:::

::: sampunkt "The area is the change in velocity"
- The **area** between the graph and the $x$-axis corresponds to the **change in velocity**
  $\Delta v$.
- Area above the axis: the velocity increases. Area below the axis: the velocity
  decreases.
- Why: $a = \dfrac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}$ gives
  $\Delta v = a \cdot \Delta t$, which is exactly the area.
:::

::: sampunkt "How to calculate"
1. Find the area up to the time you are interested in.
2. The area is the **change** $\Delta v$, not the velocity itself.
3. Add the initial velocity: $v = v_0 + \Delta v$.
- With $v_0 = 8.0$ m/s and the area $20\ \mathrm{m/s}$ the answer is $28\ \mathrm{m/s}$.
:::

::: sampunkt "The areas in the three graphs"
- *s-t* graph: the **slope** is the velocity.
- *v-t* graph: the slope is the acceleration, the **area** is the displacement.
- *a-t* graph: the **area** is the change in velocity.
:::
:::
