# Applications with digital tools

With digital tools we can differentiate functions faster than doing it by
hand. We can also differentiate functions for which we have no rules of differentiation,
and we can visualise phenomena that are hard to picture just from a
function expression. Here we use GeoGebra, but the same way of thinking works in
most graphing calculators and mathematics programs.

In GeoGebra we call the function $f$ and the variable $x$. Once the function has been
entered, there are three operations we often come back to: reading off
**the change at a certain point** with $f'(a)$, and finding out **where
the change is zero** by solving $f'(x) = 0$, either graphically (draw
$f'$ and find the zero with the Roots tool) or numerically with
the command $\text{NSolve}$.

::: formel "Three useful operations in GeoGebra"
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\text{Define the function:} && f(x) = \ldots\\[2pt]
&\text{Change at the point } x = a\text{:} && f'(a)\\[2pt]
&\text{Where is the change zero } (f'(x) = 0)\text{:} && \text{NSolve}(f'(x) = 0)\ \text{ or the Roots tool}
\end{aligned}
$$
:::

The point is that the tool takes care of the differentiation itself. Our task is to know
**which** operation answers the question: a rate of change is a
value of the derivative $f'(a)$, while "does not change at all" and "largest/smallest value"
lead to the equation $f'(x) = 0$.

::: exempel "Example 1: Change at a point in time"
**The water level at a dam is affected by both precipitation and evaporation. A model for the level is $N(t) = 25\sqrt{t}\cdot e^{-0.2t} + 10$, where $N$ is the level in metres above a reference point and $t$ is the number of months after the start of measurements $(t \geq 1)$. How is the water level changing after 4 months?**

::: handskrift
typ: dammnivader
:::

::: textlosning
The change in the water level after 4 months is the derivative of the level when $t = 4$, that
is $N'(4)$. In GeoGebra we call the function $f$ and the variable $x$, so we
define

$$
f(x) = 25\sqrt{x}\cdot e^{-0.2x} + 10
$$

and then type $f'(4)$ in the input bar. The tool gives

$$
f'(4) \approx -1.7
$$

Since $N$ is measured in metres and $t$ in months, we interpret the negative value as
the level **decreasing** by $1.7$ metres per month at this point in time.

**Answer:** The water level is decreasing by $1.7$ metres per month.
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 2: When is the change zero?"
**At what point in time does the water level in Example 1 not change at all?**

::: handskrift
typ: dammnoll
:::

::: textlosning
When the water level does not change at all, the rate of change is zero, that is
$N'(t) = 0$. We have already defined the function as
$f(x) = 25\sqrt{x}\cdot e^{-0.2x} + 10$ and solve $f'(x) = 0$. Two ways give
the same answer:

*Graphical solution.* We enter $f'(x)$, adjust the coordinate axes so that we
can see the graph and use the Roots tool. The point where $f'$ crosses
the $x$-axis has the $x$-coordinate $2.5$.

*Numerical solution.* We instead type $\text{NSolve}(f'(x) = 0)$ and read off
the solution directly:

$$
\text{NSolve}(f'(x) = 0) = \{\,x = 2.5\,\}
$$

Both methods give $t = 2.5$. (Here the level is at its highest: the derivative changes
from positive to negative.)

**Answer:** After $2.5$ months.
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 3: A function without a rule by hand"
**Find $f'(1)$ for $f(x) = x^{\sin x}$.**

::: handskrift
typ: utanhandregel
:::

::: textlosning
Here the variable $x$ is in both the base and the exponent, so none of our usual
rules of differentiation is enough to differentiate by hand. With a digital tool
it is still easy: we define

$$
f(x) = x^{\sin x}
$$

and type $f'(1)$. The tool gives

$$
f'(1) \approx 0.84
$$

**Answer:** $f'(1) \approx 0.84$
:::
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Why digital tools"
- The tool differentiates faster and handles functions you have no rules by hand for.
- It also visualises processes that are hard to see in a function expression.
- Your task is to know **which** operation the question corresponds to.
:::

::: sampunkt "Three operations"
- Define the function: $f(x) = \ldots$
- The change at a point: type $f'(a)$.
- Where the change is zero: NSolve($f'(x) = 0$) or the **Roots** tool on the graph of $f'$.
:::

::: sampunkt "Translating the question"
- "How is it changing after 4 months?" is $f'(4)$.
- "When does it not change at all?" and "the largest or smallest value" lead to $f'(x) = 0$.
- A negative answer means a decrease. Always state the unit.
:::

::: sampunkt "Functions without a rule by hand"
- $f(x) = x^{\sin x}$ has the variable in **both** the base and the exponent.
- None of the usual rules is enough, but the tool gives $f'(1) \approx 0.84$.
- Always check that the answer is reasonable in the context.
:::

:::
