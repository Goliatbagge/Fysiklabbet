# Efficiency

::: demo "Introduction: Efficiency"
Why do old incandescent bulbs get hot but not LED lamps? 90 % of the energy that
we pay for in old incandescent bulbs comes out at a [[frequency|The number of oscillations per second. SI unit hertz (Hz) = 1/s.]] that we cannot see
and is completely useless. So which frequency do they send out so much of? The answer is
**infrared**, which we cannot see but feel as warmth. What LED lamps do
is take all the energy we need and want from a light bulb and put
almost all of it into the visible part of the spectrum. Then it does not need 60 W
but only 3–5 W, since all the energy goes to visible light! LED lamps
use their energy more effectively and have a **higher efficiency** than
incandescent bulbs.
:::

A device cannot give out more energy than it receives (otherwise we would be able to
create energy). However, energy can be "lost" to other forms of energy that we
cannot use.

How effective a device is, is called its **efficiency**, written *η* (the small
Greek letter "eta"). It is a number between 0 and 1 and has no unit.

The efficiency tells us what fraction (what percentage) of the energy
supplied becomes useful energy. If you have a device with an efficiency of
0.50 = 50 %, it means that it can convert half of the supplied
energy into something useful. If it has an efficiency of 0 = 0 %, it does
nothing useful at all, and if it has an efficiency of 1 = 100 %, it converts
all the supplied energy into useful energy. The energy that does not become useful is
usually lost as [[heat|Energy transferred between objects at different temperatures. Measured in joules (J).]].

::: formel "Efficiency"
$$
\eta = \frac{E_n}{E_t} \qquad\text{or}\qquad \eta = \frac{P_n}{P_t}
$$

where

- *η* = efficiency, 0 ≤ *η* ≤ 1
- $E_n$ = useful energy
- $E_t$ = energy supplied
- $P_n$ = useful [[power|Work or energy per unit time: *P* = *W* / *t*. SI unit watt (W).]]
- $P_t$ = power supplied
:::

Examples of efficiencies:

| "Device" | Efficiency |
| --- | --- |
| Human body | ≈ 20 % |
| Petrol engine | ≈ 30 % |
| Electric motor | ≈ 90 % |
| Cooker | ≈ 100 % (since almost all the energy becomes heat, which is what we want) |

::: exempel "Example 1: The efficiency of a motor (Foundation)"
**Determine the efficiency of a motor if we supply it with 252 kJ of energy and
get 88 kJ out as [[kinetic energy|The energy an object has because of its motion: *E*ₖ = ½ · *m* · *v*².]].**

::: handskrift
typ: verkningsgrad
:::

::: textlosning
$$
\eta = \frac{E_n}{E_t}
$$

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
E_n = 88\ \mathrm{kJ} \\
E_t = 252\ \mathrm{kJ}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
\eta = \frac{88}{252} = 0.349\ldots \approx 0.35 = 35\ \%
$$

**Answer:** 0.35 or 35 %
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 2: The efficiency of a lift (Intermediate)"
**A lift that raises 220 kg to a height of 15 metres uses 43 kJ of energy.
Determine the efficiency of the lift.**

::: handskrift
typ: hissverkning
:::

::: textlosning
$$
\eta = \frac{E_n}{E_t}
$$

The useful energy is the energy used to lift the lift 15 metres and give
it [[potential energy|The energy an object has because of its position in a force field, for example *E*ₚ = *m* · *g* · *h* in a gravitational field.]].

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
E_n = \Delta E_p = m \cdot g \cdot h = 220 \cdot 9.82 \cdot 15 = 32\,406\ \mathrm{J} \\
E_t = 43\ \mathrm{kJ} = 43 \cdot 10^{3}\ \mathrm{J} = 43\,000\ \mathrm{J}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
\eta = \frac{32\,406}{43\,000} = 0.753\ldots \approx 0.75 = 75\ \%
$$

**Answer:** 0.75 or 75 %
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 3: The power of a power station (Advanced)"
**A hydroelectric power station has a head (height of fall) of 107 metres. Every second 275 tonnes of
water pass through the turbines. The efficiency of the turbines is 0.92. How much power can
the power station deliver?**

::: handskrift
typ: kraftverket
:::

::: textlosning
We set up the formula for efficiency and solve for the useful power
$P_n$.

$$
\eta = \frac{P_n}{P_t} \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad P_n = \eta \cdot P_t
$$

In the time *t* = 1 second, the energy supplied comes from the potential energy of 275 tonnes of
water, that is

$$
E_t = m \cdot g \cdot h = 275\,000\ \mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.82\ \mathrm{N/kg} \cdot 107\ \mathrm{m}
= 2.889\ldots \cdot 10^{8}\ \mathrm{J}
$$

$$
P_t = \frac{E_t}{t} = \frac{2.889\ldots \cdot 10^{8}\ \mathrm{J}}{1\ \mathrm{s}}
= 2.889\ldots \cdot 10^{8}\ \mathrm{W}
$$

With *η* = 0.92 this gives

$$
P_n = 0.92 \cdot 2.889\ldots \cdot 10^{8}
= 2.658\ldots \cdot 10^{8}\ \mathrm{W}
\approx 2.7 \cdot 10^{8}\ \mathrm{W} = 0.27\ \mathrm{GW}
$$

**Answer:** 2.7 · 10⁸ W or 0.27 GW
:::
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Efficiency"
- A measure of how **effective** a device is. Written $\eta$ (eta); it
  has no unit.
- Gives the **fraction** of the supplied energy that becomes useful.
- Always between 0 and 1, that is 0 to 100 %. A device can never give out
  more energy than it receives.
:::

::: sampunkt "The formula"
- $\eta = \dfrac{E_n}{E_t}$ or
  $\eta = \dfrac{P_n}{P_t}$
- $E_n$ is the **useful** energy and $E_t$ the energy **supplied**.
- Energy and power work equally well, as long as you use the same kind in
  the numerator and the denominator.
:::

::: sampunkt "Where the energy goes"
- The part that does not become useful usually disappears as **heat**.
- The incandescent bulb sends out about 90 % as infrared, that is heat we cannot
  see. The LED lamp puts almost everything into visible light and therefore
  manages on a few watts instead of sixty.
:::

::: sampunkt "Typical efficiencies"
- The human body about 20 %.
- The petrol engine about 30 %.
- The electric motor about 90 %.
- The cooker almost 100 %, since there the heat **is** what is useful.
:::

::: sampunkt "How to solve it"
1. Decide what the **useful** energy is. Often $\Delta E_p$ or
   $\Delta E_k$.
2. Determine the energy **supplied**.
3. Convert to the same unit, usually joules.
4. Divide and answer both as a decimal and as a percentage.
:::
:::
