# Different kinds of angles

Angles are often measured in degrees (°) and are defined so that a full turn is
360°.

## Notation for angles

In the figure an angle $v$ has been marked at the point B. It can
be denoted in different ways:

$$
v = \angle B = \angle ABC = \angle CBA
$$

## Transversal and angles at parallel lines

A line that crosses at least two other lines is called a **transversal**. If two
**parallel** lines L₁ and L₂ are crossed by a transversal, angles arise
as in the figure below.

The angles v 1, v 2, v 3 and v 4 are marked at the points of intersection."><line x1="20" y1="40" x2="240" y2="40" stroke="#1f2530" stroke-width="1.6"/><line x1="20" y1="120" x2="240" y2="120" stroke="#1f2530" stroke-width="1.6"/><line x1="75" y1="10" x2="145" y2="150" stroke="#1f2530" stroke-width="1.6"/><text x="246" y="44" font-size="12" text-anchor="start" fill="#1f2530">L₁</text><text x="246" y="124" font-size="12" text-anchor="start" fill="#1f2530">L₂</text><path d="M 103,40 A 13 13 0 0 1 95.8,51.6" fill="none" stroke="#1f2530" stroke-width="1.2"/><text x="108" y="58" font-size="12" text-anchor="start" fill="#1f2530"><tspan font-style="italic">v</tspan>₁</text><path d="M 143,120 A 13 13 0 0 1 135.8,131.6" fill="none" stroke="#1f2530" stroke-width="1.2"/><text x="148" y="138" font-size="12" text-anchor="start" fill="#1f2530"><tspan font-style="italic">v</tspan>₂</text><path d="M 117,120 A 13 13 0 0 1 124.2,108.4" fill="none" stroke="#1f2530" stroke-width="1.2"/><text x="104" y="112" font-size="12" text-anchor="end" fill="#1f2530"><tspan font-style="italic">v</tspan>₃</text><path d="M 148,120 A 18 18 0 0 0 121.9,103.9" fill="none" stroke="#1f2530" stroke-width="1.2"/><text x="144" y="104" font-size="12" text-anchor="start" fill="#1f2530"><tspan font-style="italic">v</tspan>₄</text></svg>
:::

| Angles … | are called … | and are … | Example in the figure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| placed in the same way relative to each other | **corresponding angles** | equal | $v_1 = v_2$* |
| opposite each other where two lines cross | **vertically opposite angles** | equal | $v_2 = v_3$ |
| towards the parallel lines on opposite sides of the crossing line | **alternate angles** | equal | $v_1 = v_3$* |
| that together form a straight angle | **angles on a straight line** | together 180° | $v_3 + v_4 = 180°$ |

\* Applies only if the lines L₁ and L₂ are parallel.

has a certain size. Justifications can be written briefly in brackets, see
the examples below.

::: exempel "Example 2: Auxiliary angle"
**Calculate the angle $v_1$ if the lines L₁ and L₂ are parallel.**

::: handskrift
typ: hjalpvinkel

::: textlosning
This task can be solved in several ways. We add an auxiliary angle
$v_2$ as in the figure below.

::: figur
<svg viewBox="18 0 184 116" width="291" height="184" xmlns="http://www.w3.org/2000/svg" font-family="Poppins, system-ui, sans-serif" role="img" aria-label="Two parallel lines are crossed by a transversal. :::

$$
v_2 = 115° \quad \text{(corresponding angles)}
$$

$$
v_1 + v_2 = 180° \quad \text{(angles on a straight line)}
$$

Substituting $v_2 = 115°$ gives

$$
v_1 + 115° = 180°
$$

$$
v_1 = 180° - 115° = 65°
$$

**Answer:** 65°
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Notation"
- Angles are measured in **degrees**, and a full turn is $360^\circ$.
- The same angle can be written $v$, $\angle B$, $\angle ABC$ or
  $\angle CBA$.
- The **middle** vertex in the notation is where the angle is.
:::

::: sampunkt "Transversal"
- A line that **crosses** at least two other lines.
- If it crosses two **parallel** lines, a definite pattern of
  equal angles arises.
:::

::: sampunkt "The four pairs of angles"
- **Corresponding angles**: the same position at each crossing. **Equal**.
- **Vertically opposite angles**: opposite each other at a crossing. **Equal**.
- **Alternate angles**: on opposite sides of the transversal. **Equal**.
- **Angles on a straight line**: together form a straight angle, that is
  $180^\circ$.
:::

::: sampunkt "Important condition"
- Corresponding and alternate angles are equal **only if the lines are
  parallel**.
- Vertically opposite angles and angles on a straight line **always** apply.
:::

::: sampunkt "Bisector"
- A ray that divides an angle **in half**.
:::

::: sampunkt "Always justify"
- Write the justification in brackets after each step: (alternate angles),
  (angles on a straight line), (angle sum of a triangle).
- If it does not work directly: add an **auxiliary angle** and take two steps.
:::
:::
