# Vectors and scalars

A property that can be measured in some way is called a **quantity**. Examples of
quantities are distance, speed, mass, volume and temperature. There are
two types of quantity: vectors and scalars.

A quantity that is given with

- magnitude and direction is called a **vector**. Examples of vectors are
  velocity, force and acceleration.
- magnitude only (no direction) is called a **scalar**. Examples of scalars
  are mass, time and temperature.

We represent vectors with arrows, where the length of the arrow corresponds to the vector's
magnitude and the arrow corresponds to the vector's direction. To denote a vector,
a variable with an arrow above it is often used, for example $\vec{x}$.

Vectors can be translated (moved parallel to themselves): as long as the magnitude or
the direction does not change, it is *the same vector*. Vectors that are equally large
but have opposite directions are called **opposite vectors**. Vectors that
have the same slope (but may have opposite directions or be of different sizes)
are **parallel**.

The length of a vector $\vec{u}$ is written $|\vec{u}|$ and is read
"the absolute value of $u$". The length of a vector is calculated with Pythagoras'
theorem, in the same way as distances in coordinate systems.

**Which vectors are<br>a) the same&emsp;&emsp;b) opposite&emsp;&emsp;c) parallel?**

::: handskrift
typ: vektorjamfor
:::

::: textlosning
**a)** Since $\vec{v}_1$ and $\vec{v}_4$ have the same magnitude and
direction, they are the same vector, so $\vec{v}_1 = \vec{v}_4$.

**Answer:** $\vec{v}_1$ and $\vec{v}_4$

**b)** Since $\vec{v}_1$ and $\vec{v}_2$ have the same magnitude but
opposite directions, they are opposite vectors, so $\vec{v}_2 = -\vec{v}_1$.

**Answer:** $\vec{v}_2$ and $\vec{v}_1$

**c)** Since $\vec{v}_1$, $\vec{v}_2$, $\vec{v}_3$ and $\vec{v}_4$
have the same slope, they are parallel vectors.

**Answer:** $\vec{v}_1$, $\vec{v}_2$, $\vec{v}_3$ and $\vec{v}_4$
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Vector and scalar"
- **Vector**: has both **magnitude and direction**. Velocity, force,
  acceleration.
- **Scalar**: has only **magnitude**. Mass, time, temperature.
:::

::: sampunkt "How they are drawn"
- As **arrows**: the length is the magnitude, the direction is the direction of the arrow.
- Denoted with an arrow above the variable, for example $\vec{x}$.
:::

::: sampunkt "Translation"
- A vector may be moved freely. As long as the **magnitude and direction** are
  unchanged, it is **the same vector**.
- So where in the coordinate system it is drawn does not matter.
:::

::: sampunkt "Opposite and parallel"
- **Opposite**: equally large but **opposite directions**.
- **Parallel**: the same slope. They may well have different lengths or
  opposite directions.
- So all opposite vectors are also parallel.
:::

::: sampunkt "The length of the vector"
- Written $|\vec{u}|$ and read "the absolute value of $u$".
- Calculated with **Pythagoras' theorem**, just like a distance in a
  coordinate system.
:::
:::
