# Newton's second law

::: demo "Introduction: Pushing a bicycle and a car"
We are going to push a bicycle and a car so that they get the same [[acceleration|How quickly the velocity changes. SI unit m/s². A vector quantity.]].
We need to push with a much larger [[force|An influence that can change the motion or shape of an object. SI unit newton (N). A vector quantity.]] to get the car rolling
than the bicycle. This is because the car's mass is larger, so its motion is
harder to change.
:::

The resistance to a change of motion is called **[[inertia|An object's resistance to a change in its motion. Larger mass → larger inertia.]]**. The larger the mass, the
larger the inertia (compare the car with the bicycle above). If something is acted on by a
[[resultant force|The sum of all the forces acting on an object (a vector sum). Gives the object its acceleration.]], it will get an acceleration that is **proportional**
to the resultant force (the larger the resultant force, the larger the
acceleration) and **inversely proportional** to the mass (the larger the mass, the
smaller the acceleration). This relationship is called **[[Newton's second law|Resultant force = mass × acceleration: *F* = *m* · *a*.]]** or
**the law of acceleration**.

::: formel "Newton's second law (the law of acceleration)"
$$
F_R = m \cdot a
$$

where

- $F_R$ = resultant force (N)
- *m* = mass (kg)
- *a* = acceleration (m/s²)
:::

From Newton's second law we can define 1 N. **1 N is the force needed
to give a mass of 1 kg an acceleration of 1 m/s².** If we express 1 N in
base units, this means that

$$
1\ \mathrm{N} = 1\ \mathrm{kg} \cdot 1\ \mathrm{m/s^2} = 1\ \mathrm{kg \cdot m/s^2}
$$

::: kuriosa "Airbags and superheroes"
Newton's second law explains why an airbag protects you in a crash. The cushion makes
the braking take place over a longer time, which makes the head's
acceleration smaller, and therefore also the force on the head.

**Think about it:** In superhero films you see Superman come flying in and save
a falling person just before they hit the ground. If we ignore
superpowers, would that work in reality? The answer is no: it makes
no difference whether it is Superman or the ground that stops the fall. The
violent braking causes the same large forces.
:::

**a) Calculate the acceleration of the box.**
**b) How far is it pushed in 2.0 seconds?**


::: handskrift
typ: ladaputt
:::

::: textlosning
**a)** We write down Newton's second law and solve for the acceleration *a*.

$$
F_R = m \cdot a \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad a = \frac{F_R}{m}
$$

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
F_R = (50 - 20)\ \mathrm{N} = 30\ \mathrm{N} \\
m = 5\,000\ \mathrm{g} = 5.0\ \mathrm{kg}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
a = \frac{30}{5.0} = 6.0\ \mathrm{m/s^2}
$$

**Answer:** 6.0 m/s²

**b)** [[The distance|The total length of path an object has moved. A scalar quantity, measured in metres.]] at [[constant|A value that does not change. For example the acceleration due to gravity *g* or the speed of light *c*.]] acceleration without an [[initial velocity|The velocity at the time *t* = 0 (when the motion starts). Written *v*₀.]] is calculated as

$$
s = \frac{a \cdot t^{2}}{2}
$$

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
a = 6.0\ \mathrm{m/s^2} \\
t = 2.0\ \mathrm{s}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
s = \frac{6.0 \cdot 2.0^{2}}{2} = \frac{24}{2} = 12\ \mathrm{m}
$$

**Answer:** 12 m
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 2: Resultant force on the car"
**A car accelerates from 0 to 90 km/h in 15 seconds. The car has a mass of 1.2 tonnes.
What is the average resultant force on the car during
the acceleration?**

::: handskrift
typ: bilkraft
:::

::: textlosning
We write down Newton's second law.

$$
F_R = m \cdot a
$$

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
m = 1\,200\ \mathrm{kg} \\
a = \dfrac{\Delta v}{\Delta t} = \dfrac{90/3.6\ \mathrm{m/s}}{15\ \mathrm{s}}
   = \dfrac{25\ \mathrm{m/s}}{15\ \mathrm{s}} = 1.666\ldots\ \mathrm{m/s^2}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
F_R = 1\,200 \cdot 1.666\ldots = 2\,000\ \mathrm{N} = 2.0\ \mathrm{kN}
$$

**Answer:** 2.0 kN
:::
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Inertia"
- **Inertia** is the resistance to a change of motion.
- Larger mass means larger inertia. That is why a much larger force is needed
  to get a car moving than a bicycle.
:::

::: sampunkt "Newton's second law"
- $F_R = m \cdot a$, also called **the law of acceleration**.
- The acceleration is **proportional** to the resultant force and
  **inversely proportional** to the mass.
- $F_R$ is the resultant force (N), $m$ the mass (kg) and $a$ the acceleration
  (m/s²).
:::

::: sampunkt "The definition of 1 newton"
- **$1\ \mathrm{N}$ is the force that gives a mass of $1\ \mathrm{kg}$ an acceleration of
  $1\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$.**
- In base units:
  $1\ \mathrm{N} = 1\ \mathrm{kg \cdot m/s^2}$.
:::

::: sampunkt "How to solve the tasks"
1. First calculate the **resultant** force, that is the driving force
   minus the opposing force.
2. Convert the mass to kilograms.
3. Substitute into $a = \dfrac{F_R}{m}$.
4. If a distance or velocity is needed: continue with the formulas of motion.
:::

::: sampunkt "Why the airbag protects you"
- The cushion makes the braking take place over a **longer time**.
- A longer time means a smaller acceleration, and a smaller acceleration means
  a smaller force on the head.
- The same applies to Superman: it makes no difference whether it is the ground or
  a superhero that stops the fall just as abruptly.
:::
:::
