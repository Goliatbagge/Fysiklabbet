To produce a **turning motion**, a force is needed whose effect depends on the distance
to the **pivot**. The pivot is the axis that an object
turns around when it rotates. If you open a door, the hinges
form the pivot, and if you loosen a nut with a spanner, the
centre of the nut forms the pivot.

The distance to the pivot is called the **lever arm**. The lever arm is the perpendicular
distance, or the shortest distance, between the force's line of action and the
pivot.

The turning effect of a force is called the **moment of a force** or **torque**, is denoted
*M* and has the [[SI unit|The International System of Units, with seven base units (m, kg, s, A, K, mol, cd).]] Nm (newton metre). The larger the force and the
longer the lever arm, the greater the torque. A force whose direction passes through the
pivot has a lever arm of 0 and therefore gives no moment.

::: formel "Moment of a force (torque)"
$$
M = F \cdot l
$$

where

- *M* = moment of a force (Nm)
- *F* = force (N)
- *l* = lever arm (m)

The direction of rotation is given either as "clockwise"/"anticlockwise" or with a sign.
A **positive** moment corresponds to a rotation **anticlockwise** and a
**negative** moment corresponds to a rotation **clockwise**.
:::

We set up the formula for the moment of a force.

$$
M = F \cdot l
$$

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
F = 34\ \mathrm{N} \\
l = 0.25\ \mathrm{m}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
M = 34 \cdot 0.25 = 8.5\ \mathrm{Nm}
$$

**Answer:** 8.5 Nm anticlockwise (or +8.5 Nm)

::: textlosning
Once again we set up the formula for the moment of a force.

$$
M = F \cdot l
$$

In this case the lever arm is **not** 1.0 m, but the [[distance|The total length of path an object has moved. A scalar quantity, measured in metres.]] from the pivot
to the point where the force's line of action meets the ground. It forms a
right-angled triangle, so we calculate the lever arm *l* using trigonometry.

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
F = 800\ \mathrm{N} \\
\cos 45^{\circ} = \dfrac{l}{1.0} \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad l = 1.0 \cdot \cos 45^{\circ} = 0.707\ldots\ \mathrm{m}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
M = 800 \cdot 0.707\ldots = 565.685\ldots\ \mathrm{Nm} \approx 570\ \mathrm{Nm} = 0.57\ \mathrm{kNm}
$$

**Answer:** 0.57 kNm clockwise (or −0.57 kNm)
:::

## Rotational equilibrium

When the anticlockwise moment is equal to the clockwise moment, no
rotation takes place, and there is **rotational equilibrium**.

::: kuriosa "Physics in everyday life: Rotational equilibrium"
When we play on a seesaw, there is a problem if one person weighs more than
the other. The heavier person pushes the board down and the lighter one gets stuck
up in the air. If the heavier person moves further in on the seesaw,
both will eventually be in the air: rotational equilibrium has been reached!
:::

::: formel "The principle of moments"
In rotational equilibrium

$$
M_1 = M_2
$$

where

- $M_1$ = the anticlockwise moment(s)
- $M_2$ = the clockwise moment(s)
:::

In rotational equilibrium

$$
M_1 = M_2
$$

We call the father's weight $F_P$ and the child's weight $F_B$. This gives

$$
F_P \cdot l_P = F_B \cdot l_B
$$

The [[gravitational force|The force with which the Earth pulls all objects towards its centre. The gravitational force gives an object its weight and is directed straight down towards the centre of the Earth.]] $F = m \cdot g$ gives

$$
m_P \cdot g \cdot l_P = m_B \cdot g \cdot l_B
$$

We divide both sides by *g* and then solve for the father's lever arm $l_P$.

$$
m_P \cdot l_P = m_B \cdot l_B \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad l_P = \frac{m_B \cdot l_B}{m_P}
$$

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
m_B = 30\ \mathrm{kg} \\
l_B = 2.0\ \mathrm{m} \\
m_P = 80\ \mathrm{kg}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
l_P = \frac{30 \cdot 2.0}{80} = \frac{60}{80} = 0.75\ \mathrm{m}
$$

**Answer:** The father should sit 0.75 m from the pivot.

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Pivot and lever arm"
- The **pivot** is the axis the object turns around.
- The **lever arm** $l$ is the **perpendicular** distance from the pivot
  to the force's line of action.
- Perpendicular force: $l$ is the whole arm. Oblique force: $l$ becomes shorter.

::: sampunkt "Moment of a force"
- The **turning effect** of a force. Denoted $M$ with the unit Nm.
- $M = F \cdot l$
- A larger force or a longer lever arm gives a larger moment.
- A force whose line of action passes **through** the pivot has
  a lever arm of 0 and gives no moment at all.
:::

::: sampunkt "The direction"
- A **positive** moment means rotation **anticlockwise**.
- A **negative** moment means rotation **clockwise**.
- Alternatively, just state clockwise or anticlockwise in words.
:::

::: sampunkt "Rotational equilibrium"
- Exists when the anticlockwise moment is equal to the clockwise moment. Then
  no rotation takes place.
- **The principle of moments**: $M_1 = M_2$.
:::

::: sampunkt "Typical task: seesaw"
- Set the moments equal:
  $m_1 \cdot g \cdot l_1 = m_2 \cdot g \cdot l_2$.
- The gravitational field strength $g$ cancels, so
  $m_1 l_1 = m_2 l_2$.
- The person who weighs twice as much should sit at **half** the distance.
:::

::: sampunkt "Oblique force"
- If the force is not perpendicular to the arm, the lever arm must be calculated using
  trigonometry.
- A crowbar of $1.0\ \mathrm{m}$ at $45^\circ$ has the lever arm
  $1.0 \cdot \cos 45^\circ$ for a vertical force.
:::
:::
