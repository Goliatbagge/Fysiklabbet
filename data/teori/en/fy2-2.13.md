# Water waves and the law of refraction

Water waves can look different.

Waves formed by

- **straight wavefronts** are called **plane waves**
- **circular wavefronts** are called **circular waves**.

**Circular waves** are produced when we have a point source (a point
dipped into the water) and give pulses that move **radially** (in the same
direction as the radius) outwards.

All parts of a wave move with the same [[velocity|Change of position per unit time, with direction (a vector). SI unit m/s.]] **perpendicular to
the wavefront**. In periodic waves (waves produced with a [[constant|A value that does not change. For example the acceleration due to gravity *g* or the speed of light *c*.]] [[frequency|Number of oscillations per second. SI unit hertz (Hz) = 1/s.]])
the distance between two adjacent wavefronts is one wavelength λ.

## Reflection

When waves meet an obstacle and are reflected, **the angle of incidence is equal
to the angle of reflection**. The angles are measured between the direction of motion of the wave
and the **normal** to the reflecting surface, a line at a right angle (90°)
to the plane of the surface.

::: formel "The law of reflection"
$$
i = r
$$

where

- *i* = angle of incidence
- *r* = angle of reflection

The angles are measured between the direction of motion of the wave and the normal (the line that
is perpendicular to the reflecting surface).
:::

## Refraction

When water waves change direction ("are refracted") as they pass over a different
depth, this is called **refraction**.

::: tips "Refraction of water waves"
Waves that go from

- **deeper water to shallower** are refracted **towards** (closer to) the normal and
  get a **reduced** wavelength and wave speed
- **shallower water to deeper** are refracted **away from** the normal and get an **increased**
  wavelength and wave speed.

In **both cases the frequency is constant**.
:::

::: formel "The law of refraction"
$$
\frac{\sin i}{\sin b} = \frac{v_1}{v_2} = \frac{\lambda_1}{\lambda_2}
$$

where

- *i*, *b* = angle of incidence and angle of refraction respectively
- $v_1, v_2$ = speed in medium 1 and medium 2 respectively
- $\lambda_1, \lambda_2$ = wavelength in medium 1 and medium 2 respectively

::: härledning "Derivation: The law of refraction"
We already know that the speed of a wave can be calculated as
$v = f \cdot \lambda$. For the deep water we therefore have
$v_1 = f \cdot \lambda_1$ and for the shallow water
$v_2 = f \cdot \lambda_2$ (the same frequency *f* in both cases).

The ratio between the speeds can therefore be written

$$
\frac{v_1}{v_2} = \frac{f \cdot \lambda_1}{f \cdot \lambda_2} = \frac{\lambda_1}{\lambda_2} \qquad \text{that is}\quad \frac{v_1}{v_2} = \frac{\lambda_1}{\lambda_2}
$$

We can also show that $\dfrac{v_1}{v_2} = \dfrac{\sin i}{\sin b}$ (a
geometric derivation of this can be found in the textbook).
:::
:::

::: kuriosa "Refraction and ocean waves"
One consequence of refraction is that **even when the wind blows along a
beach, the waves still come in almost perpendicular to the beach**, since
the wavefronts are refracted as the water gets shallower near the beach.

A more serious consequence is that the lower speed of the waves near the beach means
that waves coming from behind at a higher speed "push on" the wave
in front, and a **wave crest** forms. If the wave is large enough, a
"giant wave" forms: a **tsunami**. The 2004 tsunami killed more than 200 000 people
in Thailand and Indonesia, among other places, one of the worst natural disasters in
modern times. The tsunami off the coast of Japan in 2011 led to **the nuclear accident
at Fukushima**: the earthquake knocked out the power grid, and the tsunami then knocked
out the backup generators for cooling the reactors.

::: exempel "Example 1: Refraction from deep to shallow water"
**A wave with a wavelength of 15 metres and a speed of 8.0 m/s hits a boundary
with shallower water at an angle of incidence of 40°, and is then slowed down and gets
a wavelength of 10 metres. Determine**

**a) the angle of refraction**
**b) the frequency of the waves in the shallow water**

::: handskrift
typ: vattenbrytning
:::

::: textlosning
**a)** We set up the law of refraction with angles/wavelengths and solve for
the angle of refraction *b*.

$$
\frac{\sin i}{\sin b} = \frac{\lambda_1}{\lambda_2} \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad \sin b = \frac{\lambda_2 \cdot \sin i}{\lambda_1} \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad b = \sin^{-1}\!\left(\frac{\lambda_2 \cdot \sin i}{\lambda_1}\right)
$$

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
\lambda_2 = 10\ \mathrm{m} \\
i = 40^{\circ} \\
\lambda_1 = 15\ \mathrm{m}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
b = \sin^{-1}\!\left(\frac{10 \cdot \sin 40^{\circ}}{15}\right) = 25.373\ldots^{\circ} \approx 25^{\circ}
$$

**Answer:** 25°

**b)** We set up the formula for wave speed and solve for the frequency *f*.

$$
v = f \cdot \lambda \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad f = \frac{v}{\lambda}
$$

We do not have the speed *v* in the shallow water, but since **the frequency
is constant** we can calculate with the frequency in the deep water.

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
v = 8.0\ \mathrm{m/s} \\
\lambda = 15\ \mathrm{m}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
f_\mathrm{shallow} = f_\mathrm{deep} = \frac{8.0}{15} = 0.533\ldots\ \mathrm{Hz} \approx 0.53\ \mathrm{Hz}
$$

**Answer:** 0.53 Hz
:::
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Wavefronts"
- **Plane waves** have straight wavefronts, **circular waves** have
  circular ones.
- Circular waves come from a point source and spread **radially**
  outwards.
- The wave always moves **perpendicular to the wavefront**, and the distance between
  two wavefronts is one wavelength.
:::

::: sampunkt "The law of reflection"
- $i = r$, that is, the angle of incidence is equal to the angle of reflection.
- The angles are measured from the **normal**, the line that is perpendicular to
  the surface. Not from the surface itself.
:::

::: sampunkt "Refraction"
- The wave changes direction when it passes over a different depth.
- **Deep to shallow**: refracted **towards** the normal, the wavelength and speed
  **decrease**.
- **Shallow to deep**: refracted **away from** the normal, the wavelength and
  speed **increase**.
- **The frequency is always unchanged.** It is what links the two
  media.
:::

::: sampunkt "The law of refraction"
- $\dfrac{\sin i}{\sin b}
  = \dfrac{v_1}{v_2} = \dfrac{\lambda_1}{\lambda_2}$
- That the ratios of speeds and wavelengths are equal follows directly from
  $v = f\lambda$ with the same $f$ in both media.
:::

::: sampunkt "Refraction at the beach"
- The waves come in **almost perpendicular** to the beach even when the wind blows
  along it, since they are refracted as the water gets shallower.
- The waves behind catch up with the slower ones in front and a **wave crest**
  builds up.
- In the extreme case it becomes a **tsunami**, as in the Indian Ocean in 2004 and
  in Japan in 2011.
:::
:::
