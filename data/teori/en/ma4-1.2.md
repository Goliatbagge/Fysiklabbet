# The unit circle

A circle with radius 1 placed with its centre at the origin is called the
**unit circle**. Using the unit circle we can find the sine, cosine
and tangent of any angle at all: also of angles greater than $90^\circ$
and of negative angles, not just of the acute angles in a right-angled
triangle.

If we let a line segment from the origin form an angle $v$ with the positive
$x$-axis out to the edge of the circle, we get a right-angled triangle whose
hypotenuse is the radius of the circle, which is always equal to 1. The point $(x, y)$
where the segment meets the edge then carries all the information about the angle's
trigonometric values.

The angle $v$ gives a point $(x, y)$ on the unit circle. For all angles $v$

$$
\sin v = \frac{y}{1} = y
$$

$$
\cos v = \frac{x}{1} = x
$$

$$
\tan v = \frac{y}{x} = \frac{\sin v}{\cos v}, \qquad \cos v \neq 0
$$

So the point's coordinates $(x, y)$ can be written $(\cos v,\ \sin v)$: sine is
the point's $y$-coordinate and cosine is its $x$-coordinate.
:::

An angle is measured from the positive $x$-axis and turned **anticlockwise** the given
number of degrees. An angle greater than $360^\circ$ means going one full
turn and then continuing. That is why $v$ and $v + 360^\circ$ give the same point
on the circle and the same trigonometric values. A **negative** angle instead
corresponds to turning **clockwise**.

## Exact values for the standard angles

For some particularly common angles, the sine, cosine and
tangent can be given as exact values instead of rounded decimals. These values
are worth recognising.

::: formel "Exact values for standard angles"
$$
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
v & 0^\circ & 30^\circ & 45^\circ & 60^\circ & 90^\circ \\
\hline
\sin v & 0 & \dfrac{1}{2} & \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} & \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} & 1 \\[2mm]
\cos v & 1 & \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} & \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} & \dfrac{1}{2} & 0 \\[2mm]
\tan v & 0 & \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3} & 1 & \sqrt{3} & \text{undef.}
\end{array}
$$

**Memory trick:** the sine row is
$\dfrac{\sqrt{0}}{2},\ \dfrac{\sqrt{1}}{2},\ \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2},\ \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2},\ \dfrac{\sqrt{4}}{2}$.
The root in the numerator simply counts up $0, 1, 2, 3, 4$, and the cosine row is the same
thing backwards. Tangent is found from $\tan v = \dfrac{\sin v}{\cos v}$; at
$90^\circ$ we have $\cos v = 0$, so $\tan 90^\circ$ is undefined.
:::

**Find<br>a)&nbsp;$\cos v$&emsp;&emsp;b)&nbsp;$\sin v$&emsp;&emsp;c)&nbsp;$\tan v$&emsp;&emsp;d)&nbsp;$v$&emsp;&emsp;e)&nbsp;$\sin 419.3^\circ$&emsp;&emsp;f)&nbsp;$\sin(-59.3^\circ)$.**

::: handskrift
typ: enhetscirkelavlas
:::

::: textlosning
**a)** Cosine is the point's $x$-coordinate, so $\cos v = 0.51$.

**b)** Sine is the point's $y$-coordinate, so $\sin v = 0.86$.

**c)** Tangent is the quotient of sine and cosine:

$$
\tan v = \frac{\sin v}{\cos v} = \frac{0.86}{0.51} = 1.686\ldots \approx 1.69
$$

**d)** The angle is found with an inverse function, for example from the sine:

$$
\sin v = 0.86 \quad\Rightarrow\quad v = \sin^{-1}(0.86) = 59.316\ldots^\circ \approx 59.3^\circ
$$

**e)** The angle $419.3^\circ$ is greater than $360^\circ$. We subtract one full
turn to find the corresponding angle:

$$
\sin 419.3^\circ = \sin(419.3^\circ - 360^\circ) = \sin 59.3^\circ = 0.86
$$

**f)** The angle $-59.3^\circ$ is a turn of $59.3^\circ$ clockwise. The point
is then a reflection of the point in the task in the $x$-axis, so the $y$-coordinate
changes sign:

$$
\sin(-59.3^\circ) = -0.86
$$

**Answer:** a) 0.51&emsp;&emsp;b) 0.86&emsp;&emsp;c) 1.69&emsp;&emsp;d) 59.3°&emsp;&emsp;e) 0.86&emsp;&emsp;f) −0.86
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 2: Exact value for a standard angle"
**Find the exact value of $\cos 30^\circ$ and check using
the unit circle.**

::: handskrift
typ: exaktcos
:::

::: textlosning
From the table of standard angles we read the cosine row at $30^\circ$:

$$
\cos 30^\circ = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}
$$

This is the $x$-coordinate of the point on the unit circle that corresponds to
$30^\circ$. As a decimal it is

$$
\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = 0.866\ldots \approx 0.87
$$

which agrees with the point lying quite far to the right (close to the edge in the
$x$-direction) but only a little way up, since $\sin 30^\circ = \dfrac{1}{2}$.

**Answer:** $\cos 30^\circ = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \approx 0.87$
:::
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "The unit circle"
- The **unit circle** has radius 1 and its centre at the origin.
- It gives the sine, cosine and tangent of **all** angles, including obtuse and negative ones.
- The point on the edge has the coordinates $(\cos v,\ \sin v)$.
:::

::: sampunkt "The definitions"
- $\sin v = y$ and $\cos v = x$.
- $\tan v = \dfrac{\sin v}{\cos v} = \dfrac{y}{x}$, where $\cos v \neq 0$.
- The angle is measured from the positive $x$-axis and turned **anticlockwise**. A negative angle is turned clockwise.
:::

::: sampunkt "Exact standard values"
- $\sin$: $0$, $\dfrac{1}{2}$, $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$, $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, $1$ for $0^\circ$, $30^\circ$, $45^\circ$, $60^\circ$, $90^\circ$.
- $\cos$ is the same row **backwards**.
- $\tan$: $0$, $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3}$, $1$, $\sqrt{3}$, undefined.
- Memory trick: the numerators of the sine row are $\sqrt{0}$, $\sqrt{1}$, $\sqrt{2}$, $\sqrt{3}$, $\sqrt{4}$, all divided by 2.
:::

::: sampunkt "Calculation tricks"
- Subtract whole turns: $\sin 419.3^\circ = \sin 59.3^\circ$.
- $\sin(-v) = -\sin v$ and $\cos(-v) = \cos v$, since the point is reflected in the $x$-axis.
- $\tan 90^\circ$ is undefined, since $\cos 90^\circ = 0$.
:::

:::
