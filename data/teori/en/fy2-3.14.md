# Magnetometer and the Hall effect

To measure magnetic flux density, a **magnetometer** or
**Hall effect sensor** is used.

At the end of the meter there is a thin conducting plate of width *d* through which a known
[[current|Amount of charge per unit time: *I* = *Q* / *t*. SI unit ampere (A).]] *I* flows. It is placed perpendicular to the [[magnetic field|Region around a magnet or current-carrying conductor where other magnetic objects experience a force.]], see the figure.

A current through the plate means that we have electrons moving in a
magnetic field. The electrons are then affected by a magnetic force $F_\mathrm{m}$,
which in the figure points to the left.

The left part of the plate then becomes negatively charged, the right part positively
charged, and an electric force $F_\mathrm{e}$ pointing to the right arises. When [[equilibrium|When the resultant force on an object is zero. The object is then at rest or in uniform motion.]]
has been reached between the magnetic and the electric force, a certain
voltage *U* forms across the edges of the plate. The fact that a current-carrying conductor gets a
voltage perpendicular to the direction of the current is called the **Hall effect**, and the voltage
is sometimes called the **Hall voltage**.

::: härledning "Derivation: Hall voltage"
When equilibrium has been reached between the magnetic and the electric force,

$$
F_\mathrm{m} = F_\mathrm{e}
$$

$F_\mathrm{m} = q \cdot v \cdot B$ and $F_\mathrm{e} = q \cdot \mathbb{E}$
(where $\mathbb{E}$ is the electric field strength, from Physics level 1) give

$$
q \cdot v \cdot B = q \cdot \mathbb{E}
$$

We divide both sides by *q*.

$$
v \cdot B = \mathbb{E}
$$

$\mathbb{E} = \dfrac{U}{d}$ (from Physics level 1) gives

$$
v \cdot B = \frac{U}{d}
$$

We solve for the Hall voltage *U* and get

$$
U = v \cdot d \cdot B
$$
:::

Since both *v* (the [[velocity|Change of position per unit time, with direction (a vector). SI unit m/s.]] of the electrons) and *d* are [[constant|A value that does not change. For example the acceleration due to gravity *g* or the speed of light *c*.]], the
Hall voltage *U* depends directly on the magnetic flux density *B*, which can then be
determined.

So the magnetometer calculates the magnetic flux density with the
formula

$$
B = \frac{U}{v \cdot d}
$$

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Hall effect"
- The fact that a current-carrying conductor gets a voltage **perpendicular to the
  direction of the current** when it lies in a magnetic field.
- The voltage is called the **Hall voltage**.
:::

::: sampunkt "What happens in the plate"
- The current means electrons in motion, which are affected by a **magnetic
  force** to the side.
- One edge becomes negative, the other positive, and an **electric force**
  builds up in the opposite direction.
- When $F_m = F_e$ there is equilibrium and the voltage is stable.
:::

::: sampunkt "The formula"
- $U = v \cdot d \cdot B$
- $d$ is the width of the plate and $v$ the drift velocity of the charges.
- Derived from $q v B = q \mathbb{E}$ with
  $\mathbb{E} = \dfrac{U}{d}$.
:::

::: sampunkt "Use"
- A **magnetometer** or **Hall effect sensor** measures magnetic
  flux density.
- The current $I$ and the width $d$ are known, so the measured voltage gives
  $B$ directly.
- The same principle is used in contactless position sensors, for example in
  the crankshaft sensors of cars.
:::
:::
