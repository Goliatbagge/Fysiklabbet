# Solids of revolution

Imagine you have a lathe. You fix a piece of wood and let it spin
quickly around its own axis while you shape it. The result is an
object that is completely symmetric and round, like a chess piece, a vase or a
chair leg. All of these are examples of solids of revolution.

A three-dimensional figure created by rotating a two-dimensional curve
or region around an axis of rotation (usually the $x$- or $y$-axis) is called
a **solid of revolution**. One method of finding the volume of these solids is
to use integrals to add up infinitely thin slices, which is called
**the disc method**.

## Derivation: the disc method for rotation about the *x*-axis

We start from a function $y = f(x)$ and the region between the graph and the $x$-axis in
an interval $a \leq x \leq b$. When we rotate that region one full turn around
the $x$-axis, it sweeps out a solid of revolution.

We divide the solid into discs. Each disc can be seen as a cylinder with radius
$r = y$ and height $h = \Delta x$. The volume of one disc is then the base area
(the circle) times the height:

$$
V_\text{disc} = \pi r^2 h = \pi\cdot y^2\cdot \Delta x
$$

The volume of the whole solid $V$ is the sum of the volumes of all these discs:

$$
V = \sum_{x=a}^{b} \pi\cdot y^2\cdot \Delta x
$$

To get the exact volume we let the height $\Delta x$ of each disc become
infinitely small ($dx$) and add up an infinite number of discs with an integral.
Then $V_\text{disc} = \pi\cdot y^2\, dx$ and the sum becomes:

$$
V = \int_a^b \pi\cdot y^2\, dx = \pi\int_a^b y^2\, dx
$$

::: formel "Volume for rotation about the *x*-axis"
When a function $y = f(x)$ rotates around the $x$-axis in the interval
$a \leq x \leq b$, the volume $V$ is calculated with the formula
$$
V = \pi\int_a^b y^2\, dx
$$
:::

The answer is often given in volume units (v.u.). Sometimes you are not told the interval
(start and end). Then you usually have to work out where the curve crosses the $x$-axis
to find your limits of integration.

::: exempel "Example 1: Volume when the interval is not given"
**The region bounded by the curve $y = 3x - x^2$ and the $x$-axis rotates around
the $x$-axis. Calculate the volume of the solid of revolution.**

::: handskrift
typ: rotationsvolym
:::

::: textlosning
We start by finding the limits of integration. Since the region is bounded by
the $x$-axis, the zeros of the function (the intersections with the $x$-axis) give the limits:

$$
3x - x^2 = 0 \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad x(3 - x) = 0
$$

The zero product property gives $x_1 = 0$ and $x_2 = 3$, so the limits of integration are
$x = 0$ and $x = 3$. Substituting into $V = \pi\int_a^b y^2\, dx$ gives

$$
V = \pi\int_0^3 (3x - x^2)^2\, dx
$$

We use the square rule and then the fundamental theorem of calculus:

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= \pi\int_0^3 (9x^2 - 6x^3 + x^4)\, dx = \pi\left[\frac{9x^3}{3} - \frac{6x^4}{4} + \frac{x^5}{5}\right]_0^3\\[4pt]
&= \pi\left[3x^3 - \frac{3x^4}{2} + \frac{x^5}{5}\right]_0^3 = \pi\left(3\cdot 3^3 - \frac{3\cdot 3^4}{2} + \frac{3^5}{5}\right)\\[4pt]
&= \pi\left(81 - \frac{243}{2} + \frac{243}{5}\right) = \pi\cdot \frac{810 - 1\,215 + 486}{10} = \pi\cdot \frac{81}{10} = 8.1\pi
\end{aligned}
$$

**Answer:** $8.1\pi \approx 25.4$ v.u.
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 2: Rotating the square root curve"
**The region between the curve $y = \sqrt{x}$ and the $x$-axis, from $x = 0$ to
$x = 4$, rotates around the $x$-axis. Calculate the volume of the solid of revolution.**

::: handskrift
typ: rotationrot
:::

::: textlosning
Here the interval is given, $a = 0$ and $b = 4$. Since $y^2 = (\sqrt{x})^2 = x$,
the integral becomes simple:

$$
V = \pi\int_0^4 (\sqrt{x})^2\, dx = \pi\int_0^4 x\, dx = \pi\left[\frac{x^2}{2}\right]_0^4 = \pi\cdot \frac{16}{2} = 8\pi
$$

**Answer:** $8\pi \approx 25.1$ v.u.
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 3: A cone"
**The region between the line $y = x$, the $x$-axis and the line $x = 3$ rotates around
the $x$-axis. Calculate the volume of the solid of revolution.**

::: handskrift
typ: rotationkon
:::

::: textlosning
The solid of revolution is a cone with base radius $r = 3$ and height $h = 3$.
With $y^2 = x^2$ we get

$$
V = \pi\int_0^3 x^2\, dx = \pi\left[\frac{x^3}{3}\right]_0^3 = \pi\cdot \frac{27}{3} = 9\pi
$$

Check with the known volume of a cone, $V = \dfrac{\pi r^2 h}{3} = \dfrac{\pi\cdot 3^2\cdot 3}{3} = 9\pi$: the same answer.

**Answer:** $9\pi \approx 28.3$ v.u.
:::
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Solid of revolution"
- A **solid of revolution** is formed when a region rotates one full turn about an axis.
- The volume is calculated with **the disc method**: the solid is divided into infinitely thin discs.
- Each disc is a cylinder with volume $\pi r^2 h$.
:::

::: sampunkt "The formula"
- $V = \pi \int_a^b y^2\,dx$ for rotation about the $x$-axis.
- The radius is the $y$-value of the curve and the thickness is $dx$.
- The answer is given in volume units, v.u.
:::

::: sampunkt "Method"
1. Find the limits of integration. If they are missing: find the **zeros** of the curve.
2. Square the function expression, using the square rule if needed.
3. Integrate term by term and substitute the limits.
- It is a good idea to keep $\pi$ outside the integral the whole way.
:::

::: sampunkt "Simplifications"
- $(\sqrt{x})^2 = x$, which makes the integral much simpler.
- Check the answer against a known solid when possible: the line $y = x$ gives a cone.
- Square the whole expression, not term by term.
:::

:::
