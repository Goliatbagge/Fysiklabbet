# Summary: Forces

This chapter was about what makes things move or stay still:
Newton's three laws, the gravitational force and the normal force, gravitation between masses,
friction, how the gravitational force is split into components on an inclined plane, and
forces that turn things, moments. After the chapter you should be able to draw correct
force diagrams, calculate with Newton's second law, decide the sign of the acceleration in
applications such as inclined planes and pulleys, and solve for an unknown force with
the principle of moments.

## Concepts to know

- **force**: an influence that can change an object's motion or shape; SI unit
  N; written *F* and is a vector quantity.
- **resultant force ($F_R$)**: the vector sum of all forces acting on
  an object; it is zero when the forces are in equilibrium.
- **component**: part of a force split in a given direction, for example along
  and perpendicular to an inclined plane.
- **Newton's first law (the law of inertia)**: if $F_R = 0$ the object stays at
  rest or moves at constant velocity.
- **inertia**: an object's resistance to a change in its motion; larger
  mass gives larger inertia.
- **Newton's second law (the law of acceleration)**: the relationship $F_R = m \cdot a$
  between resultant force, mass and acceleration.
- **Newton's third law**: for every force on an object (action) there is an
  equal and opposite force of the same kind on another object (reaction).
- **balancing forces**: forces that act on the same object and keep it in
  equilibrium; they differ from reaction forces, which always act on two different
  objects.
- **gravitational force ($F_G$) and gravitational field strength ($g$)**: the gravitational force is given by
  $F_G = m \cdot g$; the gravitational field strength is 9.82 N/kg in Sweden.
- **normal force ($F_N$)**: the contact force from a surface on an object,
  always directed perpendicular to the surface.
- **coefficient of friction ($\mu$)**: the fraction of the normal force that becomes
  frictional force; it has no unit.
- **tension ($F_S$)**: the force in a taut rope or line; the same
  throughout the string around a frictionless pulley.
- **pivot**: the axis or point an object turns around.
- **lever arm ($l$)**: the perpendicular (shortest) distance between the force's
  line of action and the pivot.
- **moment ($M$)**: a force's turning effect, $M = F \cdot l$; SI unit
  Nm.
- **rotational equilibrium**: the anticlockwise moment is as large as the clockwise
  moment, so that the object does not turn; the pivot can then be chosen
  freely.

## Formulas

::: formel "The formulas of the chapter"
**Newton's second law**

$$
F_R = m \cdot a
$$

where

- $F_R$ = resultant force (N)
- *m* = mass (kg)
- *a* = acceleration (m/s²)

**Newton's third law**

$$
\vec{F}_{A \text{ on } B} = -\vec{F}_{B \text{ on } A}
$$

**Gravitational force**

$$
F_G = m \cdot g
$$

where

- $F_G$ = gravitational force (N)
- *m* = mass (kg)
- *g* = gravitational field strength (9.82 N/kg in Sweden)

**Newton's law of gravitation**

$$
F_G = G \cdot \frac{m_1 \cdot m_2}{r^{2}}
$$

where

- $F_G$ = gravitational force (N)
- *G* = the gravitational constant ($6.67 \cdot 10^{-11}\ \mathrm{Nm^2/kg^2}$)
- *m*₁ = mass of object 1 (kg)
- *m*₂ = mass of object 2 (kg)
- *r* = the distance between the centres of mass of the objects (m)

**Frictional force**

$$
F_f = \mu \cdot F_N
$$

where

- $F_f$ = frictional force (N)
- *μ* = coefficient of friction (no unit)
- $F_N$ = normal force (N)

**Inclined planes: components of the gravitational force**

$$
F_1 = m \cdot g \cdot \sin \alpha
\qquad\qquad
F_2 = m \cdot g \cdot \cos \alpha
$$

where

- *F*₁ = component of the gravitational force down the plane (N)
- *F*₂ = component of the gravitational force perpendicular to the plane (N)
- *m* = mass of the object (kg)
- *g* = gravitational field strength (N/kg)
- *α* = angle of inclination of the plane to the horizontal

**Trigonometry in a right-angled triangle**

$$
\sin v = \frac{a}{c}, \qquad \cos v = \frac{b}{c}, \qquad \tan v = \frac{a}{b}
$$

where *a* is the opposite side, *b* is the adjacent side and *c* is the hypotenuse
for the angle *v*.

**Moment**

$$
M = F \cdot l
$$

where

- *M* = moment (Nm)
- *F* = force (N)
- *l* = lever arm (m)

**The principle of moments (rotational equilibrium)**

$$
M_1 = M_2
$$

where

- $M_1$ = the anticlockwise moment or moments
- $M_2$ = the clockwise moment or moments
:::

## Important relationships and methods

- If the resultant force is zero ($F_R = 0$) the object is either at rest
  or moving at constant velocity (Newton's first law). Otherwise
  it accelerates according to $F_R = m \cdot a$.
- Distinguish between **balancing forces** (act on the same object, can be different
  types of force, add up to zero) and **reaction forces** (act on two
  different objects, always the same type of force, according to Newton's third law).
  The reaction force to a gravitational force is always another gravitational force, never a
  normal force.
- The normal force $F_N$ is always perpendicular to the contact surface: straight up on a
  horizontal table, but slanting out from an inclined plane or horizontally out from a
  wall.
- On an inclined plane: at rest or at constant velocity $F_N = F_2$ and
  $F_f = F_1$; if the object accelerates, instead $F_R = F_1 - F_f$ and
  $a = F_R/m$.
- The angle between the gravitational force and its component perpendicular to the plane is
  always equal to the angle of inclination of the plane. This follows from the fact that the
  angles in a triangle add up to 180°.
- For a block pulled with a spring balance: at rest and at constant velocity
  $F_f = F_\text{drag}$; if the block accelerates, $F_f < F_\text{drag}$.
- In a pulley system: first calculate for **the whole system** to find the
  common acceleration ($F_R$ = the difference between the gravitational forces,
  *m* = the total mass), then calculate for **a single body** to find
  the tension $F_S$.
- The tension in a string is the same throughout the string (even around
  frictionless pulleys). In a tug of war it is the friction against the ground that decides, not
  different forces in the rope itself.
- The sign of the acceleration is decided by how the **velocity** (with its sign, as a
  vector) changes, not by how the speed changes. A lift slowing down on the way
  down has a velocity that becomes less negative, that is it increases, so
  the acceleration is positive even though the speed decreases.
- In rotational equilibrium the pivot can be chosen freely. A force whose
  line of action passes through the chosen pivot has lever arm 0 and therefore
  gives no moment. So choose the pivot where an unknown force
  acts, and it disappears from the equation.
- The closer to the centre of gravity a support is, the larger the share of the weight it
  carries.
- An object topples when the vertical line through its centre of gravity falls outside
  the base of support (the extension section Stability).

## Before the test

- Can you explain Newton's three laws in your own words and give your own example of
  each one?
- Can you distinguish between balancing forces and reaction forces, and explain why
  the reaction force to a gravitational force is never a normal force?
- Do you know the formula for the gravitational force ($F_G = m \cdot g$) and do you know the difference
  between mass and weight?
- Do you know that the normal force is always perpendicular to the contact surface, whether
  the surface is horizontal, inclined or vertical?
- Can you set up and calculate with Newton's law of gravitation and explain
  why the gravitational field strength on Earth is exactly 9.82 N/kg?
- Do you know the formula for frictional force ($F_f = \mu \cdot F_N$) and can you decide whether
  an object slides or stays still?
- Can you split the gravitational force into components on an inclined plane and calculate
  the normal force, frictional force and acceleration?
- Do you know how to decide the sign of the acceleration from how the velocity
  (not the speed) changes, for example in a lift or throwing example?
- Can you calculate the acceleration and tension in a pulley system by
  first looking at the whole system and then at a single body?
- Can you define pivot and lever arm, and calculate moments
  with $M = F \cdot l$?
- Can you choose the pivot freely in rotational equilibrium and solve for an unknown
  force with the principle of moments $M_1 = M_2$?
- Can you draw a correct force diagram with the correct points of application for $F_G$,
  $F_N$, $F_f$ and $F_S$?
