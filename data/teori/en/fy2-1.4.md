# Period, frequency, radians and angular velocity

## Period and frequency

A motion that repeats itself, for example a rotation or the motion of a pendulum,
is called **periodic**. The time for the motion to repeat itself, for example the time for
one revolution or the time for one oscillation, is called the **[[period|The time for one complete oscillation or one complete wave: *T* = 1 / *f*.]]**, is denoted *T* and
has the [[SI unit|The International System of Units, with seven base units (m, kg, s, A, K, mol, cd).]] s (second). The number of repetitions (revolutions or oscillations) that
take place each second is called the **[[frequency|The number of oscillations per second. SI unit hertz (Hz) = 1/s.]]**, is denoted *f* and has the SI unit Hz
(hertz).

If a circular motion has the frequency 10 Hz, it therefore rotates 10 revolutions in one
second. The time for one revolution, that is, the period, is then 1/10 of a second.

::: formel "Period and frequency"
$$
T = \frac{1}{f} \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad f = \frac{1}{T}
$$

where

- *T* = period (s)
- *f* = frequency (Hz or s⁻¹)
:::

## Radians

In a circle, every angle at the centre gives rise to an arc. We let
**the length of the arc** become a measure of the angle instead. We call this angular measure
**radians**. If an angle forms an arc that is as long
as the radius of the circle, the angle is 1 rad (radian).

Since the circumference of a circle is $2\pi \cdot r$, the angle for
a full revolution (360°) is $2\pi$ rad. Usually the unit for
radians is not written out; we simply write $360^{\circ} = 2\pi$.

::: formel "Degrees and radians: examples"
$$
360^{\circ} = 2\pi \qquad 180^{\circ} = \pi \qquad 90^{\circ} = \frac{\pi}{2} \qquad 1^{\circ} = \frac{\pi}{180}
$$

$$
1\ \mathrm{rad} = \frac{180^{\circ}}{\pi} \approx 57.3^{\circ}
$$
:::

## Angular velocity

How far an object moves (the **distance**) per unit time is called [[velocity|Change of position per unit time, with direction (a vector). SI unit m/s.]]. How large an **angle** an object turns
through per unit time is called **angular velocity**, is denoted ω (small omega) and
has the SI unit rad/s (radians per second).

::: formel "Angular velocity"
$$
\omega = \frac{2\pi}{T} = 2\pi \cdot f
$$

where

- *ω* = angular velocity (rad/s)
- *T* = period (s)
- *f* = frequency (Hz)

::: härledning "Derivation: Angular velocity"
If an object turns through the angle α in the time *t* seconds, it has the angular velocity

$$
\omega = \frac{\alpha}{t} \qquad (1)
$$

If a full revolution is completed, the angle of rotation is $\alpha = 2\pi$ rad
and the time is $t = T$ (one period). Substituting these expressions into (1) gives us

$$
\omega = \frac{2\pi}{T} \qquad (2)
$$

We also know from before that $f = \dfrac{1}{T}$. Substituting into (2) gives

$$
\omega = \frac{2\pi}{T} = 2\pi \cdot \frac{1}{T} = 2\pi \cdot f
$$

Q.E.D.
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 1: Merry-go-round"
**A merry-go-round rotates one revolution in 10 seconds. Find the angular velocity in<br>a) degrees/s&emsp;&emsp;b) rad/s.**

::: handskrift
typ: karusell
:::

::: textlosning
**a)** The angular velocity in degrees per second.

$$
\omega = \frac{\alpha}{t} = \frac{360^{\circ}}{10} = 36^{\circ}/\mathrm{s}
$$

**Answer:** 36°/s

**b)** Remember: $360^{\circ} = 2\pi$ rad. The angular velocity of the merry-go-round in
radians is then

$$
\omega = \frac{\alpha}{t} = \frac{2\pi}{10}\ \mathrm{rad/s} = \frac{\pi}{5}\ \mathrm{rad/s} = 0.628\ldots\ \mathrm{rad/s} \approx 0.63\ \mathrm{rad/s}
$$

**Answer:** 0.63 rad/s
:::
:::

## Speed in a circular path

Remember that **velocity** is a [[vector|A quantity with both magnitude and direction, for example velocity, force or acceleration. Differs from a scalar, which has only magnitude.]] with magnitude and direction, while
**[[speed|The magnitude of velocity, without direction (a scalar). Measured in m/s or km/h.]]** is a [[scalar|A quantity with only magnitude, without direction. For example time, mass, energy, temperature.]] with only magnitude. If an object moves in a circular path,
the **speed** can therefore be [[constant|A value that does not change. For example the acceleration due to gravity *g* or the speed of light *c*.]], but the **velocity** is not,
since the direction changes all the time.

::: formel "Speed in a circular path"
$$
v = \omega \cdot r = \frac{2\pi \cdot r}{T}
$$

where

- *v* = speed in the direction of motion (m/s)
- *ω* = angular velocity (rad/s)
- *r* = radius of the path (m)
- *T* = period (s)

:::

::: härledning "Derivation: Speed in a circular path"
The speed in the direction of motion is found with the formula

$$
v = \frac{s}{t} \qquad (1)
$$

If we look at one revolution, the [[distance|The total length of path an object has moved. A scalar quantity, measured in metres.]] is the same as the circumference of the circle,
that is, $s = 2\pi \cdot r$. The time to complete one revolution is one period *T*,
so the time for one revolution is $t = T$. Substituting into (1) gives

$$
v = \frac{2\pi \cdot r}{T} \qquad (2)
$$

We know from before that the angular velocity is $\omega = \dfrac{2\pi}{T}$,
which substituted into (2) gives

$$
v = \frac{2\pi \cdot r}{T} = \frac{2\pi}{T} \cdot r = \omega \cdot r
$$

Q.E.D.
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 2: LP record"
**An LP record has a diameter of 30 cm and spins at 33 revolutions/minute. What speed does
an eraser get if it is placed 5.0 cm from the outer edge of the record?**

::: figur
<svg viewBox="52 22 210 190" width="266" height="241" xmlns="http://www.w3.org/2000/svg" font-family="Poppins, system-ui, sans-serif" role="img" aria-label="An LP record seen from above with radius 15 cm. An eraser sits 5.0 cm from the outer edge, that is, at the path radius 10 cm from the centre."><circle cx="150" cy="116" r="88" fill="#f4efe4" stroke="#1f2530" stroke-width="1.6"/><circle cx="150" cy="116" r="2.6" fill="#1f2530"/><line x1="150" y1="116" x2="91.1" y2="50.6" stroke="#1f2530" stroke-width="1.5"/><text x="106" y="99" text-anchor="middle" font-size="15" fill="#1f2530">15 cm</text><line x1="150" y1="116" x2="208.7" y2="116" stroke="#2563c9" stroke-width="1.5" stroke-dasharray="5 4"/><text x="179.3" y="108" text-anchor="middle" font-size="15" fill="#2563c9">10 cm</text><line x1="208.7" y1="116" x2="238" y2="116" stroke="#d13b2e" stroke-width="1.5" stroke-dasharray="5 4"/><rect x="200.7" y="109" width="16" height="14" rx="2" fill="#e17a6a" stroke="#b04030" stroke-width="1.3"/><text x="221" y="143" text-anchor="middle" font-size="15" fill="#d13b2e"5.0 cm</text></svg>
:::

::: handskrift
typ: lpskiva
:::

::: textlosning
We set up the formula for speed.

$$
v = \omega \cdot r \qquad (1)
$$

First we find the angular velocity.

$$
\omega = 2\pi \cdot f \qquad (2)
$$

The frequency in Hz:

$$
f = \frac{33\ \mathrm{rev}}{1\ \mathrm{min}} = \frac{33\ \mathrm{rev}}{60\ \mathrm{s}} = 0.55\ \mathrm{rev/s} = 0.55\ \mathrm{Hz}
$$

Substituting $f = 0.55$ Hz into (2) gives

$$
\omega = 2\pi \cdot 0.55 = 1.1\pi\ \mathrm{rad/s}
$$

Then we find the eraser's path radius *r*. The radius of the LP record is
$\dfrac{30\,\mathrm{cm}}{2} = 15$ cm. If the eraser is 5.0 cm from
the edge, its path radius is $(15 - 5.0)\ \mathrm{cm} = 10\ \mathrm{cm} = 0.10\ \mathrm{m}$.

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
\omega = 1.1\pi\ \mathrm{rad/s} \\
r = 0.10\ \mathrm{m}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
v = \omega \cdot r = 1.1\pi \cdot 0.10 = 0.345\ldots\ \mathrm{m/s} \approx 0.35\ \mathrm{m/s}
$$

**Answer:** 0.35 m/s
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Period and frequency"
- A motion that repeats itself is called **periodic**.
- The **period** $T$ is the time for one repetition, that is, one revolution or one
  oscillation. The SI unit is s.
- The **frequency** $f$ is the number of repetitions per second. The SI unit is Hz.
- $T = \dfrac{1}{f}$ and $f = \dfrac{1}{T}$.
:::

::: sampunkt "Radians"
- The angle is measured as **the length of the arc**, in units of the radius.
- An arc as long as the radius corresponds to 1 rad.
- $360^\circ = 2\pi$ and $180^\circ = \pi$.
- $1\ \mathrm{rad} = \dfrac{180^\circ}{\pi} \approx 57.3^\circ$.
:::

::: sampunkt "Angular velocity"
- How large an **angle** an object turns through per unit time. Denoted $\omega$
  with the unit rad/s.
- $\omega = \dfrac{2\pi}{T} = 2\pi f$
- Follows from a full revolution being $2\pi$ rad and taking the time $T$.
:::

::: sampunkt "Speed in a circular path"
- $v = \omega \cdot r = \dfrac{2\pi r}{T}$
- Follows from $v = \dfrac{s}{t}$ with the circumference $2\pi r$ in the time $T$.
- The speed therefore depends on the **radius**: further out on the merry-go-round you move
  faster, despite the same angular velocity.
:::

::: sampunkt "Speed or velocity"
- In a circular path the **speed** can be constant.
- The **velocity** never is, since the direction changes all the time.
:::
:::
