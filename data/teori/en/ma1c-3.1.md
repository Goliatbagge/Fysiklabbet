# Per cent, per mille and ppm

Hundredths are called **per cent**. Thousandths are called **per mille**. Millionths
are called **ppm** (parts per million). These are used to state proportions.

::: formel "Per cent, per mille and ppm"
| "Percentage form" | Fraction form | Decimal form |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 % | $\dfrac{1}{100}$ | 0.01 |
| 1 ‰ | $\dfrac{1}{1\,000}$ | 0.001 |
| 1 ppm | $\dfrac{1}{1\,000\,000}$ | 0.000 001 |
:::

In calculations, per mille and ppm are used in the same way as per cent.

::: formel "Calculating a proportion"
$$
\text{proportion} = \frac{\text{part}}{\text{whole}}
$$

**NOTE!** The proportion is obtained in fraction form or decimal form.
:::

To determine the part or the whole, we solve for it in the formula
above.

::: exempel "Example 1: Find the proportion"
**How many per mille is 120 g of 15 kg?**

::: handskrift
typ: andelpromille
:::

::: textlosning
We are asked for the *proportion*. To get the correct proportion we need
the same unit for the part and the whole. We convert, for example, 15 kg to grams and
get $15\ \mathrm{kg} = 15\,000\ \mathrm{g}$. So the part = 120 g and
the whole = 15 000 g. We substitute into the formula for the proportion and get

$$
\text{proportion} = \frac{120}{15\,000} = \frac{1}{125} = 0.008
$$

To convert from decimal form to per mille we multiply by
1 000 (‰ = *thousand*ths):

$$
0.008 = 0.008 \cdot 1\,000\ \text{‰} = 8\ \text{‰}
$$

**Answer:** 8 ‰
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 2: Find the part"
**What is 3.5 ‰ of 32 000 kr?**

::: handskrift
typ: andeldelen
:::

::: textlosning
We are asked for the *part*. We solve for the part in the formula for the proportion and
get

$$
\text{proportion} = \frac{\text{part}}{\text{whole}}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\text{part} = \text{proportion} \cdot \text{whole} \quad (1)
$$

Remember that the proportion must be given in decimal or fraction form:

$$
\text{proportion} = 3.5\ \text{‰} = \frac{3.5}{1\,000} = 0.0035
$$

Substituting proportion = 0.0035 and whole = 32 000 kr into formula (1) above gives

$$
\text{part} = 0.0035 \cdot 32\,000\ \mathrm{kr} = 112\ \mathrm{kr}
$$

**Answer:** 112 kr
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 3: Find the whole"
**The concentration of a certain substance in seawater is 5 ppm. How much
water is needed to collect 30 grams of the substance?**

::: handskrift
typ: andelhela
:::

::: textlosning
We are asked for the *whole*. We solve for the whole in the formula for the proportion
and get

$$
\text{proportion} = \frac{\text{part}}{\text{whole}}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\text{whole} = \frac{\text{part}}{\text{proportion}}
$$

Substituting the part = 30 grams and the proportion
$= 5\ \text{ppm} = \dfrac{5}{1\,000\,000} = 0.000\,005$ gives

$$
\text{whole} = \frac{30\ \text{grams}}{0.000\,005}
= 6\,000\,000\ \text{grams} = 6\,000\ \mathrm{kg} = 6\ \text{tonnes}
$$

**Answer:** 6 tonnes
:::
:::

::: härledning "Tool: The percentage triangle"
Some people find the percentage triangle a good help in percentage calculations. It
also works excellently for per mille and ppm. Cover the quantity you are looking for,
and the triangle shows the formula: if the remaining quantities are next to
each other they are to be multiplied, if they are above each other they are to be divided.

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Three ways of stating proportions"
- **Per cent**: hundredths. $1\ \% = \dfrac{1}{100} = 0.01$.
- **Per mille**: thousandths.
  $1\ ‰ = \dfrac{1}{1\,000} = 0.001$.
- **ppm**: millionths, "parts per million".
  $\dfrac{1}{1\,000\,000}$.
- All three are calculated in **exactly the same way**.
:::

::: sampunkt "The basic formula"
- $\text{proportion} = \dfrac{\text{part}}{\text{whole}}$
- The proportion is always calculated in **fraction form or decimal form** and converted
  to per cent at the end.
:::

::: sampunkt "The three types of question"
- The **proportion** is sought: divide the part by the whole.
- The **part** is sought:
  $\text{part} = \text{proportion} \cdot \text{whole}$.
- The **whole** is sought:
  $\text{whole} = \dfrac{\text{part}}{\text{proportion}}$.
:::

::: sampunkt "The units must be the same"
- Convert to the same unit before you divide.
- $120\ \mathrm{g}$ of $15\ \mathrm{kg}$ becomes $\dfrac{120}{15\,000}$, not
  $\dfrac{120}{15}$.
:::

::: sampunkt "The percentage triangle"
- Cover the quantity you are looking for.
- If the remaining ones are **next to** each other: multiply.
- If they are **above** each other: divide.
:::
:::
