# Density

Which weighs most: 1 kg of lead or 1 kg of cotton wool? The answer, of course, is that they weigh the same.
However, a much larger volume of cotton wool is needed compared with lead. How tightly
packed a material is, is called its **[[density|Mass per unit volume: *ρ* = *m* / *V*. SI unit kg/m³. Decides whether an object floats in a medium.]]**, written *ρ* (small "rho"), and its
[[SI unit|The International System of Units, with seven base units (m, kg, s, A, K, mol, cd).]] is kg/m³.

::: demo "Feel the density"
We pass round cubes of the same size made of aluminium, copper, lead and iron. Based on what
you already know, which substance do you think each cube is made of?

The lead cube weighs the most, so it has the highest density, and it is quite soft. Aluminium
is a light, pale metal. It weighs the least. Copper is recognised by its reddish-brown
colour. Iron smells of blood! (Or really, it is blood that smells of iron, since
it contains exactly that.)
:::

::: formel "Density"
$$
\rho = \frac{m}{V}
$$

where

- *ρ* = density (kg/m³)
- *m* = mass (kg)
- *V* = volume (m³)
:::

## Converting units: kg/m³ and g/cm³

::: formel "Converting units: kg/m³ and g/cm³"
The conversion factor between g/cm³ and kg/m³ is 1 000, so

$$
1\ \mathrm{g/cm^3} = 1\,000\ \mathrm{kg/m^3}
$$

$$
1\ \mathrm{kg/m^3} = \frac{1}{1\,000}\ \mathrm{g/cm^3} = 0.001\ \mathrm{g/cm^3}
$$

::: härledning "Derivation"
Another common unit for density is g/cm³, so it can be useful to be able to
switch quickly between them. We do a unit analysis.

$$
1\ \mathrm{kg/m^3} \;=\; \frac{1\ \mathrm{kg}}{1\ \mathrm{m^3}}
\;=\; \frac{1\,000\ \mathrm{g}}{1\,000\,000\ \mathrm{cm^3}}
\;=\; \frac{1}{1\,000}\ \mathrm{g/cm^3}
$$

This means that if we want to switch from kg/m³ to g/cm³ we **divide**
by 1 000. Conversely, if we want to switch from g/cm³ to kg/m³ we
**multiply** by 1 000. The conversion factor is 1 000.
:::
:::

::: tips "The density of water"
The density of water is good to know by heart since we often calculate with it.
It is also easy to remember.

$$
\rho_\text{water} \approx 1\,000\ \mathrm{kg/m^3}
$$

which means that 1.0 litre of water weighs 1.0 kg.
:::

::: exempel "Example 1: Gold die (Foundation)"
**A cubic gold die has a side of 1.5 cm. How much does it weigh?**

::: handskrift
typ: guldtarning
:::

::: textlosning
We set up the formula for density and solve for the mass *m*.

$$
\rho = \frac{m}{V} \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad m = \rho \cdot V
$$

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
\rho = 19.3 \cdot 10^{3}\ \mathrm{kg/m^3} \quad (\text{from a table}) \\
V = 1.5\ \mathrm{cm} \cdot 1.5\ \mathrm{cm} \cdot 1.5\ \mathrm{cm}
   = 1.5^{3}\ \mathrm{cm^3} = 3.375\ \mathrm{cm^3}
   = 3.375 \cdot 10^{-6}\ \mathrm{m^3}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
m = 19.3 \cdot 10^{3} \cdot 3.375 \cdot 10^{-6}
= 0.0651\ldots\ \mathrm{kg} \approx 0.065\ \mathrm{kg} = 65\ \mathrm{g}
$$

**Answer:** 65 g
:::
:::

The highest density in the universe? **Neutron stars**, with a density of 4 · 10¹⁴ g/cm³.
The empty space in the atom is instead packed with neutrons that are (reluctantly)
pressed together by the strong gravity. One cubic centimetre therefore weighs
400 million tonnes (!). You can see how small such a tightly packed star becomes in
the simulation [The neutron star to scale](fysik2-neutronstjarna.html): the whole
star next to the Earth in 3D, at exactly the same scale.

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Density"
- How **tightly packed** a material is. Written $\rho$ (rho), with
  the SI unit kg/m³.
- $\rho = \dfrac{m}{V}$
- A kilogram of lead and a kilogram of cotton wool weigh the same. The difference is
  the volume.
:::

::: sampunkt "Solving for"
- Mass: $m = \rho \cdot V$
- Volume: $V = \dfrac{m}{\rho}$
:::

::: sampunkt "Converting units"
- The conversion factor is **1 000**.
- $1\ \mathrm{g/cm^3} = 1\,000\ \mathrm{kg/m^3}$
- From kg/m³ to g/cm³: divide by 1 000. The other way:
  multiply.
:::

::: sampunkt "The density of water, know it by heart"
- $\rho_\text{water} \approx 1\,000\ \mathrm{kg/m^3}$
- So one litre of water weighs one kilogram.
:::

::: sampunkt "Common trap"
- Convert the volume to **cubic metres** before substituting.
- $1\ \mathrm{cm^3} = 10^{-6}\ \mathrm{m^3}$, not $10^{-2}$.
:::

::: sampunkt "Extremes"
- Lowest density among solids: **aerogel**, 0.0011 g/cm³.
- Highest among the elements: **osmium**, 22 g/cm³. Gold is at 19.
- Highest in the universe: **neutron stars**, about
  $4 \cdot 10^{14}\ \mathrm{g/cm^3}$. One cubic centimetre weighs 400
  million tonnes.
:::
:::
