# Summary: Thermal physics

The chapter is about the difference between heat and temperature, how much
energy is needed to heat, cool, melt and vaporise substances, how
the body handles heat and cold, and the four laws of thermodynamics.
You should be able to calculate heating, cooling and phase changes, set up
energy balances and explain the results with the right concepts.

## Concepts to know

- **Internal energy**: the sum of the potential energy of the atoms (in chemical
  bonds) and their kinetic energy in a material.
- **Heat**: energy that spontaneously flows from a warmer system to
  a colder one. Measured in joules (J). Physically there is no such thing as cold.
- **Temperature**: a measure of the average kinetic energy of the atoms. SI unit
  kelvin (K).
- **Absolute zero**: the lowest possible temperature,
  $0\ \mathrm{K} = -273\ ^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$, where all thermal motion
  stops.
- **Conduction, radiation, flow**: the three ways heat is transferred:
  through contact between materials, by radiation (needs no medium) and
  by a "swirling" gas or liquid (convection).
- **Specific heat capacity** ($c$): the energy needed to raise the
  temperature of $1\ \mathrm{kg}$ of a substance by $1\ \mathrm{K}$. Unit
  $\mathrm{J/(kg \cdot K)}$, different for different substances and states.
- **Specific latent heat of fusion** ($l_s$): the energy needed to melt
  $1\ \mathrm{kg}$ of a substance. Unit $\mathrm{J/kg}$.
- **Specific latent heat of vaporisation** ($l_å$): the energy needed to
  vaporise $1\ \mathrm{kg}$ of a substance. Unit $\mathrm{J/kg}$.
- **Phases (states of matter)**: solid, liquid and gas, and the six
  changes between them: melting, freezing, vaporisation, condensation,
  sublimation and deposition.
- **Phase change**: takes place at constant temperature. Energy supplied or given off
  goes into breaking or forming bonds, not into changing the
  temperature.
- **Sweating and evaporation**: the body's way of cooling itself: evaporation
  requires energy, which is taken from the skin.
- **The four laws of thermodynamics**: fundamental principles about equilibrium (0),
  the conservation of energy (1), the direction of heat (2) and absolute zero (3).

## Formulas

### The temperature scale

::: formel "The kelvin scale"
$$
T_K = T_C + 273
$$

where

- $T_K$ = temperature (K)
- $T_C$ = temperature (°C)
:::

### Heating, cooling and energy balance

::: formel "Heating and cooling"
$$
E = c \cdot m \cdot \Delta T
$$

(written $Q = c \cdot m \cdot \Delta T$ in the formula booklet)

where

- *E*, *Q* = energy absorbed/given off (J)
- *c* = specific heat capacity (J/(kg · K)), different for different substances and states
- *m* = mass (kg)
- $\Delta T$ = temperature change (K or °C)
:::

::: formel "Energy given off and energy absorbed (without energy losses)"
$$
E_\text{avg} = E_\text{upp}
$$

where

- $E_\text{avg}$ = energy given off
- $E_\text{upp}$ = energy absorbed
:::

### Energy in phase changes

::: formel "Energy for melting and freezing"
$$
E = l_s \cdot m
$$

where

- *E* = melting energy (J)
- $l_s$ = specific latent heat of fusion (J/kg), looked up in a table
- *m* = mass (kg)
:::

::: formel "Energy for vaporisation/condensation"
$$
E = l_å \cdot m
$$

where

- *E* = vaporisation energy (J)
- $l_å$ = specific latent heat of vaporisation (J/kg), looked up in a table
- *m* = mass (kg)
:::

### The laws of thermodynamics

::: formel "The four laws of thermodynamics"
**0.** Two systems in contact with each other will exchange energy until they
are in equilibrium.

**1.** Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only converted.

**2.** Heat flows from hot to cold. Heat never flows from cold
to hot by itself.

**3.** All processes stop at absolute zero.
:::

## Important relationships and methods

- When substances are mixed or in contact without energy losses, the
  energy balance $E_\text{avg} = E_\text{upp}$ applies. Write down what gives off
  and what absorbs energy, and solve for the quantity asked for (for example
  the final temperature or the amount of melted ice).
- During a phase change the temperature is constant: the curve in a
  temperature-time graph becomes horizontal at the melting and boiling points.
- Tasks that combine heating **and** a phase change (for example ice that
  melts in hot water) are solved by splitting the energy into steps
  (heating + melting/vaporisation) and setting up an energy balance between
  all the steps.
- Specific heat capacity, latent heat of fusion and latent heat of vaporisation
  depend on the substance and are looked up in a table. They also differ between different
  states of the same substance (for example ice and liquid water).
- A material that feels colder than another does not have to have a lower
  temperature. It is often because the material conducts heat better
  (for example aluminium compared with plastic).
- The body regulates its temperature by sweating (evaporation cools) and
  by shivering (chemical energy becomes kinetic energy and then heat).
- Wind feels colder because warmed air close to the skin is replaced by colder
  air faster. The same principle means you are cooled by blowing hard on
  the skin, while you are warmed by blowing gently.

## Before the test

- Can you explain the difference between heat, temperature and internal energy?
- Can you convert between Celsius and kelvin?
- Can you name and explain the three ways heat is transferred (conduction,
  radiation, flow)?
- Can you calculate the energy used in heating or cooling with
  $E = c \cdot m \cdot \Delta T$?
- Can you set up an energy balance ($E_\text{avg} = E_\text{upp}$) to
  solve mixing and melting problems?
- Can you name all six phase changes and explain why the temperature
  does not change during a phase change?
- Can you calculate the energy in melting/freezing and vaporisation/condensation
  with $l_s$ and $l_å$ respectively?
- Can you explain how the body warms and cools itself (sweating, shivering,
  insulating clothes)?
- Can you give an account of the four laws of thermodynamics?
