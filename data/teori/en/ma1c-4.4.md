# From graph to equation

We can now state that every straight line can be described by an equation of
the form $y = kx + m$, where $k$ and $m$ are constants (numbers). This equation
is called **the equation of a straight line in *k*-form**.

We will now investigate how we can find the values of $k$ and $m$ from a
graph.

the graphs of $y = 2x - 1$, $y = 2x$ and $y = 2x + 1$ to see the

We see that the line
- $y = 2x - 1$ crosses the *y*-axis at $y = -1$
- $y = 2x$ crosses the *y*-axis at $y = 0$
- $y = 2x + 1$ crosses the *y*-axis at $y = 1$

The $m$-value therefore corresponds to the point where the line **crosses the *y*-axis**. The point where a line crosses
the *y*-axis is also called the ***y*-intercept**, and in linear models it corresponds to
the "starting value".

We see that the line
- $y = 2x + 1$ has slope **2** (it goes 2 steps *up* for every step to the right)
- $y = 3x + 1$ has slope **3** (it goes 3 steps *up* for every step to the right)
- $y = -2x + 1$ has slope **−2** (it goes 2 steps *down* for every step to the right)

The $k$-value therefore corresponds to the **slope** of the line. The slope of a line is
also called the **gradient**, and in linear models it corresponds to
the rate of change.

::: formel "The equation of a straight line (in k-form)"
All straight lines (except vertical ones) can be described by the equation

$$
y = kx + m
$$

where $k$ corresponds to the slope of the line and $m$ corresponds to where the line crosses
the *y*-axis.
:::

A positive $k$-value makes the line slope upwards (seen from left to
right) and a negative $k$-value makes the line slope downwards. The $k$-value
can be calculated with the "step method", where you draw a step between
any two points on the line and imagine that you are walking *to the right*.
The height of the step then corresponds to the change in the *y*-direction, which is written
$\Delta y$, and the length of the step corresponds to the change in the *x*-direction, which is
written $\Delta x$. The Greek letter $\Delta$ is pronounced "delta" and
means *change*. $\Delta y$ is therefore read "delta *y*" and means how
much *y* changes.

::: formel "Calculating the slope of a line (*k*-value)"
$$
k = \frac{\text{height of the step}}{\text{length of the step}}
= \frac{\text{change in the } y\text{-direction}}{\text{change in the } x\text{-direction}}
= \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}
$$

::: textlosning
To find the equation of the line we determine its $k$-value and
$m$-value and then substitute them into the equation of a straight line $y = kx + m$.

$k = 2$ (the line goes up two steps for every step we go to the right)

$m = 3$ (where the line crosses the *y*-axis)

Substituting $k = 2$ and $m = 3$ into the equation of a straight line gives
$y = 2x + 3$.

**Answer:** $y = 2x + 3$

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "The equation of a straight line"
- $y = kx + m$
- All straight lines except **vertical** ones can be written like this.
:::

::: sampunkt "The m-value"
- Where the line **crosses the $y$-axis**.
- $y = 2x - 1$ crosses at $-1$, $y = 2x$ at 0 and $y = 2x + 1$ at 1.
- Read it off directly in the graph where the line crosses the $y$-axis.

::: sampunkt "The k-value"
- The **slope** of the line, also called the **gradient**.
- Positive $k$: the line slopes upwards. Negative $k$: downwards.
- In a linear model $k$ is the **rate of change**.
:::

::: sampunkt "The step method"
- $k = \dfrac{\text{height of the step}}{\text{length of the step}}
  = \dfrac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}$
- Draw the step between two points on the line and always go **to the
  right**.
- If you go downwards, $\Delta y$ is negative.
:::

::: sampunkt "How to find the equation from a graph"
1. Read off $m$ where the line crosses the $y$-axis.
2. Calculate $k$ with a step between two clear points.
3. Substitute both into $y = kx + m$.
:::
