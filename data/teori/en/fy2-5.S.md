# Summary: Astronomy

This chapter put physics into a cosmic perspective: the structure and scale of the
universe, how astronomers measure distances to stars using
parallax, why the Moon has phases, why the sky is blue and sunsets
red, how black holes form and are detected, and how stars are born from
clouds of gas. After the chapter you should know the scales of the universe, be able to calculate with the
parallax formula, explain the phases of the Moon and Rayleigh scattering, and
describe escape velocity, the Schwarzschild radius and the stages of
star formation.

## Concepts to know

- **Light year**: the distance light travels in one year, $\approx 9.5 \cdot 10^{15}\ \mathrm{m}$; a measure of distance, not of time.
- **Astronomical unit (AU)**: the Earth's mean distance from the Sun,
  $\approx 1.50 \cdot 10^{11}\ \mathrm{m}$.
- **Parallax**: the apparent shift of a nearby object against
  the background when the observer moves.
- **Parsec (pc)**: the distance that gives a parallax of exactly 1 arcsecond with
  1 AU as the baseline; $1\ \mathrm{pc} \approx 3.26$ light years.
- **Synodic and sidereal month**: the synodic month (new moon to new moon) takes 29.5
  days; the sidereal month (one revolution measured against the fixed stars) takes 27.3 days.
- **Umbra and penumbra**: the umbra is the part
  of the shadow that no direct sunlight reaches (total eclipse), the penumbra
  the part reached by light from part of the solar disc (partial eclipse).
- **Angular diameter**: how wide a celestial body appears to be in the sky.
  The Sun and the Moon both measure about 0.5°, which is what makes
  total solar eclipses possible.
- **Nodes**: the two points where the Moon's orbit crosses the ecliptic. Eclipses
  can only occur when new moon or full moon falls near a node.
- **Rayleigh scattering**: scattering of light by air molecules, strongest for
  short wavelengths ($\sigma \propto 1/\lambda^4$); the cause of the blue sky
  and red sunsets.
- **Escape velocity**: the lowest speed an object must have to escape completely
  from the gravity of a celestial body.
- **Event horizon**: the boundary surface around a black hole; inside it
  nothing, not even light, can get out.
- **Schwarzschild radius**: the radius of the event horizon, the distance at which
  the escape velocity becomes equal to the speed of light.
- **Singularity**: the point at the centre of a black hole where, according to
  theory, the mass is compressed to infinite density.
- **Protostar**: a hot, collapsing clump of gas on its way to becoming a
  star, before fusion has ignited.
- **The main sequence**: the stable stage a star reaches when
  the radiation pressure from fusion exactly balances gravity
  (hydrostatic equilibrium).

## Formulae

::: formel "The chapter's formulae"
**Light year**

$$
1\ \text{light year} = c \cdot t \approx 9.5 \cdot 10^{15}\ \mathrm{m}
$$

where

- *c* = the speed of light, $3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\ \mathrm{m/s}$
- *t* = one year ≈ $3.16 \cdot 10^{7}\ \mathrm{s}$

**Astronomical unit**

$$
1\ \mathrm{AU} \approx 1.50 \cdot 10^{8}\ \mathrm{km} = 1.50 \cdot 10^{11}\ \mathrm{m}
$$

**Arcminutes and arcseconds**

$$
1^{\circ} = 60' = 60 \cdot 60'' = 3\,600''
$$

where an arcminute is written $'$ and an arcsecond is written $''$.

**The parallax method**

$$
r = \frac{1}{p}
$$

where

- *r* = distance to the star (pc)
- *p* = parallax angle (arcseconds, $''$)

**Parsecs to light years**

$$
1\ \mathrm{pc} \approx 3.26\ \text{light years}
$$

**Angular diameter**

$$
\alpha \approx \frac{2R}{d}
$$

where

- *α* = angular diameter in radians (valid for small angles)
- *R* = radius of the body (m)
- *d* = distance to the body (m)

**The wavelength dependence of Rayleigh scattering**

$$
\sigma \propto \frac{1}{\lambda^{4}}
$$

where

- *σ* = probability/intensity of scattering
- *λ* = wavelength of the light (m)

**Escape velocity**

$$
v_f = \sqrt{\frac{2GM}{r}}
$$

where

- $v_f$ = escape velocity (m/s)
- *G* = the gravitational constant, $6.67 \cdot 10^{-11}\ \mathrm{N \cdot m^2/kg^2}$
- *M* = mass of the celestial body (kg)
- *r* = distance from the centre (m)

**The Schwarzschild radius**

$$
r_s = \frac{2GM}{c^{2}}
$$

where

- $r_s$ = Schwarzschild radius (m)
- *G* = the gravitational constant, $6.67 \cdot 10^{-11}\ \mathrm{N \cdot m^2/kg^2}$
- *M* = mass of the black hole (kg)
- *c* = the speed of light, $3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\ \mathrm{m/s}$

**Hydrogen fusion (simplified)**

$$
4\ {}^{1}_{1}\mathrm{H} \longrightarrow {}^{4}_{2}\mathrm{He} + \text{energy}
$$

The mass difference between the four protons and the helium nucleus is released as
energy according to $E = mc^{2}$.
:::

## Important relationships and methods

- The universe is built up in levels with clear jumps in scale: the solar system
  (about 0.001 light years) → the Milky Way (about 100 000 light years) → the Local Group
  (about 10 million light years) → the Laniakea supercluster (about 500 million light years) →
  the observable universe (about 93 billion light years). Each step is thousands
  of times larger than the previous one.
- The finite speed of light means that we always see distant celestial bodies
  as they looked **in the past**: the light from a star 1 000 light years away shows
  how it looked 1 000 years ago.
- From the Earth's surface the parallax method only works out to about 100 light years
  (the atmosphere disturbs the measurement); the Gaia satellite manages tens of thousands of light years.
- In the northern hemisphere, a D-shaped light (right side lit) means that
  the Moon is waxing towards full moon, and a C-shaped light (left side
  lit) that it is waning towards new moon; the reverse holds in the southern hemisphere.
- Eclipses require the Sun, Earth and Moon to lie almost exactly in line;
  they do not happen every month because the Moon's orbit is tilted about 5° to
  the Earth's orbit around the Sun.
- A solar eclipse happens at **new moon** (the Moon's shadow falls on the Earth) and
  a lunar eclipse at **full moon** (the Moon enters the Earth's shadow). A
  solar eclipse is only visible from the narrow band of the umbra, a lunar eclipse
  from the whole night side of the Earth.
- The Moon glows copper red during a total lunar eclipse because red
  sunlight is refracted in the Earth's atmosphere and bent into the umbra, while the
  blue light is scattered away (Rayleigh scattering).
- Rayleigh scattering is strongest for short wavelengths ($\propto 1/\lambda^4$): blue light is scattered out of the sunbeam in all directions and gives a
  blue sky; near the horizon sunlight passes through up to about 38
  air masses, so that blue and green are also scattered away and only
  red light gets through.
- A black hole forms when a star heavier than about 8 solar masses dies in
  a supernova and the core collapses without anything being able to stop it;
  lighter stars become white dwarfs instead.
- A black hole is never seen directly but is detected indirectly: hot radiation
  from an accretion disc, the orbits of stars circling it, or
  gravitational waves (first detected by LIGO in 2015).
- Star formation is a tug of war between gravity (pulling together) and
  pressure (holding out); a collapsing gas is heated by its own released
  potential energy, just like the air in a bicycle pump.
- The heavier a star is, the shorter it lives: despite more fuel,
  it burns its hydrogen so much faster that it still runs out first.

## Before the test

- Can you describe the levels of the universe from the solar system to superclusters and give
  an approximate size for each level?
- Do you know why distances in space are given in light years, AU or parsecs
  instead of metres?
- Do you know the parallax method and the formula $r = 1/p$, and can you explain why it
  only works for relatively nearby stars?
- Can you convert between arcseconds, parsecs and light years?
- Can you explain why the Moon has phases, and the difference between a synodic
  and a sidereal month?
- Do you know when solar and lunar eclipses occur, and why they do not happen
  every month?
- Can you explain Rayleigh scattering and why it makes the sky blue and
  sunsets red?
- Do you know the formulae for escape velocity and the Schwarzschild radius, and can you
  explain how they are related?
- Can you describe how a black hole forms and how astronomers detect
  one that cannot be seen?
- Can you describe the stages of star formation, from a cloud of gas to a stable
  main-sequence star, and explain why heavy stars live for a shorter time?
