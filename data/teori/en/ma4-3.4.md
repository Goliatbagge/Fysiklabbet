# The area between two curves

In the previous section we calculated the area between a curve and the $x$-axis. Now we take
the next step and find the area of a region that lies **between two
curves**. The method is a review from Mathematics continuation level 1c.

Imagine two curves $y = f(x)$ and $y = g(x)$ where $f(x)$ lies above $g(x)$
in an interval. We get the area of the region between them by integrating the
**difference** between the upper and the lower function.

::: formel "Area between two curves"
$$
A = \int_a^b \big(f(x) - g(x)\big)\,dx
$$

where $f(x)$ is the **upper** function and $g(x)$ the **lower** one.

Often we cannot see directly between which $x$-values the region lies. Then we start
by **finding the points of intersection**: we set the function expressions equal and
solve the equation. The $x$-coordinates of the points of intersection become the
lower and upper limits of integration, $a$ and $b$.

::: exempel "Example 1: Area of the shaded region"
**Calculate the area of the shaded region bounded by the line $y = x + 1$ and the parabola $y = x^2 - 5$.**

::: handskrift
typ: areamellan
:::

::: textlosning
Since we cannot see between which $x$-values the area lies, we start by
calculating the points of intersection. We set the function expressions equal and solve
the equation:

$$
x^2 - 5 = x + 1
$$

We collect all terms on one side and solve with the $pq$ formula:

$$
x^2 - x - 6 = 0
$$

$$
x = \frac{1}{2} \pm \sqrt{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^2 + 6} = \frac{1}{2} \pm \sqrt{\frac{25}{4}} = \frac{1}{2} \pm \frac{5}{2}
$$

$$
x_1 = \frac{1}{2} - \frac{5}{2} = -2 \qquad x_2 = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{5}{2} = 3
$$

So the limits of integration are $x = -2$ and $x = 3$. In the interval the line
$y = x + 1$ is the upper function and the parabola $y = x^2 - 5$ the lower one, so

$$
A = \int_{-2}^{3} \big((x + 1) - (x^2 - 5)\big)\,dx = \int_{-2}^{3} (-x^2 + x + 6)\,dx
$$

We find the integral using antiderivatives:

$$
A = \left[\, -\frac{x^3}{3} + \frac{x^2}{2} + 6x \,\right]_{-2}^{3}
$$

$$
= \left(-\frac{27}{3} + \frac{9}{2} + 18\right) - \left(\frac{8}{3} + 2 - 12\right) = \frac{27}{2} - \left(-\frac{22}{3}\right) = \frac{81}{6} + \frac{44}{6} = \frac{125}{6}
$$

**Answer:** $A = \dfrac{125}{6}$ a.u. $\approx 20.8$ a.u.
:::
:::

Sometimes the region is bounded by **more than two** curves. Then the upper (or
lower) function changes along the way, and we have to split the area into several integrals,
one for each subinterval. It pays to **simplify the function expressions**
before integrating.

::: exempel "Example 2: Simplify the expressions before integrating"
**Calculate the area of the shaded region bounded by the lines $y = 2x$, $y = \dfrac{x}{2}$ and $y = 6 - x$.**

::: handskrift
typ: areatrelinjer
:::

::: textlosning
The lines intersect at $x = 0$, $x = 2$ and $x = 4$. In the interval $x = 0$
to $x = 2$, $y = 2x$ is the upper function and $y = \dfrac{x}{2}$ the lower function. In
the interval $x = 2$ to $x = 4$, $y = 6 - x$ is the upper function and
$y = \dfrac{x}{2}$ the lower function. So we set up the area as the sum of two
integrals:

$$
A = \int_{0}^{2} \left(2x - \frac{x}{2}\right) dx + \int_{2}^{4} \left(6 - x - \frac{x}{2}\right) dx
$$

We simplify the function expressions to make the integration easier:

$$
A = \int_{0}^{2} \frac{3x}{2}\,dx + \int_{2}^{4} \left(6 - \frac{3x}{2}\right) dx
$$

We calculate one integral at a time:

$$
\int_{0}^{2} \frac{3x}{2}\,dx = \left[\, \frac{3x^2}{4} \,\right]_{0}^{2} = \frac{3\cdot 2^2}{4} - \frac{3\cdot 0^2}{4} = \frac{12}{4} - 0 = 3
$$

$$
\int_{2}^{4} \left(6 - \frac{3x}{2}\right) dx = \left[\, 6x - \frac{3x^2}{4} \,\right]_{2}^{4} = (24 - 12) - (12 - 3) = 12 - 9 = 3
$$

To get the total area we add these, so $A = 3 + 3 = 6$ a.u.

**Answer:** $A = 6$ a.u.
:::
:::

If you have access to a digital tool, the area can be found directly: in
GeoGebra the command `IntegralBetween(upper function, lower function, start
x-value, end x-value)` gives the area between the curves, exactly in CAS mode and as an
approximate value in the standard view.

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "The formula"
- $A = \int_a^b \big(f(x) - g(x)\big)\,dx$, where $f(x)$ is the **upper** and $g(x)$ the **lower** function.
- Always put brackets around the lower function, so that all the signs change.
:::

::: sampunkt "Finding the limits"
- If the limits cannot be seen: find the **points of intersection**.
- Set the function expressions equal to each other and solve the equation.
- The $x$-coordinates of the points of intersection become $a$ and $b$.
:::

::: sampunkt "More than two curves"
- If the upper or lower function changes along the way, the area must be **split**.
- Set up one integral per subinterval and add them together.
- Simplify the function expressions before you integrate: $2x - \dfrac{x}{2} = \dfrac{3x}{2}$.
:::

::: sampunkt "Digital tool"
- In GeoGebra: `IntegralBetween(upper function, lower function, start x-value, end x-value)`.
- CAS mode gives an exact answer, the standard view an approximate value.
- Areas are given in area units, a.u.
:::
