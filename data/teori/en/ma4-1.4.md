# More about trigonometric equations

When we solve a trigonometric equation we get infinitely many solutions,
which we write with a **period term** ($+\, n \cdot 360^\circ$ for sine and
cosine, $+\, n \cdot 180^\circ$ for tangent, where $n$ is an integer). Often
we only want the solutions that lie in a certain **interval**. Then we
first find all solutions and then test which integers $n$ give
solutions within the interval.

::: formel "Solving trigonometric equations in an interval"
1. Give all solutions to the equation with a period term (as usual).
2. Find the values of $n$ that give solutions in the interval and state
   the solutions.
:::

Two tricks are particularly useful when the equation does not have the standard form
$\sin x = a$ directly.

::: formel "Tips"
- The **zero product method** also applies to trigonometric equations: a
  product is zero if (at least) one of the factors is zero.
- In some equations we can use the fact that
  $\dfrac{\sin x}{\cos x} = \tan x$ to eliminate a term with $x$.
:::

::: exempel "Example 1: Solutions in an interval"
**Solve the equation $\sin(3x + 30^\circ) = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ in the interval $180^\circ \le x < 450^\circ$.**

::: handskrift
typ: trigintervall
:::

::: textlosning
We start by finding all solutions. Since
$\sin 60^\circ = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, the equation gives two cases.

**Case 1:**

$$
3x + 30^\circ = 60^\circ + n \cdot 360^\circ \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad 3x = 30^\circ + n \cdot 360^\circ \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad x = 10^\circ + n \cdot 120^\circ
$$

**Case 2:**

$$
3x + 30^\circ = 180^\circ - 60^\circ + n \cdot 360^\circ = 120^\circ + n \cdot 360^\circ
$$

$$
3x = 90^\circ + n \cdot 360^\circ \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad x = 30^\circ + n \cdot 120^\circ
$$

Now we test which integers $n$ give solutions in the interval
$180^\circ \le x < 450^\circ$.

In case 1 ($x = 10^\circ + n \cdot 120^\circ$), $n = 2$ gives the solution
$x = 250^\circ$ and $n = 3$ gives $x = 370^\circ$. In case 2
($x = 30^\circ + n \cdot 120^\circ$), $n = 2$ gives the solution $x = 270^\circ$
and $n = 3$ gives $x = 390^\circ$. Other integers ($n \le 1$ and $n \ge 4$) give
solutions outside the interval.

**Answer:** 250°, 270°, 370° and 390°
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 2: The zero product method"
**Solve the equation $\sin(2x) \cdot \cos(3x) = 0$.**

::: handskrift
typ: nollprodukttrig
:::

::: textlosning
The left-hand side is a product that must equal zero. According to the
zero product method, the product is zero if (at least) one of the factors is zero, so
we solve $\sin(2x) = 0$ and $\cos(3x) = 0$ separately.

$\sin(2x) = 0$ gives the two cases

$$
2x = 0^\circ + n \cdot 360^\circ \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad x = n \cdot 180^\circ
$$

$$
2x = 180^\circ + n \cdot 360^\circ \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad x = 90^\circ + n \cdot 180^\circ
$$

These two can be combined into $x = n \cdot 90^\circ$ (every other solution
$0^\circ, 90^\circ, 180^\circ, \ldots$).

$\cos(3x) = 0$ gives

$$
3x = \pm 90^\circ + n \cdot 360^\circ \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad x = \pm 30^\circ + n \cdot 120^\circ
$$

**Answer:** $x = n \cdot 90^\circ$ and $x = \pm 30^\circ + n \cdot 120^\circ$
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 3: Eliminate a term using tangent"
**Solve the equation $4\cos x - 5\sin x = 0$.**

::: handskrift
typ: tangenselim
:::

::: textlosning
We add $5\sin x$ to both sides and then divide both sides by
$\cos x$:

$$
4\cos x = 5\sin x \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad \frac{4\cos x}{\cos x} = \frac{5\sin x}{\cos x} \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad 4 = 5 \cdot \frac{\sin x}{\cos x}
$$

We use the fact that $\dfrac{\sin x}{\cos x} = \tan x$, which gives

$$
4 = 5\tan x \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad \tan x = \frac{4}{5} = 0.8
$$

Tangent has the period $180^\circ$, so we get all solutions with a single
period term:

$$
x = \tan^{-1}(0.8) = 38.659\ldots^\circ \approx 38.7^\circ + n \cdot 180^\circ
$$

**Answer:** $38.7^\circ + n \cdot 180^\circ$
:::
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Solutions in an interval"
1. Give all solutions with a period term as usual.
2. Test which integers $n$ give solutions within the interval.
- Every case must be tested separately, and most values of $n$ give solutions outside.
:::

::: sampunkt "The zero product method"
- A product is zero if **at least one** factor is zero.
- The method also applies to trigonometric equations: $\sin(2x) \cdot \cos(3x) = 0$ is solved as two equations.
- It often helps to take out a common factor first to create a product.
:::

::: sampunkt "Eliminating with tangent"
- If the equation contains both $\sin x$ and $\cos x$: divide both sides by $\cos x$.
- Use the fact that $\dfrac{\sin x}{\cos x} = \tan x$.
- $4\cos x - 5\sin x = 0$ gives $\tan x = 0.8$, so $x \approx 38.7^\circ + n \cdot 180^\circ$.
:::

::: sampunkt "Angle with a coefficient"
- Solve for the whole argument first, for example $3x + 30^\circ$, and then divide everything by the coefficient.
- The period term shrinks by the same factor: $n \cdot 360^\circ$ becomes $n \cdot 120^\circ$.
- That is why several solutions fit in the same interval.
:::

:::
