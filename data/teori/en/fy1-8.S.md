# Summary: Theory of relativity

This short chapter gave a taste of Einstein's idea that time, length and
mass are not absolute quantities. The starting point is that the speed of light in
a vacuum is always the same, regardless of how the source and the observer move.
This has three consequences: time dilation, length contraction, and that mass and
energy are linked through $E = m \cdot c^2$.

## Concepts to know

- **The speed of light in a vacuum**: constant, $c \approx 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\ \mathrm{m/s}$,
  regardless of how the light source or the observer moves.
- **The Michelson–Morley experiment**: the 1887 experiment that showed that
  the speed of light does not add to the Earth's motion through space.
- **Moving frame**: a system (for example a bus or a spaceship) that moves
  at a constant speed *v* relative to an observer.
- **Proper time**, $t_0$: the time of an event measured by someone who is
  inside the moving frame.
- **Time dilation**: time passes more slowly for a moving object, seen
  by an observer at rest.
- **Length contraction**: an object's length in the direction of motion becomes shorter
  at high speeds, seen by an observer at rest.
- **Relativistic mass**: an object's mass increases the closer to the speed of light
  it gets.
- **Mass–energy equivalence**: mass and energy are interchangeable quantities
  according to $E = m \cdot c^2$.

## Formulas

::: formel "The chapter's formulas"
**Time dilation**

$$
t = \frac{t_0}{\sqrt{1 - \dfrac{v^2}{c^2}}}
$$

where

- $t_0$ = the time of an event measured inside a moving frame, proper time (s)
- *t* = the time of the same event seen from outside the moving frame (s)
- *v* = the speed of the moving frame (m/s)
- *c* = the speed of light ($\approx 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\ \mathrm{m/s}$)

**Length contraction**

$$
l = l_0 \cdot \sqrt{1 - \frac{v^2}{c^2}}
$$

where

- $l_0$ = the length in the direction of motion measured inside a moving frame (m)
- *l* = the length in the direction of motion seen from outside the moving frame (m)
- *v* = the speed of the moving frame (m/s)
- *c* = the speed of light ($\approx 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\ \mathrm{m/s}$)

**Relativistic mass**

$$
m = \frac{m_0}{\sqrt{1 - \dfrac{v^2}{c^2}}}
$$

where

- $m_0$ = the mass measured inside a moving frame (kg)
- *m* = the mass seen from outside the moving frame (kg)
- *v* = the speed of the moving frame (m/s)
- *c* = the speed of light ($\approx 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\ \mathrm{m/s}$)

**Energy of mass**

$$
E = m \cdot c^2
$$

where

- *E* = energy (J)
- *m* = mass (kg)
- *c* = the speed of light ($\approx 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\ \mathrm{m/s}$)
:::

## Important relationships and methods

- The speed of light *c* is always the same, regardless of how the light source or
  the observer moves. This is what gives rise to time dilation,
  length contraction and relativistic mass.
- $t_0$, $l_0$ and $m_0$ are measured by someone who is inside
  the moving frame; *t*, *l* and *m* are measured by an observer who sees the system
  moving past.
- At everyday speeds ($v \ll c$), $\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2} \approx 1$
  and the effects are vanishingly small. Only close to the speed of light do they
  become noticeable.
- The closer *v* gets to *c*, the more *t* and *m* increase, and the more
  *l* decreases.
- The effect is real and must be compensated for in practice, for example
  when synchronising the clocks of GPS satellites with clocks at the Earth's surface.
- $E = m \cdot c^2$ shows that mass and energy are the same quantity in different
  forms. Since $c^2$ is enormous, a small mass corresponds to an
  enormous amount of energy.
- Gravitational fields also affect how fast time passes: time flows
  more slowly in a strong gravitational field than in a weak one, which has been confirmed
  by studies of the atomic clocks of GPS satellites.

## Before the test

- Can you explain why the Michelson–Morley experiment went against the
  classical expectation?
- Can you describe in words what time dilation, length contraction and relativistic
  mass mean?
- Do you know the formula for time dilation and what $t_0$ and *t* stand for?
- Do you know the formula for length contraction and what $l_0$ and *l* stand for?
- Do you know the formula for relativistic mass?
- Can you calculate how much time is dilated at a given speed, for
  example $v = 0.87 c$?
- Can you explain why light is always measured to have the speed *c*, regardless of
  the motion of the source or the observer?
- Do you know the formula $E = m \cdot c^2$ and what it says about the relationship between
  mass and energy?
- Do you know why relativistic effects are unnoticeable in everyday life but
  important to take into account for, for example, GPS satellites?
