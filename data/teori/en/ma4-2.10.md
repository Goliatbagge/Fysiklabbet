# Optimisation problems with digital tools

Many optimisation problems lead to functions that are awkward or impossible
to differentiate and solve by hand. Then we can instead use a digital
tool, for example GeoGebra. The rule of thumb is that we draw the graph of the
function we want to optimise and then read off any maximum and
minimum points with the tool for extreme points.

If we instead want to know when a quantity changes fastest, we look for the
largest value of the rate of change, that is of the **derivative**. Then we let
the tool differentiate the function and investigate the graph of the derivative.

This is how we proceed with a digital tool:

1. Enter the function to be optimised in the input bar.
2. Adjust the coordinate axes so that the whole graph is visible.
3. Use the tool for extreme points and click on the graph.
4. Read off the coordinates of the extreme point. The point's $y$-coordinate is the
   largest or smallest value we are looking for.

::: exempel "Example 1: Maximum concentration"
**A new type of medicine is being developed. After a patient has taken a dose, the
concentration in the body is $K(t) = 5t^{1.4}\cdot e^{-0.25t} + 1.5\cos(0.8t) + 3$
mg/litre, where $t$ is the time in hours after the medicine was taken and
$t \geq 0$. For the medicine to be effective but not toxic, the doctors want to
know the highest concentration in the bloodstream. Calculate the maximum
concentration.**

::: handskrift
typ: maxkoncentration
:::

::: textlosning
We enter the function $K(t)$ in GeoGebra and adjust the coordinate axes so that
the graph is visible. Then we use the tool for extreme points, click on the graph
and read off the maximum point:

$$
A = (7.09,\ 17.41)
$$

The maximum concentration is the $y$-coordinate of the point $A$.

**Answer:** The maximum concentration is approximately $17.4\ \mathrm{mg/litre}$.
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 2: The fastest rate of increase"
**The same medicine as in example 1. The doctors also want to know when the medicine
is absorbed fastest, that is when the concentration is increasing the most.
Find the point in time when the concentration is increasing fastest, and state the maximum
rate of increase.**

::: handskrift
typ: snabbastokning
:::

::: textlosning
The rate of increase is the rate of change, that is the derivative $K'(t)$. The greatest increase
occurs where $K'(t)$ has its largest value. We let GeoGebra differentiate
the function and draw the graph of the derivative. Since the model only applies for
$t \geq 0$, we look for the derivative's maximum point in that region and read off:

$$
A = (0.62,\ 3.83)
$$

The point in time is the $x$-coordinate and the maximum rate of increase is the $y$-coordinate.

**Answer:** The concentration is increasing fastest after approximately $0.62\ \mathrm{h}$, and the
maximum rate of increase is then approximately $3.8\ \mathrm{mg/litre}$ per hour.
:::
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Why digital"
- Many optimisation problems give functions that are hard or impossible to differentiate by hand.
- Then the graph is drawn instead and the extreme point is read off.
- The tool does the calculating. Your task is to choose the right graph to look at.
:::

::: sampunkt "Method"
1. Enter the function to be optimised in the input bar.
2. Adjust the coordinate axes so that the whole graph is visible.
3. Use the tool for extreme points and click on the graph.
4. Read off the coordinates. **The $y$-coordinate** is the largest or smallest value you are looking for.
:::

::: sampunkt "Largest value or fastest change"
- If the **largest value** is wanted: investigate the graph of $f$ itself.
- If the **fastest change** is wanted: let the tool differentiate and investigate the graph of $f'$.
- There the $x$-coordinate gives the point in time and the $y$-coordinate the maximum rate.
:::

::: sampunkt "Interpreting the answer"
- Always state the unit: the $y$-coordinate on $f$ has the function's unit, on $f'$ the unit per unit of time.
- Stay within the model's range of validity, for example $t \geq 0$.
- The maximum concentration gives 17.4 mg/litre, while the fastest increase gives 3.8 mg/litre per hour.
:::

:::
