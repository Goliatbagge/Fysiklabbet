# Summary: Motion

The chapter is about how the motion of an object is described with vectors, graphs
and formulas. You have met average speed as the first concept of motion, the difference
between speed and velocity, the difference between distance and displacement, how
slope and area in *s-t*, *v-t* and *a-t* graphs are interpreted, and how
the formulas for constant acceleration (including Torricelli's equation) are
used to solve motion problems, sometimes without having to take the detour
via the time at all.

## Concepts to know

- **distance**: the total length of path an object has moved; a scalar,
  written *s*.
- **displacement**: the vector between the start and end positions, written Δ*s*;
  differs from the distance as soon as the motion changes direction.
- **vector and scalar**: a vector has both magnitude and direction (for example
  velocity, force, acceleration); a scalar has magnitude only (for example speed,
  time, mass).
- **average speed**: total distance travelled divided by total time,
  $v_\mathrm{m} = \dfrac{s}{t}$; a scalar quantity with no direction.
- **average velocity**: the mean velocity over a time interval,
  $v_m = \dfrac{\Delta s}{\Delta t}$; corresponds to the slope of the secant in an
  *s-t* graph.
- **instantaneous velocity**: the velocity at a certain time; corresponds to the
  slope of the tangent in an *s-t* graph.
- **acceleration**: how quickly the velocity changes, written *a*, SI unit
  m/s²; a vector quantity.
- **deceleration**: negative acceleration, that is braking.
- **acceleration due to gravity**: the acceleration a freely falling object gets from
  the gravitational force, written *g*, with $g \approx 9.82\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$ in
  Sweden.
- **free fall**: motion where only the gravitational force acts on the object (no
  air resistance); all objects then fall equally fast, regardless of mass.
- **initial velocity**: the velocity at the time *t* = 0, written
  *v*₀.
- ***s-t*, *v-t* and *a-t* graphs**: graphs with position, velocity
  and acceleration respectively on the *y*-axis and time on the *x*-axis.
- **Torricelli's equation**: "the timeless equation"; links *v*,
  *v*₀, *a* and *s* without the time *t* needing to be known.

## Formulas

::: formel "Average speed"
$$
v_\mathrm{m} = \frac{s}{t}
$$

where

- *v*ₘ = average speed (m/s)
- *s* = distance (m)
- *t* = time (s)
:::

::: formel "Converting units: m/s and km/h"
$$
\mathrm{m/s} \xrightarrow{\;\times 3.6\;} \mathrm{km/h}
\qquad
\mathrm{km/h} \xrightarrow{\;\div 3.6\;} \mathrm{m/s}
$$
:::

::: formel "Vector addition (perpendicular vectors)"
$$
v^2 = v_1^2 + v_2^2 \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad v = \sqrt{v_1^2 + v_2^2}
$$

Applies when two vectors (for example velocities) act perpendicular to each other.
The resultant is found by moving the vectors parallel to themselves and then using
Pythagoras' theorem.
:::

::: formel "Average velocity and average acceleration"
$$
v_m = \frac{\Delta s}{\Delta t} = \frac{s_2 - s_1}{t_2 - t_1}
$$

$$
a_m = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t} = \frac{v_2 - v_1}{t_2 - t_1}
$$

where

- Δ*s* = displacement (m)
- Δ*v* = change in velocity (m/s)
- Δ*t* = time interval (s)
- *v*ₘ = average velocity (m/s)
- *a*ₘ = average acceleration (m/s²)
:::

::: formel "Motion with constant acceleration"
$$
v = v_0 + a \cdot t
$$

$$
s = (s_0) + v_0 \cdot t + \frac{a \cdot t^{2}}{2}
$$

$$
v_m = \frac{v_0 + v}{2}
$$

where

- *v* = velocity (m/s)
- *v*₀ = initial velocity (m/s)
- *a* = acceleration (m/s²)
- *t* = time (s)
- *s* = displacement (m)
- *s*₀ = starting position (m)
- *v*ₘ = average velocity at constant acceleration (m/s)
:::

::: formel "Special cases"
Without a starting velocity, $v_0 = 0$, which gives

$$
v = a \cdot t \qquad s = \frac{a \cdot t^{2}}{2}
$$

In free fall $a = -g$ where $g \approx 9.82\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$, which
gives

$$
v = v_0 - g \cdot t \qquad s = v_0 \cdot t - \frac{g \cdot t^{2}}{2}
$$
:::

::: formel "Torricelli's equation"
$$
v^{2} - v_0^{2} = 2 a s
$$

where

- *v* = final velocity (m/s)
- *v*₀ = initial velocity (m/s)
- *a* = acceleration (m/s²)
- *s* = displacement (m)

Applies at constant acceleration and is used when the time *t* is not given.
:::

## Important relationships and methods

- Average speed and average velocity are not the same thing: the average speed
  $v_\mathrm{m} = \dfrac{s}{t}$ is calculated on the whole distance
  travelled and has no direction, while the average velocity
  $v_m = \dfrac{\Delta s}{\Delta t}$ is calculated on the displacement and is a
  vector. For a motion back and forth to the starting point, the
  average velocity is zero while the average speed is not.
- Convert to SI units before substituting values: divide km/h by 3.6 to
  get m/s, multiply m/s by 3.6 to get km/h.
- In an *s-t* graph the slope of the secant corresponds to the average velocity over an
  interval, and the slope of the tangent to the instantaneous velocity at a point.
- In a *v-t* graph the slope of the line corresponds to the acceleration. The area
  between the graph and the *t*-axis corresponds to the displacement: positive above
  the axis, negative below.
- The distance travelled in a *v-t* graph is found by counting ALL
  areas as positive (absolute values), unlike the displacement.
- In an *a-t* graph the area between the graph and the *t*-axis corresponds to
  the change in velocity Δ*v*.
- Sign convention: a positive direction is chosen (often upwards or to the right);
  motion in the opposite direction gets negative values for position, velocity and
  acceleration.
- The formula $v = \dfrac{s}{t}$ applies ONLY at constant velocity. For
  accelerated motion the formulas for constant acceleration (or
  Torricelli's equation) must be used instead.
- Torricelli's equation uses the displacement *s*, not the distance, applies
  only in one dimension and only at constant acceleration (so not with,
  for example, air resistance, spring force or circular motion).
- Difficult systems of equations with two unknowns (for example *a* and *t*) can be solved
  graphically or with the CAS function in GeoGebra. Always show how
  the system of equations was set up and which method of solution was used.

## Before the test

- Do you know the formula for average speed and can you solve it for distance and for time?
- Can you switch between m/s and km/h without a calculator (conversion factor 3.6)?
- Can you explain the difference between average speed and average velocity?
- Can you tell distance and displacement apart, and decide whether a quantity is a
  vector or a scalar?
- Can you add perpendicular vectors by moving them parallel to themselves and using
  Pythagoras' theorem?
- Can you read off and interpret the slope (secant/tangent) in an *s-t* graph?
- Can you read off and interpret the slope and the area in a *v-t* graph, and
  tell displacement and distance travelled apart?
- Can you interpret the area in an *a-t* graph as a change in velocity?
- Can you calculate with signs for braking and motion in the negative direction?
- Do you know the formulas for velocity and displacement at constant
  acceleration, including the special cases without a starting velocity and in free
  fall?
- Can you use Torricelli's equation when the time is not given, and do you know when
  it must NOT be used?
- Can you set up and solve a system of equations with two unknowns, for
  example in GeoGebra?
