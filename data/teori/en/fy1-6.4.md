# The body and heat

## The body in heat

Normal body temperature is 37 °C, and it is important for the body to keep
this [[temperature|A measure of how fast the atoms/molecules in a substance are moving. SI unit kelvin (K).]] in order to function. During hard exertion or at high
temperatures the body therefore has to cool itself down in some way. We do that
by **sweating**. When the skin secretes sweat, the sweat evaporates,
and evaporation requires energy, which is taken from the skin, which is then cooled.

We also feel that evaporation cools the body when we step out of the shower and
start to feel cold, especially if we were to leave the bathroom!

## The body in cold

The body radiates [[heat|Energy transferred between objects at different temperatures. Measured in joules (J).]]. A human body at rest is like a radiator with a
[[power|Work or energy per unit time: *P* = *W* / *t*. SI unit watt (W).]] of 100 W. The heat we radiate disappears into the surrounding air,
since the air is (usually) colder than the temperature of the skin, which is about 33 °C,
and we "cool down". We prevent this by putting on clothes. Clothes,
especially thick ones, insulate well and keep the heat close to the body, and we
stay warm even when the air is cold.

In our evolutionary past we had fur that stood up when it got cold,
to insulate better. A remnant of this is that in the cold we get
"goose bumps", a bumpy surface on the skin.

If we have no clothes to keep us warm, we start to shiver. Chemical energy
in the body becomes [[kinetic energy|The energy an object has because of its motion: *E*ₖ = ½ · *m* · *v*².]], which is converted into heat. 25 % of the movement
becomes heat because of our "low" efficiency.

If it is windy, we also experience it as colder. That is because the
warmed air close to our bodies is blown away and replaced by colder air,
which cools our bodies.

If we are in a sauna with an air temperature of 80 °C and blow
hard on the body, however, we experience it as very hot! That is because
we replace the 33-degree air close to our skin with 80-degree air, which then
heats our bodies instead.

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "The body in heat"
- Normal body temperature is $37\ ^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and must be kept
  stable.
- The cooling takes place by **sweating**: the sweat **evaporates**, and
  evaporation requires energy, which is taken from the skin.
- The same thing makes you feel cold when you step out of the shower.
:::

::: sampunkt "The body in cold"
- A person at rest radiates roughly like a **$100\ \mathrm{W}$ radiator**.
- The temperature of the skin is about $33\ ^\circ\mathrm{C}$, warmer than
  the air, so heat radiates away.
- **Clothes insulate** and keep the heat in. Goose bumps are a remnant from
  the time when fur was raised to insulate better.
:::

::: sampunkt "Shivering"
- Chemical energy becomes kinetic energy, which becomes heat.
- About 25 % of the movement becomes heat, since the efficiency of the body is
  low.
:::

::: sampunkt "Why wind feels cold"
- The wind blows away the warmed air closest to the skin and replaces it
  with colder air.
- In a sauna at $80\ ^\circ\mathrm{C}$ it is the other way round: the hot air
  replaces the cooler air at the skin, and it burns.
:::
:::
