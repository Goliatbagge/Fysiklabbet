# Air pressure and total pressure

::: demo "The upside-down glass of water"
A glass is filled to the brim (the edge) with water. A thin plastic sheet is placed on top
and the glass is then turned upside down. The plastic sheet stays in place! The [[air pressure|The pressure from the Earth's atmosphere. At sea level about 101 325 Pa (1 atm).]]
pushing upwards under the plastic sheet is therefore larger than the weight downwards of
the water inside the glass.
:::

::: demo "The Magdeburg hemispheres"
Two hemispheres are put together and we pump out the air between them. When we try to
pull the hemispheres apart, we notice that a very large [[force|An influence that can change an object's motion or shape. SI unit newton (N). A vector quantity.]] is needed. When the air
inside the hemispheres is pumped out, only the air pressure from outside acts. This is so
large that a very large force is needed to get the hemispheres apart.
:::

::: demo "Air pressure on a rubber mat"
A rubber mat is placed on a table so that there is no air between the mat and
the table. The air pressure will then only act from above. Try to lift
the mat off the table! Once again a large force is needed to overcome
the air pressure.
:::

In the same way that the weight of a liquid causes a liquid pressure at a certain
depth, the weight of the air causes an **air pressure** or **gas pressure**: a
[[pressure|Force per unit area: *p* = *F* / *A*. SI unit pascal (Pa) = N/m².]] that is larger than you might think.

::: demo "The growing foam ball"
Place a foam ball in a vacuum chamber. Then pump out the air. The foam ball
grows and becomes large. When the air is pumped out of the vacuum chamber, the
air pressure disappears and the air bubbles in the foam ball expand, which makes
the foam ball appear to grow.
:::

Normal air pressure at sea level is 101.3 kPa (101 300 Pa). This pressure has
been given the unit **atm** (atmosphere). There are many different units for air pressure.

::: kuriosa "Different units for pressure"
| Unit | Value | Name |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Pa | 1 N/m² (the SI unit) | "pascal", "newton per square metre" |
| 1 atm | 101 300 Pa | "atmosphere" |
| 1 bar | 100 000 Pa | "bar" |
| 1 psi | ≈ 6 895 Pa | "psi" (pounds per square inch) |
| 1 mmHg | ≈ 133.3 Pa | "millimetres of mercury" |
:::

We live in a "sea of air" (the atmosphere). The higher up in the atmosphere we
go, the lower the air pressure becomes. This is partly because the air there has
a lower [[density|Mass per unit volume: *ρ* = *m* / *V*. SI unit kg/m³. Decides whether an object floats in a medium.]], and partly because we have a smaller volume of air above us (compare
with the depth of liquid).

Since liquids are hard to compress, the air pressure on a
liquid surface is transmitted through the whole liquid and in all directions. In a liquid
there is therefore both air pressure and liquid pressure. The combined pressure from
the air pressure and the liquid pressure is called the **total pressure**.

::: formel "Total pressure"
$$
p = p_0 + \rho \cdot g \cdot h
$$

where

- *p* = total pressure (Pa)
- $p_0$ = air pressure on the liquid surface (Pa)
- *ρ* = density of the liquid (kg/m³)
- *g* = gravitational field strength (N/kg)
- *h* = depth of liquid (m)
:::

::: exempel "Example 1: Diving to a depth of 3 metres"
**You dive in a lake where the air pressure is 101.3 kPa. At a depth of 3.0 metres, determine<br>a) the liquid pressure&emsp;&emsp;b) the total pressure<br>c) the compressive force on your eardrum if
it has an area of 80 mm².**

::: handskrift
typ: dyktryck
:::

::: textlosning
**a)**

$$
p = \rho \cdot g \cdot h
$$

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
\rho = 998\ \mathrm{kg/m^3} \\
g \approx 9.82\ \mathrm{N/kg} \\
h = 3.0\ \mathrm{m}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
p = 998 \cdot 9.82 \cdot 3.0 = 29\,401.08\ \mathrm{Pa}
\approx 29\,000\ \mathrm{Pa} = 29\ \mathrm{kPa}
$$

**Answer:** 29 kPa

**b)**

$$
p = p_0 + \rho \cdot g \cdot h
$$

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
p_0 = 101.3\ \mathrm{kPa} = 101\,300\ \mathrm{Pa} \\
\rho = 998\ \mathrm{kg/m^3} \\
g \approx 9.82\ \mathrm{N/kg} \\
h = 3.0\ \mathrm{m}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
p = 101\,300 + 998 \cdot 9.82 \cdot 3.0
= 130\,701.08\ \mathrm{Pa} \approx 0.13\ \mathrm{MPa}
$$

**Answer:** 0.13 MPa

**c)** The pressure that will give a compressive force on your eardrum is only
the liquid pressure. The air pressure is also present inside the ear, so it will cancel out the
outer air pressure.

$$
p = \frac{F}{A} \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad F = p \cdot A
$$

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
p = 29\,401.08\ \mathrm{Pa} \quad (\text{the liquid pressure from part a}) \\
A = 80\ \mathrm{mm^2} = 80 \cdot 10^{-6}\ \mathrm{m^2}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
F = 29\,401.08 \cdot 80 \cdot 10^{-6} = 2.352\ldots\ \mathrm{N} \approx 2.4\ \mathrm{N}
$$

**Answer:** 2.4 N
:::
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Air pressure"
- The air's own weight gives an **air pressure**, just as the weight of a liquid gives
  liquid pressure.
- Normal air pressure at sea level is
  $101.3\ \mathrm{kPa}$, which is called **1 atm**.
- Higher up in the atmosphere the pressure is lower: the air is thinner and there
  is less air above.
:::

::: sampunkt "You can tell that air pressure is large"
- **The upside-down glass**: the air pressure from below holds up the water.
- **The Magdeburg hemispheres**: with no air inside, an enormous force is needed to
  pull them apart.
- **The foam ball in a vacuum**: without outer pressure the air bubbles inside grow.
:::

::: sampunkt "Total pressure"
- $p = p_0 + \rho \cdot g \cdot h$
- $p_0$ is the air pressure **on the liquid surface**, the rest is the liquid pressure.
- In other words: everything pressing from above, added together.
:::

::: sampunkt "The eardrum: only the liquid pressure"
- The air pressure is **also present inside the ear** and cancels out the outer air pressure.
- That is why only the liquid pressure is used in
  $F = p \cdot A$ when the force on the eardrum is calculated.
:::

::: sampunkt "Other units of pressure"
- $1\ \mathrm{atm} = 101\,300\ \mathrm{Pa}$
- $1\ \mathrm{bar} = 100\,000\ \mathrm{Pa}$
- 1 psi is about $6\,895\ \mathrm{Pa}$ and 1 mmHg about
  $133.3\ \mathrm{Pa}$.
:::
:::
