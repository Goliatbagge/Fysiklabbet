# Parallel and vertical lines and the general form of the equation of a straight line

## Parallel and vertical lines

If two lines have the same slope they are called **parallel**. Parallel
lines therefore have the *same* *k*-value. The lines $y = 2x + 1$ and
$y = 2x - 3$ are for example parallel, since both have $k = 2$.

A line that goes straight up and down is called **vertical**. Vertical lines cannot
be described by the equation of a straight line in k-form; instead they are described by where
they lie in the *x*-direction. The vertical line through (4, 1) and (4, 5)
is for example described by $x = 4$, since all its points have
the *x*-coordinate 4.

::: formel "The equation of a vertical line"
$$
x = a
$$

where $a$ is the position of the line in the *x*-direction.
:::

## The equation of a straight line in general form

*All* straight lines can be described by **the equation of a straight line in general
form**. In general form all the terms of the equation of the line are collected on one side,
and the other side then becomes 0. The line $y = 2x + 1$ is for example written
$2x - y + 1 = 0$ in general form.

::: formel "The equation of a straight line in general form"
$$
ax + by + c = 0
$$

where $a$, $b$ and $c$ are constants.
:::

::: exempel "Example 1: Parallel line and general form"
**A line is described by $y = 3x - 5$. Give the equation of<br>a) a parallel
line that passes through (1, 2)<br>b) the line in general form.**

::: handskrift
typ: parallell
:::

::: textlosning
**a)** The $k$-value of the line is 3. The parallel line must therefore have the same
$k$-value. Substituting $k = 3$ into RLE in k-form gives

$$
y = 3x + m
$$

Since (1, 2) lies on the line,

$$
2 = 3 \cdot 1 + m
$$

$$
2 = 3 + m
$$

Subtract 3 from both sides:

$$
2 - 3 = 3 + m - 3
$$

$$
-1 = m
$$

Substituting $k = 3$ and $m = -1$ into RLE gives $y = 3x - 1$.

**Answer:** $y = 3x - 1$

**b)** We have the equation $y = 3x - 5$. We collect all the terms on one side
by subtracting $y$ from both sides:

$$
y - y = 3x - 5 - y
$$

$$
0 = 3x - 5 - y
$$

We swap LHS and RHS and sort the terms in "alphabetical order".

**Answer:** $3x - y - 5 = 0$
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 2: *k*-value from general form"
**Give the $k$-value of the line described by $2x + 3y - 6 = 0$.**

::: handskrift
typ: allmanform
:::

::: textlosning
We rewrite the equation in k-form by *solving for y*, and then
identify the $k$-value:

$$
2x + 3y - 6 = 0
$$

We start by eliminating the terms $2x$ and $-6$ from the LHS:

$$
2x + 3y - 6 - 2x + 6 = 0 - 2x + 6
$$

$$
3y = -2x + 6
$$

We divide both sides by 3:

$$
\frac{3y}{3} = \frac{-2x}{3} + \frac{6}{3}
$$

$$
y = -\frac{2}{3}x + 2
$$

and now we can easily see that $k = -\dfrac{2}{3}$.

**Answer:** $k = -\dfrac{2}{3}$
:::
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Parallel lines"
- Lines with the **same slope**, that is the same $k$-value.
- They have different $m$-values, otherwise it is the same line.
- Parallel line through a given point: keep $k$, calculate a new $m$.
:::

::: sampunkt "Vertical lines"
- A vertical line can **not** be written in the form $y = kx + m$, because
  its slope is not defined.
- It is written $x = a$, where $a$ is its position in the $x$-direction.
- The corresponding horizontal line is written $y = b$, which is
  $y = kx + m$ with $k = 0$.
:::

::: sampunkt "General form"
- $ax + by + c = 0$
- **All** straight lines can be written like this, even vertical ones.
- All terms are collected on one side so that the other side is 0.
:::

::: sampunkt "Switching between the forms"
- **To general form**: move all the terms to one side.
- **To k-form**: solve for $y$, and $k$ and $m$ can be read off directly.
- $2x + 3y - 6 = 0$ gives
  $y = -\dfrac{2}{3}x + 2$, so
  $k = -\dfrac{2}{3}$.
:::
:::
