# The principle of conservation of energy

We have previously established that energy exists in different forms: electrical energy,
[[thermal energy|Energy transferred between objects at different temperatures. Measured in joules (J).]], [[kinetic energy|The energy an object has because of its motion: *E*ₖ = ½ · *m* · *v*².]], [[gravitational potential energy|The energy an object has because of its position in a force field, for example *E*ₚ = *m* · *g* · *h* in a gravitational field.]] and more.

::: demo "Euler's disk"
We stand a thick metal disk (a so-called "Euler's disk") on its edge and
spin it. What it loses in potential energy turns into kinetic energy.
Since the [[friction|The force that opposes motion between two surfaces in contact.]] is extremely low, the conversion happens slowly. We hear how
its [[velocity|Change of position per unit time, with direction (a vector). SI unit m/s.]] increases the further down the centre of gravity gets.

::: minisim
typ: eulersdisk
:::
:::

::: formel "The principle of conservation of energy"
Energy can neither be created nor destroyed, only converted from one form into
another.
:::

[[The principle of conservation of energy|Energy can neither be created nor destroyed, only converted between different forms.]] is one of the cornerstones of physics. It means that the total
energy in a **closed system** (a system from which no energy can leak out) is
**[[constant|A value that does not change. For example the acceleration due to gravity *g* or the speed of light *c*.]]**.

In mechanics we have two basic forms of energy: **potential energy**
and **kinetic energy**. The sum of the potential energy $E_p$ and the kinetic energy
$E_k$ at a certain moment is called **[[mechanical energy|The sum of the kinetic energy and the potential energy in a system.]]**, written $E_m$.

::: formel "Mechanical energy"
$$
E_m = E_p + E_k
$$

where

- $E_m$ = mechanical energy
- $E_p$ = potential energy
- $E_k$ = kinetic energy
:::

::: formel "Conservation of mechanical energy"
Without energy losses

$$
E_{p1} + E_{k1} = E_{p2} + E_{k2}
$$

where

- $E_{p1}$ = potential energy at moment 1
- $E_{k1}$ = kinetic energy at moment 1
- $E_{p2}$ = potential energy at moment 2
- $E_{k2}$ = kinetic energy at moment 2
:::

If we have a system **without frictional heat** or energy losses, the
mechanical energy is constant at different moments.

If we have a system **with frictional heat**, the thermal energy $E_v$ can be calculated
either as the difference in mechanical energy between two moments or as the
[[work|Energy transferred when a force acts along a distance: *W* = *F* · *s* · cos *α*. SI unit joule (J).]] done by the frictional force.

::: formel "Thermal energy / frictional heat"
$$
E_v = \Delta E_m \qquad\text{alternatively}\qquad E_v = F_f \cdot s
$$

where

- $E_v$ = thermal energy / frictional heat (J)
- $\Delta E_m$ = difference in mechanical energy (J)
- $F_f$ = frictional force (N)
- *s* = [[distance|The total length of path an object has moved. A scalar quantity, measured in metres.]] (m)
:::

::: exempel "Example 1: Brachiosaurus throws up"
**A 14-metre-tall Brachiosaurus throws up. At what [[speed|The magnitude of velocity, without direction (a scalar). Measured in m/s or km/h.]] will the vomit hit
the ground? We ignore [[air resistance|The force from the air that slows down objects moving through it. Depends on the object's shape, size and speed.]] and assume that the vomit's
[[initial velocity|The velocity at the time *t* = 0 (when the motion starts). Written *v*₀.]] is 0.**

::: handskrift
typ: brachiosaurus
:::

::: textlosning
The principle of conservation of energy gives

$$
E_{p1} + E_{k1} = E_{p2} + E_{k2}
$$

We put the zero level at the ground. Then

$$
m \cdot g \cdot h + 0 = 0 + \frac{m \cdot v^{2}}{2}
\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad
m \cdot g \cdot h = \frac{m \cdot v^{2}}{2}
$$

Divide both sides by *m* and then solve for the velocity *v*.

$$
g \cdot h = \frac{v^{2}}{2}
\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad
v^{2} = 2 \cdot g \cdot h
\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad
v = \sqrt{2 \cdot g \cdot h}
$$

Notice that the mass does **not** matter for how fast the vomit hits
the ground!

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
g \approx 9.82\ \mathrm{N/kg} \\
h = 14\ \mathrm{m}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
v = \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.82 \cdot 14} = \sqrt{274.96} \approx 16.58\ \mathrm{m/s}
$$

**Answer:** 17 m/s (or about 60 km/h!)
:::
:::

::: textlosning
We put the zero level at the sea surface. The principle of conservation of energy gives

$$
E_{p1} + E_{k1} = E_{p2} + E_{k2}
$$

$$
m g h_1 + \frac{m v_1^{2}}{2} = 0 + \frac{m v_2^{2}}{2}
$$

Since every term contains *m*, we divide both sides by *m*.

$$
g h_1 + \frac{v_1^{2}}{2} = \frac{v_2^{2}}{2}
$$

We multiply both sides by 2 to get rid of the denominators and then solve
for $v_2$.

$$
2 g h_1 + v_1^{2} = v_2^{2}
\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad
v_2 = \sqrt{2 g h_1 + v_1^{2}}
$$

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
g \approx 9.82\ \mathrm{N/kg} \\
h_1 = 12\ \mathrm{m} \\
v_1 = 14\ \mathrm{m/s}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
v_2 = \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.82 \cdot 12 + 14^{2}} = \sqrt{431.68}
= 20.776\ldots\ \mathrm{m/s} \approx 21\ \mathrm{m/s}
$$

**Answer:** 21 m/s
:::

::: exempel "Example 3: Eddie on the hill"
**Eddie with his bicycle has a mass of 82 kg. He passes the top of a hill at a speed of
10 m/s. The hill is 20 metres high. The speed at the bottom is 19 m/s. How much
energy has become frictional heat?**

::: handskrift
typ: eddiebacken
:::

::: textlosning

We call the frictional heat $E_v$. The principle of conservation of energy gives

$$
E_{p1} + E_{k1} = E_{p2} + E_{k2} + E_v
$$

$$
m g h + \frac{m v_1^{2}}{2} = 0 + \frac{m v_2^{2}}{2} + E_v
$$

We solve for $E_v$

$$
E_v = m g h + \frac{m v_1^{2}}{2} - \frac{m v_2^{2}}{2}
$$

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
m = 82\ \mathrm{kg} \\
g \approx 9.82\ \mathrm{N/kg} \\
h = 20\ \mathrm{m} \\
v_1 = 10\ \mathrm{m/s} \\
v_2 = 19\ \mathrm{m/s}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
E_v = 82 \cdot 9.82 \cdot 20 + \frac{82 \cdot 10^{2}}{2} - \frac{82 \cdot 19^{2}}{2}
= 16\,104.8 + 4\,100 - 14\,801 = 5\,403.8\ \mathrm{J} \approx 5\,400\ \mathrm{J} = 5.4\ \mathrm{kJ}
$$

**Answer:** 5.4 kJ
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "The principle of conservation of energy"
- **Energy can neither be created nor destroyed, only converted** from one
  form into another.
- In a **closed system**, where no energy leaks out, the total
  energy is constant.
- One of the cornerstones of physics.
:::

::: sampunkt "Mechanical energy"
- $E_m = E_p + E_k$, that is potential energy plus kinetic energy.
- Without energy losses the mechanical energy is **constant** over time.
:::

::: sampunkt "The energy equation"
- $E_{p1} + E_{k1} = E_{p2} + E_{k2}$
- Write it out as
  $m g h_1 + \dfrac{m v_1^{2}}{2}
  = m g h_2 + \dfrac{m v_2^{2}}{2}$.
- The mass appears in every term and **cancels out**. That is why the mass
  does not matter for the final velocity in free fall.
:::

::: sampunkt "Standard results"
- Fall from the height $h$ without initial velocity:
  $v = \sqrt{2gh}$.
- Throw from the height $h_1$ with the speed $v_1$:
  $v_2 = \sqrt{2gh_1 + v_1^{2}}$.
- The angle of the throw does not matter for the speed on impact, only the height and
  the initial speed.
:::

::: sampunkt "Frictional heat"
- With friction, mechanical energy disappears as heat.
- $E_v = \Delta E_m$ (the difference in mechanical energy) or
  $E_v = F_f \cdot s$ (the work done by the frictional force).
- Both ways give the same answer and can be used as a check.
:::

::: sampunkt "How to solve it"
1. Choose a zero level, usually at the bottom.
2. Write out the energy at point 1 and point 2.
3. Set them equal, cancel $m$ if it appears in all terms.
4. Solve for what you are looking for.
:::
:::
