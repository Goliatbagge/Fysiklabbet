# The graph of a quadratic function

A function where the greatest exponent of the variable term is 2 is called a
**quadratic function**. $f(x) = x^2 - 6$ and $g(x) = 3x^2 - 5x + 7$ are
examples of quadratic functions. $x^2$ and $3x^2$ are called
**quadratic terms** (second-degree terms).

We will mainly use digital tools to draw
graphs, but for understanding it is good to be able to do it by hand
as well. We draw the quadratic function $f(x) = x^2$ by hand.

We start by drawing up a table of values to get coordinates
to plot. We choose to go from $x = -3$ to $x = 3$. To
calculate the $y$-coordinate when $x = -3$ we substitute −3 into our function
$f(x) = x^2$ and get $f(-3) = (-3)^2 = 9$. Then we do the same
for the other $x$-values.

| $x$ | $y = f(x)$ |
| --- | --- |
| −3 | $(-3)^2 = 9$ |
| −2 | $(-2)^2 = 4$ |
| −1 | $(-1)^2 = 1$ |
| 0 | $0^2 = 0$ |
| 1 | $1^2 = 1$ |
| 2 | $2^2 = 4$ |
| 3 | $3^2 = 9$ |

We plot the coordinates in a coordinate system and then join
the points. This gives us the graph of the quadratic function.

## The parts of the parabola

We see that the graph of the quadratic function forms an "arch". This
shape is called a **parabola**. The parabola can either have a "peak" where we
find the graph's **maximum point** and greatest value, or a "valley" where we
find the graph's **minimum point** and least value. A collective name for
maximum and minimum points is **stationary point**. A stationary point can thus
be either a maximum or a minimum point. Whether a stationary point
is a maximum or a minimum point is called the **nature** of the point.

We also see that the graph is symmetric about a vertical line called
the **line of symmetry**. The stationary point lies on the line of symmetry (we will
make use of this later on). The equation of the line of symmetry is given by its
$x$-coordinate. If a line of symmetry lies, for example, at 3 on the $x$-axis, the
equation of the line of symmetry is $x = 3$. Sometimes it is also written $x_s = 3$.

The $x$-values where the value of the function is 0 and where the graph of the function
crosses the $x$-axis are called **zeros**. The zeros are given by their
$x$-coordinate. If a graph crosses the $x$-axis at 5, one zero is
$x = 5$. A quadratic function can have two zeros, one zero or no
zeros.

::: tips "The shape of the parabola"
If the quadratic term (for example $x^2$ or $t^2$) in a quadratic function
is

- **positive**, the graph has a **minimum point** (Memory rule: a positive
  $x^2$-term gives a "happy mouth")
- **negative**, the graph has a **maximum point** (Memory rule: a negative
  $x^2$-term gives a "sad mouth")
:::

**a) the greatest value&emsp;&emsp;b) the zeros&emsp;&emsp;c) the coordinates of the stationary point&emsp;&emsp;d) the equation of the line of symmetry**

**a)** We see that the curve has a "peak" and therefore its greatest value
at $y = 4$. (If it is difficult to read off the greatest or least
value, we can use the tool "Extremum" in Geogebra.)

**Answer:** The greatest value is 4.

**b)** The zeros are where the graph crosses the $x$-axis, that is at $x = -3$ and
at $x = 1$. (If it is difficult to read off the zeros, we can use
the tool "Roots" in Geogebra.)

**Answer:** $x = -3$ and $x = 1$

**c)** The coordinates of the stationary point are, in this case, the coordinates of
the maximum point, that is (−1, 4).

**Answer:** (−1, 4)

**d)** The line of symmetry is the vertical line that divides the graph into two
identical halves. It always passes through the stationary point. Since
the stationary point lies at $x = -1$, the line of symmetry must also lie
at $x = -1$. So the equation of the line of symmetry is $x_s = -1$.

**Answer:** $x_s = -1$

## Investigate the parabola yourself

Investigate how the coefficients $a$, $b$ and $c$ in
$y = ax^2 + bx + c$ shape the parabola. The starting position shows the example's
function $g(x) = -x^2 - 2x + 3$. In particular, try the sign of $a$: a positive
$a$ gives a "happy mouth" (minimum point), a negative $a$ gives a "sad mouth"
(maximum point). $c$ moves the graph up and down and is where the graph crosses
the *y*-axis.

::: graf
titel: y = ax^2 + bx + c
uttryck: a*x^2 + b*x + c
ekvation: y = {a}x^2 + {b}x + {c}
a: -1, -3, 3, 0.5
b: -2, -6, 6, 1
c: 3, -5, 5, 1
x: -6, 6
y: -6, 6
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Quadratic function"
- The greatest exponent of the variable term is **2**.
- $f(x) = x^2 - 6$ and $g(x) = 3x^2 - 5x + 7$.
- The graph is called a **parabola**.
:::

::: sampunkt "Stationary point"
- **Maximum point**: the peak of the parabola, where the function has its **greatest**
  value.
- **Minimum point**: the valley of the parabola, where it has its **least** value.
- Common name: **stationary point**. Which kind it is is called the
  **nature** of the point.
:::

::: sampunkt "The sign determines the shape"
- **Positive** $x^2$-term: **minimum point**, that is a "happy mouth".
- **Negative** $x^2$-term: **maximum point**, that is a "sad mouth".
:::

::: sampunkt "Line of symmetry"
- The vertical line that divides the parabola into two equal halves.
- **The stationary point always lies on the line of symmetry.**
- Given by its $x$-coordinate: $x_s = -1$.
:::

::: sampunkt "Zeros"
- The $x$-values where the graph **crosses the $x$-axis**, that is where
  $f(x) = 0$.
- A quadratic function has **two, one or no** zeros.
:::

::: sampunkt "What the coefficients do"
- $a$ controls the shape: the sign gives the direction, the size how narrow the parabola
  becomes.
- $c$ is where the graph **crosses the $y$-axis**.
- $b$ shifts the parabola sideways together with $a$.
:::
:::
