# Formulas and trigonometric equations

Sometimes a trigonometric equation becomes easier to solve if we first rewrite
it using earlier relationships: the Pythagorean identity, the addition and
subtraction formulas and the double angle formulas. The idea is to get
the whole left-hand side expressed in *one and the same* trigonometric function of *one
and the same* angle, so that the equation becomes an ordinary equation we already know how to
solve.

Remember the relationships below.

::: formel "The Pythagorean identity and tangent"
For all angles $v$

$$
\sin^2 v + \cos^2 v = 1
\qquad\qquad
\tan v = \frac{\sin v}{\cos v}
$$
:::

::: formel "Addition and subtraction formulas"
$$
\sin (u + v) = \sin u \cdot \cos v + \cos u \cdot \sin v
$$

$$
\sin (u - v) = \sin u \cdot \cos v - \cos u \cdot \sin v
$$

$$
\cos (u + v) = \cos u \cdot \cos v - \sin u \cdot \sin v
$$

$$
\cos (u - v) = \cos u \cdot \cos v + \sin u \cdot \sin v
$$
:::

::: formel "Double angle formulas"
$$
\sin 2v = 2 \sin v \cos v
$$

$$
\cos 2v = \cos^2 v - \sin^2 v = 2\cos^2 v - 1 = 1 - 2\sin^2 v
$$
:::

Once we have the equation in the form $\sin(\text{something}) = a$ or
$\cos(\text{something}) = a$, it usually gives **two cases** of solutions (Case 1
and Case 2), since in one turn there are two angles with the same sine
or cosine value. We include both cases and add the period
($n \cdot 2\pi$ in radians or $n \cdot 360^\circ$ in degrees) to get
*all* solutions.

::: exempel "Example 1: Rewrite using the double angle (radians)"
**Solve the equation $\sin x \cos x = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}$ and answer in radians.**

::: handskrift
typ: dubbelekvation
:::

::: textlosning
The left-hand side resembles the expression for $\sin 2x$. We multiply both sides
by 2 so that we can rewrite the left-hand side as $\sin 2x$.

$$
2 \sin x \cos x = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}
\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad
\sin 2x = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}
$$

Since $\sin \dfrac{\pi}{3} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, we get two cases.

$$
\textbf{Case 1:}\quad 2x = \frac{\pi}{3} + n \cdot 2\pi
\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad
x = \frac{\pi}{6} + n \cdot \pi
$$

$$
\textbf{Case 2:}\quad 2x = \pi - \frac{\pi}{3} + n \cdot 2\pi = \frac{2\pi}{3} + n \cdot 2\pi
\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad
x = \frac{\pi}{3} + n \cdot \pi
$$

**Answer:** $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6} + n \cdot \pi$ and $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3} + n \cdot \pi$
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 2: The zero product method (degrees)"
**Solve the equation $\cos 2x - 4\cos x + 1 = 0$ and answer in degrees.**

::: handskrift
typ: nollproduktcos
:::

::: textlosning
Since the second term contains $\cos x$, we choose the form of the double
angle formula that gives something with $\cos x$, that is $\cos 2x = 2\cos^2 x - 1$.

$$
2\cos^2 x - 1 - 4\cos x + 1 = 0
\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad
2\cos^2 x - 4\cos x = 0
$$

Both terms contain the factor $\cos x$, so we take it out and use
the zero product method.

$$
\cos x\,(2\cos x - 4) = 0
$$

A product is zero if one of the factors is zero.

$$
\textbf{Case 1:}\quad \cos x = 0
\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad
x = \pm 90^\circ + n \cdot 360^\circ
$$

$$
\textbf{Case 2:}\quad 2\cos x - 4 = 0
\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad
\cos x = 2
$$

Case 2 has no solutions, since $-1 \le \cos x \le 1$.

**Answer:** $x = \pm 90^\circ + n \cdot 360^\circ$
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 3: Substitution and the *pq* formula (degrees)"
**Solve the equation $2\sin x - \cos^2 x - 2 = 0$ and answer in degrees.**

::: handskrift
typ: substitutionpq
:::

::: textlosning
The first term contains $\sin x$, so we use the Pythagorean identity
to write $\cos^2 x = 1 - \sin^2 x$.

$$
2\sin x - (1 - \sin^2 x) - 2 = 0
\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad
\sin^2 x + 2\sin x - 3 = 0
$$

Both terms with $x$ contain $\sin x$, so we can make a **substitution**.
We set $\sin x = t$ and solve with the *pq* formula.

$$
t^2 + 2t - 3 = 0
\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad
t = -1 \pm \sqrt{1^2 + 3} = -1 \pm 2
$$

$$
t_1 = -1 - 2 = -3
\qquad\qquad
t_2 = -1 + 2 = 1
$$

Now we substitute back $t = \sin x$.

$$
\textbf{Case 1:}\quad \sin x = -3 \qquad \text{has no solutions, since } -1 \le \sin x \le 1
$$

$$
\textbf{Case 2:}\quad \sin x = 1
\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad
x = 90^\circ + n \cdot 360^\circ
$$

(The other case $x = 180^\circ - 90^\circ + n \cdot 360^\circ$ gives the same
solutions.)

**Answer:** $x = 90^\circ + n \cdot 360^\circ$
:::
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "The idea"
- Rewrite the equation so that the whole side is expressed in **a single** trigonometric function of **a single** angle.
- The tools are the Pythagorean identity, the addition and subtraction formulas and the double angle.
- Then the equation becomes an ordinary equation you already know how to solve.
:::

::: sampunkt "Choosing the right formula"
- If the expression contains $\sin x \cos x$: use $\sin 2x = 2\sin x\cos x$ backwards.
- If there is both $\cos 2x$ and $\cos x$: choose the form $\cos 2v = 2\cos^2 v - 1$.
- If there is both $\sin x$ and $\cos^2 x$: replace $\cos^2 x$ with $1 - \sin^2 x$.
- Always choose the form that gives the **same** function as the rest of the equation.
:::

::: sampunkt "Three methods"
- **The zero product method:** take out a common factor and set each factor equal to zero.
- **Substitution:** set $\sin x = t$ and solve the quadratic equation with the *pq* formula, then substitute back.
- **The double angle:** multiply by 2 to get $\sin 2x$.
:::

::: sampunkt "Finishing correctly"
- Sine and cosine give **two cases** per turn. Include both.
- Add the period: $n \cdot 2\pi$ in radians or $n \cdot 360^\circ$ in degrees.
- Reject impossible cases: $\sin x$ and $\cos x$ always lie between $-1$ and $1$.
:::

:::
