# The equation zⁿ = w

We will now solve power equations with complex numbers of the form $z^n = w$, where $n$
is a positive integer and $w$ is a complex number. The trick is to rewrite
both sides in polar form and then compare them.

## A first equation: z³ = 64

Say we are to solve the equation $z^3 = 64$. It has the obvious real
solution $z = 4$ since $4^3 = 4\cdot 4\cdot 4 = 64$. To also find the
imaginary solutions we rewrite both sides in polar form.

Let $z = r(\cos v + i\sin v)$ be the polar form of an arbitrary
complex number. According to de Moivre's formula we then have for the left-hand side and
the right-hand side:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{LHS} &= z^3 = r^3(\cos 3v + i\sin 3v)\\
\text{RHS} &= 64 = 64(\cos 0^\circ + i\sin 0^\circ)
\end{aligned}
$$

Here we have used the fact that the number $64$ in the complex plane has the modulus
$r = 64$ and the argument (the angle to the positive real axis) $v = 0^\circ$.
The equation can then be written

$$
r^3(\cos 3v + i\sin 3v) = 64(\cos 0^\circ + i\sin 0^\circ)
$$

**Comparing** the two sides gives one relationship for the modulus and
one for the argument:

$$
r^3 = 64 \qquad (1) \qquad\qquad \cos 3v + i\sin 3v = \cos 0^\circ + i\sin 0^\circ \qquad (2)
$$

From equation $(1)$ we get $r = \sqrt[3]{64} = 4$. From equation $(2)$ the
arguments must be equal, but since an angle can be increased by whole turns without
the point moving,

$$
3v = 0^\circ + n\cdot 360^\circ \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad v = \frac{0^\circ}{3} + \frac{n\cdot 360^\circ}{3} = n\cdot 120^\circ
$$

where $n$ is an integer. If we substitute $n = 0, 1, 2$ we get three different
arguments: $v = 0^\circ,\ 120^\circ,\ 240^\circ$. With $n = 3$ we get
$v = 360^\circ$, which is the same solution as $n = 0$. The trigonometric
functions are periodic, so we get no more solutions. So the equation
$z^3 = 64$ has **three** solutions. Substituting $r = 4$ and
the arguments gives:

$$
\begin{aligned}
z_1 &= 4(\cos 0^\circ + i\sin 0^\circ) = 4\\
z_2 &= 4(\cos 120^\circ + i\sin 120^\circ) = 4\left(-\frac{1}{2} + i\cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right) = -2 + 2\sqrt{3}\,i\\
z_3 &= 4(\cos 240^\circ + i\sin 240^\circ) = 4\left(-\frac{1}{2} - i\cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right) = -2 - 2\sqrt{3}\,i
\end{aligned}
$$

The three solutions are evenly spaced on a circle with radius
$\sqrt[3]{|64|} = 4$ length units and form the vertices of a regular
3-gon (an equilateral triangle) around the origin.

::: formel "The equation zⁿ = w"
The equation $z^n = w$, where $n$ is a positive integer,

- has exactly $n$ roots (solutions),
- has its roots in the complex plane on a circle with radius
  $r = \sqrt[n]{|w|}$, evenly spaced as the vertices of a regular $n$-gon,
- is solved by rewriting both sides in polar form and then comparing
  modulus and argument.

The arguments are given by $\displaystyle v = \frac{\arg w + k\cdot 360^\circ}{n}$ for
$k = 0, 1, 2, \ldots, n-1$ (the counter $k$ runs through the $n$ roots).
:::

::: exempel "Example 1: z⁴ = −81i"
**Solve the equation $z^4 = -81i$ and answer in<br>a) polar form&emsp;&emsp;b) the form $a + bi$.**

::: handskrift
typ: binomiskfyra
:::

::: textlosning
**a)** We get four solutions since the exponent is $4$. Let
$z = r(\cos v + i\sin v)$ and write both sides in polar form. The number $-81i$ has
the modulus $r = |-81i| = 81$ and the argument $v = 270^\circ$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{LHS} &= z^4 = r^4(\cos 4v + i\sin 4v)\\
\text{RHS} &= -81i = 81(\cos 270^\circ + i\sin 270^\circ)
\end{aligned}
$$

Comparing gives $r^4 = 81 \Leftrightarrow r = \sqrt[4]{81} = 3$ and

$$
4v = 270^\circ + n\cdot 360^\circ \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad v = \frac{270^\circ}{4} + \frac{n\cdot 360^\circ}{4} = 67.5^\circ + n\cdot 90^\circ
$$

For $n = 0, 1, 2, 3$ (four solutions) the arguments are

$$
v_0 = 67.5^\circ,\quad v_1 = 157.5^\circ,\quad v_2 = 247.5^\circ,\quad v_3 = 337.5^\circ
$$

Substituting $r = 3$ and these angles into $z = r(\cos v + i\sin v)$ gives
the solutions.

**Answer (a):**
$$
\begin{aligned}
z_1 &= 3(\cos 67.5^\circ + i\sin 67.5^\circ) & z_2 &= 3(\cos 157.5^\circ + i\sin 157.5^\circ)\\
z_3 &= 3(\cos 247.5^\circ + i\sin 247.5^\circ) & z_4 &= 3(\cos 337.5^\circ + i\sin 337.5^\circ)
\end{aligned}
$$

**b)** We multiply $3$ into the brackets and calculate the sine and cosine:

$$
\begin{aligned}
z_1 &= 3\cos 67.5^\circ + 3i\sin 67.5^\circ \approx 1.15 + 2.77i\\
z_2 &\approx -2.77 + 1.15i\\
z_3 &\approx -1.15 - 2.77i\\
z_4 &\approx 2.77 - 1.15i
\end{aligned}
$$

**Answer (b):** $z_1 \approx 1.15 + 2.77i$, $z_2 \approx -2.77 + 1.15i$,
$z_3 \approx -1.15 - 2.77i$, $z_4 \approx 2.77 - 1.15i$.
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 2: z³ = −4 + 4i√3"
**Solve the equation $z^3 = -4 + 4i\sqrt{3}$.**

::: handskrift
typ: binomisktre
:::

::: textlosning
We get three solutions. Let $z = r(\cos v + i\sin v)$ and rewrite both sides in
polar form. The left-hand side becomes $z^3 = r^3(\cos 3v + i\sin 3v)$.

For the right-hand side we find the modulus and argument. The modulus is

$$
r = |-4 + 4i\sqrt{3}| = \sqrt{4^2 + (4\sqrt{3})^2} = \sqrt{16 + 48} = \sqrt{64} = 8
$$

We get the argument from $\tan v = \dfrac{4\sqrt{3}}{-4} = -\sqrt{3}$. Since
$-4 + 4i\sqrt{3}$ lies in the second quadrant (negative real part, positive
imaginary part), $90^\circ < v < 180^\circ$, which gives $v = 120^\circ$.
So $\text{RHS} = 8(\cos 120^\circ + i\sin 120^\circ)$ and the equation becomes

$$
r^3(\cos 3v + i\sin 3v) = 8(\cos 120^\circ + i\sin 120^\circ)
$$

Comparing gives $r^3 = 8 \Leftrightarrow r = \sqrt[3]{8} = 2$ and

$$
3v = 120^\circ + n\cdot 360^\circ \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad v = \frac{120^\circ}{3} + \frac{n\cdot 360^\circ}{3} = 40^\circ + n\cdot 120^\circ
$$

For $n = 0, 1, 2$ (three solutions) the arguments are
$v_0 = 40^\circ$, $v_1 = 160^\circ$, $v_2 = 280^\circ$. Substituting $r = 2$
gives the solutions.

**Answer:**
$$
\begin{aligned}
z_1 &= 2(\cos 40^\circ + i\sin 40^\circ)\\
z_2 &= 2(\cos 160^\circ + i\sin 160^\circ)\\
z_3 &= 2(\cos 280^\circ + i\sin 280^\circ)
\end{aligned}
$$
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "The number of solutions"
- The equation $z^n = w$ has **exactly $n$** roots.
- They lie on a circle with radius $r = \sqrt[n]{|w|}$.
- The roots are evenly spaced as the vertices of a regular $n$-gon around the origin.
:::

::: sampunkt "The method"
1. Write **both sides** in polar form. Use de Moivre's formula on the left-hand side.
2. **Compare** the moduli separately and the arguments separately.
3. The modulus gives $r^n = |w|$, so $r = \sqrt[n]{|w|}$.
:::

::: sampunkt "The arguments"
- $v = \dfrac{\arg w + k \cdot 360^\circ}{n}$ for $k = 0,\ 1,\ \ldots,\ n-1$.
- The period term must be included, since an angle can be increased by whole turns.
- **Divide the whole expression** by $n$, including the period term.
- At $k = n$ the first solution is repeated. So there are no more roots.
:::

::: sampunkt "The form of the right-hand side"
- Find $|w|$ with $\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$ and $\arg w$ with the tangent.
- Check the quadrant: $-4 + 4i\sqrt{3}$ lies in the second quadrant, so $v = 120^\circ$.
- Answer in polar form, or multiply in $r$ for the form $a + bi$.
:::
