# Applications of trigonometric functions

Sine functions turn up in applications where something happens periodically, for
example sound waves and alternating current. Such oscillations are also called
**harmonic**. The time for one complete oscillation is called the **period** (periodic time), is
often denoted $T$, and is the same thing as the period of the function. A shift in the $x$-direction is called a
**phase shift**. In applications, radians are the most common measure of angle.

::: formel "Amplitude and period"
The function $y = A \sin Bx$ has the amplitude $|A|$ and the period

$$
T = \frac{2\pi}{B} \ \text{(radians)} \qquad \text{or} \qquad \frac{360^\circ}{B}
$$
:::

::: formel "In general for sine and cosine functions"
The function

$$
y = A \sin(Bx + C) + D
$$

- has the amplitude $|A|$
- has the period $T = \dfrac{2\pi}{B}$ (or $\dfrac{360^\circ}{B}$), often called the *periodic time* in applications
- is shifted $-\dfrac{C}{B}$ in the $x$-direction compared with the graph of $y = \sin Bx$, often called the *phase shift* in applications
- is shifted $D$ units in the $y$-direction compared with the graph of $y = \sin Bx$, the *equilibrium position* in applications

The same also applies to cosine functions.
:::

::: exempel "Example 1: Amplitude and period"
**A weight on a spring oscillates harmonically according to the relationship $y = 4.5 \sin 10.5t$, where $y$ is the position in the $y$-direction relative to the equilibrium position in cm and $t$ is the time in seconds. Find the oscillation's<br>a) amplitude&emsp;&emsp;b) period.**

::: handskrift
typ: fjaderamplitud
:::

::: textlosning
**a)** We compare the function with the form $y = A \sin Bt$ and identify
$A = 4.5$ and $B = 10.5$. The amplitude is

$$
|A| = |4.5| = 4.5\ \text{cm}
$$

**b)** With $B = 10.5$ the period is

$$
T = \frac{2\pi}{B} = \frac{2\pi}{10.5} = 0.598\ldots\ \text{s} \approx 0.60\ \text{s}
$$

**Answer:** a) 4.5 cm  b) 0.60 s
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 2: Set up a sine model"
**A lemming population varies cyclically over a four-year period. At its largest (a "lemming year") the population is 10 000 individuals and at its lowest 2 000 individuals. At $t = 0$ the population is at its mean level and is about to increase. Set up a sine function $N(t)$ that describes the number of lemmings after the time $t$ years.**

::: handskrift
typ: lammelmodell
:::

::: textlosning
We start from the general form and find the constants $A$, $B$, $C$
and $D$:

$$
N(t) = A \sin(Bt + C) + D \qquad (1)
$$

$A$ is determined by the **amplitude**, which is half the distance between the maximum and
minimum value:

$$
A = \frac{10\,000 - 2\,000}{2} = \frac{8\,000}{2} = 4\,000
$$

$B$ is determined by the **period** according to $T = \dfrac{2\pi}{B}$. The period is
$T = 4$ years:

$$
4 = \frac{2\pi}{B} \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad B = \frac{2\pi}{4} = 1.570\ldots \approx 1.57
$$

$C$ is determined by the **phase shift**. Since the population is at its
mean level (the equilibrium position) and on its way up at $t = 0$, there is no
phase shift:

$$
C = 0
$$

$D$ is determined by the **equilibrium position**, the value midway between the maximum and
minimum value:

$$
D = \frac{10\,000 + 2\,000}{2} = \frac{12\,000}{2} = 6\,000
$$

Substituting $A = 4\,000$, $B = 1.57$, $C = 0$ and $D = 6\,000$ into (1)
gives

$$
N(t) = 4\,000 \sin 1.57t + 6\,000
$$

**Answer:** $N(t) = 4\,000 \sin 1.57t + 6\,000$
:::
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Concepts"
- A periodic oscillation is called **harmonic**.
- The time for one complete oscillation is the **period** $T$ (periodic time).
- A shift in the $x$-direction is called a **phase shift**.
- In applications radians are usually used.
:::

::: sampunkt "The model"
$y = A\sin(Bt + C) + D$
- $|A|$ is the amplitude,
- $T = \dfrac{2\pi}{B}$ is the period,
- $C$ gives the phase shift,
- $D$ is the equilibrium position.
:::

::: sampunkt "Finding the constants"
- $A$ is half the difference between the largest and smallest value.
- $D$ is the mean of the largest and smallest value, that is the equilibrium position.
- $B$ is found from the period: $B = \dfrac{2\pi}{T}$.
- $C = 0$ if the process starts at the equilibrium position and is on its way up.
:::

::: sampunkt "Typical example"
- A population between 2 000 and 10 000 with the period 4 years gives $A = 4\,000$ and $D = 6\,000$.
- $B = \dfrac{2\pi}{4} \approx 1.57$ and $C = 0$.
- The model becomes $N(t) = 4\,000\sin 1.57t + 6\,000$.
:::
