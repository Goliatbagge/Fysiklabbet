# Millikan's oil drop experiment

**Millikan's oil drop experiment** is a classic experiment (Robert Millikan,
American, 1909) in which the elementary charge 1.602 · 10⁻¹⁹ C was determined for
the first time. He sprayed small oil drops into an [[electric field|The region around a charge where another charge is affected by an electric force.]] between
two plates, where some drops hovered because [[equilibrium|When the resultant force on an object is zero. The object is then at rest or moving uniformly.]] arose between the [[gravitational force|The force with which the Earth pulls all objects towards its centre. The gravitational force gives an object its weight and points straight down towards the centre of the Earth.]]
downwards and the electric [[force|An influence that can change an object's motion or shape. SI unit newton (N). Vector quantity.]] upwards. He then discovered that the oil drops'
electric charge was always a multiple of the same number: 1.602 · 10⁻¹⁹.
The elementary charge had been discovered!

::: härledning "Determining the elementary charge"
We apply a [[voltage|Energy difference per charge between two points. SI unit volt (V).]] across two charged plates at a certain distance from
each other. We then spray in oil drops, some of which will hover.

The oil drop hovers in equilibrium when the upward electric force balances the downward gravitational force.
:::

Since the oil drops are stationary, the upward electric force
$F_e$ must be equal to the downward gravitational force $F_G$, so

$$
F_e = F_G \qquad (1)
$$

From earlier theory we have $F_e = Q \cdot \mathbb{E}$ and
$F_G = m \cdot g$. Substituting into (1) gives

$$
Q \cdot \mathbb{E} = m \cdot g
$$

We solve for the charge *Q* and get

$$
Q = \frac{m \cdot g}{\mathbb{E}}
$$

The electric field strength $\mathbb{E}$ can be determined with the formula
$\mathbb{E} = U/d$, which inserted above gives

$$
Q = \frac{m \cdot g}{\frac{U}{d}} = \frac{m \cdot g \cdot d}{U}
$$

(the mass *m* of the oil drop was determined from its fall time).

Repeated experiments gave charges of

- −1.6 · 10⁻¹⁹ C
- −3.2 · 10⁻¹⁹ C
- −4.8 · 10⁻¹⁹ C
- −6.4 · 10⁻¹⁹ C

**Conclusion:** All charges are made up of a certain number of
elementary charges with the magnitude 1.6 · 10⁻¹⁹ C. This is the charge of the electron and
the proton!
:::

::: textlosning
Since the oil drop hovers, the upward electric force
$F_e = Q \cdot \mathbb{E}$ must be equal to the downward gravitational force
$F_G = m \cdot g$. This gives

$$
F_e = F_G \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad Q \cdot \mathbb{E} = m \cdot g
$$

We solve for the charge *Q* and get

$$
Q = \frac{m \cdot g}{\mathbb{E}}
$$

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
m = 2.15 \cdot 10^{-14}\ \mathrm{kg} \\
g \approx 9.82\ \mathrm{N/kg} \\
\mathbb{E} = \dfrac{U}{d} = \dfrac{2.2 \cdot 10^3\ \mathrm{V}}{5.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{m}} = 440\,000\ \mathrm{V/m}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
Q = \frac{2.15 \cdot 10^{-14} \cdot 9.82}{440\,000}
= 4.798\ldots \cdot 10^{-19}\ \mathrm{C}
$$

To get the number of electrons *n*, we divide this total charge on
the oil drop *Q* by the charge of one electron $q_e$.

$$
n = \frac{Q}{q_e} = \frac{4.798\ldots \cdot 10^{-19}}{1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}}
= 2.995\ldots \approx 3
$$

**Answer:** 3 electrons
:::
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "What the experiment showed"
- Robert Millikan, 1909. The **elementary charge** was determined here for the first
  time.
- Oil drops were sprayed into a field between two plates. Some drops
  **hovered**.
- The charge of the drops was always a **multiple** of the same number:
  $1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\ \mathrm{C}$.
:::

::: sampunkt "The equilibrium"
- A hovering drop has $F_e = F_G$.
- So $Q \cdot \mathbb{E} = m \cdot g$.
- Solve for: $Q = \dfrac{m \cdot g}{\mathbb{E}}$.
:::

::: sampunkt "With the plate separation"
- Substitute $\mathbb{E} = \dfrac{U}{d}$, which gives
  $Q = \dfrac{m \cdot g \cdot d}{U}$.
- The mass of the drop was determined from its fall time.
:::

::: sampunkt "The conclusion"
- The measured values were
  $-1.6 \cdot 10^{-19}$,
  $-3.2 \cdot 10^{-19}$,
  $-4.8 \cdot 10^{-19}\ \mathrm{C}$ and so on.
- **All charges are whole multiples of the elementary charge.** Charge
  is therefore quantised.
- Number of excess electrons: $n = \dfrac{Q}{e}$.
:::
:::
