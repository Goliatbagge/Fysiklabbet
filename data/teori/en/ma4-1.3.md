# Solving trigonometric equations

At an earlier level of mathematics we solved trigonometric equations in
the interval $0^\circ \leq v \leq 360^\circ$ with a calculator using the principle
below.

::: formel "Solving trigonometric equations in the interval 0° ≤ v ≤ 360° with a calculator"
1. Isolate the trigonometric function.
2. Solve the equation as usual, using inverse functions.
3. Find the second solution. For an equation with

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{sine:}\quad & v_2 = 180^\circ - v_1 \\[2pt]
\text{cosine:}\quad & v_2 = 360^\circ - v_1 \\[2pt]
\text{tangent:}\quad & v_2 = v_1 + 180^\circ
\end{aligned}
$$
:::

Now we will find **all** solutions to trigonometric equations. A
trigonometric equation has infinitely many solutions: the unit circle repeats
itself every turn, so to each solution we can add or subtract a
whole number of turns.

For **sine**, in the interval $0^\circ \leq v \leq 360^\circ$ we get
two solutions: $v_1$ and $180^\circ - v_1$. To get all solutions we can
add or subtract a whole number $n$ of turns on the unit circle, that is
$n \cdot 360^\circ$.

For **cosine**, in the interval $0^\circ \leq v \leq 360^\circ$ we
get two solutions: $v_1$ and $360^\circ - v_1 = -v_1$. To get all
solutions we can, in the same way as for sine, add or subtract
a whole number $n$ of turns on the unit circle, that is $n \cdot 360^\circ$.

For **tangent** we showed at an earlier level that the period is $180^\circ$.
So for a solution $v_1$ we can add or subtract a whole number of
$180^\circ$, that is $n \cdot 180^\circ$.

We summarise the conclusions above and get three different ways of finding
all solutions, depending on which trigonometric function we have.

::: formel "Solving trigonometric equations"
If $v$ is a solution to $\sin x = a$, then

$$
x = v + n \cdot 360^\circ \qquad \text{and} \qquad x = 180^\circ - v + n \cdot 360^\circ
$$

If $v$ is a solution to $\cos x = a$, then

$$
x = \pm v + n \cdot 360^\circ
$$

If $v$ is a solution to $\tan x = a$, then

$$
x = v + n \cdot 180^\circ
$$

where $n$ is an integer.
:::

::: exempel "Example 1: Solve cos x = 0.8. Answer to one decimal place."
**Solve the equation $\cos x = 0.8$.**

::: handskrift
typ: cosekvation
:::

::: textlosning
We have an equation with cosine, so we take $\pm$ the solution from the calculator and
add the term $n \cdot 360^\circ$.

$$
\cos^{-1}(0.8) = 36.869\ldots^\circ
$$

$$
x = \pm 36.869\ldots^\circ + n \cdot 360^\circ \approx \pm 36.9^\circ + n \cdot 360^\circ
$$

**Answer:** $x \approx \pm 36.9^\circ + n \cdot 360^\circ$
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 2: Solve sin 4x = −0.3. Answer to one decimal place."
**Solve the equation $\sin 4x = -0.3$.**

::: handskrift
typ: sinekvationfyra
:::

::: textlosning
We have an equation with sine, so we take the solution from the calculator and add
the term $n \cdot 360^\circ$. Then we take $180^\circ$ minus the solution
from the calculator and add the term $n \cdot 360^\circ$.

$$
\sin^{-1}(-0.3) = -17.457\ldots^\circ
$$

**First solution.** We isolate $x$ by dividing the whole equation by 4:

$$
4x = -17.457\ldots^\circ + n \cdot 360^\circ
$$

$$
\frac{4x}{4} = \frac{-17.457\ldots^\circ}{4} + \frac{n \cdot 360^\circ}{4} \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad x = -4.364\ldots^\circ + n \cdot 90^\circ \approx -4.4^\circ + n \cdot 90^\circ
$$

**Second solution.** We use $180^\circ$ minus the calculator's solution:

$$
4x = 180^\circ - (-17.457\ldots^\circ) + n \cdot 360^\circ = 197.457\ldots^\circ + n \cdot 360^\circ
$$

$$
\frac{4x}{4} = \frac{197.457\ldots^\circ}{4} + \frac{n \cdot 360^\circ}{4} \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad x = 49.364\ldots^\circ + n \cdot 90^\circ \approx 49.4^\circ + n \cdot 90^\circ
$$

**Answer:** $x \approx -4.4^\circ + n \cdot 90^\circ$ and $x \approx 49.4^\circ + n \cdot 90^\circ$
:::
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Infinitely many solutions"
- The unit circle repeats itself every turn, so a trigonometric equation has infinitely many solutions.
- All solutions are given with a **period term**, where $n$ is an integer.
:::

::: sampunkt "All solutions"
- $\sin x = a$ with the solution $v$: $x = v + n \cdot 360^\circ$ **and** $x = 180^\circ - v + n \cdot 360^\circ$.
- $\cos x = a$ with the solution $v$: $x = \pm v + n \cdot 360^\circ$.
- $\tan x = a$ with the solution $v$: $x = v + n \cdot 180^\circ$.
:::

::: sampunkt "Why they differ"
- Sine has **two** different solutions per turn, reflected in the vertical axis.
- Cosine has two solutions reflected in the horizontal axis, which gives the compact $\pm v$.
- Tangent has the period $180^\circ$, so only one term.
:::

::: sampunkt "Angle with a coefficient"
- If it says $\sin 4x = a$, first solve for the whole argument $4x$, **with** the period term.
- Then divide the **whole** equation, period term included, by the coefficient.
- $4x = -17.5^\circ + n \cdot 360^\circ$ gives $x \approx -4.4^\circ + n \cdot 90^\circ$.
:::
