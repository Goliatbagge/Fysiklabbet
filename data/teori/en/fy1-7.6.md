### Ammeter

A current meter is called an **ammeter**. The [[current|Amount of charge per unit time: *I* = *Q* / *t*. SI unit ampere (A).]] that flows through the component
being measured must also pass through the ammeter. So the ammeter is connected
**in series** with the component. So that the ammeter does not affect the current
through the component, it must have a **low [[resistance|Opposition to electric current. According to Ohm's law: *U* = *R* · *I*. SI unit ohm (Ω).]]**.

### Voltmeter

A voltage meter is called a **voltmeter**. The [[voltage|Energy difference per charge between two points. SI unit volt (V).]] is measured between two points.
We say that we measure the voltage **across** a component. So the voltmeter is connected
**in parallel** with the component. So that the voltmeter does not affect
the current through the component, it must have a **high resistance**.

Three lamps in series. The voltage *U* of the battery is shared as
partial voltages *U*₁, *U*₂, *U*₃ across the lamps.
:::

Since the current can only take one path, **the current is the same** through each
lamp. The voltage from the battery is written *U*. The partial voltages across each
lamp are written $U_1$, $U_2$ and $U_3$. The sum of the partial voltages is equal
to the total voltage from the battery.

$$
U = U_1 + U_2 + U_3
$$

If one lamp breaks, the whole circuit is broken and all the lamps go out.

Three lamps in parallel. The total current *I* divides into
the partial currents *I*₁, *I*₂, *I*₃ through the branches.

All the lamps are connected directly to the battery and therefore get **the same
voltage**. The current out of the battery is written *I*. It then divides
between the different branches. The partial currents through each lamp are written
$I_1$, $I_2$, $I_3$. The sum of the partial currents is equal to the total
current out of the battery (according to Kirchhoff's first law).

$$
I = I_1 + I_2 + I_3
$$

If one lamp breaks, the current can still pass through the other lamps (which will then
shine unchanged).

::: formel "The brightest lamps"
If we have **identical** lamps, the lamp that gets the largest current
shines brightest.
:::

**a)** The lamp that shines brightest is the one that gets the largest current. The current
out of the battery branches off to the upper part with lamp 2 and to the

most dimly.

**Answer:** Lamps 3 and 4

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Circuit diagram"
- A simplified diagram of how the components are connected, with their own
  symbols for wire, voltage source, lamp, resistor, switch,
  voltmeter and ammeter.
- On the battery symbol the **long line is the positive terminal**.
:::

::: sampunkt "The meters"
- An **ammeter** measures current and is connected **in series**. It must have a **low**
  resistance, so that it does not affect the current.
- A **voltmeter** measures voltage **across** a component and is connected
  **in parallel**. It must have a **high** resistance.
:::

::: sampunkt "Series connection"
- The components are in a row, and the current can only take one path.
- **The current is equally large** through all the components.
- The voltage is **shared**: $U = U_1 + U_2 + U_3$.
- If one lamp breaks, the circuit is broken and **all** of them go out.
:::

::: sampunkt "Parallel connection"
- The components are across each other, all directly connected to the battery.
- **The voltage is equally large** across all the components.
- The current is **divided**: $I = I_1 + I_2 + I_3$.
- If one lamp breaks, the others carry on shining **unchanged**.
:::

::: sampunkt "Which lamp shines brightest?"
- Among **identical** lamps, the one that gets the **largest current** shines brightest.
- If one more lamp is connected **in series**, the first one shines **more dimly**, since
  the resistance increases.
- If it is connected **in parallel**, the first one shines **unchanged**, since
  the voltage across it is the same.
:::
:::
