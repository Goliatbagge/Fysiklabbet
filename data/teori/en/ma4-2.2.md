# Interpretations of the derivative

We review how the derivative can be interpreted, from Mathematics continuation level 1c. Before we extend
the derivative to new types of function, it is worth reminding ourselves what a
value of the derivative really means, both graphically and in applications.

**Graphically**, the derivative corresponds to the slope of the tangent (gradient
or $k$-value) at a point. A tangent is a straight line that touches the curve at
a single point, and the value of the derivative at that point is exactly the slope of the
straight line.

**In applications**, the derivative stands for a rate of change.
If for example we have the distance as a function of time, $s(t)$, and differentiate, we
get $s'(t)$, which stands for how the distance changes with time, that is
$\dfrac{s}{t}$, which corresponds to a velocity.

::: formel "The derivative in motion"
$$
\begin{aligned}
s(t) &\qquad \text{distance as a function of time}\\[2pt]
s'(t) = v(t) &\qquad \text{velocity as a function of time}\\[2pt]
s''(t) = v'(t) = a(t) &\qquad \text{acceleration as a function of time}
\end{aligned}
$$
:::

**The unit of the derivative** is "the unit on the $y$-axis (the function, the dependent
variable)" per "the unit on the $x$-axis (the variable, the independent variable)".
If for example $V(t)$ stands for the number of litres in a bathtub after $t$ minutes,
then the unit of the derivative $V'(t)$ is litres/minute.

::: exempel "Example 1: Find the equation of the tangent"
**Find the equation of the tangent at the point where $x = 3$ on the curve
$y = x^2 + 4x - 7$.**

::: handskrift
typ: tangentekv
:::

::: textlosning
A tangent is a straight line and can be described by the equation of a straight line
$y = kx + m$. We need to find $k$ and $m$.

The $k$-value stands for the slope of the tangent, which is the same thing as the derivative at
the point where $x = 3$, that is $k = y'(3)$. We differentiate and substitute
$x = 3$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= x^2 + 4x - 7\\
y' &= 2x + 4\\
k = y'(3) &= 2\cdot 3 + 4 = 6 + 4 = 10
\end{aligned}
$$

Substituting $k = 10$ into the equation of a straight line gives $y = 10x + m$.

Finally we find $m$ by substituting a point that the tangent passes
through. The tangent meets the curve $y = x^2 + 4x - 7$ where $x = 3$, so we substitute
$x = 3$ to get the point's $y$-coordinate:

$$
y = 3^2 + 4\cdot 3 - 7 = 9 + 12 - 7 = 14
$$

So the tangent passes through the point $(3,\ 14)$. Substituting $x = 3$ and
$y = 14$ into $y = 10x + m$ gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
14 &= 10\cdot 3 + m\\
14 &= 30 + m\\
14 - 30 &= m\\
m &= -16
\end{aligned}
$$

Substituting $k = 10$ and $m = -16$ into the equation of a straight line gives the tangent's
equation $y = 10x - 16$.

**Answer:** $y = 10x - 16$
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 2: Interpret a value of the derivative"
**The amount of water in a bathtub is given by $V(t)$ litres after $t$ minutes. What
does it mean that $V'(3) = 8$?**

::: handskrift
typ: tolkaderivata
:::

::: textlosning
The derivative stands for a rate of change, and the unit of $V'(t)$ is
litres per minute. So the value $V'(3) = 8$ is read off at the point where $t = 3$.

It means that at the time $t = 3$ minutes the amount of water in the bathtub is increasing
at the rate of $8$ litres/minute.

**Answer:** At $t = 3$ min the amount of water is increasing by $8$ litres/minute.
:::
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Two interpretations"
- **Graphically:** the derivative is the slope of the tangent, that is its $k$-value, at the point.
- **In applications:** the derivative is a rate of change.
- $f(a)$ is the value, $f'(a)$ is how fast the value changes.
:::

::: sampunkt "The unit"
- The unit is the unit on the $y$-axis per the unit on the $x$-axis.
- Litres and minutes give litres per minute.
- A negative value of the derivative means that the quantity is **decreasing**.
:::

::: sampunkt "Motion"
- $s(t)$ is the distance as a function of time.
- $s'(t) = v(t)$ is the velocity.
- $s''(t) = v'(t) = a(t)$ is the acceleration.
:::

::: sampunkt "The equation of the tangent"
1. Differentiate and calculate $k = f'(a)$.
2. Calculate the point's $y$-coordinate, $f(a)$.
3. Substitute the point into $y = kx + m$ and solve for $m$.
- For $y = x^2 + 4x - 7$ at $x = 3$: $k = 10$, the point $(3, 14)$, so $y = 10x - 16$.
:::

:::
