# Energy and work

## Energy

The potential to produce motion is called **energy**, written *E*, and its [[SI unit|The International System of Units, with seven base units (m, kg, s, A, K, mol, cd).]] is
J (joule).

Energy can appear in different forms, for example:

- **[[thermal energy|Energy transferred between objects at different temperatures. Measured in joules (J).]]** (in combustion or from [[friction|The force that opposes motion between two surfaces in contact.]])
- **radiant energy** (from the Sun)
- **electrical energy** (electrons that move)
- **[[kinetic energy|The energy an object has because of its motion: *E*ₖ = ½ · *m* · *v*².]]** (a car driving, wind or rushing water)
- **[[potential energy|The energy an object has because of its position in a force field, for example *E*ₚ = *m* · *g* · *h* in a gravitational field.]]** (energy stored by increased height)
- **chemical energy** (in the food we eat, fuels or processes in batteries)
- **nuclear energy** (energy stored in atoms)

Energy can be converted from one form into another.

## Work

The energy that is converted when a [[force|An influence that can change an object's motion or shape. SI unit newton (N). A vector quantity.]] causes a [[displacement|The vector between the start and end positions. Differs from distance, which is the total length of path.]] is called
**[[work|Energy transferred when a force acts along a distance: *W* = *F* · *s* · cos *α*. SI unit joule (J).]]**, written *W*, and its SI unit is Nm
(newton metre) or J (joule). Work requires that we subject an object
to a force that gives a **displacement in the direction of the force**.

Even though work has the same unit as energy, it is **not** a form of energy
but rather a process in which energy changes from one form into another.

::: formel "Work"
$$
W = F_s \cdot s
$$

where

- *W* = work (Nm or J)
- $F_s$ = the force in the direction of motion (N)
- *s* = [[distance|The total length of path an object has moved. A scalar quantity, measured in metres.]] (m)
:::

::: demo "Think about it: Work"
When do you do the most work: if you lift a 1-kilogram mass 2 metres up or
a 2-kilogram mass 1 metre up? The answer is that **the work is the same**. It is
easier to lift 1 kg, but the distance is longer. How much work is
done therefore depends on how large a force you need to apply **and** how
far you move the object.
:::

::: exempel "Example 1: Energy conversions"
**Describe which energy conversions take place when you a) push a box across
the floor, b) press a switch so that a lamp lights up.**

::: handskrift
typ: energiomvandling
:::

::: textlosning
**a)** Chemical energy in your muscles is converted and becomes kinetic energy (the box
moves) and thermal energy (frictional heat is produced against the floor).

**Answer:** Chemical energy → kinetic energy + thermal energy

**b)** When you press the switch, electrical energy inside the lamp is converted into
radiant energy (light) and thermal energy. Note that it is **not** chemical
energy in your fingers that is converted into electrical energy. The chemical
energy is only used to physically change the position of the switch.

**Answer:** Electrical energy → radiant energy (light) + thermal energy
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 2: Sanna pushes a park bench"
**Sanna pushes a park bench 3.0 metres forwards with a force of 240 N. How much work
does she do?**

::: handskrift
typ: parkbank
:::

::: textlosning
We write the formula for work.

$$
W = F_s \cdot s
$$

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
F_s = 240\ \mathrm{N} \\
s = 3.0\ \mathrm{m}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
W = 240 \cdot 3.0 = 720\ \mathrm{Nm}
$$

**Answer:** 720 Nm
:::
:::

::: handskrift
typ: dravagn

::: textlosning
We write the formula for work.

$$
W = F_s \cdot s
$$

The cart moves **to the right**. Therefore only
the force component to the right *F*₂ does work, and it is the force
that corresponds to $F_s$. We calculate *F*₂ with trigonometry.

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
F_s = F_2 = F \cdot \cos v = 35\ \mathrm{N} \cdot \cos 26^{\circ} = 31.457\ldots\ \mathrm{N} \\
s = 100\ \mathrm{m}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
W = 31.457\ldots \cdot 100 = 3\,145.779\ldots\ \mathrm{Nm}
\approx 3\,100\ \mathrm{Nm} = 3.1\ \mathrm{kNm}
$$

**Answer:** 3.1 kNm
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 4: Martin carries the box"
**Martin walks with a box 30 metres to the right at [[constant|A value that does not change. For example the acceleration due to gravity *g* or the speed of light *c*.]] [[velocity|Change of position per unit time, with direction (a vector). SI unit m/s.]]. He holds
it up with an upward force of 180 N. How much work does he do?**

::: handskrift
typ: barlada
:::

::: textlosning
We write the formula for work.

$$
W = F_s \cdot s
$$

The direction of motion is **to the right at constant [[speed|The magnitude of velocity, without direction (a scalar). Measured in m/s or km/h.]]**. If we ignore
[[air resistance|The force from the air that slows down objects moving through it. Depends on the object's shape, size and speed.]], the force in the direction of motion is therefore $F_s$ = 0! Martin
does apply an upward force to hold up the box, but no motion
takes place in that direction. That the force in the direction of motion is 0 means that
the work is also equal to 0.

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
F_s = 0 \\
s = 30\ \mathrm{m}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
W = 0 \cdot 30 = 0
$$

**Answer:** No work is done (0 Nm).
:::
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Energy"
- **Energy** ($E$) is the potential to produce motion. The SI unit is J (joule).
- Energy can be **converted** between forms, but never disappear.
:::

::: sampunkt "Forms of energy"
- **Thermal energy**: combustion, friction.
- **Radiant energy**: the Sun, light.
- **Electrical energy**: electrons in motion.
- **Kinetic energy**: a car, wind, rushing water.
- **Potential energy**: stored by increased height.
- **Chemical energy**: food, fuel, batteries.
- **Nuclear energy**: stored in atomic nuclei.
:::

::: sampunkt "Work"
- **Work** ($W$) is the energy that is converted when a force gives a
  displacement. The SI unit is Nm or J.
- $W = F_s \cdot s$, where $F_s$ is the force **in the direction of motion**.
- Work is not a form of energy, but a **process** in which energy changes form.
:::

::: sampunkt "Only the force along the motion counts"
- If the force is at an angle $v$, the component along the motion is used:
  $F_s = F \cdot \cos v$.
- If the force is **perpendicular** to the motion, the work is **zero**. Carrying a
  box straight ahead does no work, however heavy it is.
:::

::: sampunkt "Force times distance"
- Lifting $1\ \mathrm{kg}$ two metres gives **the same** work as lifting
  $2\ \mathrm{kg}$ one metre.
- Both the size of the force and the length of the distance matter.
:::
:::
