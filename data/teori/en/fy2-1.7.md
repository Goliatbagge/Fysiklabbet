# Conical pendulum

A [[pendulum|An object that swings back and forth about an equilibrium position. Simple pendulum: *T* = 2*π* · √(*L* / *g*).]] in which the string forms a cone is called a **conical pendulum**.
Instead of swinging back and forth like an ordinary pendulum, the
bob sweeps round in a horizontal circle while the string forms a cone.

::: minisim
typ: koniskpendel
:::

::: formel "Period of a conical pendulum"
$$
T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{l \cdot \cos \alpha}{g}}
$$

where

- *T* = [[period|The time for one complete oscillation or one complete wave: *T* = 1 / *f*.]] (s)
- *l* = length of the pendulum (m)
- *α* = angle of deflection from the vertical
- *g* = [[acceleration due to gravity|The acceleration a freely falling object gets from the gravitational force, about 9.82 m/s² at the Earth's surface. Denoted *g*.]] (m/s²) (9.82 m/s² in Sweden)

::: härledning
The bob is acted on by two forces: the [[gravitational force|The force with which the Earth pulls all objects towards its centre. The gravitational force gives an object its weight and is directed straight down towards the centre of the Earth.]] $F_G = m \cdot g$ straight down and
the [[tension|The force acting along a taut rope, line or spring.]] $F_S$ along the string (see the figure below). Together they must give a
resultant force directed in towards the centre of the circular path: the
[[centripetal force|The force needed to keep an object in circular motion, directed towards the centre of the circle.]] $F_C$. We therefore resolve the tension $F_S$ into a
vertical and a horizontal component:

Vertically, the bob does not move. The vertical component of the tension therefore
supports the gravitational force:

$$
F_S \cdot \cos \alpha = m \cdot g \qquad (1)
$$

Horizontally, the horizontal component of the tension makes up the whole centripetal force.
With $F_C = \dfrac{4\pi^{2} \cdot m \cdot r}{T^{2}}$ from the section on
circular motion we get

$$
F_S \cdot \sin \alpha = \frac{4\pi^{2} \cdot m \cdot r}{T^{2}} \qquad (2)
$$

We divide (2) by (1), side by side. Then both the tension
$F_S$ and the mass *m* cancel:

$$
\frac{F_S \cdot \sin \alpha}{F_S \cdot \cos \alpha} = \frac{\tfrac{4\pi^{2} \cdot m \cdot r}{T^{2}}}{m \cdot g}
\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad
\frac{\sin \alpha}{\cos \alpha} = \frac{4\pi^{2} \cdot r}{g \cdot T^{2}}
$$

The string, the vertical line from the ceiling and the radius of the path form a right-angled
triangle, in which the length of the string *l* is the hypotenuse:

From the triangle, the radius of the path is $r = l \cdot \sin \alpha$. Substituting gives

$$
\frac{\sin \alpha}{\cos \alpha} = \frac{4\pi^{2} \cdot l \cdot \sin \alpha}{g \cdot T^{2}}
$$

We divide both sides by $\sin \alpha$ and solve for $T^{2}$:

$$
\frac{1}{\cos \alpha} = \frac{4\pi^{2} \cdot l}{g \cdot T^{2}}
\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad
T^{2} = \frac{4\pi^{2} \cdot l \cdot \cos \alpha}{g}
$$

Taking the square root of both sides gives the period:

$$
T = \sqrt{\frac{4\pi^{2} \cdot l \cdot \cos \alpha}{g}} = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{l \cdot \cos \alpha}{g}}
$$

Q.E.D.

::: exempel "Example 1: Chair swing ride"
**At an amusement park there is a chair swing ride where the seats hang from chains 5.0 metres
long. When it spins at a certain [[velocity|Change of position per unit time, with direction (a vector). SI unit m/s.]], the chains form an
angle of 30° with the vertical. How long does it take for one of the swings
to go round one full revolution?**

::: handskrift
typ: slanggunga
:::

::: textlosning
We set up the formula for the period of a conical pendulum.

$$
T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{l \cdot \cos \alpha}{g}}
$$

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
l = 5.0\ \mathrm{m} \\
\alpha = 30^{\circ} \\
g \approx 9.82\ \mathrm{m/s^{2}}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{5.0 \cdot \cos 30^{\circ}}{9.82}} = 4.172\ldots\ \mathrm{s} \approx 4.2\ \mathrm{s}
$$

**Answer:** 4.2 s
:::
:::

::: textlosning
We start from the formula for the period of a conical pendulum.

$$
T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{l \cdot \cos \alpha}{g}}
$$

The vertical part of the string, $l \cdot \cos \alpha$, is exactly the height $h$ of the path below
the ceiling mount. We replace it and then solve for $g$ by squaring both
sides:

$$
T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{h}{g}}
\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad
T^{2} = \frac{4\pi^{2} \cdot h}{g}
\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad
g = \frac{4\pi^{2} \cdot h}{T^{2}}
$$

Ten revolutions took $22.0\ \mathrm{s}$, so one revolution takes a tenth of the time.

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
T = \dfrac{22.0\ \mathrm{s}}{10} = 2.20\ \mathrm{s} \\[6pt]
h = 1.20\ \mathrm{m}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
g = \frac{4\pi^{2} \cdot 1.20}{2.20^{2}} = 9.788\ldots\ \mathrm{m/s^{2}} \approx 9.79\ \mathrm{m/s^{2}}
$$

Both $1.20\ \mathrm{m}$ and $22.0\ \mathrm{s}$ have three significant figures, so
the answer gets three. The table value is $9.82\ \mathrm{m/s^{2}}$: the measurement is
within half a per cent of it. Notice that neither the mass of the cow
nor the radius of the path needed to be measured. Both disappeared from the formula.

**Answer:** $g \approx 9.79\ \mathrm{m/s^{2}}$

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Conical pendulum"
- The bob sweeps round in a **horizontal circle** while the string forms a
  cone.
- Unlike an ordinary pendulum, it does not swing back and forth.
:::

::: sampunkt "The forces"
- Only two forces act: the **gravitational force** $F_G = mg$ straight down and
  the **tension** $F_S$ along the string.
- Their resultant is the **centripetal force**, directed in towards the centre of
  the circle.
- Resolve the tension into a vertical and a horizontal component.
:::

::: sampunkt "The two equations"
- **Vertically** the bob does not move, so
  $F_S \cos \alpha = mg$.
- **Horizontally** the horizontal component makes up the whole centripetal force,
  $F_S \sin \alpha = \dfrac{4\pi^{2} m r}{T^{2}}$.
- If they are divided side by side, both $F_S$ and $m$ cancel.
:::

::: sampunkt "The period"
- $T = 2\pi \sqrt{\dfrac{l \cos \alpha}{g}}$
- $l$ is the length of the pendulum and $\alpha$ the angle of deflection from the vertical.
- **The mass plays no part**, just as for an ordinary pendulum.
- A larger angle of deflection gives a **shorter** period, since
  $\cos \alpha$ decreases.
- The vertical part of the string is the height below the mount, $h = l \cos \alpha$. Measure
  $h$ and $T$ and you get $g = \dfrac{4\pi^{2} h}{T^{2}}$.
:::

::: sampunkt "The radius of the path"
- The radius of the circle is $r = l \sin \alpha$, not the length of the pendulum.
- It is a common mistake to substitute $l$ instead of $r$.
:::
:::
