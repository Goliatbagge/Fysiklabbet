# Induced current and Lenz's law

We have seen earlier that a conductor moving in a [[magnetic field|Region around a magnet or current-carrying conductor where other magnetic objects are acted on by a force.]] gets an
induced [[voltage|Energy difference per charge between two points. SI unit volt (V).]], an **emf**, between its ends. If a circuit is connected to
the ends of the conductor, a [[current|Amount of charge per unit time: *I* = *Q* / *t*. SI unit ampere (A).]] will flow, with the conductor as the voltage source.
A current that arises when a circuit is connected to an induced voltage
is called an **induced current**. How large the current is, is determined by **[[Ohm's law|The relationship between voltage, current and resistance: *U* = *R* · *I*.]]**.

::: formel "Ohm's law (revision)"
$$
U = R \cdot I \qquad \text{or}\qquad e = R \cdot I
$$

where

- *U*, *e* = voltage or induced voltage (V)
- *R* = [[resistance|Opposition to electric current. According to Ohm's law: *U* = *R* · *I*. SI unit ohm (Ω).]] (Ω)
- *I* = current (A)
:::

::: textlosning
**a)** We solve for the current *I* from Ohm's law $e = R \cdot I$:

$$
I = \frac{e}{R} \qquad (1)
$$

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
e = l \cdot v \cdot B = 0.15 \cdot 2.5 \cdot 0.80 = 0.30\ \mathrm{V} \\
R = 0.75\ \mathrm{\Omega}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
I = \frac{0.30}{0.75} = 0.40\ \mathrm{A}
$$

**Answer:** 0.40 A

**b)** We are looking for the magnetic force on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field.

$$
F = B \cdot I \cdot l
$$

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
B = 0.80\ \mathrm{T} \\
I = 0.40\ \mathrm{A}\ (\text{from part a}) \\
l = 0.15\ \mathrm{m}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
F = 0.80 \cdot 0.40 \cdot 0.15 = 0.048\ \mathrm{N} = 48\ \mathrm{mN}
$$

The direction of the force is given by the right-hand rule. The current (thumb) is upwards
through the conductor and the magnetic field (index finger) points inwards. The middle finger
shows that the direction of the force is **to the left**.

**Answer:** 48 mN to the left.
:::
:::

We note that the induced current in the example above gives rise
to a force that **opposes the motion** (the conductor is pulled to the right and the
induced force is to the left).

::: demo "Demonstration: Eddy currents"
We let a non-magnetic and a magnetic metal cylinder fall through a copper
tube each. The **magnetic metal cylinder falls more slowly**!
**Eddy currents** induced in the copper tube create a magnetic field that
opposes the fall.
:::

The induced current in the demonstration above gives rise to a
magnetic field that **opposes the change in the magnetic field** (an increase in
the magnetic field, for example upwards, induces a magnetic field downwards).

## Lenz's law

Induced currents arise either from a conductor that **moves in a
magnetic field** or from a **change in the magnetic field** near a
conductor. Whatever the induction phenomenon, currents are induced with
a direction that **opposes the cause** of their origin. This is called
**Lenz's law**.

::: formel "Lenz's law"
An induced current has such a direction that **the cause of its origin
is opposed**.
:::

::: demo "Demonstration: Thomson's ring"
A long iron core is placed in a coil. We then thread an **aluminium ring**
over the iron core so that it rests on the coil. We send a short current through the coil
and the aluminium ring **is thrown upwards**!

We repeat the experiment with a:

- **plastic ring** (nothing happens: not conducting)
- **aluminium ring with a slit right through it** (nothing happens: the current cannot
  go round)
- **aluminium ring half as tall** (is thrown half as high: halved
  current, halved force)
- **copper ring** (is thrown half as high: double the weight because of the higher
  [[density|Mass per unit volume: *ρ* = *m* / *V*. SI unit kg/m³. Decides whether an object floats in a medium.]] halves the height)

**Explanation:** When the current flows through the coil a magnetic field is created that
is carried through the iron core and creates a magnetic field inside the aluminium ring.
According to Lenz's law a current is induced in the ring that creates a magnetic field
**opposite** to the one from the coil. The ring now acts as an electromagnet
with reversed polarity. Since like poles repel and the ring rests
loosely, it is thrown upwards.
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Induced current"
- If a circuit is connected to the induced voltage, an **induced
  current** flows. The conductor then acts as a voltage source.
- Its size is given by **Ohm's law**: $e = R \cdot I$.
:::

::: sampunkt "Lenz's law"
- **An induced current has such a direction that the cause of its
  origin is opposed.**
- If the conductor is pulled to the right, the magnetic force on it is to the left.
- If a field increases upwards, a field downwards is induced.
:::

::: sampunkt "Two ways to induce"
- A **conductor moving** in a magnetic field.
- A **change in the magnetic field** near a conductor.
- Lenz's law applies in both cases.
:::

::: sampunkt "Eddy currents"
- A magnet falling through a copper tube falls **more slowly**.
- Eddy currents are induced in the tube and create a field that opposes
  the fall.
:::

::: sampunkt "Thomson's ring"
- An aluminium ring over a coil **is thrown off** when the current is switched on.
- The ring becomes an electromagnet with **reversed** polarity, and like poles
  repel.
- A plastic ring does not move, because it does not conduct. A ring with a
  **slit** does not move either, because the current cannot go round.
:::

::: sampunkt "The energy principle behind Lenz's law"
- If the induced current strengthened its own cause, energy would
  be created out of nothing.
- So Lenz's law is the principle of conservation of energy in magnetic form.
:::
:::
