# Number sets and negative numbers

## Number sets

All numbers can be divided into so-called **[[number sets|A collection of numbers with a common property, for example all integers or all numbers on the number line.]]**.

::: formel "Number sets"
**Natural numbers:** contain all non-negative integers, for example 0, 1, 2, 3 …
and so on. The set of natural numbers is denoted $\mathbb{N}$ and can be written
$\mathbb{N} = \{0,\ 1,\ 2,\ 3,\ \ldots\}$.

**Integers:** contain all whole numbers, for example −3, 0, 5 and 25. The set of integers
is denoted $\mathbb{Z}$ and can be written
$\mathbb{Z} = \{\ldots,\ -2,\ -1,\ 0,\ 1,\ 2,\ \ldots\}$.

**Rational numbers:** contain all numbers that can be written as a fraction,
for example $\dfrac{1}{3}$, −0.25 and 5. The set of rational numbers is denoted
$\mathbb{Q}$ and can be written $\mathbb{Q} = \{$all numbers that can be written in
the form $\dfrac{a}{b}$ where $a$ and $b$ are integers, $b \neq 0\}$.

**Real numbers:** contain all numbers on the number line. This also
includes the **[[irrational numbers|Numbers that cannot be written as a fraction of two integers, for example π and √2. Their decimal expansion is infinite and never repeats.]]**, that is, numbers that *cannot* be written as a
fraction, for example π (pi) and $\sqrt{2}$. Irrational numbers can also be seen as numbers with
an infinite decimal expansion that does not repeat. The set of real numbers
is denoted $\mathbb{R}$ and can be written $\mathbb{R} = \{$all numbers on the
number line$\}$.
:::

So all natural numbers are also integers, rational numbers and real numbers.
All integers are also rational numbers and real numbers, but not necessarily
natural numbers.

::: exempel "Example 1: Rational number that is not an integer"
**Give a rational number that is not an integer.**

::: handskrift
typ: talmangd
:::

::: textlosning
A rational number is a number that can be written as a fraction $\dfrac{a}{b}$ where
$a$ and $b$ are integers and $b \neq 0$. If we choose, for example, $\dfrac{1}{3}$ we get
a number that lies between the integers 0 and 1. It is therefore rational but
not an integer.

The task has infinitely many correct answers: every fraction that **does not divide evenly**
will do, and it makes no difference whether the number is written as a fraction or as a decimal.

Numbers that work as answers:

- $\dfrac{5}{2}$, which is the same number as 2.5
- $-\dfrac{3}{4}$, since negative numbers are also rational
- 0.7, which can be written $\dfrac{7}{10}$
- $-1.25$, which can be written $-\dfrac{5}{4}$

Numbers that do **not** work as answers:

- 4 and $-6$, which are integers
- $\dfrac{8}{2}$, since the fraction divides evenly and is therefore the integer 4
- $\sqrt{2}$ and $\pi$, which cannot be written as a fraction of integers and
  are therefore not even rational

**Answer:** For example $\dfrac{1}{3}$ ($\dfrac{5}{2}$, $-\dfrac{3}{4}$ and 0.7 are also
correct answers)
:::
:::

## Negative numbers

Numbers that are less than 0 are called **negative numbers**. On the number line they lie to
the left of 0. Negative numbers are written with a minus sign and often in brackets in
calculations.

::: tips "Review: inequality signs"
Signs that show that two sides are of different size are called **inequality signs** and
are written < or >.

- < means "less than"
- \> means "greater than"

A way to remember it is that the inequality sign "opens its mouth" towards the larger value.
:::

::: exempel "Example 2: Insert the correct inequality sign"
**Insert the correct inequality sign, < or >, between the numbers.<br>
a) 23&emsp;19&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;b) −20&emsp;−3**

::: handskrift
typ: olikhet
:::

::: textlosning
**a)** The number furthest to the right on the number line is the largest number.

**Answer:** $23 > 19$

**b)** The further to the left on the number line a number lies, the smaller it
is, and −20 lies to the left of −3.

**Answer:** $(-20) < (-3)$
:::
:::

## Rules of arithmetic for negative numbers

::: formel "Addition and subtraction"
$$
a + (-b) = a - b
\qquad\qquad
a - (-b) = a + b
$$

In words: **like signs** (minus/minus or plus/plus) together can be replaced by
a plus sign. **Unlike signs** (plus/minus or minus/plus) together can be
replaced by a minus sign.

Memory rule: "minus minus gives plus" and "plus minus gives minus".
:::

::: exempel "Example 3: Addition and subtraction"
**Calculate<br>a)&nbsp;$4 - (-9)$&emsp;&emsp;b)&nbsp;$25 + (-10)$**

::: handskrift
typ: negadd
:::

::: textlosning
**a)** Two minus signs together are replaced by a plus sign:

$$
4 - (-9) = 4 + 9 = 13
$$

**Answer:** 13

**b)** Plus and minus together are replaced by a minus sign:

$$
25 + (-10) = 25 - 10 = 15
$$

**Answer:** 15
:::
:::

::: formel "Multiplication and division"
$$
a \cdot (-b) = (-a) \cdot b = -(a \cdot b)
\qquad\qquad
(-a) \cdot (-b) = a \cdot b
$$

$$
\frac{a}{(-b)} = \frac{(-a)}{b} = -\frac{a}{b}
\qquad\qquad
\frac{(-a)}{(-b)} = \frac{a}{b}
$$

In words: **like signs** multiplied or divided by each other give a
**positive** value. **Unlike signs** multiplied or divided by each
other give a **negative** value.

Memory rule: "minus times minus gives plus" and "plus times minus gives minus".
:::

::: exempel "Example 4: Multiplication and division"
**Calculate<br>a)&nbsp;$4 \cdot (-3)$&emsp;&emsp;b)&nbsp;$(-5) \cdot (-9)$&emsp;&emsp;c)&nbsp;$\dfrac{35}{(-7)}$&emsp;&emsp;d)&nbsp;$\dfrac{(-42)}{(-7)}$**

::: handskrift
typ: negmult
:::

::: textlosning
**a)** Unlike signs give minus: $4 \cdot (-3) = -12$

**Answer:** −12

**b)** Like signs give plus: $(-5) \cdot (-9) = 45$

**Answer:** 45

**c)** Unlike signs give minus: $\dfrac{35}{-7} = -5$

**Answer:** −5

**d)** Like signs give plus: $\dfrac{-42}{-7} = 6$

**Answer:** 6
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 5: Watch out with the subtraction"
**Calculate $(-5) - 3$**

::: handskrift
typ: termometer
:::

::: textlosning
$$
(-5) - 3 = -8
$$

**Answer:** −8

**NOTE!** The answer is not 8. There are not two minus signs *together*, and it is not
minus *times* minus either. Think of a thermometer: −5 degrees, and then the
temperature falls by 3 degrees. That gives −8 degrees.
:::
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "The number sets"
- **Natural numbers** $\mathbb{N}$: all non-negative integers, that is
  $0, 1, 2, 3, \ldots$
- **Integers** $\mathbb{Z}$: the negative ones too,
  $\ldots, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, \ldots$
- **Rational numbers** $\mathbb{Q}$: all numbers that can be written as a fraction
  $\dfrac{a}{b}$ with integers and $b \neq 0$.
- **Real numbers** $\mathbb{R}$: all numbers on the number line.
:::

::: sampunkt "Irrational numbers"
- Numbers that **cannot** be written as a fraction, for example $\pi$ and
  $\sqrt{2}$.
- Their decimal expansion is infinite **and never repeats**.
- They belong to the real numbers but not to the rational numbers.
:::

::: sampunkt "The sets lie inside each other"
- Every natural number is also an integer, rational and real.
- Every integer is rational and real, but not necessarily natural.
:::

::: sampunkt "Inequality signs"
- $<$ means less than, $>$ means greater than.
- The sign **opens** towards the larger value.
- Among negative numbers, the one with the **largest absolute value** is the smallest: $-20 < -3$.
:::

::: sampunkt "Addition and subtraction with signs"
- $a + (-b) = a - b$
- $a - (-b) = a + b$
- **Like signs together become plus, unlike signs together become minus.**
:::

::: sampunkt "Multiplication and division with signs"
- $(-a) \cdot (-b) = a \cdot b$ and
  $a \cdot (-b) = -(a \cdot b)$.
- $\dfrac{-a}{-b} = \dfrac{a}{b}$ and
  $\dfrac{a}{-b} = -\dfrac{a}{b}$.
- **Like signs give positive, unlike signs give negative.**
:::
:::
