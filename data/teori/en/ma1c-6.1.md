# The tangent of an angle

To calculate sides and angles in triangles we can use the
**trigonometric functions**. The most common are tangent (tan), sine
(sin) and cosine (cos).

First we revise some basic concepts in right-angled triangles.
The longest side (the side opposite the right angle) is called the
**hypotenuse**. The two shorter sides are called **legs**. The leg
opposite an angle is called the **opposite side**. The leg that forms the angle
is called the **adjacent side**. See the figure:

The ratio (the division) between the sides in a *right-angled triangle* can
be written with the trigonometric functions. **NOTE! Applies only to
right-angled triangles!**

::: formel "Trigonometric functions"
::: figur
<svg viewBox="36 28 212 128" width="288" height="174" xmlns="http://www.w3.org/2000/svg" font-family="Poppins, system-ui, sans-serif" role="img" aria-label="A right-angled triangle with the vertical leg a, the horizontal leg b, the hypotenuse c and the angle v at the bottom right."><polygon points="60,40 60,130 240,130" fill="none" stroke="#1f2530" stroke-width="1.5"/><polyline points="60.0,118.0 72.0,118.0 72.0,130.0" fill="none" stroke="#1f2530" stroke-width="1.2"/><path d="M218.0,130.0 A22,22 0 0 1 220.3,120.2" fill="none" stroke="#1f2530" stroke-width="1.3"/><text x="48" y="90" font-size="14" text-anchor="end" fill="#1f2530"><tspan font-style="italic">a</tspan></text><text x="150" y="148" font-size="14" text-anchor="middle" fill="#1f2530"><tspan font-style="italic">b</tspan></text><text x="158" y="76" font-size="14" fill="#1f2530"><tspan font-style="italic">c</tspan></text><text x="196" y="124" font-size="13" text-anchor="end" fill="#1f2530"><tspan font-style="italic">v</tspan></text></svg>
:::

$$
\tan v = \frac{\text{opposite side}}{\text{adjacent side}} = \frac{a}{b}
$$

$$
\sin v = \frac{\text{opposite side}}{\text{hypotenuse}} = \frac{a}{c}
$$

$$
\cos v = \frac{\text{adjacent side}}{\text{hypotenuse}} = \frac{b}{c}
$$
:::

The trigonometric functions can be calculated with a calculator. Find the buttons
"tan", "sin" and "cos" on your calculator!

Finally, a few details:

There are different ways of writing angles. ∠ABC means the angle that
arises if you start from vertex *A*, go to vertex *B* and then to vertex
*C*. See the example below:

If we are to give a length but the unit is missing, the notation
l.u. (length units) is used. See one of the examples below.

typ: tangenssidor

::: textlosning
**a)** We are to look at the ratio between the opposite and adjacent
side for the angle $u$:

$$
\tan u = \frac{\text{opposite side}}{\text{adjacent side}} = \frac{4}{3}
$$

**Answer:** $\dfrac{4}{3}$

**b)** We now look at the ratio between the opposite and adjacent
side for the angle $v$:

$$
\tan v = \frac{\text{opposite side}}{\text{adjacent side}} = \frac{3}{4} = 0.75
$$

**Answer:** $\dfrac{3}{4}$ or 0.75
:::

**Find the length of the side $x$. The figures are not to scale.**

::: handskrift
typ: okandsida

::: textlosning
The angle 30° has the opposite side $x$ and the adjacent side
1.3 m:

$$
\tan 30\degree = \frac{x}{1.3}
\qquad
x = 1.3 \cdot \tan 30\degree
$$

We enter it on the calculator and get

$$
x = 0.750\ldots\ \mathrm{m} \approx 0.75\ \mathrm{m}
$$

**Answer:** 0.75 m

**b)**

::: figur
<svg viewBox="30 32 300 142" width="438" height="208" xmlns="http://www.w3.org/2000/svg" font-family="Poppins, system-ui, sans-serif" role="img" aria-label="A right-angled triangle with the vertical leg 5.0, the angle 22 degrees at the bottom right and the unknown horizontal leg x."><polygon points="80,40 80,150 320,150" fill="#b5d68f" stroke="#1f2530" stroke-width="1.5"/><polyline points="80.0,138.0 92.0,138.0 92.0,150.0" fill="none" stroke="#1f2530" stroke-width="1.2"/><path d="M296.0,150.0 A24,24 0 0 1 298.2,140.0" fill="none" stroke="#1f2530" stroke-width="1.3"/><text x="272" y="142" font-size="12" text-anchor="end" fill="#1f2530">22°</text><text x="68" y="100" font-size="13" text-anchor="end" fill="#1f2530">5.0</text><text x="200" y="168" font-size="13" text-anchor="middle" fill="#1f2530"><tspan font-style="italic">x</tspan></text></svg>

The angle 22° has the opposite side 5.0 and the adjacent side
$x$:

$$
\tan 22\degree = \frac{5.0}{x}
\qquad
x = \frac{5.0}{\tan 22\degree} = 12.375\ldots \approx 12\ \text{l.u.}
$$

(Since the unit is missing for the length 5.0 in the task, we give it in
length units.)

**Answer:** 12 l.u.
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "The parts of the triangle"
- **Hypotenuse**: the longest side, opposite the right angle.
- **Legs**: the two shorter sides.
- **Opposite side**: the leg that lies opposite the angle.
- **Adjacent side**: the leg that forms the angle.
- Which leg is which **depends on which angle** you start from.
:::

::: sampunkt "The three functions"
- $\tan v
  = \dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}$
- $\sin v
  = \dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}$
- $\cos v
  = \dfrac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}}$
:::

::: sampunkt "The condition"
- **Applies only to right-angled triangles.** Without a right angle the
  formulas do not work.
:::

::: sampunkt "Finding an unknown side"
1. Mark the angle and decide which sides are known and which are sought.
2. Choose the function that links exactly those.
3. Solve for the side you are looking for.
:::

::: sampunkt "Notation"
- $\angle ABC$ is the angle at vertex **B**, that is the **middle**
  vertex.
- If the unit is missing, **l.u.** is written for length units.
:::
:::
