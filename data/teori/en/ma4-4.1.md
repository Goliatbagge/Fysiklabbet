# Imaginary and complex numbers

When we have the equation $x^2 = a$ with the solution $x = \pm\sqrt{a}$, we have
previously said that the equation has no real solutions if $a$ is negative. For
example, the equation

$$
x^2 = -9 \quad\Rightarrow\quad x = \pm\sqrt{-9}
$$

has no real solutions: no real number can be squared to give something negative.

If we introduce a new number, $i$, with the property that $i^2 = -1$, we can still
find solutions. We try again and rewrite $-9$ as a product that includes $-1$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2 &= -9\\
x^2 &= 9\cdot (-1)
\end{aligned}
$$

Since $-1 = i^2$ by definition, we can rewrite the equation as
$x^2 = 9\cdot i^2$, and then solve it as usual by taking square roots:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= \pm\sqrt{9i^2}\\
x &= \pm 3i
\end{aligned}
$$

The number $i$ is called the **imaginary unit**.

::: formel "The imaginary unit"
For the imaginary unit $i$,
$$
i^2 = -1
$$
:::

Numbers that contain the number $i$ are called **imaginary numbers** and can be seen as
the opposite of real numbers. A number can consist of both a real part and an
imaginary part; these are written in the form $a + bi$, where $a$ is the **real part** and
$b$ is the **imaginary part**. All numbers that can be written in the form $a + bi$ are called
**complex numbers**. A number that consists only of an imaginary part is called a
**purely imaginary number**.

All real numbers and all purely imaginary numbers can be written in the form $a + bi$
(with $b = 0$ and $a = 0$ respectively). This means that the real numbers are a
subset of the complex numbers: the set of complex numbers $\mathbb{C}$ includes
all the other sets of numbers, including the natural numbers $\mathbb{N}$, the integers
$\mathbb{Z}$, the rational numbers $\mathbb{Q}$ and the real numbers
$\mathbb{R}$.

| Concept | Definition | Example |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Complex number | A number of the form $a + bi$ where $a$ and $b$ are real numbers | $3 + 4i$, $2i$ and $7$ |
| Real number | A number of the form $a + bi$ where $b = 0$ | $7$ and $\pi$ |
| Imaginary number | A number of the form $a + bi$ where $b \neq 0$ | $3 + 4i$ and $2i$ |
| Purely imaginary number | A number of the form $a + bi$ where $a = 0$ and $b \neq 0$ | $2i$ and $\dfrac{5i}{3}$ |

When calculating with imaginary numbers our usual rules apply, whether it is
addition, subtraction, multiplication or division. But remember
that $i^2 = -1$.

::: formel "The square root of negative numbers"
A quick way of taking the square root of negative numbers is
$$
\sqrt{-a} = \sqrt{a}\cdot i
$$
This means, for example, that $\sqrt{-9} = \sqrt{9}\cdot i = 3i$.
:::

::: exempel "Example 1: Calculate with imaginary numbers"
**Calculate<br>a)&nbsp;$12i - 5i + 3i$&emsp;&emsp;b)&nbsp;$(-6i)\cdot (4i)$&emsp;&emsp;c)&nbsp;$\dfrac{10i}{2i} + 3i$**

::: handskrift
typ: imaginara
:::

::: textlosning
We use the usual rules and remember that $i^2 = -1$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{a)}\quad & 12i - 5i + 3i = (12 - 5 + 3)i = 10i\\[4pt]
\text{b)}\quad & (-6i)\cdot (4i) = -24i^2 = -24\cdot (-1) = 24\\[4pt]
\text{c)}\quad & \frac{10i}{2i} + 3i = 5 + 3i
\end{aligned}
$$

**Answer:** a) $10i$&emsp;&emsp;b) $24$&emsp;&emsp;c) $5 + 3i$
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 2: Solve an equation with a square root"
**Solve the equation $x^2 + i^2 + 65 = 0$.**

::: handskrift
typ: rotimaginar
:::

::: textlosning
We use the fact that $i^2 = -1$, then solve for $x^2$ and then take
square roots.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2 + i^2 + 65 &= 0\\
x^2 - 1 + 65 &= 0\\
x^2 + 64 &= 0\\
x^2 &= -64\\
x &= \pm\sqrt{-64} = \pm\sqrt{64\cdot (-1)} = \pm\sqrt{64\cdot i^2} = \pm 8i
\end{aligned}
$$

**Answer:** $x = \pm 8i$
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 3: Solve a squared bracket"
**Solve the equation $(x + 9)^2 = -49$.**

::: handskrift
typ: kvadreradparentes
:::

::: textlosning
We take the square root of both sides and then solve for $x$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x + 9)^2 &= -49\\
x + 9 &= \pm\sqrt{-49} = \pm 7i\\
x &= -9 \pm 7i
\end{aligned}
$$

**Answer:** $x_1 = -9 - 7i$ and $x_2 = -9 + 7i$
:::
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "The imaginary unit"
- $i$ is the **imaginary unit**, defined by $i^2 = -1$.
- With $i$, the equation $x^2 = -9$ gets the solutions $x = \pm 3i$, which otherwise had no solution.
- Quick rule: $\sqrt{-a} = \sqrt{a} \cdot i$.
:::

::: sampunkt "The concepts"
- **Complex number:** a number of the form $a + bi$, where $a$ is the **real part** and $b$ the **imaginary part**.
- **Real number:** $b = 0$.
- **Imaginary number:** $b \neq 0$.
- **Purely imaginary number:** $a = 0$ and $b \neq 0$.
:::

::: sampunkt "The sets of numbers"
- The real numbers are a **subset** of the complex numbers.
- $\mathbb{C}$ includes all the other sets of numbers: $\mathbb{N}$, $\mathbb{Z}$, $\mathbb{Q}$ and $\mathbb{R}$.
:::

::: sampunkt "Calculating with imaginary numbers"
- The usual rules apply. Treat $i$ like any other number.
- Use $i^2 = -1$ in the last step: $(-6i)(4i) = -24i^2 = 24$.
- Solve equations as usual and take the root last: $(x + 9)^2 = -49$ gives $x = -9 \pm 7i$.
:::

:::
