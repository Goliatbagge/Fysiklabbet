# Newton's third law

::: demo "Introduction: Newton's third law"
Roll backwards on your chair and put your feet on the chair legs. Take hold of
the table in front of you with your hands. You are now going to roll FORWARDS towards the table by pulling with
your hands. In which direction do you pull with your hands? You pull THE TABLE BACKWARDS with
your hands! An equally large [[force|An influence that can change the motion or shape of an object. SI unit newton (N). A vector quantity.]] in the opposite direction acts on you, which makes YOU MOVE
FORWARDS. [[Newton's third law|For every force (action) there is an equally large force in the opposite direction (reaction).]]!

To swim FORWARDS we push water BACKWARDS. To stand UP we push
DOWN with our feet. To walk FORWARDS we push BACKWARDS on the floor with our foot.
A rocket that travels UPWARDS shoots gas DOWNWARDS.
:::

Every force on an object has an equally large, but opposite, force on another
object. This relationship is called **Newton's third law**. If you press your hand against
a wall, **your hand acts on the wall** with a certain pushing force. Then
**the wall acts on your hand** with an equally large pushing force in the opposite direction.

One force is the other force's **counterforce**, or **reaction force**.

::: formel "Newton's third law (the law of action and reaction)"
For every force on an object there is an equally large, but oppositely directed, force
of the same kind on *another* object.

$$
\vec{F}_{A \text{ on } B} = -\vec{F}_{B \text{ on } A}
$$
:::

Let us look at another situation. A weight stands on a table. It is in
equilibrium of forces. Two forces act on the weight: **[[the gravitational force|The force with which the Earth pulls all objects towards its centre. The gravitational force gives an object its weight and is directed straight down towards the centre of the Earth.]]** downwards and
**[[the normal force|The force a surface exerts perpendicular to an object resting on it.]]** upwards.

These two forces are **not** force and counterforce according to Newton's third law.
Instead they are **equilibrium forces**. It is important to distinguish between
counterforces and equilibrium forces. So the counterforce to a gravitational force
([[gravitation|The force that acts between all masses. At the Earth's surface it is called the gravitational force (weight).]]) is not a normal force from, for example, a table,
but must be another gravitational force, since it has to be of the same kind.

::: formel "Equilibrium forces vs counterforces"
**Equilibrium forces:** act on one and the same object and keep it in
[[equilibrium|When the resultant force on an object is zero. The object is then at rest or in uniform motion.]].

**Counterforces:** act on two different objects and are always the same kind of force.
:::

::: demo "Newton's third law: The Earth and the Moon"
What makes the Moon orbit the Earth? Answer: **The Earth** acts on
**the Moon** with a gravitational force that pulls the Moon towards the Earth. But
does the Moon also act on the Earth with a gravitational force? Answer: **Yes**.

How large is the Moon's gravitational force on the Earth compared with the Earth's
gravitational force on the Moon? Smaller, because the Moon's mass is much smaller?
The answer is that these two forces are **exactly equally large**! How can that
be? Well, the effect of the equally large force from the Moon on the Earth is much
smaller because of the Earth's large mass and [[inertia|An object's resistance to a change in its motion. Larger mass → larger inertia.]].

::: exempel "Example 1: The counterforce to the gravitational force on an apple"
**What is the counterforce to the gravitational force acting downwards on an apple?**

The counterforce to the downward gravitational force on an apple comes from the Earth.
So the counterforce must be an equally large gravitational force from the apple upwards on
the Earth. The apple therefore pulls the Earth up with the same force as the Earth
pulls the apple down. However, the Earth has a much larger mass than the apple (and
so a larger inertia), which is why the effect on the Earth is not as
large as the effect on the apple.

The magnet will indeed give a forward force on the piece of metal, but
the piece of metal will give an exactly equally large backward force on the magnet
(which is attached to the car). The [[resultant force|The sum of all the forces acting on an object (a vector sum). Gives the object its acceleration.]] on the car is therefore
**zero**, and (unfortunately) no driving force will arise.

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Newton's third law"
- For every force on an object there is an **equally large and oppositely
  directed** force **of the same kind** on **another** object.
- $\vec{F}_{A \text{ on } B} = -\vec{F}_{B \text{ on } A}$
- Called the law of **action and reaction**. The other force is the
  **counterforce** or **reaction force**.
:::

::: sampunkt "Everyday examples"
- Swimming forwards: push water backwards.
- Walking forwards: push your foot backwards against the floor.
- A rocket upwards: shoot gas out downwards.
- Hand against the wall: the wall pushes back just as hard.
:::

::: sampunkt "Counterforces or equilibrium forces"
- **Counterforces**: act on **two different** objects and are always **the same
  kind** of force.
- **Equilibrium forces**: act on **one and the same** object and keep it
  in equilibrium.
- The weight on the table: $F_G$ and $F_N$ are **equilibrium forces**, not force and
  counterforce.
- The counterforce to the gravitational force on an apple is the apple's gravitational force **upwards
  on the Earth**, not the table's normal force.
:::

::: sampunkt "Equally large, different effect"
- The Earth pulls on the Moon exactly as hard as the Moon pulls on the Earth.
- The effect is still completely different, because the Earth's mass and inertia are so
  much larger.
:::

::: sampunkt "Two classic traps"
- **The tug of war**: the tension in the rope is equally large in both directions. It is
  **the friction against the ground** that decides who wins.
- **The magnet on the car**: the magnet pulls the piece of metal forwards, but the piece of metal
  pulls the magnet just as much backwards. The resultant on the car is zero and
  nothing happens.
:::
:::
