# Powers with positive integer exponents

In the same way that $3 + 3 + 3 + 3 + 3$ can be written $3 \cdot 5$,
$3 \cdot 3 \cdot 3 \cdot 3 \cdot 3$ can be written $3^5$ (read "3 to the power
of 5").

We say that $3^5$ is a **power**. The parts of a power are called the **base**
(what is raised) and the **exponent** (what it is raised to). So in the example
$3^5$ the base is 3 and the exponent is 5.

::: tips "Power form and factor form"
When a number is written

- as a **power** (with a base and an exponent), it is written in **power form**
- with its **factors** (as a multiplication), it is written in
  **factor form** or **as a product**.

Example: $3^5$ is written in power form. $3 \cdot 3 \cdot 3 \cdot 3 \cdot 3$
is written in factor form.
:::

::: tips "Ways of reading"
A

- **two** in the exponent can also be read "squared", so $7^2$ can be read
  "7 squared"
- **three** in the exponent can also be read "cubed", so $7^3$ can be read
  "7 cubed".
:::

To calculate with powers we use the **laws of exponents**.

::: formel "The laws of exponents"
| Laws of exponents (general) | Laws of exponents (examples) | Justification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $a^x \cdot a^y = a^{x+y}$ | $7^2 \cdot 7^3 = 7^{2+3} = 7^5$ | $7 \cdot 7 \;\cdot\; 7 \cdot 7 \cdot 7 = 7^5$ |
| $\dfrac{a^x}{a^y} = a^{x-y}$ | $\dfrac{7^5}{7^3} = 7^{5-3} = 7^2$ | $\dfrac{7 \cdot 7 \cdot 7 \cdot 7 \cdot 7}{7 \cdot 7 \cdot 7} = 7 \cdot 7 = 7^2$ |
| $(a^x)^y = a^{x \cdot y}$ | $(7^2)^3 = 7^{2 \cdot 3} = 7^6$ | $7^2 \cdot 7^2 \cdot 7^2 = 7^{2+2+2} = 7^6$ |
| $\left(\dfrac{a}{b}\right)^{\!x} = \dfrac{a^x}{b^x}$ | $\left(\dfrac{5}{3}\right)^{\!2} = \dfrac{5^2}{3^2}$ | $\dfrac{5}{3} \cdot \dfrac{5}{3} = \dfrac{5 \cdot 5}{3 \cdot 3} = \dfrac{5^2}{3^2}$ |
| $(ab)^x = a^x \cdot b^x$ | $(3 \cdot 4)^2 = 3^2 \cdot 4^2$ | $3 \cdot 4 \;\cdot\; 3 \cdot 4 = 3 \cdot 3 \cdot 4 \cdot 4 = 3^2 \cdot 4^2$ |
:::

::: exempel "Example 1: Signs and powers"
**Calculate<br>a)&nbsp;$(-9)^2$&emsp;&emsp;b)&nbsp;$-9^2$**

::: handskrift
typ: potenstecken
:::

::: textlosning
**a)** The whole number −9 is the base, so

$$
(-9)^2 = (-9) \cdot (-9) = 81
$$

(when two negative numbers are multiplied, the product is positive).

**Answer:** 81

**b)** Here the base is only 9. The minus sign is *outside* the power
(note the difference compared with the previous part):

$$
-9^2 = -9 \cdot 9 = -81
$$

**Answer:** −81
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 2: Write as a power"
**Write 64 as a power with base 4.**

::: handskrift
typ: potensskriv
:::

::: textlosning
Think "4 to the power of something should be 64", or alternatively "how many fours must
be multiplied for the product to be 64?".

Trying:

$$
4 \cdot 4 = 16 \quad \text{(too small)}
$$

$$
4 \cdot 4 \cdot 4 = 16 \cdot 4 = 64 \quad \text{(correct!)}
$$

**Answer:** $4^3$
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 3: Expand a bracket"
**Expand $(7x)^2$.**

::: handskrift
typ: potensutveckla
:::

::: textlosning
*All the factors* inside the bracket are to be raised to the power of 2:

$$
(7x)^2 = 7^2 \cdot x^2 = 49x^2
$$

**Answer:** $49x^2$
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 4: Simplify a fraction with powers"
**Simplify $\dfrac{42x^5}{x^2}$.**

::: handskrift
typ: potensforenkla
:::

::: textlosning
We use the law of exponents for division:

$$
\frac{42x^5}{x^2} = 42x^{5-2} = 42x^3
$$

**Answer:** $42x^3$
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 5: Exponential equation with the same base"
**Solve the equation $8^m \cdot 8^5 = 8^7$.**

::: handskrift
typ: potensekvation
:::

::: textlosning
Rewrite both sides as a single power of 8. Then you can set the exponents
equal and solve the equation from there:

$$
8^m \cdot 8^5 = 8^7
$$

$$
8^{m+5} = 8^7
$$

Now both sides are written as a single power of 8. Then the exponents must be
equal, so

$$
m + 5 = 7
$$

$$
m = 2
$$

**Answer:** $m = 2$
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 6: Simplify a fractional expression with powers"
**Simplify $\dfrac{27^{5x}}{3^x + 3^x + 3^x}$.**

::: handskrift
typ: potensbrak
:::

::: textlosning
When we simplify fractional expressions with powers, we should first aim to
rewrite both the numerator and the denominator as *one* power. Then we rewrite
the powers so that they have the same base. After that it usually becomes easy. We
start by rewriting the denominator as a multiplication:

$$
\frac{27^{5x}}{3^x + 3^x + 3^x} = \frac{27^{5x}}{3 \cdot 3^x}
$$

Then we rewrite the denominator as *one* power:

$$
\frac{27^{5x}}{3 \cdot 3^x} = \frac{27^{5x}}{3^1 \cdot 3^x} = \frac{27^{5x}}{3^{1+x}}
$$

Now we have a single power in both the numerator and the denominator. Now we rewrite them
with the same base. Since $27 = 3^3$,

$$
\frac{27^{5x}}{3^{1+x}} = \frac{(3^3)^{5x}}{3^{1+x}} = \frac{3^{15x}}{3^{1+x}}
= 3^{15x - (1+x)} = 3^{15x - 1 - x} = 3^{14x - 1}
$$

**Answer:** $3^{14x-1}$
:::
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Power, base and exponent"
- $3^5$ means $3 \cdot 3 \cdot 3 \cdot 3 \cdot 3$.
- The **base** is the number that is raised, the **exponent** the number it is raised to.
- **Power form**: $3^5$. **Factor form**: $3 \cdot 3 \cdot 3 \cdot 3 \cdot 3$.
- The exponent 2 is read "squared", the exponent 3 "cubed".
:::

::: sampunkt "The laws of exponents"
- $a^x \cdot a^y = a^{x+y}$
- $\dfrac{a^x}{a^y} = a^{x-y}$
- $(a^x)^y = a^{x \cdot y}$
- $(ab)^x = a^x b^x$ and
  $\left(\dfrac{a}{b}\right)^{x} = \dfrac{a^x}{b^x}$
:::

::: sampunkt "The condition for the first two"
- Adding or subtracting exponents requires **the same base**.
- So $2^3 \cdot 5^3$ cannot be combined into a power of
  2 or 5.
:::

::: sampunkt "Watch out for the sign"
- $(-9)^2 = 81$: the minus sign is part of the base.
- $-9^2 = -81$: only the nine is raised, the minus sign is outside.
- So the bracket decides everything.
:::

::: sampunkt "Common types of task"
- Rewrite a number as a power with a given base: $64 = 4^3$.
- Expand a bracket: $(7x)^2 = 49x^2$. **Both** the number and the variable
  are raised.
- Solve an exponential equation with the same base: compare the exponents directly.
:::
:::
