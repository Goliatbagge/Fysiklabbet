# Powers of complex numbers and de Moivre's formula

When we have a complex number as the base with a certain exponent, we have **powers of
complex numbers**. Multiplying a number in rectangular form
$a + bi$ by itself over and over quickly becomes unmanageable. If the number is instead written in polar form, there is
a shortcut: **de Moivre's formula**. It builds on the multiplication rule
from the previous section (in multiplication the moduli are multiplied and the arguments
added) and applies to positive integer exponents.

::: formel "de Moivre's formula"
$$
z^n = \bigl(r(\cos v + i\sin v)\bigr)^n = r^n\bigl(\cos nv + i\sin nv\bigr)
$$

where $n$ is a positive integer.
:::

In words: **raise the modulus to the power $n$ and multiply the argument by $n$**.
So the modulus $r$ becomes $r^n$, and the angle $v$ becomes $nv$.

## Derivation

From the previous section we have the formula for multiplying two complex numbers:

$$
z\cdot w = |z|\cdot |w|\bigl(\cos(u + v) + i\sin(u + v)\bigr)
$$

If we replace $w$ with $z$ (so that $u = v$) we get the square:

$$
z\cdot z = z^2 = |z|\cdot |z|\bigl(\cos(v + v) + i\sin(v + v)\bigr) = |z|^2\bigl(\cos 2v + i\sin 2v\bigr)
$$

In the same way we get $z^3$ by multiplying by $z$ once more:

$$
z\cdot z\cdot z = z^3 = |z|\cdot |z|\cdot |z|\bigl(\cos(v + v + v) + i\sin(v + v + v)\bigr) = |z|^3\bigl(\cos 3v + i\sin 3v\bigr)
$$

In general, then,

$$
z^n = |z|^n\bigl(\cos nv + i\sin nv\bigr)
$$

and with $|z| = r$ we get de Moivre's formula:

$$
z^n = r^n\bigl(\cos nv + i\sin nv\bigr) \qquad \textbf{(QED)}
$$

::: exempel "Example 1: Calculate a power and simplify"
**Calculate $z^4$ for $z = 2\left(\cos\dfrac{\pi}{8} + i\sin\dfrac{\pi}{8}\right)$. Simplify the answer as far as possible.**

::: handskrift
typ: moivrepotens
:::

::: textlosning
We start by identifying $n$, $r$ and $v$:

$$
n = 4 \text{ (the degree)}, \qquad r = 2 \text{ (the modulus)}, \qquad v = \frac{\pi}{8} \text{ (the argument)}
$$

Substituting into de Moivre's formula $z^n = r^n(\cos nv + i\sin nv)$ gives:

$$
z^4 = 2^4\left(\cos 4\cdot \frac{\pi}{8} + i\sin 4\cdot \frac{\pi}{8}\right) = 16\left(\cos\frac{\pi}{2} + i\sin\frac{\pi}{2}\right)
$$

Since $\cos\dfrac{\pi}{2} = 0$ and $\sin\dfrac{\pi}{2} = 1$ we get:

$$
z^4 = 16(0 + i) = 16i
$$

**Answer:** $z^4 = 16i$
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 2: Answer in the form a + bi"
**Let $z = \cos 10^\circ + i\sin 10^\circ$. Calculate $z^{27}$ and give the result in the form $a + bi$.**

::: handskrift
typ: moivreform
:::

::: textlosning
We identify $n$, $r$ and $v$ as $n = 27$, $r = 1$ and $v = 10^\circ$.
Substituting into de Moivre's formula gives:

$$
z^{27} = 1^{27}\bigl(\cos 27\cdot 10^\circ + i\sin 27\cdot 10^\circ\bigr) = \cos 270^\circ + i\sin 270^\circ
$$

Since $\cos 270^\circ = 0$ and $\sin 270^\circ = -1$:

$$
z^{27} = 0 + i\cdot (-1) = -i
$$

**Answer:** $-i$, or alternatively $0 - i$
:::
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "de Moivre's formula"
- $z^n = \big(r(\cos v + i\sin v)\big)^n = r^n\big(\cos nv + i\sin nv\big)$.
- In words: **raise the modulus to the power $n$ and multiply the argument by $n$**.
- The formula applies to positive integer exponents.
:::

::: sampunkt "Why it holds"
- Follows directly from the multiplication rule, applied to $z$ times itself.
- $z^2$ gives the modulus $r^2$ and the argument $2v$, $z^3$ gives $r^3$ and $3v$, and so on.
:::

::: sampunkt "Method"
1. Identify $n$, $r$ and $v$ from the task.
2. Substitute into the formula.
3. Simplify using known values of sine and cosine.
- $z = 2\left(\cos\dfrac{\pi}{8} + i\sin\dfrac{\pi}{8}\right)$ gives $z^4 = 16\left(\cos\dfrac{\pi}{2} + i\sin\dfrac{\pi}{2}\right) = 16i$.
:::

::: sampunkt "The right form first"
- If the number is given in rectangular form, it must be rewritten in **polar form** before the formula can be used.
- Multiplying $a + bi$ by itself over and over quickly becomes unmanageable.
- Answer in the form the task asks for.
:::

:::
