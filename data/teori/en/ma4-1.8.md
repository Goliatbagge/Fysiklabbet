# Addition and subtraction formulas

So far we have calculated the sine and cosine of a single angle. Sometimes
we instead need an expression for the sine or cosine of a **sum** or
**difference** of two angles, $u + v$ and $u - v$ respectively. A common
misconception is that $\sin(u + v)$ would be $\sin u + \sin v$. That is not
the case. Instead, the addition and subtraction formulas below apply.

::: formel "Addition and subtraction formulas"
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sin(u + v) &= \sin u\cos v + \cos u\sin v\\
\sin(u - v) &= \sin u\cos v - \cos u\sin v\\
\cos(u + v) &= \cos u\cos v - \sin u\sin v\\
\cos(u - v) &= \cos u\cos v + \sin u\sin v
\end{aligned}
$$
:::

Notice the change of sign: in the sine formulas the sign on the right-hand side follows
the one on the left-hand side ($+$ gives $+$, $-$ gives $-$), while the cosine formulas change
sign ($+$ gives $-$, $-$ gives $+$).

## Proof of the subtraction formula for cosine

The formulas can be proved geometrically. We show the subtraction formula for cosine,
$\cos(u - v)$; the others are proved in a similar way.

We mark two arbitrary angles $u$ and $v$ on the unit circle, where $u > v$.
The angle between the corresponding arms is then $u - v$. We call the points where the arms
meet the unit circle $P$ and $Q$ respectively, and we call the line segment between them
$PQ$. Since the radius is $1$, the points have the coordinates
$P = (\cos v,\ \sin v)$ and $Q = (\cos u,\ \sin u)$.

The idea is to write $(PQ)^2$ in **two different ways** and then set the expressions
equal to each other.

The cosine rule $c^2 = a^2 + b^2 - 2ab\cos C$ gives, with the two radii $1$ and
the included angle $u - v$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(PQ)^2 &= 1^2 + 1^2 - 2\cdot 1\cdot 1\cdot \cos(u - v)\\
(PQ)^2 &= 2 - 2\cos(u - v) \qquad (1)
\end{aligned}
$$

The distance formula $d^2 = (x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2$ gives, with the points'
coordinates:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(PQ)^2 &= (\cos u - \cos v)^2 + (\sin u - \sin v)^2\\
(PQ)^2 &= \cos^2 u - 2\cos u\cos v + \cos^2 v + \sin^2 u - 2\sin u\sin v + \sin^2 v\\
(PQ)^2 &= \sin^2 u + \cos^2 u + \sin^2 v + \cos^2 v - 2\cos u\cos v - 2\sin u\sin v
\end{aligned}
$$

The Pythagorean identity ($\sin^2 u + \cos^2 u = 1$ and
$\sin^2 v + \cos^2 v = 1$) gives:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(PQ)^2 &= 1 + 1 - 2\cos u\cos v - 2\sin u\sin v\\
(PQ)^2 &= 2 - 2\cos u\cos v - 2\sin u\sin v \qquad (2)
\end{aligned}
$$

Setting $(1) = (2)$ we get:

$$
2 - 2\cos(u - v) = 2 - 2\cos u\cos v - 2\sin u\sin v
$$

We subtract $2$ from both sides:

$$
-2\cos(u - v) = -2\cos u\cos v - 2\sin u\sin v
$$

and divide both sides by $-2$:

$$
\cos(u - v) = \cos u\cos v + \sin u\sin v \qquad \textbf{(QED)}
$$

The other addition and subtraction formulas can be shown in a similar way.

## Double angle formulas

If we set $v = u$ in the addition formulas, we get formulas for the **double
angle** $2v$. They are useful when an expression contains both an angle and
its double.

::: formel "Sine and cosine of the double angle"
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sin 2v &= 2\sin v\cos v\\[4pt]
\cos 2v &= \cos^2 v - \sin^2 v\\
\cos 2v &= 2\cos^2 v - 1\\
\cos 2v &= 1 - 2\sin^2 v
\end{aligned}
$$
:::

We prove the formula for $\sin 2v$ using the addition formula for sine:

$$
\sin 2v = \sin(v + v) = \sin v\cos v + \cos v\sin v = 2\sin v\cos v \qquad \textbf{(QED)}
$$

and the formula for $\cos 2v$ using the addition formula for cosine:

$$
\cos 2v = \cos(v + v) = \cos v\cos v - \sin v\sin v = \cos^2 v - \sin^2 v \qquad \textbf{(QED)}
$$

We then get the last two forms of $\cos 2v$ by replacing $\sin^2 v$
with $1 - \cos^2 v$ (or the other way round) using the Pythagorean identity.

::: exempel "Example 1: Simplify an expression"
**Simplify $\sin(x + 30^\circ) - \sin(x - 30^\circ)$.**

::: handskrift
typ: additionsforenkla
:::

::: textlosning
We use the addition and subtraction formula for sine on each term.
The terms with $\sin x\cos 30^\circ$ cancel each other out:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sin(x + 30^\circ) - \sin(x - 30^\circ) &= \sin x\cos 30^\circ + \cos x\sin 30^\circ - \sin x\cos 30^\circ + \cos x\sin 30^\circ\\
&= 2\cos x\sin 30^\circ = 2\cos x\cdot \frac{1}{2} = \cos x
\end{aligned}
$$

**Answer:** $\cos x$
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 2: Find sin 75° exactly"
**Find the exact value of $\sin 75^\circ$.**

::: handskrift
typ: sinexakt
:::

::: textlosning
We do not have a table value for $75^\circ$, but we can write $75^\circ$ as a
sum of two angles with known values: $45^\circ$ and $30^\circ$. Then we
use the addition formula for sine.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sin 75^\circ &= \sin(45^\circ + 30^\circ) = \sin 45^\circ\cos 30^\circ + \cos 45^\circ\sin 30^\circ\\
&= \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} + \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\cdot \frac{1}{2} = \frac{\sqrt{3} + 1}{2\sqrt{2}}
\end{aligned}
$$

This is an exact and simplified answer. If we want to answer without a root sign in the denominator,
we multiply numerator and denominator by $\sqrt{2}$:

$$
\frac{(\sqrt{3} + 1)\sqrt{2}}{2\sqrt{2}\cdot \sqrt{2}} = \frac{\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2}}{2\cdot 2} = \frac{\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2}}{4}
$$

**Answer:** $\dfrac{\sqrt{3} + 1}{2\sqrt{2}}$ or $\dfrac{\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2}}{4}$
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 3: Show an identity"
**Show that $\dfrac{\sin 2x}{1 + \cos 2x} = \tan x$.**

::: handskrift
typ: dubbelidentitet
:::

::: textlosning
We start from the left-hand side (LHS) and rewrite the double angles using
$\sin 2x = 2\sin x\cos x$ and $\cos 2x = \cos^2 x - \sin^2 x$:

$$
\text{LHS} = \frac{\sin 2x}{1 + \cos 2x} = \frac{2\sin x\cos x}{1 + \cos^2 x - \sin^2 x}
$$

Here it looks as if we are stuck. But if we instead use the form
$\cos 2x = 2\cos^2 x - 1$ in the denominator, the $1$s cancel:

$$
\frac{2\sin x\cos x}{1 + 2\cos^2 x - 1} = \frac{2\sin x\cos x}{2\cos^2 x} = \frac{\sin x}{\cos x} = \tan x = \text{RHS}
$$

So the left-hand side is equal to the right-hand side, which was to be shown. **(QED)**
:::
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Addition and subtraction formulas"
- $\sin(u + v) = \sin u\cos v + \cos u\sin v$
- $\sin(u - v) = \sin u\cos v - \cos u\sin v$
- $\cos(u + v) = \cos u\cos v - \sin u\sin v$
- $\cos(u - v) = \cos u\cos v + \sin u\sin v$
:::

::: sampunkt "Signs and pitfalls"
- $\sin(u + v)$ is **not** $\sin u + \sin v$. That is the most common mistake.
- The sine formulas keep the sign from the left-hand side.
- The cosine formulas **change** sign: plus gives minus and minus gives plus.
:::

::: sampunkt "The double angle"
- $\sin 2v = 2\sin v\cos v$
- $\cos 2v = \cos^2 v - \sin^2 v = 2\cos^2 v - 1 = 1 - 2\sin^2 v$
- They follow from the addition formulas with $u = v$, and the three forms of $\cos 2v$ from the Pythagorean identity.
- Choose the form that contains the same function as the rest of the expression.
:::

::: sampunkt "Using the formulas"
- **Exact values:** split the angle into two known ones. $\sin 75^\circ = \sin(45^\circ + 30^\circ) = \dfrac{\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2}}{4}$.
- **Simplification:** write out both terms, and half of them often cancel.
- **Identities:** start from one side and rewrite until it becomes the other.
- Root signs in the denominator are removed by multiplying numerator and denominator.
:::

:::
