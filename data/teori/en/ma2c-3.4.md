# Quadratic equations and inequalities with graphing tools

We will now look at how to solve quadratic equations and inequalities
graphically with the help of graphing tools, for example Geogebra. At an
earlier level we solved linear equations with graphing tools, and the
method is exactly the same for quadratic equations. The difference is that
we can now get two solutions.

::: tips "Solving equations with graphing tools"
We have an equation LHS = RHS (left-hand side = right-hand side).

1. Draw the equations $y = \mathrm{LHS}$ and $y = \mathrm{RHS}$ in
   Geogebra.
2. Find the **points of intersection** and read off their $x$-coordinates. These are
   the solutions!
:::

If we have a quadratic equation that is equal to 0, we can alternatively
solve the equation by finding the zeros.

::: tips "Solving equations equal to 0 with graphing tools"
We have an equation LHS = 0.

1. Draw the equation $y = \mathrm{LHS}$.
2. Find the **zeros** and read off their $x$-coordinates. These are
   the solutions!
:::

We can also solve inequalities with graphing tools.

::: tips "Solving inequalities with graphing tools"
We have an inequality, for example LHS < RHS or LHS > RHS.

1. Draw $f(x) = \mathrm{LHS}$ and $g(x) = \mathrm{RHS}$.
2. Find the points of intersection and read off their $x$-coordinates. These give
   the limits of the interval!
3. If the graph of $f(x)$ lies
   - **below** the graph of $g(x)$, then $f(x) < g(x)$
   - **above** the graph of $g(x)$, then $f(x) > g(x)$.
:::

::: textlosning
**a)** WHAT: We draw the functions $y = x^2 - 4x + 5$ and $y = 2$ in
Geogebra (see the figure above) and find the coordinates of the points of intersection.

HOW: The $x$-coordinates of the points of intersection give the solutions, that is $x_1 = 3$ and
$x_2 = 1$.

**Answer:** $x_1 = 3$ and $x_2 = 1$

**b)** Here we have an equation that is equal to 0, so we can examine
the zeros.

WHAT: We draw the function $y = x^2 - 2x - 15$ in Geogebra and find
the zeros with the tool "Roots".

HOW: The $x$-coordinates of the zeros give the solutions, that is $x_1 = 5$ and
$x_2 = -3$.

**Answer:** $x_1 = 5$ and $x_2 = -3$

::: textlosning
**a)** HOW: The $x$-coordinates of the points of intersection give the limits of the interval, that is
$x = -5$ and $x = -1$. Since $x^2 + 6x + 6 < 1$, we have
$f(x) < g(x)$, that is $f(x)$ must be **less** than $g(x)$. This holds
when the graph of $f(x)$ is **below** the graph of $g(x)$, that is between
$x = -5$ and $x = -1$. This gives the interval $-5 < x < -1$.

**Answer:** $-5 < x < -1$

**b)** We have the same functions, so the graphs and the points of intersection will
be the same as in part a, so we look at the same graph again.

HOW: The $x$-coordinates of the points of intersection again give the limits of the interval,
that is $x = -5$ and $x = -1$. Since $x^2 + 6x + 6 > 1$, we have
$f(x) > g(x)$, that is $f(x)$ must be **greater** than $g(x)$. This holds
when the graph of $f(x)$ is **above** the graph of $g(x)$, that is to the
left of $x = -5$ and to the right of $x = -1$. This gives the intervals
$x < -5$ and $x > -1$.

**Answer:** $x < -5$ and $x > -1$
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Equation, both sides"
1. Draw $y = \mathrm{LHS}$ and $y = \mathrm{RHS}$.
2. Find the **points of intersection** and read off their $x$-coordinates.
- Now there can be **two** solutions, unlike linear
  equations.
:::

::: sampunkt "Equation equal to zero"
- Draw only $y = \mathrm{LHS}$ and use the tool **Roots**.
- Quicker when the equation is already equal to 0.
:::

::: sampunkt "Inequalities"
1. Draw $f(x) = \mathrm{LHS}$ and $g(x) = \mathrm{RHS}$.
2. The $x$-coordinates of the points of intersection give the **limits** of the interval.
3. Decide where $f$ lies **below** or **above** $g$.
:::

::: sampunkt "Interpret the interval correctly"
- $f(x) < g(x)$ holds where the graph lies **below**. For a parabola this is
  usually an interval **between** the points of intersection.
- $f(x) > g(x)$ holds where it lies **above**, that is **outside**
  the points of intersection. The answer is then **two** intervals.
:::

::: sampunkt "Presenting your solution"
- Write **what** you drew and **how** you read off the values.
- Give the solutions as $x_1$ and $x_2$, or the intervals with
  inequality signs.
:::
:::
