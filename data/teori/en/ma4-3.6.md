# Probability density functions

At earlier levels of mathematics you have worked with dice and coins. There is a
limited number of outcomes (for example $1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6$). These are called
**discrete** events, and the possible outcomes form a *discrete sample space*.
We calculated the probability with the classical definition of probability,
$P(\text{event}) = \dfrac{\text{number of favourable outcomes}}{\text{number of possible outcomes}}$.

But imagine instead that we measure the *time* it takes before a light bulb
breaks. The time could be $1000$ hours, or $1000.5$ hours, or
$1000.523$ hours: there are infinitely many possible values. This is called a
**continuous sample space**. Since there are infinitely many exact numbers, the
probability of getting *exactly* one specific value is zero. Therefore we look
instead at the probability of ending up within an *interval*, for example "what is
the probability that the person is between $170\ \mathrm{cm}$ and $180\ \mathrm{cm}$?".

To calculate probabilities in an interval we use a function called a
**probability density function**. The graph of the function shows how the probability is
"distributed", and the probability for an interval is the **area under the graph**.

::: formel "Probability with a density function"
The probability $P$ that the outcome ends up between the numbers $a$ and $b$ is equal to the area
under the graph (the integral) of the probability density function $f(x)$ between these points:
$$
P(a \leq X \leq b) = \int_a^b f(x)\,dx
$$
:::

A probability density function has to meet two requirements. The value must never be negative,
since a probability cannot be negative. And the total probability is $100\ \%$,
so the area under the *whole* curve must be exactly $1$.

::: formel "Requirements for a probability density function"
$$
f(x) \geq 0 \quad \text{for all } x
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x)\,dx = 1
$$
:::

There are three common types of distribution. In a **uniform
probability distribution** (rectangle) the probability is the same everywhere within
an interval: the graph is a horizontal line (a "roof"), and since the area is a
rectangle it is often enough to calculate $\text{base}\cdot\text{height}$. A
**normal distribution** (bell curve) is a symmetric hill with its peak at
the mean $\mu$; its function is complicated and the integral is calculated with a
digital tool. An **exponential distribution** (slide) starts high and
decreases quickly towards zero, and there you can often calculate by hand with an
antiderivative.

The figure shows a uniform distribution. The area of the whole rectangle is $1$, and the
shaded part between $a$ and $b$ is the probability $P(a \leq X \leq b)$.

::: exempel "Example 1: Find the constant k"
**The function $f(x) = k\cdot x^2$ is defined in the interval $0 \leq x \leq 3$. Find the value of the constant $k$ so that $f(x)$ is a probability density function.**

::: handskrift
typ: tathetskonstant
:::

::: textlosning
The condition for a probability density function is that the total area under the curve is $1$. We
set up the integral for the whole interval, set it equal to $1$ and solve
the equation.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^3 k\cdot x^2\,dx &= 1\\[4pt]
\left[\frac{k\cdot x^3}{3}\right]_0^3 &= 1\\[6pt]
\left(\frac{k\cdot 3^3}{3}\right) - \left(\frac{k\cdot 0^3}{3}\right) &= 1\\[6pt]
\frac{k\cdot 27}{3} - 0 &= 1\\[4pt]
9k &= 1\\[4pt]
k &= \frac{1}{9}
\end{aligned}
$$

Substituting $k = \dfrac{1}{9}$ into the original function $f(x) = k\cdot x^2$ gives

$$
f(x) = \frac{1}{9}\cdot x^2 = \frac{x^2}{9}
$$

**Answer:** $f(x) = \dfrac{x^2}{9}$
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 2: Lifetime (exponential distribution)"
**The lifetime of a certain electronic component is described by the probability density function $f(t) = 0.0005\cdot e^{-0.0005t}$ for $t \geq 0$, where $t$ is the time in hours. Calculate the probability that the component breaks within $1000$ hours.**

::: handskrift
typ: livslangd
:::

::: textlosning
We are looking for the probability $P$. That it breaks "within $1000$ hours" means that
the time $t$ lies in the interval between $0$ and $1000$. So we are looking for the area under
the curve from $t = 0$ to $t = 1000$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(0 \leq t \leq 1000) &= \int_0^{1000} 0.0005\cdot e^{-0.0005t}\,dt = \left[0.0005\cdot \frac{e^{-0.0005t}}{-0.0005}\right]_0^{1000}\\[6pt]
&= \left[-e^{-0.0005t}\right]_0^{1000} = -e^{-0.0005\cdot 1000} - \left(-e^{-0.0005\cdot 0}\right)\\[4pt]
&= -e^{-0.5} + e^{0} = 1 - e^{-0.5} = 0.393\ldots \approx 0.39
\end{aligned}
$$

**Answer:** The probability is approximately $0.39 = 39\ \%$.
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 3: Crisp packets (normal distribution)"
**A factory fills packets with crisps. The machine is set to fill each packet with $200$ grams, but since the process is not perfect the weight varies randomly according to a normal distribution with mean $200$ grams and standard deviation $5$ grams. Calculate the probability that a randomly chosen packet of crisps weighs<br>a) between $195$ grams and $210$ grams&emsp;&emsp;b) over $210$ grams.**

::: handskrift
typ: chipspasar
:::

::: textlosning
The function for a normal distribution has no simple antiderivative, so here
we use a digital tool (for example the probability calculator in
GeoGebra). We choose "Normal distribution" and enter the mean $\mu = 200$ and
the standard deviation $\sigma = 5$.

**a)** We are looking for a *closed* interval and enter the limits $195$ and $210$.
The digital tool marks the region under the bell curve and gives the probability

$$
P(195 \leq X \leq 210) = 0.818\ldots \approx 0.82 = 82\ \%
$$

**b)** Now we are looking for an interval *open* upwards: the probability that the weight is over
$210$ grams, that is $P(X > 210)$. With the same mean and standard deviation
the tool gives

$$
P(X > 210) = 0.0227\ldots \approx 0.023 = 2.3\ \%
$$

**Answer:** a) $82\ \%$&emsp;&emsp;b) $2.3\ \%$
:::
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Discrete and continuous"
- **Discrete** sample space: a limited number of outcomes, like the six faces of a die.
- **Continuous** sample space: infinitely many possible values, like a lifetime in hours.
- The probability of an **exact** value is then zero. That is why we calculate with **intervals**.
:::

::: sampunkt "Probability density function"
- $P(a \leq X \leq b) = \int_a^b f(x)\,dx$. The probability is the **area under the graph**.
- Two requirements: $f(x) \geq 0$ for all $x$, and the total area $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x)\,dx = 1$.
- The second requirement is used to find an unknown constant.
:::

::: sampunkt "Three distributions"
- **Uniform** (rectangle): the same probability everywhere. Calculate base times height.
- **Normal distribution** (bell curve): symmetric about the mean $\mu$. Requires a digital tool.
- **Exponential distribution** (slide): starts high and decreases. Can be calculated by hand.
:::

::: sampunkt "Method"
1. Determine which interval the question is about. "Within 1000 hours" means from 0 to 1000.
2. Set up the integral and calculate, or use the probability calculator.
3. Preferably answer both as a decimal and as a percentage.
:::

:::
