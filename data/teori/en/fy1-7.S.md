# Summary: Electricity

The chapter went from static electricity (charge, induction, Coulomb's law)
to electric circuits (current, voltage, resistance, Ohm's and Kirchhoff's
laws, power) and on to electric fields, potential, the Faraday cage and
Millikan's experiment. After the chapter you should be able to calculate forces between
charges, analyse series and parallel circuits, and calculate with
electric field strength and potential.

## Concepts to know

- **Electric charge**: a fundamental property of matter, positive or
  negative, denoted *Q* or *q*, unit C (coulomb).
- **Elementary charge**: the smallest unit of charge,
  $q_e \approx 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}$ C, the charge of the electron and the proton.
- **Conductor, insulator, semiconductor**: how easily electrons move in a
  material (metals/salt solutions; glass/plastic/wood; silicon/selenium).
- **Induction**: redistribution of charges in a neutral object caused
  by an external electric field.
- **Coulomb's law**: the force between two point charges, decreases with the square
  of the distance.
- **Electric current**: charge per unit time, denoted *I*, unit A
  (ampere); directed from plus to minus, opposite to the motion of the electrons.
- **Kirchhoff's first law**: the sum of the currents into a point is equal to
  the sum out of the point.
- **Voltage**: energy difference per charge between two points, denoted
  *U*, unit V (volt).
- **Resistance**: opposition to electric current, denoted *R*, unit Ω (ohm).
- **Resistivity**: a material property that determines how much a conductor
  opposes current, denoted *ρ*, unit Ω·m.
- **Ohm's law**: the relationship between voltage, resistance and current,
  $U = R \cdot I$.
- **Series connection**: components connected "in a row"; the same current through all.
- **Parallel connection**: components connected "across each other"; the same
  voltage across all.
- **Equivalent resistance**: the single resistance that can replace several
  resistors in a circuit.
- **Electric power**: energy converted per unit time, denoted *P*,
  unit W (watt).
- **Electric field**: the region around a charge where another charge
  is affected by an electric force; drawn out of plus, into minus.
- **Electric field strength**: how strong an electric field is, denoted
  $\mathbb{E}$, unit N/C or V/m.
- **Uniform field**: a field that is equally strong everywhere, for example between two
  parallel charged plates.
- **Electric potential**: "electrical potential energy" at a point, denoted
  *V*, unit V; unlike voltage it has a sign.
- **Earth**: zero level of potential (0 V) in a circuit; leads away
  unwanted charge/current.
- **Kirchhoff's second law**: the sum of all changes in potential in a closed
  circuit is zero.
- **Faraday cage**: a metal enclosure inside which the resultant
  electric field is always zero.

## Formulas

::: formel "The chapter's formulas"
**Charge**

$$
Q = n \cdot q_e
$$

where

- *Q* = total charge (C)
- *n* = number of excess or missing electrons
- $q_e$ = the elementary charge $\approx 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}$ C

**Coulomb's law**

$$
F = k \cdot \frac{|Q_1| \cdot |Q_2|}{r^2}
$$

where

- *F* = electric force (N)
- *k* = Coulomb's constant $\approx 8.99 \cdot 10^9\ \mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C^2}$
- $|Q_1|$, $|Q_2|$ = magnitude of the charges (C), signs are ignored
- *r* = distance between the charges (m)

**Electric current and Kirchhoff's first law**

$$
I = \frac{Q}{t}
\qquad\qquad
\sum I_\text{in} = \sum I_\text{out}
$$

where

- *I* = current (A)
- *Q* = charge (C)
- *t* = time (s)

**Voltage and electrical energy**

$$
U = \frac{E}{Q} \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad E = Q \cdot U
$$

where

- *U* = voltage between two points (V)
- *E* = electrical (potential) energy or energy difference (J)
- *Q* = charge (C)

**Ohm's law**

$$
U = R \cdot I
$$

where

- *U* = voltage (V)
- *R* = resistance (Ω)
- *I* = current (A)

**Resistance of a wire**

$$
R = \rho \cdot \frac{l}{A}
$$

where

- *R* = resistance of the wire (Ω)
- *ρ* = resistivity (Ω·m)
- *l* = length of the wire (m)
- *A* = cross-sectional area of the wire (m²)

**Equivalent resistance**

$$
R_\text{tot} = R_1 + R_2 + \ldots \qquad (\text{series})
$$

$$
\frac{1}{R_\text{tot}} = \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2} + \ldots \qquad (\text{parallel})
$$

where

- $R_\text{tot}$ = equivalent resistance (Ω)
- $R_1$, $R_2$, … = resistance 1, resistance 2, … (Ω)

**Electric power**

$$
P = U \cdot I = R \cdot I^2 = \frac{U^2}{R}
$$

where

- *P* = electric power (W)
- *U* = voltage across the component (V)
- *I* = current through the component (A)
- *R* = resistance of the component (Ω)

**Electric field strength**

$$
\mathbb{E} = \frac{F}{Q}
\qquad
\mathbb{E} = k \cdot \frac{Q}{r^2}\ (\text{sphere})
\qquad
\mathbb{E} = \frac{U}{d}\ (\text{plates})
$$

where

- $\mathbb{E}$ = electric field strength (N/C or V/m)
- *F* = electric force on a charge in the field (N)
- *Q* = magnitude of the charge (or of the sphere's charge) (C)
- *r* = distance to the centre of the sphere (m)
- *d* = plate separation (m)

**Potential, voltage and Kirchhoff's second law**

$$
U = |V_A - V_B|
\qquad\qquad
\sum \Delta V = 0
$$

where

- *U* = voltage between point A and point B (V)
- $V_A$, $V_B$ = potential at point A and point B respectively (V)

**Millikan's oil drop experiment**

$$
Q = \frac{m \cdot g \cdot d}{U}
$$

where

- *Q* = charge on the oil drop (C)
- *m* = mass of the oil drop (kg)
- *g* = gravitational field strength (N/kg)
- *d* = plate separation (m)
- *U* = voltage between the plates (V)
:::

## Important relationships and methods

- According to Newton's third law, the electric force between two charges is
  always equally large on both charges, regardless of whether the charges themselves are
  of different sizes.
- When calculating with Coulomb's law: ignore the signs of the charges in the
  calculation itself. Instead, determine the direction of the force by reasoning: like
  signs repel, unlike signs attract.
- The direction of current is defined from plus to minus, opposite to
  the actual direction of motion of the electrons.
- The ammeter (A) is connected in series with the component and should have a low resistance.
  The voltmeter (V) is connected in parallel with the component and should have a high
  resistance. Neither meter should then affect the current it measures.
- Series connection: the current is the same through all components, and the voltages across them
  add up to the battery voltage. If one component breaks, the whole
  circuit is broken.
- Parallel connection: the voltage is the same across all components, and the branch currents
  add up to the total current. If one component breaks, the others
  continue to work unchanged.
- More resistors in series increase the total resistance. More resistors in
  parallel decrease the total resistance: the equivalent resistance is
  always smaller than the smallest individual resistance.
- Complex circuits are solved step by step: reduce parts of the circuit (for example a
  parallel connection) to an equivalent resistance before Ohm's law and
  Kirchhoff's laws are used on the rest of the circuit.
- Potential walk: start at an earthed point where $V = 0$. Going through a
  battery from the negative terminal to the positive terminal → the potential increases by *U*; from
  the positive terminal to the negative terminal → it decreases by *U*. Going through a resistor against
  the direction of the current → the potential increases by $R \cdot I$; with the direction of the current →
  it decreases by $R \cdot I$.
- Inside a Faraday cage, the resultant electric field is always zero.
  That is why you are safe in a car during a thunderstorm thanks to the metal body, not the
  insulating tyres.
- Millikan's oil drop experiment showed that all charge is a whole-number multiple
  of the elementary charge $1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}$ C.

## Before the test

- Can you explain the difference between a conductor, an insulator and a semiconductor, and
  what induction means?
- Do you know Coulomb's law, and can you determine the direction of the force from the
  signs of the charges?
- Do you know the definition of current ($I = Q/t$) and Kirchhoff's first law?
- Do you know the definition of voltage ($U = E/Q$) and Ohm's law ($U = R \cdot I$)?
- Do you know the formula for the resistance of a wire and how length, area and material
  affect the resistance?
- Can you draw circuit diagrams and decide how an ammeter and a
  voltmeter should be connected?
- Can you calculate the equivalent resistance of resistors in series and parallel,
  also in complex (mixed) circuits?
- Do you know the three formulas for electric power and when to use
  which one?
- Do you know the three formulas for electric field strength (the definition, a charged
  sphere, between two plates)?
- Can you do a potential walk in a circuit and use Kirchhoff's second law to
  determine an unknown current?
- Do you know why the resultant electric field is always zero inside a
  Faraday cage?
- Can you describe Millikan's oil drop experiment and what it proved about
  the elementary charge?
