# Polar form

A complex number in the form $z = a + bi$ is said to be in **rectangular form**. We have
seen that a complex number can also be represented by an arrow in the complex
plane. The length $r$ of the arrow is the same as the **modulus of $z$**, which
is written $|z|$.

::: formel "Modulus"
$$
|z| = r = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}
$$
:::

The angle $v$ that the arrow makes with the positive real axis is called
**the argument of $z$** and is written $\arg z$. The argument is found using the tangent, but
you have to think about which **quadrant** the number lies in.

::: formel "Argument"
$$
\arg z = v \qquad\qquad \tan v = \frac{b}{a}
$$
:::

If $z$ lies in the first or fourth quadrant ($a > 0$), the calculator's
$\tan^{-1}$ gives the correct angle directly. If $z$ lies in the second or third quadrant
($a < 0$), you have to add $180^\circ$ (or $\pi$) to the calculator's answer
to end up on the right side.

Complex numbers can be described in a form other than $a + bi$ which makes them
easy to interpret as arrows with a length and an angle: the so-called
**polar form**.

::: formel "Polar form"
$$
z = r(\cos v + i\sin v)
$$
:::

The relationships between the forms follow directly from the right-angled triangle that
the arrow forms with the axes: $a = r\cos v$ and $b = r\sin v$.

## Modulus and distance

The modulus also measures distance in the complex plane. On the real number line,
$|-3 - 0| = |-3| = 3$ is the distance between $-3$ and $0$, and $|-3 - 1| = |-4| = 4$
the distance between $-3$ and $1$. In the same way, in the complex
plane:

::: formel "Modulus and distance"
$$
|z - w| \text{ is the distance between } z \text{ and } w
$$
:::

::: exempel "Example 1: From polar to rectangular form"
**Write $4\sqrt{2}(\cos 135^\circ + i\sin 135^\circ)$ in the form $a + bi$.**

::: handskrift
typ: polartillrekt
:::

::: textlosning
We expand the expression by multiplying in the factor $4\sqrt{2}$:

$$
4\sqrt{2}\cos 135^\circ + 4\sqrt{2}\,i\sin 135^\circ
$$

From the table we have $\cos 135^\circ = -\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$ and
$\sin 135^\circ = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$. Substituting gives:

$$
4\sqrt{2}\cdot\left(-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right) + 4\sqrt{2}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,i
= -\frac{4\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{2}} + \frac{4\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{2}}\,i = -4 + 4i
$$

**Answer:** $-4 + 4i$
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 2: Read off an arrow and write it in polar form"
**An arrow points to the point $z = 5 - 4i$. Write $z$ in polar form.**

::: handskrift
typ: rekttillpolar
:::

::: textlosning
We compare with the form $z = a + bi$ and see that $a = 5$ and $b = -4$. The number
lies in the fourth quadrant ($a > 0$), so $\tan^{-1}$ gives the correct angle directly.

The modulus:

$$
r = |z| = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2} = \sqrt{5^2 + (-4)^2} = \sqrt{25 + 16} = \sqrt{41} \approx 6.4
$$

The argument:

$$
\tan v = \frac{b}{a} = \frac{-4}{5} \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad v = \tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{-4}{5}\right) \approx -38.7^\circ
$$

Substituting $r = \sqrt{41}$ and $v \approx -38.7^\circ$ into
$z = r(\cos v + i\sin v)$ gives:

$$
z = \sqrt{41}\,(\cos(-38.7^\circ) + i\sin(-38.7^\circ))
$$

**Answer:** $z = \sqrt{41}\,(\cos(-38.7^\circ) + i\sin(-38.7^\circ))$
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 3: Interpret a modulus equation as a circle"
**Mark in the complex plane the numbers $z$ for which $|z + (-3 - 2i)| = 2$.**

::: handskrift
typ: absolutcirkel
:::

::: textlosning
To make the interpretation easier we rewrite the expression inside the modulus as a
**difference**. We take out a minus sign and change the signs inside
the bracket:

$$
|z - (3 + 2i)| = 2
$$

According to the relationship for distance, $|z - w|$ means the distance between $z$ and $w$.
Here $w = 3 + 2i$, so we are to mark all points whose distance to
$3 + 2i$ is exactly $2$. This corresponds to a **circle** with centre
$3 + 2i$ and radius $2$.

**Answer:** A circle with centre $3 + 2i$ and radius $2$
:::
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Modulus"
- $|z| = r = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$ is the **length** of the arrow.
- Follows from Pythagoras' theorem in the right-angled triangle the arrow forms with the axes.
:::

::: sampunkt "Argument"
- $\arg z = v$ is the angle to the **positive real axis**, and is found from $\tan v = \dfrac{b}{a}$.
- If $z$ lies in the first or fourth quadrant ($a > 0$), the calculator gives the correct angle directly.
- If $z$ lies in the second or third quadrant ($a < 0$), $180^\circ$ must be added.
- **Always check the quadrant** before you answer.
:::

::: sampunkt "Polar form"
- $z = r(\cos v + i\sin v)$, compared with the **rectangular** form $z = a + bi$.
- The relationships are $a = r\cos v$ and $b = r\sin v$.
- Polar form makes the number easy to interpret as an arrow with a length and an angle.
:::

::: sampunkt "Modulus as distance"
- $|z - w|$ is the **distance** between $z$ and $w$ in the complex plane.
- $|z - w| = k$ therefore describes a **circle** with centre $w$ and radius $k$.
- Rewrite the expression as a difference first: $|z + (-3 - 2i)|$ becomes $|z - (3 + 2i)|$.
:::
