# Velocity selector and mass spectrometer

## Velocity selector

A device for filtering out charged particles with particular [[velocities|Change of position per unit time, with direction (a vector). SI unit m/s.]]
is called a **velocity selector**.

::: formel "Velocity in a velocity selector"
$$
v = \frac{\mathbb{E}}{B}
$$

where

- *v* = velocity of the particle (m/s)
- $\mathbb{E}$ = [[electric field|Region around a charge where another charge experiences an electric force.]] (V/m)
- *B* = magnetic flux density (T)

::: härledning "Derivation: Velocity in a velocity selector"
Electrons are fired from an electron gun and accelerated by an
accelerating voltage *U* to a velocity *v*. The electrons pass two
electrically charged parallel plates that generate a downward
electric field $\mathbb{E}$, which in turn is located in a perpendicular
uniform magnetic field *B* directed into the plane as in the figure below.

For electrons that pass in a straight line through a hole in a screen on the other
side, the upward electric force $F_\mathrm{e}$ is equal in
size to the downward magnetic force $F_\mathrm{m}$. Then

$$
F_\mathrm{e} = F_\mathrm{m}
$$

From earlier theory we have $F_\mathrm{e} = q \cdot \mathbb{E}$ and
$F_\mathrm{m} = q \cdot v \cdot B$, which gives

$$
q \cdot \mathbb{E} = q \cdot v \cdot B
$$

We divide both sides by *q* and then solve for the velocity *v*.

$$
\mathbb{E} = v \cdot B \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad v = \frac{\mathbb{E}}{B} \quad \text{QED}
$$
:::
:::

Only electrons with the velocity $v = \dfrac{\mathbb{E}}{B}$ will
pass through the velocity selector.

By adjusting the electric field $\mathbb{E}$ and the magnetic
flux density *B* we can filter out particles with particular velocities!

## Mass spectrometer

A velocity selector used to determine the mass of unknown
(charged) particles is called a **mass spectrometer**.

::: formel "Mass with a mass spectrometer"
$$
m = \frac{q \cdot B \cdot r}{v}
$$

where

- *m* = mass of the particle (kg)
- *q* = charge of the particle (C)
- *B* = magnetic flux density (T)
- *r* = radius of curvature (m)
- *v* = velocity of the particle (m/s)

::: härledning "Derivation: Mass with a mass spectrometer"
After the velocity selector has filtered out particles with a particular
velocity, we let them leave the electric field, and they will then
be affected only by a magnetic field. The magnetic force on
the particle will then act as a [[centripetal force|The force needed to keep an object in circular motion, directed towards the centre of the circle.]] (just as when
the electron beam was bent in the electron tube when we determined the mass
of the electron).

If a photographic plate is placed where the particle leaves the electric field,
it can record where the particle hits, and from that
the radius of the particle's path can be determined. See the figure below.

The magnetic force $F_\mathrm{m}$ acts as a centripetal force
$F_\mathrm{c}$, so

$$
F_\mathrm{m} = F_\mathrm{c}
$$

From earlier theory we have $F_\mathrm{m} = q \cdot v \cdot B$ and
$F_\mathrm{c} = \dfrac{m \cdot v^2}{r}$. This gives

$$
q \cdot v \cdot B = \frac{m \cdot v^2}{r}
$$

We divide both sides by *v* and solve for the mass *m*.

$$
q \cdot B = \frac{m \cdot v}{r} \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad m = \frac{q \cdot B \cdot r}{v} \quad \text{QED}
$$
:::
:::

Since the velocity *v* can easily be determined with a velocity selector,
we can in this way easily **weigh charged particles**!

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Velocity selector"
- A device that filters out charged particles with **one particular
  velocity**.
- An electric and a magnetic field are placed **perpendicular** to
  each other, so that the forces point in opposite directions.
:::

::: sampunkt "The condition"
- Particles that go straight through have
  $F_e = F_m$, that is,
  $q \mathbb{E} = q v B$.
- The charge cancels and what remains is
  $v = \dfrac{\mathbb{E}}{B}$.
- **Only the speed matters**, not the charge or the mass. All particles with
  the right speed pass.
- By adjusting $\mathbb{E}$ and $B$ we choose which speed is let
  through.
:::

::: sampunkt "Mass spectrometer"
- A velocity selector followed by a pure magnetic field, to **weigh**
  charged particles.
- Without the electric field the magnetic force becomes a
  **centripetal force** and the path curves.
- A photographic plate records where the particle hits, which gives
  the radius of the path.
:::

::: sampunkt "The formula"
- Set $F_m = F_C$, that is,
  $q v B = \dfrac{m v^{2}}{r}$.
- The speed cancels once and what remains is
  $m = \dfrac{q B r}{v}$.
- Heavier particles get a **larger** radius of the path, since they are harder to
  deflect.
:::
:::
