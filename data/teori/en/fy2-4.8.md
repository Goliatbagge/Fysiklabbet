# Bohr's atomic model and energy levels

According to **Bohr's atomic model**, electrons are found in orbits that
represent different energy states, and it is these that give the unique
spectrum of an element.

When an electron is **de-excited** (moves to an orbit with lower energy),
a [[photon|A quantum of electromagnetic radiation. Carries the energy *E* = *h* · *f*, where *h* is Planck's constant.]] is emitted (sent out) with an energy that corresponds to the decrease
in the electron's [[potential energy|The energy an object has because of its position in a force field, for example *E*ₚ = *m* · *g* · *h* in a gravitational field.]].

When the electron falls from level 2 to level 1, a photon is emitted with an energy that corresponds to the difference between the levels.
:::

::: formel "Energy in a quantum jump"
$$
h \cdot f = E_n - E_m
$$

alternatively

$$
\frac{h \cdot c}{\lambda} = E_n - E_m
$$

where

- $h \cdot f = \dfrac{h \cdot c}{\lambda}$ = energy of the emitted photon (J)
- *h* $\approx 6.626 \cdot 10^{-34}$ Js ([[Planck's constant|The natural constant *h* ≈ 6.626 · 10⁻³⁴ J·s. Links the energy of a photon to its frequency.]])
- *f* = [[frequency|Number of oscillations per second. SI unit hertz (Hz) = 1/s.]] of the light (Hz)
- *c* $\approx 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}$ m/s ([[speed|Displacement per unit time, with direction (a vector). SI unit m/s.]] of light in a vacuum)
- $E_n$ = energy at the higher energy level (J)
- $E_m$ = energy at the lower energy level (J)
:::

The lowest energy state of the atom, when the electron is in the innermost
allowed orbit closest to the nucleus, is called the **ground state**. If the electron
absorbs energy and is **excited** (moves to an orbit above
the ground state), this is called an **excited state**. The energy
required to free an electron completely from the nucleus is called the
**ionisation energy**. For the hydrogen atom the ionisation energy is **13.6 eV**
above the ground state.

These states are illustrated in **energy level diagrams**, where
**the ionisation limit is chosen as the zero level** for the energy of the atom. All
energy states in which the electron is bound to the nucleus then have **negative
values**.

::: formel "Energy levels of the hydrogen atom"
$$
E_n \approx -\frac{13.6}{n^{2}}\ \mathrm{eV}
$$

where

- $E_n$ = energy at energy level *n*
- *n* = energy level (*n* = 1 corresponds to the ground state, *n* = 2 to the first
  excited state, and so on)
:::

::: exempel "Example 1: Light emitted when hydrogen is de-excited from n = 3"
**An electron in a hydrogen atom is excited to energy level 3 and then
de-excited. Use the energy level diagram above and determine:**
**a) which of the lines gives the shortest [[wavelength|The distance between two wave crests (or other identical points) in a wave. Written *λ*. SI unit metre (m).]] of the emitted light**
**b) which wavelengths can be emitted.**

::: handskrift
typ: kvantsprang
:::

::: textlosning
**a)** The line that corresponds to the largest jump in energy will also
emit the light with the greatest energy and therefore the shortest wavelength,
that is, the line from *n* = 3 to *n* = 1.

**Answer:** The line that goes from energy level 3 to energy level 1.

**b)** We solve for the wavelength *λ* in the formula for the energy of a photon

$$
E = \frac{h \cdot c}{\lambda} \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad \lambda = \frac{h \cdot c}{E}
$$

We need to determine the energy *E* of the emitted photon with the formula for
the energy in different quantum jumps, $E = E_n - E_m$. Two combinations of jumps
are possible: from level 3 directly to level 1 (one photon), or from
level 3 to level 2 and then on from level 2 to level 1 (two photons).
We read the energy levels from the diagram above.

**Jump 3 → 1:**

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
h \approx 6.626 \cdot 10^{-34}\ \mathrm{Js} \\
c = 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\ \mathrm{m/s} \\
E = E_3 - E_1 = (-1.51) - (-13.6) = 12.09\ \mathrm{eV} = 12.09 \cdot 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\ \mathrm{J} = 1.936\ldots \cdot 10^{-18}\ \mathrm{J}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
\lambda = \frac{6.626 \cdot 10^{-34} \cdot 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}}{1.936\ldots \cdot 10^{-18}} = 1.0256\ldots \cdot 10^{-7}\ \mathrm{m} \approx 103\ \mathrm{nm}
$$

**Jump 3 → 2:**

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
h \approx 6.626 \cdot 10^{-34}\ \mathrm{Js} \\
c = 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\ \mathrm{m/s} \\
E = E_3 - E_2 = (-1.51) - (-3.40) = 1.89\ \mathrm{eV} = 1.89 \cdot 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\ \mathrm{J} = 3.027\ldots \cdot 10^{-19}\ \mathrm{J}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
\lambda = \frac{6.626 \cdot 10^{-34} \cdot 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}}{3.027\ldots \cdot 10^{-19}} = 6.5608\ldots \cdot 10^{-7}\ \mathrm{m} \approx 656\ \mathrm{nm}
$$

**Jump 2 → 1:**

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
h \approx 6.626 \cdot 10^{-34}\ \mathrm{Js} \\
c = 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\ \mathrm{m/s} \\
E = E_2 - E_1 = -3.40 - (-13.6) = 10.2\ \mathrm{eV} = 10.2 \cdot 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\ \mathrm{J} = 1.634\ldots \cdot 10^{-18}\ \mathrm{J}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
\lambda = \frac{6.626 \cdot 10^{-34} \cdot 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}}{1.634\ldots \cdot 10^{-18}} = 1.215\ldots \cdot 10^{-7}\ \mathrm{m} \approx 122\ \mathrm{nm}
$$

**Answer:** 103 nm (UV), or 656 nm (visible red) and 122 nm (UV).
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Bohr's atomic model"
- The electrons are found in **fixed orbits** that correspond to different
  energy states.
- **Excitation**: the electron absorbs energy and jumps outwards.
- **De-excitation**: the electron falls inwards and emits a **photon**.
:::

::: sampunkt "Energy in a quantum jump"
- $h f = E_n - E_m$, or
  $\dfrac{h c}{\lambda} = E_n - E_m$.
- The energy of the photon corresponds **exactly** to the difference between the levels.
- This is why the spectrum is discrete: only certain jumps are possible.
:::

::: sampunkt "The states"
- **Ground state**: the electron in the innermost allowed orbit.
- **Excited state**: the electron is higher up.
- **Ionisation energy**: the energy required to free the electron
  completely. For hydrogen $13.6\ \mathrm{eV}$.
:::

::: sampunkt "Energy level diagram"
- **The ionisation limit is the zero level**, so all bound states have
  **negative** values.
- $E_n \approx -\dfrac{13.6}{n^{2}}\ \mathrm{eV}$ for hydrogen.
- The levels lie **closer together** the higher up you go.
:::

::: sampunkt "The series for hydrogen"
- **Lyman**: ends at $n = 1$, gives ultraviolet light.
- **Balmer**: ends at $n = 2$, gives **visible** light.
- **Paschen**: ends at $n = 3$, gives infrared.
- The largest energy jump gives the **shortest** wavelength.
:::
:::
