# Conservation of momentum

Since the impulses on two colliding objects are equal but opposite,
the resultant impulse in the system will be 0. Since impulse
corresponds to a change in momentum, this means that the change in
momentum in the system is also 0. **The momentum in a closed system is
therefore [[constant|A value that does not change. For example the acceleration due to gravity *g* or the speed of light *c*.]].**

::: formel "The law of conservation of momentum"
$$
m_A \cdot v_{A0} + m_B \cdot v_{B0} = m_A \cdot v_A + m_B \cdot v_B
$$

where

- $m_A$, $m_B$ = mass of object A and B respectively
- $v_{A0}$, $v_{B0}$ = [[initial velocity|The velocity at the time *t* = 0 (when the motion starts). Written *v*₀.]] of object A and B respectively
- $v_A$, $v_B$ = final velocity of object A and B respectively
:::

A collision in which the objects

- **bounce off each other** is called an **elastic collision**.
- **stick together** is called an **inelastic collision**.

The law of conservation of momentum applies in both these cases.

For inelastic collisions the law of conservation of momentum can be written in a somewhat simpler form.

::: formel "The law of conservation of momentum in a perfectly inelastic collision"
$$
m_A \cdot v_A + m_B \cdot v_B = (m_A + m_B) \cdot v
$$

where

- $m_A$, $m_B$ = mass of object A and B respectively
- $v_A$, $v_B$ = initial velocity of object A and B respectively
- *v* = final velocity (shared after the collision)
:::

**a) What velocity does Boel get?**
**b) How large an impulse was Agnes subjected to?**
**c) They pushed against each other for 0.15 seconds. What average force acted?**

::: handskrift
typ: inlines
:::

::: textlosning
**a)** We define the positive direction as to the right. We set up
the law of conservation of momentum and solve for Boel's velocity $v_B$.

$$
m_A \cdot v_{A0} + m_B \cdot v_{B0} = m_A \cdot v_A + m_B \cdot v_B
\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad
v_B = \frac{m_A \cdot v_{A0} + m_B \cdot v_{B0} - m_A \cdot v_A}{m_B}
$$

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
m_A = 60\ \mathrm{kg} \\
v_{A0} = 0 \\
m_B = 85\ \mathrm{kg} \\
v_{B0} = 0 \\
v_A = 0.50\ \mathrm{m/s}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
v_B = \frac{60 \cdot 0 + 85 \cdot 0 - 60 \cdot 0.50}{85}
= -0.352\ldots\ \mathrm{m/s} \approx -0.35\ \mathrm{m/s}
$$

**Answer:** −0.35 m/s or 0.35 m/s to the left.

**b)** We set up the impulse-momentum theorem.

$$
I = m \cdot \Delta v
$$

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
m = 60\ \mathrm{kg} \\
\Delta v = v_2 - v_1 = (0.50 - 0)\ \mathrm{m/s} = 0.50\ \mathrm{m/s}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
I = 60 \cdot 0.50 = 30\ \mathrm{Ns}
$$

**Answer:** 30 Ns

**c)** We set up the formula for impulse and solve for the [[force|An influence that can change an object's motion or shape. SI unit newton (N). A vector quantity.]] *F*.

$$
I = F \cdot \Delta t \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad F = \frac{I}{\Delta t}
$$

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
I = 30\ \mathrm{Ns} \quad (\text{from part b}) \\
\Delta t = 0.15\ \mathrm{s}
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
F = \frac{30}{0.15} = 200\ \mathrm{N} = 0.20\ \mathrm{kN}
$$

**Answer:** 0.20 kN or 200 N
:::
:::

::: exempel "Example 2: Railway wagons that couple together"
**Two railway wagons collide and couple together after the collision. Wagon A
has a mass of 10 tonnes and moves with a velocity of 5.0 m/s to the right. Wagon B has a mass of 15
tonnes and moves with a velocity of 3.0 m/s to the left. Determine their final
velocity after the collision.**

::: handskrift
typ: tagvagnar
:::

::: textlosning
Since the wagons couple together, it is an **inelastic collision**. We
define the positive direction as to the right, set up the law of conservation of momentum for a
perfectly inelastic collision and solve for the final velocity *v*.

$$
m_A \cdot v_A + m_B \cdot v_B = (m_A + m_B) \cdot v
\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad
v = \frac{m_A \cdot v_A + m_B \cdot v_B}{m_A + m_B}
$$

$$
\left[ \begin{array}{l}
m_A = 10\ \mathrm{tonnes} = 10\,000\ \mathrm{kg} \\
v_A = 5.0\ \mathrm{m/s} \\
m_B = 15\ \mathrm{tonnes} = 15\,000\ \mathrm{kg} \\
v_B = -3.0\ \mathrm{m/s} \quad (\text{minus sign: direction to the left})
\end{array} \right]
$$

$$
v = \frac{10\,000 \cdot 5.0 + 15\,000 \cdot (-3.0)}{10\,000 + 15\,000}
= 0.2\ \mathrm{m/s}
$$

**Answer:** 0.2 m/s (to the right)
:::
:::

::: demo "Think about it: Action films and momentum"
In action films you often see a person (often the hero) steadily
standing still while firing shots at people who fly several metres backwards when
they are hit. Is that reasonable? Since momentum is conserved, the person
who fired the shot should then fly just as far backwards!
:::

::: sammanfattning "Summary"

::: sampunkt "Conservation of momentum"
- In a collision the impulses are equal and opposite, so the total
  impulse is zero.
- **The momentum in a closed system is therefore constant.**
:::

::: sampunkt "The law of conservation of momentum"
- $m_A v_{A0} + m_B v_{B0} = m_A v_A + m_B v_B$
- In other words: the sum of the momenta **before** is equal to the sum
  **after**.
- Applies to all collisions.
:::

::: sampunkt "Elastic and inelastic collisions"
- **Elastic collision**: the objects **bounce** apart and each has its own velocity
  afterwards.
- **Inelastic collision**: the objects **stick together** and continue with a
  shared velocity.
- The law of conservation of momentum applies in both cases.
:::

::: sampunkt "Perfectly inelastic collision"
- $m_A v_A + m_B v_B = (m_A + m_B) \cdot v$
- Solve for the shared velocity:
  $v = \dfrac{m_A v_A + m_B v_B}{m_A + m_B}$.
:::

::: sampunkt "Calculate with signs"
- Decide the **positive direction** first and stick to it.
- A velocity in the other direction is substituted with a **minus sign**.
- A negative answer only means that the motion is in the negative direction.
:::

::: sampunkt "Pushing apart, for example on inline skates"
- Before: both are standing still, so the total momentum is zero.
- After: the momenta must still add up to zero, so they are
  equal and opposite.
- The heavier person therefore gets a **lower** speed.
:::
:::
